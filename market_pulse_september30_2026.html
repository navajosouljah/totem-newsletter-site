Brent Below $100 as Mideast Recovers. PCE Delivers the Savers’ Answer Today.

The Quick Read

Middle East crude exports recovered to 98% of pre-war levels by Tuesday, pulling Brent from Monday’s $106+ open to approximately $97 Wednesday morning - an $8-9 swing that takes oil below $100 for the first time since the Hormuz escalation. The Conference Board’s September consumer confidence index fell to 81.9 - its lowest since 2014 and 7.3 points below the 89.2 consensus - as households priced fuel costs and rate pressure into their six-month outlook simultaneously with the supply signal. Both vectors point to the same release: August PCE at 8:30 AM ET this morning. Cheaper crude narrows the energy component of August PCE; weaker consumer confidence is the demand-side data the Fed has been watching for months.

August PCE at 8:30 AM ET is the last print before October 28. July ran 3.7% YoY headline and 3.3% core; consensus expects 3.4% core and 3.7% headline for August. Three paths: core above 3.0% holds October hike odds near 73% and confirms the majority waiting case for savers; between 2.5% and 3.0% keeps the majority wait intact; below 2.5% core - made more plausible today by Brent at $97 and the consumer confidence miss - is the first scenario this cycle that genuinely tests whether October odds can fall below 70%. Q2 GDP third estimate releases alongside PCE at 8:30 AM ET; prior second estimate: +1.5% annualized.

Consumer confidence at 81.9 is the slow-building layer PCE cannot resolve today. The Expectations Index sub-component fell to 63.6 - its third consecutive monthly decline - with future income and spending views deteriorating against $4.49 gas and 22.40% APR card rates. Friday’s September payrolls closes the employment side: above 150,000 jobs with hot PCE means Q4 dual-pressure confirmed; below 100,000 with soft PCE is the first dual-softening signal of this cycle.
