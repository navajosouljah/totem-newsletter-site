Nasdaq Closes at a Record. Bitcoin Near $85K on Biggest ETF Inflow in 11 Months.

The Quick Read

The S&P 500 posted its best session since early August on Monday - up 1.5% to 7,764.70 - while the Nasdaq climbed 2.3% to a record 27,122.09, carried by chip stocks and the same three signals driving markets since September 16: WTI extending its slide below $95, the 10-year Treasury retreating from 5.01% to approximately 4.95%, and diplomatic engagement on Iran holding. Bitcoin amplified the move, hitting $87,000 Monday on $999 million in ETF inflows - the largest single-day total in 11 months - as institutions treated the CLARITY Act failure as a buying window, not a verdict. That is the mechanism: risk appetite returns when the geopolitical premium reprices and the rate ceiling softens, and all three assets moved on the same signal.

The week's pivotal events are the Trump-Xi bilateral summit Thursday September 24 - Xi arrives Washington Wednesday September 23 - and PCE inflation on Friday September 25, the Federal Reserve's preferred measure. PCE consensus expects core at approximately +0.21% month-over-month, which holds November hike odds near 60-65% without raising them further. A core reading at or above +0.3% MoM near-locks November; below +0.2% starts softening the path. The Trump-Xi summit carries the week's highest uncertainty: a tariff-truce signal is directly positive for technology, semiconductors, and copper; a breakdown reverses the current optimism trade. The Freddie Mac PMMS also releases Thursday - its first survey window with the 10-year back below 5%.

Two slow pressures continue. The CD reprice window enters Day 6 at 4.50% APY - the best 1-year rate available, closing approximately September 26-28 as traditional banks finish matching. And oil's slide persists: WTI at $93.18 this morning is down approximately $10-12 from the September 11 disruption peak near $105, tracking to an October pump window near $4.25-4.35 per gallon if the diplomatic hold sustains.
