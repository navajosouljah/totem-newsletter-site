Market Pulse • Tuesday, August 25, 2026

Iran Oil Exports Near Zero as Brent Falls to $91 on Resolution Signals - Corn Drops to 57% G/E. S&P Closes 7,653, Chip Stocks Lead Decline. Bitcoin Surges to $80,200 as Nvidia Reports Wednesday.

Warsh keynotes Friday as corn, energy, and trade policy all trend hawkish. Wednesday's Nvidia close is the one catalyst that can break the Nasdaq's two-week slide before he speaks.

The Quick Read

Iran's central bank governor confirmed Monday that oil exports have effectively fallen to zero under the new US sanctions package - the revenue compression that Monday's "Economic D-Day" announcement was designed to force. Yet Brent fell further to $91 Tuesday rather than rising: zero export revenue raises the probability of a forced Oman-mediated negotiation rather than Iran's threatened strait closure. The mechanism is direct: no export income makes the sanctions-relief framework Iran's rational alternative to a military confrontation that forfeits the same revenue. Brent at $91 is the market's probabilistic average between rapid resolution and the closure scenario that would spike prices above $100. That is the whole mechanism.

Monday's USDA crop progress report confirmed the week's first new hawkish data point: corn dropped to 57% good-to-excellent, a 3-point decline from August 16's 60% and the first back-to-back weekly deterioration since late July. That is a third concurrent CPI input alongside Brent near $91 and gas at approximately $4.10. Today is the last positioning session before Nvidia reports Wednesday after close. Q3 hyperscaler AI capex guidance - not the revenue beat - is the number the Nasdaq's direction depends on heading into Warsh's Friday keynote at Jackson Hole.

BTC surged to $80.2K Tuesday while XRP pulled back to $1.48, partly unwinding the CLARITY Act premium that drove XRP's divergence Friday and Monday. BTC's advance is institutional-ETF-driven, not legislative. The September 15 convergence - CLARITY Act floor vote, FOMC Day 1, Canada tariff day seven - is 21 days out and unchanged. Canada's September 8 retaliatory tariffs arrive in 14 days: the first trade-policy inflation that registers in September CPI rather than August, compressing the data window the FOMC has to assess trade-policy pass-through before November.

How Tuesday Opens

  • Why Brent fell to $91 when Iran's oil exports are near zero - the paradox the energy section explains. Zero Iranian export revenue should tighten global supply and push Brent higher. It didn't because the market is reading zero revenue as the mechanism that accelerates a forced Oman-mediated negotiation rather than the complete closure scenario Rezaei threatened. Saudi Arabia and the UAE have also provided incremental production compensation. The real threshold to watch: whether Bessent activates secondary sanctions specifically targeting Iran's largest buyers - China and India collectively absorb the vast majority of Iranian crude. That escalation would remove Saudi compensation as the offset and force the bifurcated read to resolve in one direction. Brent at $91 is pricing the probability that the secondary sanction trigger has not yet been pulled.
  • Two consecutive G/E declines to 57% - what the September WASDE revision probability looks like now. The August 12 WASDE confirmed corn yield at 180.7 bpa, already below the 183 bpa July estimate and below analyst consensus of 182.4 bpa. Historically, two consecutive weekly good-to-excellent declines below 60% through late August have corresponded with USDA WASDE downward yield revisions in September. A September WASDE revision below 180 bpa would push corn futures above the current $4.84/bushel and add a second food-channel CPI escalation landing in the September data window - simultaneously with Canada's September 8 retaliatory tariffs and just before August CPI on September 11. The crop deterioration is not over: the final kernel-fill weeks before harvest remain the highest drought-stress window of the growing season, and the drought footprint has not materially contracted.
  • What Nvidia needs to say Wednesday - not what it needs to earn. The revenue beat ($93-95 billion vs. $91 billion management guide) is already priced into chip stocks. The language that breaks the Nasdaq's two-week derating: Q3 hyperscaler AI capex guidance confirming that Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta are accelerating - not pausing - their $775-800 billion in 2026 combined capex commitments despite elevated rates and two active trade escalations. Micron fell 5.8% Monday, AMD dropped more than 3%, Broadcom shed more than 2% - the derating is in the chip subsector specifically, not in broad equities. A confident Q3 guide from Jensen Huang reverses that specific subsector pressure before Warsh speaks Friday. A cautious guide acknowledging rate-sensitivity in hyperscaler capex decisions extends the derating and hands Warsh an actively deteriorating Nasdaq as his Jackson Hole backdrop.
  • Bitcoin at $80.2K while Nasdaq closed at 25,980 - two different audiences trading two different September theses. The equity market is selling chip stocks on rate-and-capex-sensitivity concerns. The institutional crypto market is buying BTC through spot ETF flows because Bitcoin at this stage of the cycle has a different rate-sensitivity profile than semiconductor equities: the same institutional players reducing Nasdaq exposure are adding BTC exposure as a non-correlated real asset. BTC's $2,600 move from Monday's $77,578 to $80,200+ is not a risk-on signal in the traditional sense - it is a specific institutional reallocation. XRP pulling back to $1.48 while BTC extended confirms the CLARITY Act legislative premium that drove XRP's Friday and Monday divergence has partially deflated, while BTC's advance is independent of any single legislative binary.
  • Warsh has evaded every guidance opportunity since May - Friday changes the calculus. Since taking office, Warsh has shortened the post-meeting statement, given deliberately evasive press conference answers on the September path, and curtailed forward guidance at every available opportunity. Jackson Hole is structurally different: it is his first formal keynote as Fed Chair on the most-watched monetary policy stage, 19 days before September 15-16, with three concurrent hawkish CPI inputs (Brent, corn, Canada tariffs) and hike odds near 50-55%. The conditional framework - specifying exactly what data combination determines September - is his most powerful option in this environment: it buys time through the September 4 jobs report and September 11 CPI without pre-committing to either direction. A keynote as evasive as his prior press conferences would be the week's most consequential communication failure.
