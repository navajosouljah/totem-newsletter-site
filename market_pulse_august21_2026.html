Friday Briefing • Friday, August 21, 2026

Bitcoin Tops $77K on a Second Day of $600M+ ETF Inflows. Walmart Beats Q2 Revenue by $1 Billion but Q3 Guidance Misses Consensus - Stock Falls. Mortgage Rates 6.65%, First Decline Since Hormuz Began.

Yields reversed every basis point of Wednesday's buyback relief by Thursday's close. The consumer is slowing, and Bitcoin doesn't care.

The Quick Read

The most consequential move Thursday was in yields, not equities. The 30-year Treasury reversed from 5.19% back to 5.25% and the 10-year returned to 4.70%, erasing all of Wednesday's buyback-driven compression. The S&P 500 closed at 7,715.47 (+0.31%), Dow at 53,453.98 (+0.21%), and Nasdaq at 26,342.08 (+0.20%) - volatile intraday, but Walmart's revenue beat anchored sentiment enough to close green. Bitcoin did not follow yields higher: it extended to $77,000 Friday morning as ETF inflows reached $606 million Thursday (up from Wednesday's $517 million) and August's cumulative BTC ETF total hit $2.07 billion, a 2026 high. When crypto extends on a day Treasury yields fully reverse, the institutional bid is structural, not rate-driven. That is the whole mechanism.

Walmart delivered the consumer verdict the week needed: $187.9 billion in revenue (beat $186.8B consensus), $0.81 non-GAAP EPS (beat $0.74), US comp sales +2.6% confirming trade-down dynamics, eCommerce +23%. But Q3 revenue guidance of $185.6 billion came in 1.4% below estimates, and the stock fell despite the beat. A retailer gaining trade-down share from weaker competitors and still guiding Q3 below consensus is signaling that the slowdown has volume, not just sentiment. Freddie Mac confirmed a PMMS of 6.65% Thursday noon - down 2 basis points from 6.67%, the first downward move in mortgage rates since the Hormuz crisis began.

Brent holds near $93 on Day 8 of the Hormuz standoff, but CBSNews reported Thursday evening that Iran-Oman talks are "getting close" to a new framework - the first credible diplomatic signal since the MOU expired August 13. A deal before Jackson Hole (August 27-29) rewrites the September FOMC calculus: lower oil, lower August CPI, compressed September hike odds from 31%. Warsh's August 28 keynote is next week's defining event regardless of whether Hormuz resolves first.

How Friday Opens

  • What the yield reversal means for November 4. The 30-year at 5.25% - having fully reversed Wednesday's buyback-driven compression to 5.19% in one session - shows the buyback ceiling controls direction of advance, not level. Treasury's $4 billion per operation commitment through November 4 means the 30-year should not breach 5.33% again while the program runs. But the equilibrium has shifted from the pre-Hormuz 4.80-5.00% range to 5.19-5.30%, and it reverts to market-clearing the day the program ends. Any corporate borrower pricing 30-year debt this quarter is pricing against an artificially held rate - the real constraint on forward debt cost is the November 4 expiration, not today's yield.
  • The crypto/yield divergence is regime change, not a correlation bounce. Bitcoin gained 11.45% Wednesday as yields fell; it extended to $77,000 Thursday-Friday as yields fully reversed. This is not the real-yield correlation that explained Wednesday's rally - that correlation would have faded when yields rebounded. When institutions add $606 million to BTC ETFs on a day the 30-year climbs back to 5.25%, they are treating Bitcoin as a fixed portfolio weight, not a rate bet. That is a more durable form of institutional support than the rate-channel correlation and it changes the asset's forward behavior. Ethereum's Thursday ETF flow of $221 million - also rising on the yield-reversal day - confirms the pattern extends across the digital asset class.
  • Walmart's $185.6 billion Q3 guidance is the consumer tell the Fed was waiting for. The miss on Q3 revenue guidance (1.4% below estimates) is not about Walmart's competitive position - it is about the household at the margin. When the lowest-price large-format US retailer, which structurally gains trade-down share during consumer stress, guides Q3 revenue below consensus, the consumer deterioration has reached its last defensive anchor. A Walmart revenue guidance miss strengthens the September FOMC hold case more than any single macro print this week: it confirms that the demand destruction the Warsh majority cited in the July minutes is real and accelerating, not anecdotal.
  • What 6.65% actually means for housing - and what it does not. Two basis points of mortgage rate relief from 6.67% to 6.65% is the direction change the housing market needed, but not the magnitude. The builder survey's threshold for meaningful buyer demand recovery is approximately 6.50%. The gap between today's 6.65% PMMS and that threshold is 15 basis points - requiring approximately 45 basis points of 10-year Treasury compression from today's 4.70%, which means Brent below $85 and September hike odds below 15%. The direction changed on Thursday; the housing market's fundamental affordability constraint is unchanged. August existing home sales data (due in late August) will be the first read on whether even the directional shift moves any buyer off the sidelines.
  • The Hormuz diplomatic window has a concrete timeline. The prior US-Iran MOU framework took approximately 14 days to negotiate after Oman's first substantive back-channel signal. If Thursday's "getting close" reporting is accurate and a new framework emerges before Jackson Hole opens August 27, the downstream sequence has asset-class consequences at each step: Brent falls from $93 toward $82-85, August CPI consensus gets revised down, September hike odds compress from 31% toward 15-18%, and Warsh's August 28 keynote frames a post-Hormuz macro rather than a stagflation defense. That full sequence, resolved before September 11, is worth approximately 200-250 basis points in September hike probability compression and $8-10 in Brent.

Household Finance

Walmart's Q2 FY2027 results Thursday morning provided the week's definitive consumer data point - and the read is nuanced. Revenue of $187.9 billion beat the $186.8 billion consensus, non-GAAP EPS of $0.81 beat $0.74, and US comp sales grew 2.6%, confirming the trade-down dynamic that Walmart structurally benefits from when lower-income households consolidate spending at the lowest-price retailer. But Q3 revenue guidance of $185.6 billion came in 1.4% below estimates, the stock fell despite the beat, and Walmart's implicit message is that even trade-down consumers are pulling back - the slowdown has volume, not just sentiment.
