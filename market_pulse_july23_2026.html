Weekly Briefing • Thursday, July 23, 2026

Alphabet Delivers, Tesla Stumbles. Rate-Hike Odds Triple on Oil. Gas Hits $4.09.

The AI trade got its first real report card overnight and it read both ways. Oil is now rewriting the Fed conversation six days before the meeting, and the tariff regime changes at midnight tonight. The tape looks calm. The calendar is anything but.

This Week in the News

  • Alphabet posted $119.8 billion in Q2 revenue, up 24%, with Google Cloud up 82% to $24.8 billion. The stock fell after hours anyway as management raised 2026 capital spending guidance again.
  • Tesla beat on revenue but missed on profit. Revenue of $28.24 billion rose 26%, but adjusted EPS of $0.33 missed the $0.51 estimate and operating margin thinned to 1.4% as capex more than doubled.
  • Fed funds futures now price a 34% chance of a rate hike at next week's meeting, up from 10% a week ago. Oil-fed inflation risk has markets pricing 78% odds of at least a quarter-point increase by September.
  • Brent settled at $94.07, up 3.4% Wednesday, on an eleventh night of strikes and Strait of Hormuz risk. Crude is up 19% since the conflict began, and the national gas average reached $4.09.

The Quick Read

The first AI report cards are in, and the market graded them on spending, not growth. Alphabet grew revenue 24% and still got sold on a bigger capex number; Tesla grew revenue 26% while operating income fell 57%. Oil did the deeper damage: Brent settled at $94.07 and futures now give a rate hike next week a one-in-three probability, up from one-in-ten a week ago. Gas hit $4.09 nationally. Mortgages held at 6.55% with the 30-year Treasury at 5.14%. June home sales ran at a 4.09 million pace while the median price set a record at $440,600. Cattle fell again - the one grocery line moving in the buyer's favor.

The earnings message is a repricing of the AI thesis, not a rejection of it. Alphabet's quarter was strong everywhere - cloud up 82%, operating margin at 34% - and the stock still slipped because investors have stopped paying for growth funded by open-ended capital spending. Tesla is the sharper version of the same story: more revenue, a 1.4% operating margin. Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday, Apple and Amazon Thursday. The spending question follows each of them.

The rate repricing threads everything else. A week ago the July meeting looked like a formality; $94 Brent has put a hike on the table because energy is unwinding the June inflation progress in real time. That keeps CD and savings yields attractive, keeps mortgages pinned near 6.55%, and keeps pressure on households already carrying record car payments and a 15-year high in card delinquency. The strain stays K-shaped: savers are clearing inflation with 4.3% CDs while BNPL borrowers finance groceries.

Stock Market

A flat close hid the after-hours action. The indexes marked time while earnings and oil fought it out.

S&P 500: 7,499 −0.1%
Dow Jones: 52,219 flat
Nasdaq: 25,691 −0.6%
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,960 −0.9%
Fear gauge (VIX): 16.64 −2.4%
Bottom Line
  • A VIX at 16.6 heading into an FOMC meeting with live hike odds, four megacap earnings reports, and a tariff transition is complacency, not calm. The market is priced for everything going right in a week where several things could go wrong.
  • Alphabet delivering a strong quarter and trading lower after hours is the tell on leadership. When good news gets sold because of the spending attached to it, the AI trade is being re-underwritten. The Russell losing 0.9% on the same day shows the rotation into small caps is not absorbing what leaves tech.

Interest Rates

The bond market moved before the Fed did. Yields climbed across the curve as oil repriced the inflation path.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (fourth straight hold; hike odds at July 28-29 meeting now ~34%)
2-year Treasury: 4.26%
10-year Treasury: 4.67% rising
30-year Treasury: 5.14% well above 5%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.55% third straight weekly increase; new print due today
Bottom Line
  • Hike odds tripling in one week is the bond market telling you it no longer trusts "transitory" on energy. The June CPI improvement to 3.5% is already being overtaken by $94 Brent, and next week's statement language will show whether the Fed agrees.
  • For savers the setup stays constructive: elevated yields across the curve keep high-grade bonds and cash genuinely competitive with an equity market priced for perfection. The cost lands on borrowers - a 30-year mortgage at 6.55% against record home prices is the tightest affordability squeeze of the year.

Savings & CD Rates

Top CDs hold near 4.30%, and if next week brings a hike, these rates rise rather than fall.

National avg 1-yr CD: 1.68% APY (FDIC) up from 1.65%
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.30% APY (Newtek Bank; Popular Direct 4.17%, E*TRADE 4.15%)
Best credit union CD: 6.00% APY (Financial Partners CU, 8-month promo); Golden Valley FCU 5.09% 12-month
Best high-yield savings: 4.15% APY (Forbright; CIT Bank 4.10%)
Bottom Line
  • A 4.30% CD against 3.5% CPI still clears inflation by 80 basis points, and the rate-hike repricing works in savers' favor - banks will not cut CD offers while the Fed is debating an increase. The lock-in case has strengthened, not weakened, since last week.

Energy & Gas Prices

Crude rose again and the pump followed. An eleventh night of strikes kept the supply-risk bid alive.

WTI crude: $86.83/bbl +3.0%
Brent crude: $94.07/bbl +3.4%; up 19% since the conflict began
National gas avg: $4.09/gal climbing daily
West Coast (California): $5.57/gal highest in the nation
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): $3.65/gal (Mississippi)
Bottom Line
  • The structural case still points to more supply and lower prices - US leverage has not changed - but every week the Strait of Hormuz stays disrupted pushes that resolution further out. The weekend's diplomacy signals faded, and this leg of the move flows straight into the August inflation data.
  • The K-shaped pump: California at $5.57 pays nearly $2 more per gallon than Mississippi at $3.65. "Gas is at $4.09" understates the strain for the coasts and overstates it for the Gulf - and either way, it is quietly eating the savings-rate recovery from the spring.

Commodities

Gold kept climbing, cattle kept falling, and Europe's heat moved the grain markets.

Gold / oz: $4,135 third straight session of gains
Silver / oz: $59.42 steady
Copper / lb: $6.44 −1.0%
Corn / bushel: $4.62 +1.1%
Soybeans / bushel: $12.33 +13.5¢
Live cattle / cwt: $223.20 −$3.47; down $14 from two weeks ago
Bottom Line
  • Gold at $4,135 with three straight up sessions is the safe-haven bid responding to two uncertainties at once - Hormuz and the Fed. It holds as long as either question stays open.
  • Cattle sliding from $237 to $223 in two weeks is real grocery relief, but the floor has not moved: the US herd remains the smallest since 1951. Meanwhile grain is firming on Europe's record heat - that is the new food-cost risk to watch, not beef.

Crop Weather

The US crop slipped a notch as drought expanded in the western belt. Europe's heat is the bigger global story.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent −1 pt. Silking 59% complete, dough stage 13%.
Soybeans: 66% good-to-excellent +1 pt, second straight weekly improvement.
Wheat: Winter harvest 74% complete. Spring wheat fell to 53% good-to-excellent −5 pts after a hot, dry week; North Dakota dropped 7 points.
Drought: Roughly 19% of US corn acres in drought, with extreme-drought area expanding across the western Corn Belt. Last week's heat depleted soil moisture through the Plains.
Global: Europe's record heatwave cut French maize ratings to 58% good-to-excellent, a 13-year low; the EU wheat estimate fell to 140.8Mt. Black Sea harvest remains strong.
Bottom Line
  • The US crop is still in decent shape, but the trend just flipped: corn slipped a point right as pollination meets an expanding western drought. The next two weekly reports decide whether this is a wobble or a turn - and Europe's losses mean the world has less margin for a US miss than it did a month ago.

Real Estate

June sales fell, the median price set another record, and the distress metrics kept building underneath.

Foreclosure starts (June): 26,217 +20% YoY
H1 2026 foreclosure filings: 227,548 +21% YoY
Completed foreclosures (June): 4,773 +23% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency: 11.88% highest since 2021
Existing-home sales (June): 4.09M ann. −2.4% MoM, below the 4.20M expected
Median existing-home price: $440,600 record; 36th straight month of gains
Sellers cutting asking price: 35.4% near last August's record (Redfin, latest)
Months of supply: 4.6
30-year mortgage: 6.55% elevated
Bottom Line
  • A record median price on falling volume with foreclosure starts up 20% is the frozen, K-shaped market in one line. Prices hold because supply is tight and the closed-sale mix skews to the high end, while FHA borrowers - now 11.88% delinquent - carry the strain. Volumes remain well below 2008-2010 levels, but the direction has been consistent all year.
  • Both of these are true at once: the median sets a record and 35.4% of sellers are cutting their asking price. List prices are meeting reality on the way to closing. Watch the cuts and the foreclosure pipeline, not the median.

Household Finance

Card delinquency at a 15-year high, car payments at a record, and a growing share of BNPL going to groceries.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion +5.9% YoY; avg new-offer APR 23.8%
Card balances 90+ days late: 13.1% highest in roughly 15 years
Avg. new-car payment: $777/mo third consecutive record; avg $44,156 financed at 7.0% APR
84+ month auto loans: 23.9% of new financings record
Subprime auto 60+ days delinquent: 6.11% off January's 32-year record of 6.90%
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY; 29% now use BNPL for groceries
Personal savings rate: 3.0% (May 2026)
Bottom Line
  • The honest read on cards: the stock of serious delinquency is the highest in 15 years, but the flow of new delinquencies flattened last quarter. Stress is elevated, not accelerating - a household sector under strain, not one breaking.
  • Twenty-nine percent of BNPL users now financing groceries, double the share of two years ago, is the bottom leg of the K in one number. And the 3.0% savings rate was built on cheap spring gas that no longer exists - the June reading lands July 30, with $4.09 fuel already working against it.

Crypto

The majors drifted lower while equities marked time. No bid, no panic.

Bitcoin (BTC): $65,153 −0.7%
Ethereum (ETH): $1,909 −0.3%
Solana (SOL): $77.11 flat, testing $77 support
XRP: $1.12 −1.0%
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin holding $65,000 after bouncing off $58,000 support is constructive, but the ceiling is structural: everyone who bought between $75,000 and $126,000 is sitting on a loss, and that overhead supply caps rallies at each resistance level, starting at $67,500.
  • Crypto is trading as a risk asset tied to the same macro questions as equities. The FOMC decision and the megacap earnings will move these prices more than anything happening on-chain this week.

Around the World

Europe made a three-week high on energy and defense. Emerging markets are absorbing the oil shock.

Japan: Nikkei at 66,116 −0.2%, digesting Tuesday's sharp rebound.
Europe: STOXX 600 at 647 +0.6%, highest close since July 6, led by energy and defense shares.
China: Shanghai Composite at 3,867 flat; Hang Seng −1.0%.
Emerging: MSCI EM down 7.6% over the past month oil-importer squeeze; dollar index steady at 101.1.
Bottom Line
  • Europe making highs while emerging markets lose 7.6% in a month is the oil shock sorting winners from losers: energy producers and defense budgets on one side, fuel importers with dollar debt on the other. As long as Brent holds above $90, that divergence widens.
  • Tonight's tariff transition adds a second layer for the same economies. The structure of what replaces the expiring surcharge - permanent rates versus a temporary levy - matters more to emerging-market exporters than the headline number.
