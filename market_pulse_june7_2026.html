Weekly Briefing • June 7, 2026

Tech Selloff Hits Hardest Since October. Jobs Report Kills Rate Cut Hopes.

Broadcom guidance miss triggers a 4.2% Nasdaq plunge, the VIX spikes 40%, and a hot jobs number takes Fed cuts off the table. The 30-year Treasury breaches 5%. Oil climbs on Hormuz fears. Crypto rallies as an outlier.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • Broadcom chip guidance miss triggers tech selloff: Broadcom's Q3 AI chip sales guidance of $16B fell short of the $17.2B estimate, and the company did not raise its full-year AI semiconductor forecast. The S&P 500 sank 2.6% in its worst day since October, and the Nasdaq dropped 4.2%.
  • Strong jobs report kills rate cut hopes: The June jobs report came in hot, eliminating expectations for a Fed rate cut. "Any hopes of a Fed rate cut have effectively been eliminated with this morning's strong jobs report," said Ronald Temple, chief market strategist at Lazard.
  • FOMC meeting June 16-17 with new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh: Warsh took over as Fed Chair on May 15. April CPI remains elevated at 3.8% YoY. Markets now expect no rate change at the upcoming meeting.
  • Gas prices approaching $5 national average: With the Strait of Hormuz still disrupted, gas hit $4.55/gallon nationally. JP Morgan analysts warn $5/gallon is possible in June if Hormuz remains closed.

The Quick Read

This was the week the narrative broke. The AI trade, which had carried markets for months, cracked when Broadcom's guidance missed by $1.2 billion and the company refused to raise its full-year AI chip forecast. The Nasdaq dropped 4.2% - its worst session since October - and the S&P 500 fell 2.6% in a single day. Then a scorching jobs report landed on top, killing whatever remained of the rate-cut thesis. The VIX spiked 40% to 21.51, the fastest fear-gauge move since the regional banking crisis. This is not a correction in search of a catalyst - it is a repricing of two assumptions simultaneously: that AI earnings growth is limitless, and that the Fed is coming to the rescue.

The bond market told the story most clearly. The 30-year Treasury breached 5.00% for the first time - the psychologically important level we flagged last week when it was sitting at 4.99%. The 10-year climbed to 4.54%, and the 2-year dropped to 3.62% as the curve steepened on growth fears meeting sticky inflation. With CPI still at 3.8% YoY and a hot labor market giving Chair Warsh no room to cut, the June 16-17 FOMC meeting is a formality. Rates are staying at 3.50-3.75%, and the market is now pricing in zero cuts through September. Mortgage rates climbed to 6.53%, further freezing the housing market.

Energy added to the pressure. WTI oil rose 3.5% to $93.67 as Hormuz disruptions continued, pushing gas to $4.55 nationally. JP Morgan warned $5/gallon is possible if the strait remains closed. Gold was flat at $4,339, suggesting the safe-haven bid is pausing after a massive run. The one genuine surprise was crypto: Bitcoin rallied 3.5% to $62,970, Ethereum surged 7% to $1,680, and Solana jumped 6.5% - a clear divergence from the risk-off tone in equities. Whether that is a leading indicator or a dead-cat bounce will become clear next week when Warsh speaks.

Stock Market

Broadcom-led selloff delivers the worst week for tech since October. Every major index deep in the red. Fear gauge spikes.

S&P 500: 7,383.74 −2.64%
Dow Jones: 50,866.78 −1.35%
Nasdaq: 25,709.43 −4.18%
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,833.50 −3.47%
Fear gauge (VIX): 21.51 +39.68%
Bottom Line
  • The Nasdaq dropping 4.18% is the headline, but the Russell 2000 falling 3.47% is the more concerning signal. When small caps sell off this hard alongside mega-cap tech, it is not a rotation - it is broad liquidation. There was nowhere to hide this week.
  • The VIX spiking 40% to 21.51 is the fastest single-week move since the regional banking crisis in March 2023. It broke above 20 - the line that separates complacency from concern - and is now signaling that options markets are pricing materially higher risk ahead of the FOMC meeting.
  • The S&P 500 went from record highs above 7,600 two weeks ago to 7,384 - a 2.9% drawdown from the top. That is not a crash, but the speed of the reversal and the catalysts behind it (earnings disappointment + rate repricing) are the kind that tend to have follow-through.

Interest Rates

The 30-year breaches 5%. Hot jobs data eliminates rate-cut expectations. The curve steepens on growth fears meeting sticky inflation.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (effective 3.62%)
2-year Treasury: 3.62%
10-year Treasury: 4.54%
30-year Treasury: 5.00% (breaches key level)
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.53% +5bps
Bottom Line
  • The 30-year Treasury at 5.00% is the number that matters most this week. Last week it sat at 4.99% and we flagged it as a psychologically important level. It has now broken through. This signals that the bond market is repricing term premium - investors are demanding more compensation to hold long-duration government debt.
  • The 2-year at 3.62% dropping below the fed funds effective rate of 3.62% is a flat-to-inverted front end, which historically signals the market believes the next move in rates is down - but the hot jobs report means the Fed will not act on it. This creates a standoff between what the bond market wants and what the data allows.
  • Mortgage rates climbing to 6.53% means the housing freeze deepens. Every 25bps higher eliminates another tranche of potential buyers. The dream of a spring thaw in housing is effectively over for 2026.

Savings & CD Rates

With rate cuts off the table, savers keep earning. But the window will not last forever once the cycle eventually turns.

National avg 1-yr CD: 1.98% APY
Best online savings: up to 4.40% APY (Pibank)
Best credit unions: up to 5.00% APY (DCU, first $1K)
Bottom Line
  • The hot jobs report paradoxically benefits savers: no rate cuts means high-yield savings accounts and CDs keep paying 4%+ for longer. The 242bps spread between the national average CD (1.98%) and the best online savings (4.40%) remains a free lunch for anyone willing to open an account.
  • With the VIX at 21.51 and equities selling off, parking cash in a 4.40% savings account earning a positive real return against 3.8% CPI is not a bad risk-adjusted trade right now. Cash is not trash when the market is repricing risk.

Energy & Gas Prices

Hormuz disruptions now over three months old. Gas approaching $5. Oil climbing on supply fears.

WTI oil / barrel: $93.67 +3.46%
Brent oil / barrel: $96.28 +3.43%
National gas avg / gallon: $4.55
$5/gallon risk: JP Morgan warns possible in June if Hormuz stays closed
Bottom Line
  • Gas at $4.55 nationally is up 31 cents from $4.24 just two weeks ago. That is a pace of increase that puts $5 national gas within reach by mid-June if Hormuz remains closed - exactly what JP Morgan is warning. The psychological impact of $5 gas on consumer sentiment cannot be overstated.
  • WTI at $93.67 and Brent at $96.28 are both climbing toward $100. Brent breaking triple digits would be a headline event that triggers additional consumer anxiety and political pressure. The Hormuz closure is now over three months old with no diplomatic resolution in sight.
  • Energy inflation feeding into a 3.8% CPI while the labor market runs hot creates a stagflationary setup that the Fed has no good tools to address. Cutting rates would stoke inflation further; holding rates does nothing to fix supply-driven energy costs.

Commodities

Gold pauses after a massive run. Silver and cattle pull back. Copper holds steady on industrial demand.

Gold / oz: $4,339.50 +0.06%
Silver / oz: $67.69 −1.82%
Copper / lb: $6.29 +0.37%
Corn / bushel: $4.16¾
Soybeans / bushel: $11.21¼
Cattle / lb (live): $2.42 −3.02%
Bottom Line
  • Gold flat at $4,339 after weeks of aggressive buying suggests the safe-haven trade is pausing to consolidate, not reversing. With the VIX at 21.51 and equities in selloff mode, the fact that gold did not spike higher this week is notable - it may have already priced in the current risk environment.
  • Live cattle dropping 3.02% to $2.42/lb is the sharpest weekly decline in months and may reflect demand destruction as consumers pull back on discretionary spending. When gas is approaching $5 and the stock market is falling, beef is one of the first line items consumers cut.
  • Copper holding at $6.29 (+0.37%) amid a broad risk-off week is a quiet positive for the global growth outlook. Copper does not lie - if a real recession were imminent, it would be falling alongside equities.

Crop Weather

Drought stress expanding across the Plains. Summer heat risk building for row crops.

Corn: Early-season crop development on track, but expanding drought in the central Plains raises the risk of yield loss if conditions persist through pollination in July.
Wheat: Winter wheat harvest beginning in the southern Plains. Spring wheat still developing under mixed moisture conditions in the Northern Plains.
Drought: USDA Drought Monitor shows moderate-to-severe drought expanding across Kansas, Nebraska, Oklahoma, and into parts of Missouri. June heat forecasts are above normal.
Bottom Line
  • Corn at $4.16 and soybeans at $11.21 are relatively subdued, but the drought expanding into Nebraska and Kansas heading into the critical July pollination window is the setup for a speculative rally. If the next two Drought Monitor updates show worsening conditions, grain prices could move 10-15% higher quickly.
  • The combination of drought stress, elevated diesel prices from the Hormuz crisis, and rising input costs is squeezing farm margins on all sides. Smaller operators are most vulnerable - expect consolidation pressure in the Corn Belt if conditions do not improve by August.
  • Cattle dropping 3% this week while drought expands is a demand story overpowering a supply story. Long term, the shrinking herd means higher beef prices into 2027. Short term, consumers are cutting back.

Real Estate

Mortgage rates climbing again. The spring selling season that never was. Distress indicators still elevated.

30-year mortgage rate: 6.53% +5bps
FHA delinquency rate: 11.52% (elevated)
Existing-home sales (annual pace): ~4.02M
Median home price: ~$417,800
% listings cutting price: 17.5%+
Bottom Line
  • With the hot jobs report killing rate-cut hopes, the dream of mortgage rates falling to 6% or below in 2026 is effectively dead. At 6.53% and climbing, the affordability math keeps getting worse: the median home at $417,800 requires roughly $2,830/month in principal and interest alone.
  • FHA delinquency at 11.52% continues to be the canary in the coal mine. These are the most vulnerable borrowers, and one in nine is behind on payments. Rising gas prices and sticky inflation are compounding the stress on lower-income households who are already stretched.
  • The spring selling season is functionally over without a meaningful uptick in transactions. Sales volume stuck at 4.02M annualized with 17.5% of listings cutting prices means the market is frozen in a standoff between sellers who cannot afford to cut prices and buyers who cannot afford current rates.

Household Finance

Rising gas prices and rate-cut disappointment compound the stress on an already stretched consumer.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion (record)
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion; avg household balance $11,507
Avg. credit card APR: 21.52%
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days late: 6.9% (32-year record high)
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (near all-time low)
Bottom Line
  • The jobs report being "too strong" is a double-edged sword for consumers. Employment is holding up, which prevents a recession, but it also means the Fed will not cut rates - so the 21.52% credit card APR, the $770/month car payment, and the 6.53% mortgage rate all persist. The consumer is employed but financially exhausted.
  • Gas approaching $5/gallon on top of $18.8 trillion in household debt and a 2.6% savings rate creates a combustible mix. The average household is spending roughly $200-250/month more on gas than they were a year ago. That money comes directly out of discretionary spending and savings.
  • Subprime auto delinquencies at 6.9% (a 32-year record) alongside record-high stock prices is the clearest illustration of the K-shaped economy. Asset holders are wealthier than ever; borrowers are more stressed than at any point since the Great Financial Crisis.

Crypto

The outlier this week: crypto rallies while equities sell off. Bitcoin, Ethereum, and altcoins all green.

Bitcoin (BTC): $62,969.80 +3.45%
Ethereum (ETH): $1,679.94 +7.09%
Solana: $66.21 +6.46%
XRP: $1.15 +5.63%
Bottom Line
  • Crypto rallying 3-7% while the Nasdaq drops 4.2% is the most notable divergence in months. Bitcoin at $62,970 is reclaiming ground it lost last week, and Ethereum surging 7% suggests this is more than just a dead-cat bounce - there is genuine buying pressure.
  • The decoupling from equities, if it holds, would be the first sustained evidence that crypto is beginning to trade as an alternative asset class rather than a leveraged tech proxy. One week does not make a trend, but the signal is worth watching closely.
  • Ethereum at $1,680 and Solana at $66.21 both rallying 6-7% suggests broad-based demand across L1 protocols, not just a Bitcoin-specific bid. If this is institutional rebalancing out of tech and into crypto as a hedge, it could have legs. If it is retail speculation, it fades next week.

Around the World

Global selloff led by Japan. Nikkei drops 4.2%, mirroring Nasdaq weakness. Europe and China also red.

Japan: Nikkei 225 at 63,802.06 −4.18%. The AI and semiconductor trade that drove Japan to record highs reversed sharply on the Broadcom guidance miss. Nikkei mirrored Nasdaq almost tick for tick.
Europe: Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,062.07 −0.68%. Relatively resilient given the global selloff, but rising energy costs from Hormuz and the ECB's tightening bias continue to weigh.
China: Shanghai Composite at 3,989.29 −0.95%. Slipping back toward the 4,000 level on continued property sector weakness and tepid domestic demand.
Bottom Line
  • The Nikkei dropping 4.18% - exactly matching the Nasdaq - exposes how correlated the Japanese rally has been to the global AI trade. When Broadcom missed, every chip-adjacent market sold off simultaneously. Japan went from 66,856 two weeks ago to 63,802, a 4.6% drawdown in two weeks.
  • Europe outperforming at only -0.68% is partly because it never participated as aggressively in the AI rally and partly because a weaker euro is helping exporters. But Hormuz-driven energy inflation hits European manufacturers harder than any other developed market.
  • China flirting with 4,000 on the Shanghai Composite is a line in the sand for Beijing. A sustained break below that level would likely trigger additional stimulus measures. Property, demographics, and US tech restrictions remain structural headwinds with no near-term resolution.

Upcoming Events

The FOMC with new Chair Warsh is the main event. CPI, retail sales, and options expiration all in the next two weeks.

Jun 12 - May CPI report. Key inflation read ahead of the FOMC. Markets watching for any softening from the 3.8% YoY print.
Jun 13 - May PPI report. Producer-side inflation data.
Jun 16 - May retail sales. Consumer spending pulse check after the gas price surge.
Jun 16-17 - FOMC meeting; rate decision June 17. Kevin Warsh's first meeting as Chair. Hold expected, but forward guidance and dot plot will signal the path for the rest of 2026.
Jun 19 - June options expiration (quad witching). Expect elevated volume and volatility on top of an already volatile market.
Ongoing - Strait of Hormuz crisis remains the biggest swing factor. Gas approaching $5. Any diplomatic breakthrough or further escalation moves markets immediately.
