CD Savers Earn 5.00%. PCE Cooled to 3.4%, October Hike at 35%.

The Quick Read

The Fed's September 15-16 unanimous hike to 3.75-4.00% - the first since 2023 - delivered the CD rate lift savers held out for: top 1-year CDs now pay 5.00% APY. August PCE landed at 3.4% on September 30, below the 3.7% consensus and below the 3.0% core threshold that would have kept October hike odds elevated. Together, the hike and the PCE print reset the October 27-28 calculus: hike odds fell from 66% entering September 15 to 35% as of Thursday morning. The mechanism is straightforward - the Fed moved, inflation came in soft, and the market now prices a pause as more likely than a hike at the next meeting.

Friday's September jobs report at 8:30 AM ET is the first catalyst to shift those October odds. The dot plot's median year-end 2026 rate is 4.1%, implying one more 25-bps hike - either October 27-28 or December. A September payrolls number above 150,000 with wages above 0.3% month-over-month revives the October case; below 100,000 locks in a pause. Q4 opens with an energy story that cuts two ways: Brent fell below $100 in the final days of September as Saudi Arabia's pipeline restoration added 3.5 million barrels of daily capacity, but remains near $97 on residual geopolitical risk.

The CLARITY Act failed its Senate cloture vote 49-50 on September 15 - the same day as the FOMC hike - with no Democrat voting yes despite 126 sponsor concessions. Crypto legislation likely slips to 2027; Congress recesses October 5 with no path to a 2026 re-vote. XRP dropped 9% to $1.28 on the failure and has recovered to near $1.50. Bitcoin's institutional ETF floor held at $83K but daily inflows slowed from nearly $1 billion to $134 million. Gold fell 4.7% in September as dollar strength on the rate path outweighed any war-day safe-haven bid - settling at $4,182 Thursday morning.
