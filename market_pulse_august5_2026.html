Wednesday Briefing • Wednesday, August 5, 2026

S&P 500 and Dow Both Log Record Closes as Palantir Surges 29% on 93% Revenue Growth and Enterprise AI Demand Arrives in the Income Statement. SpaceX Beats Revenue at $7.8 Billion but Falls 9% After-Hours on $18.4 Billion in Quarterly Capex. AMD Records a $11.5 Billion Quarter with Data Center Up 107%, Then Falls 9% After-Hours Anyway. September Hike Odds Fall to 56% After Soft JOLTS. ADP July Payrolls and ISM Services Release This Morning.

Tuesday delivered the cleanest day of earnings season so far: the S&P 500 rose 1.79% to a new record at 7,736.52 and the Dow jumped 907 points to 54,085.88 as Palantir's $1.935 billion quarter confirmed that enterprise AI spending is accelerating faster than analysts have consistently modeled. The overnight picture is more complicated. SpaceX beat revenue expectations by 14% and still fell 9% after-hours - the market's verdict on $18.4 billion in quarterly capital expenditures. AMD reported record revenue with data center up 107% and also fell 9% after-hours, as investors weighed whether guidance for the next quarter justified the stock's premium. Today: ADP July private payrolls at 8:15 AM ET and ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET - the two most direct previews of Friday's jobs report.

The Quick Read

What Tuesday changed: The S&P 500 closed at 7,736.52, up 1.79% - a new all-time record. The Dow added 907.47 points to 54,085.88 - also a new record. Both moves were driven primarily by Palantir's Q2 earnings: $1.935 billion in revenue, up 93% year-over-year, beating the consensus by approximately $123 million. US commercial revenue grew 149% to $764 million. The company raised full-year guidance from $7.65 billion to $8.15 billion, implying 82% annual growth. The stock gained 29% in a single session - its best day in more than two years. The number that separates Palantir's quarter from prior AI-narrative quarters is that the growth showed up in revenue from actual enterprise customers paying for AI software, not in model launches or partnership announcements.

The after-hours picture reset Wednesday's opening. SpaceX reported $7.8 billion in Q2 revenue - up 92% year-over-year and 14% above the $6.81 billion consensus. Starlink's connectivity segment generated $4.29 billion. The AI compute segment produced $2.56 billion, up 247% year-over-year. Adjusted EBITDA reached $3.5 billion, up 191%. The net loss narrowed to $541 million. None of that prevented the stock from reversing 8.56% in after-hours trading to $114.60 after gaining 9.43% in the regular session. The reason: $18.4 billion in quarterly capital expenditures. That number - larger than the annual infrastructure budgets of most countries - is the market's core concern about SpaceX's long-term free cash flow trajectory, regardless of how fast the top line is growing.

AMD added to the overnight headwinds. The company reported record revenue of $11.5 billion for Q2, up 50% year-over-year, with data center up 107% to $6.7 billion. EPS came in at $1.66 against a $1.55 estimate. Guidance for Q3 was $13 billion at the midpoint - strong by any prior measure. The stock fell 8.94% after-hours to $472.20 anyway. The market's message was not that AMD's quarter was bad. It was that AMD's valuation already required a $12 billion quarter, not an $11.5 billion one, regardless of year-over-year growth rate.

On the labor front: JOLTS June job openings came in at 7.359 million on Tuesday, slightly below the approximately 7.4 million consensus. The "low-hire, low-fire" characterization from the Indeed Hiring Lab captures the labor market's current state - hiring rate at 3.4%, quits rate at 2.0%, layoffs at 1.1%, all holding near post-2023 lows. The JOLTS data was enough to pull the September Fed rate hike probability from 66% (post-ISM Manufacturing Monday) to approximately 55-56%. That shift is not a collapse in the hike case - it is a recalibration based on a labor market that is stable but not accelerating.

Today's two morning releases are the most consequential data before Friday's BLS jobs report: ADP July private payrolls at 8:15 AM ET (June was 98,000; a number above 100,000 would partially restore the ISM Manufacturing employment signal; a number below 85,000 would reinforce the JOLTS soft-read and likely push September hike probability below 50%) and ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET (June was 54.0%; a reading above 54 alongside manufacturing at 55.6 would mean both halves of the economy are in expansion simultaneously - the first such reading in several months).

Bottom Line
  • Four consecutive weeks of declining corn ratings during grain fill is precisely the setup the USDA's August 12 crop production report was designed to capture. The first official yield estimate incorporating July's heat stress data will land seven days from today. The market is holding corn at approximately $4.63 per bushel - a price that reflects the weather premium from the rating declines but not yet the official USDA yield number that will quantify the damage. A below-trend yield estimate on August 12 would validate that premium and potentially push corn higher.   • The August 12 double-header matters beyond agricultural markets. July CPI and the USDA crop production report on the same morning means that one morning's data could show: (1) whether food prices have already begun reflecting the four-week crop decline in the measured index, and (2) whether the supply shortfall has been confirmed at a magnitude that would push food prices higher in coming months. The Fed's three dissenters - who voted for a hike on July 29 on the argument that inflation remains too elevated - will be reading both releases simultaneously on August 12. If food inflation is re-accelerating and the crop report confirms a supply shortfall, the September hike case gains an argument it did not have when the vote was taken.

The coordinated intervention last weekend bought time; narrowing rate differentials would provide the structural support that currency intervention cannot sustain beyond a few weeks. If the Fed hikes to 3.75-4.00% in September while the BoJ simultaneously lifts to 1.25%, the differential narrows from both directions.
  • The US-China manufacturing divergence that opened on Monday continues to define the medium-term industrial picture. US ISM Manufacturing at 55.6 - a four-year high. China official PMI at 49.2 - contraction for the first time in five months. The two readings are measuring the same global manufacturing cycle from opposite sides of the same trade architecture. AI data center infrastructure demand, semiconductor supply chains, and power infrastructure investment are concentrated in the US, Taiwan, and South Korea. Chinese manufacturers face a domestic demand shortfall and trade headwinds simultaneously. Caterpillar's guidance raise on Tuesday - citing AI data center construction as a driver - is the direct equipment demand link between US ISM 55.6 and where global infrastructure spending is currently landing.
