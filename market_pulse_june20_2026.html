Weekly Briefing • Friday, June 20, 2026

Iran Peace Deal Signed. Markets Closed for Juneteenth. The Week That Changed the Oil Map.

The US and Iran formally signed a 14-point peace memorandum in Geneva on Thursday while US markets observed Juneteenth. WTI crude closed Wednesday at $75.49 - down 17% from its April peak. Gas prices fell to $3.97 nationally. The Fed's hawkish dot plot from Wednesday still hangs over equities heading into Monday's open. Gold holds above $4,300. The week's biggest number: 9 of 18 FOMC officials now see a rate hike in 2026.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • Iran peace deal formally signed in Geneva on Juneteenth: The US and Iran signed a 14-point memorandum of understanding to end more than 100 days of conflict, reopen the Strait of Hormuz without tolls, and begin a 60-day window on nuclear program negotiations and sanctions relief. Oil had already priced in the deal - WTI fell from $91 to $75 over two weeks. Monday's open will be the first market reaction to the signed agreement.
  • Fed's hawkish dot plot still the dominant story for equities: Wednesday's FOMC held rates at 3.50-3.75% but 9 of 18 officials now project at least one hike this year. The median year-end rate projection rose to 3.8%. Markets sold off 1.2% on Wednesday and have not had a session to digest the news since Juneteenth closed trading Thursday.
  • Gas prices break below $4.00 nationally - $3.97 avg: The national average fell below the $4 mark for the first time since mid-May, down from $4.55 at the May peak. Every 10-cent decline saves American drivers roughly $14 billion annualized. If the Iran deal holds and oil stays in the $70s, gas could drift toward $3.75 by early July.
  • Japan's Nikkei hits 71,053 - fresh all-time high: Japanese equities rallied 1.65% on Thursday after the Iran signing eased energy import concerns. Japan is up roughly 30% year-to-date and is the standout global market story of 2026. The Bank of Japan's hike to 1.0% has not slowed the rally.

The Quick Read

The Iran peace deal is signed, gas prices broke below $4.00, and the Fed just told markets to prepare for a possible rate hike - all in the same week. Markets closed for Juneteenth on Thursday, leaving Monday as the first session to price in the formal signing. Oil at $75.49 has already fallen 17% from its April peak. The 30-year mortgage sits at 6.47%. Foreclosure filings are up 18% year-over-year but existing-home sales improved 3.2%. Crypto pulled back across the board as the hawkish Fed posture dampened risk appetite - Bitcoin at $62,445, Ethereum at $1,685.

The most important development of the week is not one event but two converging forces. The Iran deal structurally improves the global energy supply picture - the Strait of Hormuz reopening without tolls and a 60-day negotiation window on sanctions relief means more oil reaching global markets over the next two quarters. At the same time, the Fed's hawkish shift means borrowing costs are not coming down anytime soon. These forces pull in opposite directions for consumers: cheaper gas but persistent high mortgage rates, lower energy bills but expensive credit card debt at 21% APR.

For savers, this is actually a favorable environment. CD rates at 4.10-4.30% and high-yield savings at 5.00% APY are earning real positive returns above inflation. The Fed's reluctance to cut means these rates hold. For borrowers, the picture is tougher - mortgages near 6.5% and credit card APRs above 21% will not ease until the Fed changes direction. The PCE inflation reading on June 26 is the next critical data point. If falling oil prices show up in the number, the hawkish narrative softens.

Housing remains under pressure with starts down 15% and foreclosures climbing, but context matters. Existing-home sales improving 3.2% and price cuts declining from 35% to 20% suggest a market finding its footing rather than accelerating into distress. The consumer is stretched - $18.8 trillion in total household debt, savings rate at 3.6% - but retail sales beating expectations at +0.9% shows spending resilience. The K-shaped story continues: lower-income households feel real stress while middle and upper-income consumers keep spending.

Stock Market

Markets closed Thursday for Juneteenth. Wednesday's session saw broad selling after the Fed's hawkish projections. Monday will be the first reaction to the signed Iran deal.

S&P 500: 7,500.58 +1.08% day
Dow Jones: 51,564.70 +0.14% day
Nasdaq: 26,517.93 +1.91% day
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,979.77 +2.12% day
Fear gauge (VIX): 16.85 +2.74%
Bottom Line
  • Markets actually recovered on Wednesday's close after selling off sharply in the morning on the hawkish dot plot. The S&P finishing up 1.08% and the Nasdaq up 1.91% shows buyers stepped in at lower levels. The initial panic over the dot plot was met with bargain-hunting, particularly in tech.
  • The VIX at 16.85 is below the 20 threshold that signals genuine concern. This is repositioning, not fear. Monday's open will be the real test - if oil holds below $76 and the Iran signing is received positively, equities could gap higher. If the hawkish Fed tone dominates, expect choppy action.
  • Small caps leading (Russell +2.12%) is a positive breadth signal. Rate-sensitive small caps rallying more than large caps suggests the market does not fully believe a hike is coming. Watch the PCE reading on June 26 for the next directional catalyst.

Interest Rates

The Fed held but the dot plot shifted hawkish. Short-end yields jumped. Mortgages edged lower on the week as the Iran deal improved sentiment.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held, 4th consecutive meeting)
2-year Treasury: 4.20% +14 bps on the week
10-year Treasury: 4.49% +5 bps on the week
30-year Treasury: 4.93% steady
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.47% −5 bps from last week (Freddie Mac)
Bottom Line
  • The 2-year jumping 14 bps to 4.20% is the bond market pricing in the possibility of a hike. But the 30-year holding at 4.93% means long-term investors are not convinced inflation is reaccelerating. The curve is normally sloped at +29 bps (2s/10s) - still a healthy economy signal.
  • Mortgages actually fell 5 bps to 6.47% this week despite the hawkish dot plot, because the Iran deal lowered global risk premiums. This is still too high to meaningfully unfreeze the housing market (buyers need sub-6.25%), but the direction is favorable if oil stays low.
  • For savers, the hawkish Fed posture is a tailwind. CDs at 4.10-4.30% and the 2-year Treasury at 4.20% mean cash is earning a real positive return. Locking in these rates while the Fed debates its next move remains a straightforward opportunity.

Savings & CD Rates

The hawkish Fed posture means attractive CD and savings rates are here to stay - and may even edge higher if a hike materializes.

National avg 1-yr CD: 1.97% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.10-4.30% APY (Popular Direct, Bask Bank, Morgan Stanley)
Best credit union CD: 4.00% APY (Service Credit Union)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • If the Fed is now leaning toward a hike rather than a cut, CD rates at 4.10-4.30% become even more attractive on a forward-looking basis - rates could go higher, but locking in above 4% today still beats inflation over the term. The risk of waiting is that the best promotional rates get pulled, not that rates fall. This is a good environment for savers.
  • The 2-point gap between the national average CD (1.97%) and the best online options (4.30%) remains the single easiest financial move most people can make. On $50,000, that gap is $1,150/year in foregone interest. Opening an online savings account takes 15 minutes and requires zero market knowledge.

Energy & Gas Prices

Iran deal signed. Oil below $76. Gas below $4 nationally for the first time since mid-May.

WTI crude: $75.49/bbl −17% from $91 April peak
Brent crude: $77.22/bbl −2.8% from last wk
National gas avg: $3.97/gal −$0.58 from $4.55 May peak
West Coast: $5.49-5.71/gal (CA highest)
Cheapest region (South Central): $3.35/gal
Bottom Line
  • Gas breaking below $4.00 nationally is a psychological milestone. The decline from $4.55 to $3.97 in under a month puts real money back in consumers' pockets. A family filling up twice a week saves roughly $12-15/week. If oil holds in the $70s through summer, gas could reach $3.75 by early July.
  • The signed Iran deal structurally changes the oil supply picture. The Strait of Hormuz reopening, a 60-day sanctions relief negotiation window, and the IEA's existing projection of a global supply glut through 2027 all point toward lower energy costs ahead. This is the most consumer-friendly macro development of 2026.
  • The West Coast premium ($5.71 in CA vs. $3.35 in the South) remains extreme but both ends are trending down. State taxes and refinery structure create a permanent $2+ gap, but the direction is favorable everywhere.

Commodities

Gold holding above $4,300 as a store of value. Copper near records on structural demand. Cattle at record highs reflecting a generational herd cycle.

Gold / oz: $4,301 −1.2% from yesterday's $4,354 (dollar strength)
Silver / oz: $70.94 −0.7% day
Copper / lb: $6.51 near record (AI/electrification demand)
Corn / bushel: $4.14 steady
Soybeans / bushel: $11.14 −2.1% wk (Iran deal easing input costs)
Live cattle / cwt: $255.30 record levels
Bottom Line
  • Gold pulling back slightly to $4,301 on dollar strength after the hawkish dot plot is normal profit-taking after yesterday's surge to $4,354. The metal remains up strongly year-to-date and continues to attract buyers who see it as a hedge against policy uncertainty. Gold above $4,300 reflects structural demand from central banks and institutional allocators, not speculative fever.
  • Copper at $6.51 - near its all-time high - while oil falls below $76 tells an important story about where real economic demand lives. The AI data center buildout, global electrification, and energy transition require enormous amounts of copper. This is secular demand, not a trade. Copper strength alongside oil weakness means industrial activity is healthy even as geopolitical supply pressures ease.
  • Cattle at $255/cwt is a record driven by the US herd being at its smallest since 1951 (86.2 million head). This is a multi-year biological cycle - herds take 3-4 years to rebuild. Beef prices will be elevated through 2028-2029, but this is a known and predictable dynamic. It is worth noting that poultry and pork remain more affordable alternatives, and competition from those proteins helps moderate retail grocery inflation.

Crop Weather

Corn conditions holding at adequate levels. Winter wheat harvest underway - production will be low this year but markets have priced it in. Drought remains a factor to watch.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent. Planting complete. Illinois remains the risk zone (42% abnormally dry). Pollination window approaching in July - the critical period for yield determination.
Winter wheat: Kansas 15% good-to-excellent. Harvest underway in Oklahoma (23% complete). Production forecast 1,048M bushels - well below average. Futures have already adjusted.
Drought: 56% of the Lower 48 in some level of drought. Western reservoir levels low heading into summer. Conditions stable week-over-week.
El Nino watch: 87-92% probability through January 2027. Historically associated with drier Midwest conditions - a watch item for corn through July.
Bottom Line
  • The wheat situation is already reflected in futures prices - production will be low, but this has been anticipated for months and is not a new shock. Higher flour and bread costs are likely in Q3-Q4 but grocery inflation runs on slower timelines than energy. Consumers will notice modest increases at the bakery aisle, not sudden spikes.
  • Corn at 67% good-to-excellent is adequate for now. July is the make-or-break month when pollination occurs and weather becomes decisive. The El Nino probability at 87-92% warrants attention but has not materialized into crop damage yet. Worth monitoring weekly rather than reacting to today.
  • The broader drought picture (56% of the Lower 48) is a long-term infrastructure issue more than an immediate food-price shock. Water restrictions and agricultural planning are adapting in real time. This is a slow-moving challenge that local and state governments are actively managing.

Real Estate

A mixed picture: existing-home sales improved in May while foreclosures continue a gradual climb. The market is repricing, not collapsing.

Existing-home sales (May): 4.17M SAAR +3.2% MoM and YoY
Median home price (May): $429,300
Housing starts (May): 1.177M SAAR −15.4% MoM
Foreclosure filings (Apr): 42,430 properties +18% YoY
Foreclosure starts (Q1): 82,631 +26% YoY
Bank repossessions / REOs (Q1): 14,020 +45% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: ~11.5%
% listings cutting price: 20.2% in May (down from 35.4% in April)
Inventory: ~4.5 months of supply
30-year mortgage: 6.47%
Bottom Line
  • Existing-home sales rising 3.2% in May is a sign that buyers are adapting to the rate environment rather than staying permanently frozen. The market is not booming, but it is moving. Median prices holding at $429,300 with 4.5 months of inventory shows a market that is slowly normalizing from the extreme tightness of 2021-2023.
  • Foreclosure numbers are climbing from an abnormally low base. The +26% YoY in Q1 filings sounds alarming in percentage terms, but in absolute numbers (118,727 for the entire quarter), this remains well below the 300,000+ per quarter that was normal in 2009-2010. What we are seeing is a return toward historical averages after the pandemic forbearance period artificially suppressed foreclosure activity.
  • Price cuts declining from 35.4% in April to 20.2% in May is actually encouraging - it suggests sellers are adjusting expectations and pricing more realistically from the start, rather than a market in accelerating distress. The housing market is working through an adjustment. It is slow and uncomfortable for those who bought at peak prices with high rates, but the trajectory is toward equilibrium, not crisis.
  • Housing starts falling 15% reflects builders responding rationally to current demand levels. Fewer starts now does tighten supply 12-18 months out, but it also prevents overbuilding into soft demand. If and when rates decline, the building pipeline can ramp back up. This is the market self-correcting.

Household Finance

Consumer spending remains resilient. Household balance sheets are stretched but not breaking. Delinquencies are elevated in specific segments - subprime auto and BNPL - while broad consumer activity holds up.

Retail sales (May): $763.7B +0.9% MoM (beat expectations)
Total household debt: $18.8 trillion
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion; avg APR 21.0%
Avg. credit card balance (with balance): $7,886
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo record
Subprime auto 60+ day delinquent: 6.65% 32-year high
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 3.6% (March) below historical avg of 6-8%
Bottom Line
  • The consumer picture is more nuanced than a single narrative allows. Retail sales beating expectations at +0.9% shows that aggregate spending remains solid. At the same time, the savings rate at 3.6% and elevated delinquencies in subprime segments tell you that lower-income households are under real pressure while middle and upper-income consumers continue spending. This is a K-shaped consumer story, not a uniform one.
  • Subprime auto delinquencies at 6.65% (a 32-year high) and 47% of BNPL users paying late are concentrated in the credit segments that expanded most aggressively during 2021-2023. These are real stress points that affect real families, but they are contained to specific borrower pools rather than spreading across the broad credit system. Prime borrower delinquencies remain low.
  • The falling gas prices ($4.55 to $4.03) and potential for further declines represent the most direct near-term relief for stretched household budgets. Lower energy costs reduce commuting, shipping, and goods inflation simultaneously. If oil stays in the $70s through summer, the consumer squeeze eases meaningfully without requiring any Fed action.

Crypto

Crypto pulled back as the hawkish Fed dampened risk appetite. Positioning defensive heading into the weekend.

Bitcoin (BTC): $62,445 −2.89% day
Ethereum (ETH): $1,685 −3.60% day
Solana (SOL): $68.10 −4.74% day
XRP: $1.12 −3.77% day
Bottom Line
  • Crypto selling off after the hawkish Fed is textbook behavior - digital assets are sensitive to rate expectations and the dot plot shift toward hikes is negative for risk assets. The 3-5% pullback is orderly, not panicked. Bitcoin holding above $60,000 shows the floor remains intact.
  • The positioning is thin heading into the weekend. Markets were closed Thursday for Juneteenth, meaning crypto was the only major asset class trading. Volumes were light and moves were exaggerated. Monday's equity open will set the real tone.
  • Ethereum at $1,685 and Solana at $68 reflect the continued gap between growing institutional infrastructure and subdued retail participation. Both are well below their cycle highs. When macro conditions improve, these levels will look like accumulation zones in hindsight.

Around the World

Japan at fresh records. Europe benefiting from cheaper energy. China policy-supported. Emerging markets watching the July tariff deadline.

Japan: Nikkei hit 71,053 - fresh all-time high. Rallied 1.65% on Thursday after Iran signing. Exports +17% YoY. BOJ hike to 1.0% has not slowed momentum. Up ~30% YTD.
Europe: Stoxx 600 at ~637. Benefiting from cheaper energy (more energy-dependent than US). If Iran deal holds, European equities have further room. ECB holding at 2.25%.
China: Shanghai Composite holding above 4,000 on government support. Consumer remains cautious. Property market in fourth year of adjustment. Not a crisis, just slow.
Emerging: MSCI EM watching July 24 tariff expiration. If temporary 10% tariffs on 60 economies expire or get reduced, broadly positive for EM currencies. Five weeks out.
Bottom Line
  • Japan at 71,053 on the Nikkei with exports surging 17% is the strongest macro story in global markets. The weak yen makes Japanese goods competitive, the AI buildout drives semiconductor demand, and the BOJ's confidence in hiking signals underlying economic strength. For US investors with international diversification, Japan has been the clear winner.
  • The oil decline benefits Europe more than the US because European industry is more energy-intensive. If WTI stays in the $70s, European corporate margins expand without requiring ECB rate cuts. This is a clean tailwind that does not require policy action - just lower commodity prices flowing through to manufacturing and transportation costs.
  • The July 24 tariff deadline is five weeks away and already influencing positioning in emerging markets. This is worth tracking but not worth reacting to yet. Binary events are best assessed closer to their date when actual policy signals emerge. For now, it remains one of several potential catalysts in the back half of summer.
