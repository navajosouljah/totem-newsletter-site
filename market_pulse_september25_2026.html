Stocks Closed Up as Brent Hit $105. October Hike Odds at 73%.

The Quick Read

Houthi missiles struck Saudi Aramco facilities and threatened Yanbu - the western terminus of the East-West pipeline that only recently resumed partial operations after earlier drone strikes - pushing Brent from Thursday's ~$103 close to $105.69 by Friday's close. Saudi forces intercepted 6 ballistic missiles. France announced military assets for Yanbu's defense. The mechanism: the Strait of Hormuz constraint and the East-West pipeline's partial reopening had previously functioned as two separate export paths; Friday's strikes placed both under threat simultaneously. Brent at $105.69 is the market's first price for that dual-constraint scenario. October 28 FOMC hike odds jumped from Thursday's 64% to 73% as oil-driven inflation expectations repriced forward.

September 30 PCE is now the decisive data point at 73% hike odds - not just for the October call, but for the CD decision. At 73%, the expected-value case for waiting for a post-hike 4.90-5.00% CD has crossed well past majority probability. PCE above 2.5% YoY (the Brent-fueled scenario) pushes odds toward 80-85%; PCE at or below 2.0% remains the only near-term catalyst that flips the call below 50% and makes locking 4.75% now the defensible pre-PCE decision. The 10-year Treasury closed at 5.22% (up from 5.11% Thursday) as the rate market absorbed both the oil shock and the hike repricing.

Despite the oil surge, the S&P 500 closed at approximately 7,718 (+0.18%), Nasdaq approximately 26,974 (+0.13%), and Dow approximately 51,479 (+0.25%). Energy and defense names absorbed the session's gains; rate-sensitive sectors continued to face pressure. Bank of America warned Brent could reach $150+ if the Yanbu disruption proves sustained. October 2 September jobs and September 30 PCE remain the final inputs before October 28.
