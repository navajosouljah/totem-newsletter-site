August Added Just 22K Jobs. September Hike Odds Fell to a Coin Flip.

The Quick Read

August nonfarm payrolls came in at 22,000 - well below the 53,000 consensus and July's revised 79,000. Unemployment rose to 4.3%, the highest since October 2021. Wages held at 0.3% month-over-month. This is the second consecutive below-consensus print, and the mechanism it sets in motion is direct: CME FedWatch's September hike probability fell from 66% to approximately 47%, flipping from a lean-toward-hike to a coin flip. The labor market's soft edge is now confirmed data, not just the ADP prelude.

What today decides is the 10-year Treasury's close. It opened Friday at 4.7541% before the report. A settled close below 4.65% reprices next Thursday's Freddie Mac PMMS below 6.65%, opens the fall refinancing window buyers have waited for, and pulls the October-November CD forward rate down from the 4.55-4.75% range - the post-hike window that made waiting the majority play at 66% odds. Above 4.75% at Friday's close and September 15 retains enough probability to keep that calculus live.

Gold entered Friday at $4,469, up from $4,461 Thursday and trading on rate-path mechanics rather than Hormuz. Bitcoin arrived at approximately $81,200 after the largest single-day spot ETF inflow since January - $730.8 million on September 3, led by BlackRock's IBIT at $454 million - having touched $82,200 intraday Thursday. The CLARITY Act has five Senate working days remaining before September 15 cloture. September 11 carries the next scheduled data: August CPI at 8:30 AM ET and the WASDE at 11:00 AM ET.
