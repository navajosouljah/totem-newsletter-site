Sunday Briefing • Sunday, August 9, 2026

Senate Files CLARITY Act Cloture on August 8, Setting a September 15 Floor Vote - the Same Week as the FOMC. China July Exports Surge 23.9% to $397.85 Billion. Bitcoin ETFs Absorbed $853.5 Million in One Week, IBIT Took 81%. S&P 500 Rests at Record 7,757.64. Monday: Final Crop Progress. Wednesday: CPI and the First Official Corn Yield Estimate - the Two Prints That Decide September.

The weekend's most consequential development is legislative, not market: Senate Majority Leader Thune filed a cloture motion on the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act on Saturday, August 8, opening a procedural path to a floor vote during the week of September 15 - a direct collision with the September 15-16 FOMC meeting. For XRP, which has been trading near the $1.06 level with ETF inflows collapsed to $27.29 million in July, this is the first concrete Senate procedural step since the bill passed committee. China's July trade report confirmed a massive export surge: $397.85 billion in exports, +23.9% year-over-year, driving the Shanghai Composite up 1.02% on Friday and widening the US-China manufacturing divergence that has characterized 2026. Bitcoin ETFs absorbed $853.5 million in the week of August 3-7, with BlackRock's IBIT capturing $693 million (81% of total flows). The S&P 500 rests at a new all-time record of 7,757.64 as the week begins. Monday's USDA Crop Progress is the final weekly rating before Wednesday's first official corn yield estimate, which arrives simultaneously with July CPI at 8:30 AM ET. That double-header is the last significant macro input before September 15-16 - and now, by legislative calendar, also the same week the Senate votes on the future of digital asset regulation.

The Quick Read

The week ahead has two phases and they are now explicitly linked: Wednesday's 8:30 AM ET double-header - July CPI and the USDA's first official corn yield estimate - determines whether September's Fed meeting is a hold or a live hike debate. And the week of September 15 - which already contained the September 15-16 FOMC - now also contains the Senate floor vote on the CLARITY Act, the most consequential piece of crypto legislation in US history. Senate Majority Leader Thune's Saturday cloture filing closes the possibility that the CLARITY Act dies by inaction before the recess ends. It does not guarantee passage - Polymarket still prices it at approximately 30% for 2026. But it means the Senate will be forced to vote, which is a different situation than indefinite delay. The FOMC, the CLARITY Act, and whatever CPI and crop weather produce on Wednesday all land within a 48-hour window on September 15-16. The week of September 15 is now one of the most data-dense legislative and monetary weeks the market has faced in years.

The macro backdrop entering the week is unchanged from Friday's close: S&P 500 at an all-time record of 7,757.64, September hike probability at approximately 40%, the Hormuz deal unsigned, and the labor market having delivered its most ambiguous reading of the year - a negative payrolls print that simultaneously sent equities to records. None of that resolves before Wednesday. The corn crop, however, has been absorbing July heat stress silently for four weeks, and the USDA's first official yield estimate will quantify exactly how much damage was done. The four-week decline in good-to-excellent ratings from 67% to 61% has built a weather premium into corn futures. Wednesday either validates that premium or collapses it - and because food inflation is one of the three variables the FOMC dissenters are watching, the corn yield and the CPI arrive at the same moment and will be read together.

China's trade data deserves more attention than it typically receives on a US-focused Sunday briefing. Exports surging 23.9% to $397.85 billion in July - while China's official manufacturing PMI sits at 49.2%, technically in contraction - describes a country that is manufacturing and exporting aggressively even as domestic demand is soft. That combination has direct implications for US inflation: Chinese goods are arriving at US ports in volume. Whether that keeps goods deflation running or creates a new tariff flashpoint with a US economy that recorded its strongest manufacturing PMI in four years (ISM at 55.6% on August 3) depends on decisions that will not be made this week. But the US-China divergence - 55.6% ISM versus 49.2% PMI, with China exporting at record pace into a US manufacturing expansion - is the structural backdrop against which every US trade and inflation discussion is happening.

Bitcoin's institutional accumulation story continues to be the quietly impressive narrative of 2026. The $853.5 million absorbed by spot Bitcoin ETFs in the week of August 3-7 - a week that included a semiconductor sell-off, a historic payrolls miss, and a 464-point Dow drop - produced zero days of net outflows. That is not normal behavior for a risk asset in a volatile macro week. The institutions buying IBIT on those days are not trading the macro news cycle; they are running a capital allocation program that does not turn off when the Nasdaq sells off and does not accelerate when the S&P hits records. The $853.5 million weekly total is the highest since the post-launch demand surge in late 2025. The CLARITY Act floor vote in September - even at 30% passage odds - adds a legislative catalyst to an already strong institutional flow story.

Weekend Developments

Four meaningful developments arrived between Friday's close and Sunday morning. The CLARITY Act procedural step is the most market-relevant. China's trade data is the most consequential for the global economic picture. The Hormuz deal remains unsigned. Bitcoin ETF flows completed their strongest week since the launch period.

  • CLARITY Act: Thune files cloture motion Saturday, August 8 - floor vote scheduled for week of September 15. Senate Majority Leader John Thune filed a cloture motion on the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act on Saturday, opening the procedural path to a Senate floor vote during the week of September 15 - the same week as the FOMC meeting. Cloture requires 60 votes to advance the bill to a floor vote; passage requires a simple majority. The filing does not guarantee either outcome, but it changes the CLARITY Act's status from "indefinitely delayed" to "scheduled for a vote." Key unresolved legislative disputes include illicit-finance provisions (crypto exchanges' anti-money-laundering obligations), stablecoin yield treatment (whether yield-bearing stablecoins are securities), and government ethics clauses related to presidential crypto holdings. Those three disputes are the most likely sources of the additional 8-12 Republican votes needed to reach 60. Polymarket passage-in-2026 odds: approximately 30%. The XRP-specific implications are significant: the bill would classify XRP as a "digital commodity" under CFTC jurisdiction, removing the SEC jurisdiction uncertainty that has suppressed institutional allocation to XRP since the 2018 lawsuit era. XRP's ETF inflow collapse to $27.29 million in July - versus $666 million at the November 2025 launch - reflects exactly this institutional caution about legislative uncertainty. A floor vote in September is not passage, but it is the first concrete test of whether enough votes exist for passage to be realistic.
  • China July trade data: exports +23.9% to $397.85 billion; imports +27.5% to $285.35 billion. China's July trade report delivered one of the largest export-growth readings of 2026. The $397.85 billion in exports - up 23.9% year-over-year - drove the Shanghai Composite up 1.02% and the CSI 300 up 0.93% on Friday. The import surge of 27.5% to $285.35 billion is the more nuanced reading: Chinese domestic consumption is picking up even as the official manufacturing PMI sits at 49.2% (contraction). The two readings simultaneously - 49.2% PMI contraction and 23.9% export growth - describe an economy where the factory floor is busy and shipping goods abroad even as domestic order books are not fully recovered. For US inflation, the question is whether Chinese goods volume keeps goods deflation running (helpful for the Fed) or whether tariff responses to China's record trade surplus create a price shock (harmful for the Fed). The US-China trade relationship enters September with US ISM manufacturing at a four-year high and China exporting at a record pace - those two facts will generate political and legislative pressure regardless of which party controls the White House.
  • Hormuz deal: unsigned as of Sunday morning; Iran's "not subject to US interference" stance unchanged. The Iran-Oman framework for Hormuz shipping lanes remains in the same state it has been since Thursday evening: agreed in principle, unsigned in practice. Iranian officials maintained through the weekend that any arrangement "is between Iran and Oman and is not subject to US interference" - a framing the US has explicitly rejected. No joint statement from Iran and Oman has been issued. No signed document exists. WTI crude settled Friday at $78.18, reflecting approximately 50/50 odds on deal completion. The weekend produced no new signals in either direction. The deal calendar now extends into the week ahead with no announced signing ceremony or negotiating session. Oil at $78 continues to price the possibility of a deal; the risk remains that the US-Iran sovereignty gap - Iran's claim to transit fee authority versus the US's rejection of it - is not a drafting disagreement but a fundamental position conflict that Oman's mediation cannot bridge.
  • Bitcoin ETF week of August 3-7: $853.5 million in net inflows; IBIT $693 million (81% share); zero outflow days. The spot Bitcoin ETF complex completed its strongest weekly inflow period since the post-launch demand surge in late 2025. BlackRock's IBIT alone absorbed $693 million of the $853.5 million total. IBIT's cumulative inflows have approached $62 billion. The five-day inflow streak ran through a week that included a semiconductor sector rout, a 44,000 ADP print, a -23,000 BLS payrolls miss, and a 464-point Dow decline. The fact that ETF inflows were positive on every single one of those days is the clearest evidence yet that institutional Bitcoin buyers are not reacting to the same signals as equity traders. They are running a capital allocation program with an independent timeline. Bitcoin held near $65,000 through the week's volatility and into the weekend - the $63,000-$65,000 range has served as institutional accumulation territory, not distribution territory, for three consecutive weeks.

Household Finance

The household finance picture entering the week combines the S&P 500 at a record (portfolio wealth up for equity-holders), gas prices declining toward $4.00 (broadly distributed benefit), credit card APRs unchanged at 22.18% (no relief until the Fed cuts), and July consumer sentiment at its highest since February. The K-shaped divergence between asset-holders and debt-holders is the week's defining household dynamic.

University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment (July final, released July 31): 55.2 revised up from 54.0 preliminary; up from 49.5 in June; highest since February 2026; improved across all income, age, and education groups; still approximately 11% below a year earlier; the sentiment improvement alongside a record S&P 500 is the asset-holder effect - households with 401(k) exposure see portfolio wealth rising and report improved conditions even as the labor market is contracting at the margin
June retail sales (released July): $768.6 billion +0.2% month-over-month, +6.7% year-over-year; consumer spending has been resilient through the rate cycle; July retail sales will release Thursday August 14 - the next meaningful consumer data point after Wednesday's CPI
Gas prices (national avg, August 8): $4.022/gallon the week's clearest universally-distributed household benefit; applies equally across income distribution; approximately half of US states now below $4.00/gallon
Credit card APR (composite avg): ~22.18% unchanged; will not decline until the Fed cuts rates; the path from 22% APRs to 20% APRs requires at least 2-3 cuts; with September at 40% hike probability (not cut probability), APR relief for revolving-balance households remains 12-18 months away at the earliest; households carrying balances continue to pay over $220 per year per $1,000 of revolving debt
Personal savings rate (June): 2.7% spending continues to outrun income at the national level; the -23,000 July payrolls print means income growth is decelerating at exactly the moment household debt costs are at cycle highs; the savings rate below 3% provides minimal buffer if the labor market continues to contract
July retail sales (Thursday August 14): the next consumer finance data point after Wednesday's CPI; the July retail reading will capture household spending through the Hormuz price spike (gas above $4.00) and the partial recovery (gas at $4.02 by August 8); whether consumers are absorbing the Hormuz period's higher costs while maintaining spending volumes or cutting back will be visible in Thursday's release
Bottom Line
  • Consumer sentiment at 55.2 - a five-month high - is driven by the people watching their 401(k) balances hit new highs on a week when the S&P 500 set two records. It is not driven by the people who lost jobs in local government education or retail trade during July's -23,000 payroll month. The improvement in sentiment is real for the group experiencing it; it is absent for the group that is not. The Michigan survey captures the mood of respondents who have phones and are willing to answer them - a group that skews toward homeownership and employment. The 11% year-over-year decline in sentiment despite five-month highs tells the full picture: sentiment is recovering from a period that was deeply negative for many households, and it is doing so unevenly. The asset-holder recovery is faster and more visible; the wage-earner recovery is slower and less certain. Gas at $4.02 is the one data point this week that applies to both groups in roughly equal measure.

Crypto

The CLARITY Act cloture motion filed Saturday is the weekend's most significant crypto development since the bill passed committee in June. Bitcoin ETF flows completed their strongest week since the launch period. XRP is trading near $1.06 with ETF inflows collapsed - but the Senate schedule gives the CLARITY Act a live September vote for the first time. ETH holds near $1,858-$1,900 as its CLARITY Act designation as a digital commodity provides constructive legislative context.

Bitcoin (BTC): ~$64,745-$65,000 (Friday close / Sunday morning) holding the $63,000-$65,000 accumulation range for the third consecutive week; IBIT inflows of $693 million in the week of August 3-7 (81% of the $853.5 million total industry inflow); IBIT cumulative inflows approaching $62 billion; zero days of net outflows in August 2026; the uncorrelation thesis - Bitcoin not moving with equity or macro volatility - has been tested and sustained through the most volatile macro week of the summer
Bitcoin ETF weekly inflows (August 3-7): $853.5 million total strongest weekly total since the post-launch period in late 2025; IBIT $693M (81%); the five-day streak included zero outflow days through a week containing a semiconductor rout, a payrolls miss of 106,000, and a 464-point Dow decline; institutional buyers were not deterred by any of it
Ethereum (ETH): ~$1,858-$1,900 (Friday-Sunday) holding above the $1,807 support level; the CLARITY Act's digital commodity classification for ETH is constructive regulatory context; rate-sensitive positioning improved with Friday's yield decline; the Senate floor vote in September adds a legislative catalyst to ETH's medium-term positioning
XRP (Ripple): ~$1.06 (Friday-Sunday) approximately -55% from its January 2025 high and -44% from its January 2026 high of $2.41; 52-week range approximately $1.01-$3.38; the $1.00-$1.03 support level remains the key near-term floor; ETF cumulative inflows: $1.44 billion since November 2025 launch (positive but decelerating); July 2026 ETF inflows: $27.29 million (vs. $666 million launch-month); on-chain Daily Active Addresses: approximately 25,350 (second-lowest of 2026 as of mid-July; improved to near 39,500 in more recent network activity data); Ripple's August 1 escrow unlock released 1 billion XRP with approximately 300 million net new supply
CLARITY Act (Saturday update): Senate Majority Leader Thune filed cloture motion on August 8; cloture vote requires 60 votes; floor vote now scheduled for the week of September 15; key unresolved disputes: illicit-finance provisions, stablecoin yield treatment, government ethics clauses; the September 15 floor vote lands the same week as the FOMC; passage-in-2026 Polymarket odds: approximately 30%; for XRP specifically, passage converts its regulatory status from the current Digital Commodity designation (SEC/CFTC dual jurisdiction settled) to the full legislative clarity of a statutory CFTC-jurisdiction digital commodity - removing the residual institutional caution about holding XRP in regulated vehicles
Bottom Line
  • The cloture motion changes the CLARITY Act's status in a specific and important way. Before Saturday, the CLARITY Act had passed committee and the Senate had gone on recess without scheduling a vote - meaning the bill could theoretically die by inaction indefinitely. After Saturday, the bill has a scheduled Senate floor vote during the week of September 15. The distinction matters for how institutional capital positions around the bill. When a vote is "possible but not scheduled," institutions price the tail - maybe it passes, maybe it dies. When a vote is "scheduled for September 15," institutions price the odds more directly. Polymarket at 30% passage probability in 2026 is the current pricing. That 30% is not evenly distributed across outcomes - it reflects a world where the 60-vote cloture threshold is the primary obstacle, and the 3-4 Republican holdouts on illicit-finance provisions are the swing votes. If Senate Majority Leader Thune filed cloture with a belief that 60 votes are achievable, that is a signal about the whip count that the market does not yet have. XRP at $1.06 is pricing exactly the uncertainty the 30% Polymarket odds reflect. If cloture passes - meaning 60 senators vote to proceed to a floor vote - XRP's ETF inflows would likely resume sharply, because the most frequently cited institutional reason for avoiding XRP allocation is "legislative uncertainty." Cloture passage removes that excuse even before the bill passes into law.
  • Bitcoin's $853.5 million weekly inflow total deserves context. The spot Bitcoin ETF complex launched in January 2024 and its first weeks of trading produced comparable or larger inflows. But those early inflows were driven by pent-up demand from institutions that had been waiting years for a regulated vehicle. The August 3-7 inflows came two years after the launch excitement faded - which means they are not demand-pent-up but demand-ongoing. BlackRock's IBIT has now established itself as the primary institutional vehicle for Bitcoin exposure, absorbing 81% of a $853 million weekly total during a week of macro volatility. The comparison to traditional ETF flows is instructive: a $853 million week for a 2-year-old asset-class ETF in a volatile macro week is not noise. It is the institutional Bitcoin allocation program running its scheduled buying. The CLARITY Act's September vote is a legislative overlay on a Bitcoin institutional flow story that does not require legislative resolution to continue - but would likely accelerate significantly if the broader crypto regulatory framework is formalized.

Around the World

China's July trade data is the international story of the weekend. Exports surging 23.9% to $397.85 billion - combined with a manufacturing PMI at 49.2% - describes an economy exporting aggressively despite soft domestic demand. Asian markets closed Friday's session with broad gains driven by China's trade report. Japan's Nikkei held near its record levels despite technology sector headwinds.

China July exports: $397.85 billion +23.9% year-over-year; largest monthly export reading of 2026; the combination of 23.9% export growth and 49.2% manufacturing PMI (contraction) describes a factory sector that is busy making goods for export even as domestic orders are softer; drove Shanghai Composite up 1.02% and CSI 300 up 0.93% on Friday
China July imports: $285.35 billion +27.5% year-over-year; import growth at 27.5% is faster than export growth at 23.9%, meaning China is both buying more and selling more; the domestic consumption recovery - visible in import data - is running behind the export surge but is accelerating; China's overall trade surplus at these levels is the largest in recent history
Shanghai Composite (Friday close): ~3,940 +1.02%; China's trade data was the primary catalyst; government stimulus expectations remain a secondary driver; the US-China manufacturing divergence (ISM 55.6% vs PMI 49.2%) widens while China's trade surplus expands - the political implications of that combination for US trade policy will be a fall-session issue
Nikkei 225 (Friday close): 65,606.71 -0.12%; technology sector continued under modest pressure; Tokyo Electron and Advantest weak; the broader TOPIX +0.47% as domestic names offset chip-sector softness; Japan at record Nikkei levels alongside technology uncertainty reflects the same macro dynamic as the US - rate-path improvement is broadly equity-positive while individual semiconductor names work through an inventory correction
European markets (Friday): Stoxx 600 +0.24% European energy-importing economies continue to benefit structurally from oil at $78 versus mid-July's $93; the European manufacturing sector, under sustained pressure from high energy costs and Chinese competition, has a real tailwind at current crude levels
Korea: semiconductor sector stabilizing after Wednesday's severe session (Samsung -6.3%, SK Hynix -10.37%); Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan maintained Buy ratings at 3.5-3.6x forward P/E; the distinction between "inventory cycle within a secular AI expansion" and "demand destruction" is the most consequential question in technology investing; the major investment banks are voting for the former
Brazil: Copom at Selic rate 14.00% after fourth consecutive cut; Brazil's rate cycle is running 12-18 months ahead of US policy; the Brazil analog (hike - hold - cut) is the US rate cycle's most relevant international reference point
Bottom Line
  • China's $397.85 billion export month lands against a US manufacturing ISM at 55.6% - a four-year high. Those two readings describe a world in which the US and China are simultaneously expanding their manufacturing output, but at different points in the cycle and with different destinations for their goods. US manufacturing expansion at 55.6% is primarily serving domestic AI-related infrastructure demand - data centers, power infrastructure, semiconductor equipment. China's 23.9% export surge is primarily serving global goods markets where Chinese factories have rebuilt competitive position. The US-China trade relationship is not zero-sum in the current cycle, but it will not be perceived that way in Washington. A China running $397.85 billion monthly in exports - the largest reading of 2026 - into a US political environment that is already paying attention to Chinese competition is a combination that generates trade-policy pressure regardless of the economic merits. The fall legislative calendar will include this data point in multiple debates.

This Week's Events and Looking Ahead

The week of August 10 has two events that matter and two weeks from now has the event of the year. Monday's USDA Crop Progress is the final setup. Wednesday's double-header at 8:30 AM is the decision point. September 15 is the reckoning. Everything else is noise.

Monday Aug 10 - USDA Crop Progress: fifth consecutive weekly corn rating and the final reading before Wednesday's official yield estimate; sets the final market expectation entering Wednesday's number; a fifth consecutive decline in good-to-excellent ratings (currently 61%) would further validate the weather premium; stability or improvement at 61% or better would be a modest positive; Monday's release at 4:00 PM ET is the last data point before Wednesday's dual release.
Wednesday Aug 12 (8:30 AM ET) - July CPI AND USDA crop production report (first official corn yield estimate): two of the most important macro releases of the summer arrive simultaneously. July CPI consensus: approximately +0.2-0.3% MoM headline; core CPI approximately +0.2-0.3% MoM. A below-consensus core CPI (0.1% or below) would likely push September hike probability toward 20-25% and set the stage for 2026's first serious rate-cut discussion. An above-consensus core CPI (0.4%+) restores the hike case and tests the equity record at 7,757.64. The USDA corn yield relative to the approximately 181 bu/acre baseline determines whether food inflation adds to or offsets the broader CPI reading. Both releases at 8:30 AM ET.
Thursday Aug 14 - Freddie Mac PMMS (weekly mortgage rate survey): should capture Friday's 10-year decline to 4.61% and show improvement from 6.69% toward 6.60-6.65%; a cold Wednesday CPI that moves the 10-year toward 4.45-4.50% could produce a reading below 6.55%. July retail sales also release Thursday August 14 - the first consumer spending read after Hormuz's oil price spike and partial recovery.
September 14 - Senate returns from recess. CLARITY Act cloture vote now scheduled for the week of September 15 (Thune filed cloture motion August 8). The cloture vote requires 60 votes; if cloture passes, a floor vote follows with a simple majority threshold. Key swing votes: the 3-4 Republican senators whose concerns about illicit-finance provisions and stablecoin yield treatment have not yet been resolved by bill sponsors.
September 15-16 - FOMC meeting AND (per Thune cloture filing) CLARITY Act floor vote window. The September 15-16 calendar now contains: the Fed's rate decision (40% hike / 60% hold), Chair Warsh's press conference, and the Senate floor vote on the most consequential crypto legislation in US history. If Wednesday's CPI produces a September hold consensus AND the CLARITY Act passes the Senate the same week, it would be the most simultaneously positive outcome for US risk assets in the 2026 rate cycle. Neither is the base case independently; both together would be a tail event.
