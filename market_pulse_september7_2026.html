Hike Odds Ease to 55%. Brent Hits $97 Before Tuesday.

The Quick Read

The weekend's 70% rate-hike reading did not survive the CME futures open. FedWatch reset to approximately 55% Monday morning as the market absorbed three competing forces: the three-tanker strike pushing Brent to $97, gold's $130 fall on the same escalation day, and a broader repricing that split the inflation signal from the energy-cost shock. The mechanism is the collision between two inputs that normally reinforce each other - war-day oil gains and war-day rate-hike expectations - pulling apart for the second time in a week. At 55%, September 15 is still the slight majority case, but the 70%-to-55% round trip since Friday is the rate market's first honest pricing of the contradiction.

Tuesday September 8 is the first US market session to absorb all of it simultaneously: 55% hike odds, Brent at $97, gold at $4,446, Nikkei up 2% and Hang Seng down 0.9%. Three CLARITY Act Senate working days open Tuesday - the last window before the September 14 return and September 15 cloture at 2:15 PM ET. Freddie Mac PMMS lands Thursday September 10. August CPI at 8:30 AM ET and September WASDE at noon arrive Friday September 11. Every number this week feeds September 15.

Gold's second war-day decline in a week - from approximately $4,575 Friday to $4,446 Monday - signals institutional cash is moving into energy and not to traditional safe havens. The 13.1% credit-card delinquency reading and $4.15 gas sit unchanged beneath the headline debate, compressing household margin whether the Fed moves September 15 or not. Iran's Parliament Speaker hardened the weekend's tone Sunday: "any attack will receive a faster, heavier and more painful response" - no new kinetic action through Monday morning, but the rhetoric-to-action gap is the market's working assumption for Tuesday.
