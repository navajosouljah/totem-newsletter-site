Market Pulse • Saturday, October 10, 2026

Stocks Closed the Week in the Black. The Corn Harvest Just Got Bigger.

The Quick Read

The week ended where it started: higher. Friday's broad rally - Dow up 0.83%, S&P up 0.59%, Nasdaq up 0.64% - recovered most of Thursday's chip-driven loss and lifted all three indexes into positive territory for the week. The mechanism: President Trump said the US would not strike Iran before the November 3 midterms, oil eased from Thursday's spike, Treasury yields held below their 24-year highs, and buyers returned to AI names they had sold on the OpenAI revenue revision.

Friday delivered two data releases in opposite directions. The University of Michigan's preliminary October sentiment fell to 46.3, missing the 47.6 consensus and its worst reading since May, as one-year inflation expectations ticked up to 4.7%. At noon ET, USDA's WASDE raised the US corn yield forecast to 181.2 bushels per acre - sharply above the 177.7 traders had expected - sending December corn futures briefly limit-down before they recovered to close down 20.5 cents at $4.79¾. Meanwhile, Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida as a Category 2 storm at 9:39 PM ET, leaving more than 860,000 customers without power across the Southeast.

The week ahead opens Monday with bond markets closed for Columbus Day and stock markets open. Tuesday brings the first real test of third-quarter results: JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo and Citigroup all report before the open. September CPI follows Wednesday at 8:30 AM ET - the data point that will settle December hike odds, currently near 78%. Oil eased Friday as President Trump's midterm pledge reduced the Iran risk premium, even as Hurricane Isaias made Category 2 landfall near Destin, Florida late Friday night, leaving more than 860,000 customers without power. The near-term variable: how quickly operators restart the roughly 63% of Gulf of Mexico oil output that remains offline determines Monday's crude futures direction.

Brent crude (October 9 settlement): $102.91/barrel ▼ -1.3% · from $104.28 Thursday · Iran pledge the driverFriday's dip reflects the Strait of Hormuz risk premium easing after Trump's statement. Brent is still elevated relative to early September's mid-$90s, and the hurricane's impact on Gulf supply adds a counter-pressure that could slow the pullback in crude next week.
WTI crude (October 9 settlement): $90.40/barrel ▼ -1.2% · from $91.49 Thursday · ~1.28 million barrels per day offlineOperators evacuated 129 of 371 production platforms and eight of 11 rigs as Isaias approached. Post-storm surveys typically take 24-48 hours, so the earliest platform restarts would come Monday or Tuesday. Faster-than-expected restarts could push WTI toward $88; slower ones keep the $90 floor intact.
National gas average (AAA, October 8): $4.36/gallon last reading before Isaias landfall · autumn record paceGas prices typically lag crude moves by one to two weeks. The October 8 reading predates the hurricane. AAA will update Saturday; the direction depends on both crude easing and refinery status along the Gulf Coast. Diesel tracks crude more directly and matters for freight and farm costs.
Bottom line

Friday's crude easing is constructive, but Hurricane Isaias leaves a supply uncertainty that could keep prices from falling this week. The restart timeline and any refinery damage from the storm are the numbers to watch Monday morning.

Friday's WASDE report delivered the grain market's biggest surprise of the year. USDA raised the corn yield forecast to 181.2 bushels per acre, up 2.7 from September's 178.5 and 3.5 above the pre-report consensus of 177.7. December corn futures briefly traded limit-down before recovering to close at $4.79¾, down 20.5 cents. Soybeans were also revised upward to a projected record yield.

WASDE corn yield (USDA October 9, noon ET): 181.2 bpa ▲ +2.7 from September · vs expected 177.7 · production 16.03 billion bushels · ending stocks 1.849 billion buA 3.5-bpa upside surprise implies substantially larger supply than the market had priced. Larger ending stocks pressure grain prices at the farm level and flow - through feed chains - to meat, dairy and processed food costs over the coming months. The limit-down move reflects how aggressively the trade had leaned toward a smaller crop.
WASDE soybean yield (USDA October 9): 53.1 bpa ▲ record projected yield · production 4.562 billion bushels · domestic carryout raised 23 million buRecord soybean yield adds to the supply-side picture for oilseeds and animal feed. The WASDE cut export projections by 25 million bushels to partially offset the production gain, keeping the net carryout increase modest.
Corn harvest progress (USDA Crop Progress, week ending October 4): 23% harvested · 54% G/E ▼ behind 27% five-year average · wet September delayed paceHarvest is running late after a wet second half of September across Iowa and Nebraska. Hurricane Isaias's moisture track moves northeast after landfall, away from the main corn belt. The next weekly Crop Progress report is Monday, October 12.
Bottom line

The corn market learned Friday that there is more supply than expected. Futures fell sharply and may stay under pressure until the harvest confirms the yield estimate. For consumers, a larger crop is one pressure that works in their favor over the winter - the translation to store shelves takes months but the signal is clear.

Consumer confidence fell for a third straight month in Friday's preliminary October reading. The University of Michigan index landed at 46.3, missing the 47.6 consensus and its lowest level since May's near-record reading of 44.8. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7% - higher than September's 4.6% - adding a signal the Fed tracks closely for any signs of entrenchment.

University of Michigan consumer sentiment (preliminary October, released October 9): 46.3 ▼ from 48.1 in September · consensus 47.6 · five-month lowCurrent conditions fell 12.2% from September while consumer expectations edged up 2.2%. The disconnect suggests households feel squeezed now by gas prices and mortgage costs but expect some improvement - a pattern that often holds in the month before a Fed hold. The final October reading comes October 23.
One-year inflation expectations (UMich, October preliminary): 4.7% ▲ from 4.6% in September · five-year expectations 3.5% from 3.4%The tick up in near-term inflation expectations matters to the Fed as a signal of psychology. Both the one-year and five-year readings are above the Fed's 2% target, and a sustained rise above 4.5% typically draws comment from policymakers. Bank earnings Tuesday will add another layer to this picture.
Credit card balances 90+ days delinquent (New York Fed, Q2 2026): 12.9% ▲ from 12.2% in Q2 2025 · Q3 2026 report due in NovemberBank earnings Tuesday will give the first Q3 credit card data. The New York Fed's Q2 reading was highest in four years, concentrated in lower-income households. Bank CEOs' guidance on charge-off trends will be the most-watched line on Tuesday's earnings calls.
Bottom line

Three straight months of falling sentiment, with inflation expectations rising, is a yellow flag for holiday spending. Layoffs remain low - initial claims held at 197,000 - which provides a floor. But cost pressure is real for households without financial cushion, and bank earnings Tuesday will show whether that stress is spreading.
