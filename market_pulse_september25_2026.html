4.75% CDs Available Now. Mortgage Rates Hit 7.03%, October Hike Odds 64%.

The Quick Read

Thursday's S&P Global US Flash Composite PMI hit 58.4 - the strongest private-sector expansion since July 2021 - pushing October 28 FOMC hike odds from 55.1% to 64% in a single session. Manufacturing PMI reached 57.0 (a 52-month high) and Services hit 58.7 (59-month high), while price pressures registered the highest reading since October 2022. The mechanism: a resilient economy with accelerating price pressures satisfies exactly the "sustained strength" condition the Fed named as the bar for continued hiking. The 2-year Treasury jumped from 4.69% to approximately 4.90%, the 10-year held near 5.11%, and Freddie Mac released a 7.03% PMMS - the cycle's first above-7% print. Stocks closed near flat: S&P 500 at 7,704, Dow at 51,350, Nasdaq at 26,939.

September 30 PCE - the Fed's preferred inflation measure - is now the pivot for October 28. At 64% odds, PCE above 2.5% year-over-year pushes the hike case toward 75%; PCE at or below 2.0% is the only reading that flips the call below 50%. Top 1-year CDs remain at 4.75% APY. The expected-value math shifts at 64%: the majority probability now points toward a post-hike 4.90-5.00% ceiling if September 30 PCE confirms Thursday's price-pressure signal. October 2 September jobs completes the October 28 setup.

The Xi-Trump bilateral concluded Thursday with a 47-day trade truce extension to January 10 - no semiconductor breakthrough, supply-chain costs unchanged. Brent held near $103 with US-Iran talks active in New York. Bitcoin reclaimed $85,000-plus territory on a short squeeze, with approximately $2 billion in September ETF inflows and CLARITY Act retaining no scheduled Senate return date. The Nikkei rose 1.26% Friday morning, led by AI semiconductor and financial names on the trade truce's "no escalation" outcome.
