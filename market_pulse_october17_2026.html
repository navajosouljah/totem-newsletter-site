Top CDs Pay 4.50% Into the Blackout. Tesla, Meta, Netflix Report Monday.

The Quick Read

Friday's two morning releases both beat their forecasts. September import prices rose 1.1% month over month - above the 0.8% consensus - with fuel imports up 4.8%. Industrial production gained 0.7% against a 0.4% consensus and capacity utilization rose to 76.9%. The market's response was not a rate trade. The Nasdaq fell 2.1%, its worst week since June at -4.5% on the week. The Dow slipped 0.3% but closed the week up 0.7%. Hot energy-driven data landed on a market already pricing it - the week's selling came from AI and chip names that could not absorb another week of valuation uncertainty while rates stay elevated. That is the whole mechanism.

The FOMC blackout began Saturday morning. October 28 hike odds held near 15% through Friday's close - unmoved by a week of hot CPI, hot PPI, hot import prices, the lowest jobless claims since spring, and a production beat. The market has held its read: energy accounts for most of the inflation, core remains elevated but not accelerating, and a hold at October 28 remains the base case at 85% odds. The next 11 days contain no Fed commentary. What decides the week ahead is earnings.

Brent crude closed near $109.7 on Friday - a 3.7% single-day spike on supply fears - which will push gas averages higher starting around Tuesday or Wednesday next week. The earnings calendar opening Monday is the heaviest of 2026: Netflix, GM, Tesla, Meta, and more than 200 companies with market caps above $2 billion report. Top 1-year CDs still pay 4.50% APY this morning - a rate essentially locked for the 11-day blackout period. Bitcoin holds near $81,500.
