Pompliano on the Real Economy Comeback

Anthony Pompliano is one of the most followed voices in finance and macro investing. In his latest note, he makes the case that the real economy is staging a comeback - and productive capital will be rewarded. The question is how you participate.

8 min read Share

Anthony Pompliano (@APompliano) is one of the most followed and respected voices in finance and macro investing today. With over 2.17 million followers on X and more than 270,000 subscribers to his daily Pomp Letter on Substack, he has built a massive audience by delivering clear, data-driven commentary on markets, Bitcoin, technology, and the broader economy.

Who He Is

What makes his voice stand out is his ability to cut through noise with straightforward analysis, a long-term perspective, and a track record of being early on major shifts - Bitcoin, AI, macro rotations. People listen because he combines institutional experience with accessible insight - without the hype.

What He Said

In his latest Substack post, "The Real Economy Comeback," Pompliano delivers a concise mid-year market assessment. You can read the full piece at the link above.

Key Highlights from His Note

  • Many investors entered 2026 heavily weighted toward the Mag 7, which have lagged the rest of the S&P 500.
  • July has historically been the strongest month for stocks over the past 20 years.
  • Positive first-half performance in midterm years has often led to solid second-half returns (median +2.8%).
  • Early signs of rotation: non-AI stocks are breaking out while AI names have pulled back recently.

The data from James Thorne is particularly striking. As Thorne puts it: "The center of gravity in American capitalism is shifting from financial engineering back to the real economy." Capital isn't disappearing - it is moving into concrete, steel, copper, power, logistics, and machinery.

The stock divergence tells the story in numbers. While the S&P 500 was down roughly 2%, AI stocks (a basket of 50-75 names) fell approximately 4.52%, and non-AI stocks (the other 425-450 names) rose approximately 2.31%. That is a meaningful rotation happening in real time.

And critically, unlike the dot-com era, today's gains are backed by real earnings growth. As one analyst noted: "The run-up has been driven by fundamental earnings growth, while the 2000s was mostly just hopes and dreams." Forward P/E multiples in tech have actually contracted despite rising stock prices - the opposite of a bubble.

"The real economy comeback is underway. Productive assets and real problem-solving will be rewarded."

Where We Agree

Pompliano is directionally correct on two important fronts.

First, the AI buildout is real and earnings-driven. Unlike the dot-com era, where stock prices ran on hope and narrative, today's tech gains are backed by fundamental earnings growth with contracting forward P/E multiples. Micron just reported $41.5 billion in quarterly revenue. Nvidia's margins are expanding, not compressing. The companies building AI infrastructure are generating real cash flow - not burning it. When Pompliano says "fade the trend at your own peril," he is pointing at a structural shift, not a speculative bubble.

Second, the rotation back to the real economy is overdue and healthy. Capital is moving away from pure financial engineering - the low-capex, heavy-buyback model that dominated the last decade - and flowing into concrete, steel, copper, power, logistics, and machinery. As James Thorne puts it: "The center of gravity in American capitalism is shifting from financial engineering back to the real economy." This is the kind of productive capital deployment that builds long-term wealth. Companies solving hard problems in physical infrastructure, energy security, and supply chain resilience will be rewarded. Pompliano is right to be bullish on this shift.

Where It Gets Complicated

The complication is not in the thesis - it is in the execution. How investors actually participate in this real-economy comeback determines whether they capture the upside or get caught in the turbulence.

First, equity participation means accepting full market risk. Pompliano's framework points toward stocks, infrastructure plays, and thematic bets. That path brings full market volatility, valuation swings, potential drawdowns of 20-40%, and zero principal protection. The Nasdaq just fell 5% in a single week. Apple dropped 6% in one day. Microsoft is down 25% year-to-date. Even when the direction is right, the path to get there can be painful - and many investors cannot afford to wait years for a thesis to play out while their capital is underwater.

Second, sitting in cash means missing the rotation entirely. Many investors hold reserves in traditional bank accounts, money markets, or escrow structures earning 0-4% with FDIC coverage capped at $250,000. They are protected, but their capital is doing nothing productive while the shift Pompliano describes passes them by. The opportunity cost of idle capital in a productive-asset rotation is real and compounding.

Third, there is no middle ground in conventional finance. Investors are typically forced to choose between full equity risk or near-zero yield safety. Growth or protection. Offense or defense. The traditional financial system does not offer a way to participate in real-economy commerce with contractual returns, full insurance, and short-duration liquidity. That gap is exactly where Totem operates.
