Weekend Briefing • Saturday, August 15, 2026

July Retail Sales Fall 0.6% - Biggest Drop Since May 2025 and a Near-One-Point Miss vs. Consensus. UMich Consumer Sentiment Crashes to 51.0 With Inflation Expectations Rising to 4.3%. S&P 500 7,782.65 Caps Third Straight Weekly Gain. Brent Reverses to $88.38 as Iran Demands Sanctions Relief and War Reparations for Hormuz. September Hike Odds Slide to 25%.

Five weeks after the Hormuz crisis began, Friday delivered the first hard measurement of its damage to American household spending. Retail sales fell 0.6% in July - the worst since May 2025 and a 0.9 point miss against the +0.3% consensus - while University of Michigan consumer sentiment crashed to 51.0, well below the 55.0 estimate and down sharply from July's five-month high of 55.2. Brent crude reversed its mid-week retreat, rising 1.51% to $88.38 after Iran demanded US sanctions relief and war reparations as conditions for reopening Hormuz. The S&P 500 pulled back 0.21% from Thursday's all-time record but capped a third consecutive weekly gain, and the Russell 2000 set a new record - a breadth signal that distinguished Friday's slip from a risk-off session.

The Quick Read

Friday delivered the consumer's first hard confirmation that the Hormuz oil shock has reached household spending. July retail sales fell 0.6% - the largest monthly drop since May 2025 and a 0.9 percentage point miss against the +0.3% consensus. The miss was broad: motor vehicle and parts dealers fell 1.8%, nonstore retailers (online shopping) dropped 2.2%, while clothing and accessories gained 1.9% and food services edged up 0.5%. The ex-auto, ex-gas-station measure - which removes the oil-price distortion and the volatile auto component to show underlying consumer demand - fell 0.2%, confirming the pullback was not limited to the fuel categories. Year-over-year, retail sales are still up 5.0% from July 2025, but the monthly deterioration signals that the trajectory has turned: the consumer who absorbed May and June without pulling back began visibly cutting in July.

University of Michigan's August preliminary consumer sentiment reinforced the retail signal with a matching demographic pattern. The composite index fell to 51.0 from July's five-month high of 55.2 - an 8% decline that came in well below the 54.1 consensus. Current conditions dropped to 51.8 from 54.8, consumer expectations fell to 50.6 from 55.4, and expected business conditions declined 11% for the short run and 17% for the long run. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.3% - the highest since March 2026, and approaching the 4.5% level that the three July FOMC dissenters identified as a threshold indicator for sequential inflation evidence. The demographic breakdown shows Republicans falling 19% below pre-Iran-conflict levels; large declines also recorded among older consumers, lower-income households, and those without a college degree - the households most directly exposed to gas prices and credit costs.

The equity market absorbed the double miss without a broad-based selloff. The S&P 500 slipped 0.21% to 7,782.65, pulling back from Thursday's all-time closing record of 7,798.99 while capping a third consecutive weekly gain. The Dow fell 0.20% to 53,732 and the Nasdaq declined approximately 0.30% to near 26,723. The Russell 2000 - tracking small-cap companies most exposed to domestic consumer conditions - set a new all-time record on Friday, a breadth signal that distinguished the session from a risk-off event: the market's reading was that weak consumer data reduces September hike urgency, and reduced hike urgency supports equity valuations broadly. Oil reversed course: Brent rose 1.51% to $88.38 after Iran's top security official stated Hormuz reopening requires US sanctions removal and war reparations - conditions that marine insurance markets and commercial operators regard as non-starters for near-term operational planning. September hike odds fell from approximately 30-35% to approximately 25% on the combined consumer weakness and reduced hike urgency signal.

What the Week Closed With

Five developments define the weekend backdrop: the July retail sales collapse, the UMich sentiment crash, the equity market's surprisingly orderly response including a Russell 2000 record, Brent's reversal on Iran's conditions, and the Bitcoin ETF outflow that partially reversed the prior week's institutional inflow surge.

  • July retail sales fell 0.6% - the worst miss versus consensus in more than a year and the largest monthly drop since May 2025. The Census Bureau reported July retail and food services sales of $763.6 billion, down 0.6% from June and up 5.0% from July 2025. Consensus was +0.3%. The headline miss of 0.9 percentage points is the largest single-month consensus deviation since early 2025. The ex-auto and ex-gas measure fell 0.2%, ruling out the interpretation that the headline was driven entirely by the gas-station and auto categories. Food services held (+0.5%) and clothing gained (+1.9%), but the broad contraction in high-ticket and online spending confirms that July's $4.00 gas, 22.18% credit card rates, and 2.7% savings rate are producing the demand pullback that had been anticipated but not yet measured.
  • University of Michigan August preliminary consumer sentiment: 51.0 - well below the 55.0 consensus and down sharply from the 55.2 July final. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.3%. The August preliminary captures the first full week of August: $4.00+ gas, near-record equities, and Hormuz uncertainty. The composite's 7.5% decline from July's five-month high reflects a K-shaped deterioration: Republicans fell 19% below pre-Iran-conflict levels, lower-income and older consumers also saw notably large declines. The 4.3% one-year inflation expectation is the most important sub-component for the Fed: it has risen from 3.8% in June, approaches the 4.5% level the three July dissenters cited as their threshold, and suggests the supply-shock inflation is becoming embedded in household planning horizons despite demand weakness.
  • S&P 500 7,782.65 caps third consecutive weekly gain despite pulling back 0.21% from Thursday's all-time record 7,798.99. Russell 2000 sets a new record on Friday - the breadth signal of the week. The large-cap indices all retreated modestly: S&P 500 -0.21%, Dow -0.20% to 53,732, Nasdaq -0.30% to approximately 26,723. The small-cap Russell 2000 moving to a record while large caps pulled back is the session's defining breadth signal: reduced September hike odds from the retail sales miss are flowing into the rate-sensitive domestic companies that dominate the Russell 2000 index, while the large-cap technology names that led Thursday's record took a one-session breather. Reddit rose approximately 14% on the S&P 500 inclusion announcement made after Thursday's close. For the full week, the S&P 500 recorded its third consecutive weekly advance - absorbing a Hormuz oil shock, a 50% September hike probability peak, and now a consumer spending miss, and coming out the other side with a net gain.
  • Brent crude reverses to $88.38 (+1.51%) as Iran's conditions for Hormuz reopening - US sanctions removal and war reparations - eliminate any near-term commercial transit timeline. After four sessions of decline that brought Brent from near $90 to approximately $87 (Thursday close), oil reversed on Friday following Iran's official conditions for reopening the strait. The conditions - lifting US-imposed sanctions and paying unspecified war reparations - are not negotiating concessions but structural demands that would require US domestic legislation and international legal frameworks. Marine insurance markets require verified passage data and confirmed diplomatic frameworks before restoring coverage; presidential statements and diplomatic signals do not meet that threshold. The commercial shipping industry has now received Iran's formal conditions and regards them as non-starters for 2026 operational planning. The Brent premium over pre-crisis levels ($75-76) is now being priced as a prolonged disruption rather than a near-term deal scenario.
  • Bitcoin ETF net outflow of $56.2 million on Friday ($55.5 million from IBIT alone) - partially reversing the prior week's $854 million institutional inflow run. The Friday outflow is a sharp nominal reversal from the institutional inflow pace that had defined the prior week, but represents approximately 6.6% of the prior week's total inflow - a one-session rebalancing rather than a structural shift. Bitcoin closed at $63,120.69. The institutional accumulation range at $63,000-$65,000 established by last week's $854M inflow remains intact as the cost-basis anchor. The Friday outflow pattern is consistent with end-of-week profit-taking and risk-management rebalancing in the context of a consumer data disappointment, not a fundamental position change.

September hike odds fell to approximately 25% - reflecting the market's conclusion that the Fed will not hike into a deteriorating consumer, even with inflation expectations rising toward the 4.5% threshold.

Bottom Line

A hold in September is the base case; a hold that allows 4.3% inflation expectations to continue rising is a Fed credibility problem that compounds with each month of inaction.
