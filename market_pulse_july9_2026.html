Weekly Briefing • Thursday, July 9, 2026

Iran Ceasefire Collapses. Oil Surges to $74. SpaceX Joins the Nasdaq-100. Home Sales Drop Again.

The fragile stability broke this week. Trump declared the Iran ceasefire "over" from the NATO stage in Turkey, the U.S. struck 80 targets inside Iran, and oil jumped 8% in two days. SpaceX became the fastest company ever added to the Nasdaq-100 - just 15 trading days after its $75 billion IPO. Existing home sales fell 2.4% in June. The July 14 CPI report is five days away.

This Week in the News

  • Iran ceasefire collapses. Trump declared the ceasefire "over" at the NATO summit in Turkey. The U.S. struck 80+ targets inside Iran after Tehran attacked commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran retaliated with missiles against U.S. bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. Oil surged 5% on Wednesday.
  • SpaceX joins the Nasdaq-100 in record time. Just 15 trading days after its $75 billion IPO - the largest in history - SpaceX entered the Nasdaq-100 on July 7 under new fast-track rules for mega-IPOs. Over $4 billion in passive fund inflows are expected.
  • Existing home sales fell 2.4% in June. Sales totaled a seasonally adjusted 4.9 million annualized, below the expected 0.7% decline. Higher mortgage rates and elevated prices continue to freeze buyer activity.
  • The Dow swung 800 points in two days. A 577-point drop on Wednesday on Iran headlines was followed by a 223-point recovery on Thursday as oil pulled back and chipmakers rallied.

The Quick Read

The Iran ceasefire collapsing rewrote the week. Oil jumped from $69 to $74, erasing three weeks of energy disinflation in 48 hours. The Dow dropped 577 points on Wednesday - its worst day since mid-June - before recovering 223 points Thursday as markets tried to price in a new normal where $70+ oil is the floor, not the ceiling. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.55%, mortgage rates ticked up to 6.50%, and the S&P 500 closed at 7,519. Gold pulled back to $4,075 after last week's $4,170 spike. Bitcoin held steady near $62,200 despite the geopolitical noise. Existing home sales fell 2.4% in June to a 4.9 million pace.

The energy story is the one that matters most for the July 14 CPI report. Last week, falling oil was the best case for a sub-4% CPI print. This week, WTI at $74 puts that at risk. If crude stays above $72 through mid-July, the disinflation narrative that powered the Dow to a 53,055 record earlier this week weakens. The good news: gas prices at the pump have not yet reflected the crude spike - the national average held at $3.80. Refiners and inventory buffers buy time, but if the Iran conflict escalates further, the pump catches up within two weeks.

SpaceX joining the Nasdaq-100 in 15 trading days is the structural story of the week. The new fast-track rule means any mega-IPO ranked in the top 40 by market cap enters the index almost immediately, forcing billions in passive buying. This changes how IPO markets work - and how concentrated index ownership becomes. Meanwhile, the housing data keeps deteriorating: existing home sales fell, FHA delinquency remains near 12%, and foreclosure starts are running 20% above year-ago levels. The consumer below the median is still stretched.

Stock Market

Iran headlines whipsawed equities this week. The Dow dropped 577 points Wednesday then recovered Thursday. SpaceX's Nasdaq-100 entry reshapes index composition.

S&P 500: 7,519 +0.5% Thu
Dow Jones: 52,571 −0.6% from Monday's 52,900 record
Nasdaq: 25,871 +0.9% Thu (chipmakers led)
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,992 +1.2% Thu
Fear gauge (VIX): 16.06 −5.0% Thu
Bottom Line
  • The Dow swinging from a 53,055 record earlier this week to a 577-point drop Wednesday to a partial recovery Thursday shows a market that has no conviction on the Iran situation. Until there is clarity on whether this is a contained flare-up or a sustained conflict, every oil headline moves equities. The VIX dropping to 16 on Thursday suggests traders are treating this as temporary - which is exactly the setup for a volatility spike if strikes continue over the weekend.
  • SpaceX entering the Nasdaq-100 at a ~1% weighting with $4 billion in forced passive inflows is the structural shift to watch. The fast-track rule means future mega-IPOs will reshape index composition within weeks of listing. For QQQ holders, this dilutes existing positions slightly and increases concentration risk in a small number of very large private-to-public transitions.

Interest Rates

Treasury yields climbed on Iran-driven oil fears. Mortgage rates ticked up. The July 14 CPI report will determine whether the rate-cut trade survives.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held since December 2025)
2-year Treasury: 4.19% +5 bps wk
10-year Treasury: 4.55% +6 bps wk
30-year Treasury: 5.02% broke above 5% again
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.50% +7 bps from last week's 6.43%
Bottom Line
  • Last week's mortgage rate of 6.43% was the "good news is good news" trade - weak jobs data meant rate cuts were closer. This week, oil at $74 reversed that. If the Iran conflict keeps crude above $72, the July CPI print is unlikely to show the energy disinflation the market was counting on. That means rates stay elevated longer and the Q4 rate-cut timeline slips.
  • The 30-year Treasury breaking above 5% again is the bond market saying inflation is not resolved. Each time the long end crosses this threshold, it tests whether the Fed's holding stance is credible. A July CPI above 4.2% keeps the 30-year above 5% and puts mortgage rates back on a path toward 6.75%.

Savings & CD Rates

Best CD rates holding steady. The Iran flare-up may delay rate cuts, which means savers keep earning above inflation for now.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.99% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.10-4.15% APY (Popular Direct, E*TRADE, Bask Bank)
Best credit union CD: 6.00% APY (Financial Partners CU, 8-month)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • The Iran escalation is paradoxically good news for savers in the short term. If oil keeps the CPI elevated and the Fed delays cuts, these 4-6% CD rates persist longer. Last week the case was "lock in now before cuts." This week, the urgency eases slightly - but the window still closes eventually. A 4.15% 12-month CD from Popular Direct or a 6.00% 8-month credit union special both remain well above current CPI on a forward basis.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil surged on Iran strikes. Three weeks of energy disinflation erased in 48 hours. Gas prices have not yet caught up at the pump.

WTI crude: $74.20/bbl +8% from last week's $68.76
Brent crude: $75.53/bbl +5% wk
National gas avg: $3.80/gal flat (pump lags crude by 1-2 wks)
West Coast (California): $5.18/gal +$0.42 from last week
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): $3.34/gal
Bottom Line
  • The $69-to-$74 crude move this week undid the best energy story of the summer. Last week, falling oil was the clearest consumer tailwind and the strongest case for a sub-4% CPI print. Now that hinges entirely on whether Iran escalates further or this becomes another contained flare-up. History favors containment - every Iran spike since 2019 has faded within 2-4 weeks - but the ceasefire collapse makes this one different in kind.
  • Gas at $3.80 nationally has not yet reflected the crude surge. Retail prices typically lag wholesale by 7-14 days. If WTI holds above $74, expect the national average to climb toward $4.00 by late July. California at $5.18 - now $1.84 above the Gulf Coast at $3.34 - shows how state taxes and refinery constraints amplify any crude move on the coasts.

Commodities

Gold pulled back from last week's highs. Corn fell on USDA supply data. Cattle steady after last week's $6.60 drop.

Gold / oz: $4,075 −2.3% from last week's $4,170
Silver / oz: $57.70 −5.4% (lowest since Dec 2025)
Copper / lb: $6.02 −2.4%
Corn / bushel: $4.32 −2.4% (USDA signaling ample supply)
Soybeans / bushel: $11.85 +4.7% from last week
Live cattle / cwt: $237.72 −$1.51 wk
Bottom Line
  • Gold pulling back to $4,075 from $4,170 despite the Iran escalation is notable. It suggests last week's spike was driven by the weak jobs report and rate-cut hopes, not geopolitical hedging. If gold does not rally on an actual shooting war, the safe-haven bid may be fully priced in at current levels. A July CPI below 4% would be more bullish for gold than any missile strike.
  • Corn falling 2.4% on USDA data showing ample supply is a rare piece of good news on the food inflation front. But soybeans rising nearly 5% shows the crop story is mixed, not resolved. Cattle at $237.72 remain elevated relative to historical norms - the US herd at 86.2 million head (smallest since 1951) keeps a structural floor under beef prices regardless of short-term pullbacks.

Crop Weather

Corn conditions holding. Winter wheat harvest locked in at the worst level since 1965. Drought improving slightly but still affecting 125 million Americans.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent (steady wk/wk). Heat wave receding from western Corn Belt after approaching 100°F during pollination window.
Winter wheat: 48% good-to-excellent. Production forecast at 1,030M bushels - lowest since 1965. 37% rated poor-to-very poor. Harvest 48% complete.
Soybeans: 65% good-to-excellent (down 1 pt wk).
Drought: 47.8% of Lower 48 in drought. 50% of Nebraska in extreme-to-exceptional drought. 125.7 million people affected.
Bottom Line
  • Corn holding at 67% good-to-excellent after the heat wave is encouraging. The worst-case pollination damage scenario did not materialize - yet. But the next two USDA Crop Progress reports remain the highest-stakes readings of the growing season. If corn ratings slip below 64%, the supply narrative shifts quickly.
  • Winter wheat at 1,030 million bushels - the worst harvest since 1965 - is locked in and already flowing into food prices. Higher flour, bread, and cereal costs in Q3 and Q4 are a near-certainty. This operates on a separate clock from the oil-driven CPI story. You can have falling gas and rising grocery bills at the same time.

Real Estate

Home sales fell again in June. Mortgage rates ticking back up. FHA delinquency near 12%. The housing market's brief window of optimism is closing.

Existing-home sales (June): 4.90M ann. −2.4% MoM (below expectations)
Foreclosure starts Q1: 82,631 +20% YoY
Bank repossessions / REOs Q1: 14,020 +45% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.88% highest since Q2 2021
Inventory: 4.5 months existing; 10.3 months new homes
30-year mortgage: 6.50% +7 bps wk
Bottom Line
  • June existing home sales at 4.9 million - down 2.4% and below expectations - confirm that the brief rate dip to 6.43% last week was not enough to unfreeze the market. With rates now ticking back up to 6.50% on the Iran oil spike, the window of slightly cheaper borrowing is closing before it moved the needle on actual sales volume.
  • FHA delinquency holding near 12% while foreclosure starts run 20% above year-ago levels is a pipeline that takes 12-18 months to resolve. These are first-time buyers who purchased at peak prices with low down payments. Falling rates help new buyers but do nothing for borrowers already behind on payments. The distress is accumulating, not stabilizing, even though it remains well below 2008-2010 levels.

Household Finance

Consumer balance sheets remain stretched. Savings rate recovering but still historically low. The 57,000 jobs print from last week means less new income entering the system.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.33 trillion record; avg APR 21.0%
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo 20.3% of buyers now pay $1,000+/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days delinquent: 6.90% 32-year high (Fitch, Jan 2026)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 3.0% up from 2.6% in April
Bottom Line
  • The savings rate at 3.0% is still the one bright spot, but it is fragile. Last week's improvement was likely driven by falling gas prices freeing up cash. If the Iran oil spike pushes gas back toward $4.00, that savings recovery reverses. The consumer below the median is running on thin margins where a $20-per-week gas increase is the difference between saving something and saving nothing.
  • Three independent data sources - subprime auto at a 32-year high, 47% of BNPL users late, and credit card delinquencies approaching 2008 levels - tell the same story from different angles. The K-shaped split is widening: upper-income households are benefiting from record stock prices and high-yield savings accounts, while lower-income households are servicing record debt at a 21% APR with fewer new jobs entering the system.

Crypto

Crypto flat despite geopolitical noise. Bitcoin holding above $62,000. SEC "Regulation Crypto" agenda set for July.

Bitcoin (BTC): $62,214 flat wk/wk
Ethereum (ETH): $1,733 −0.2% wk
Solana (SOL): $77.06 −6.0% wk
XRP: $1.08 −3.6% wk
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin holding flat at $62,200 while the Dow swung 800 points and oil spiked 8% is the most interesting non-move of the week. Last week, BTC rallied 5% on weak jobs data. This week, it ignored the Iran escalation entirely. That decoupling - rallying on rate-cut hopes but ignoring geopolitical risk - suggests crypto is trading purely as a liquidity asset, not a safe haven. The July 14 CPI is the next catalyst.
  • The SEC's "Regulation Crypto" agenda slated for release this month could be the larger story for crypto markets in July. Clear regulatory guidelines would be constructive for institutional adoption. Solana dropping 6% and XRP falling 3.6% on the week may reflect pre-regulatory positioning more than macro factors.

Around the World

Asia recovered Thursday. Europe sold off hard on Iran oil fears. The ECB's rate path just got more complicated.

Japan: Nikkei at 67,744 −2.9% from last week's 69,744. Pulled back from 70,000 on Iran risk. Still best-performing major index of 2026.
Europe: STOXX 600 at 634 −2.9% wk. Record high from last week erased on oil surge. Higher energy costs hit European margins disproportionately.
China: Shanghai Composite at 4,037 −0.2% wk. Holding steady. Domestic stimulus offsetting global risk-off.
Emerging: Under pressure from strong dollar and rising oil. The July 24 tariff expiration on 60+ economies remains the next binary event.
Bottom Line
  • Europe's STOXX 600 dropping 2.9% from its record high last week is the Iran trade in its clearest form. European industry is more energy-dependent than the U.S., so a sustained move in oil above $72 raises input costs, compresses margins, and forces the ECB to hold rates higher for longer. Last week's story was European equities benefiting from falling oil. That story reversed in 48 hours.
  • The July 24 tariff expiration on 60+ economies is now 15 days away and still underpriced. If temporary tariffs become permanent while oil is elevated, emerging markets face a double squeeze - higher import costs and capital flight to the dollar. The combination of Iran risk and tariff uncertainty makes the next three weeks unusually dense with binary outcomes.
