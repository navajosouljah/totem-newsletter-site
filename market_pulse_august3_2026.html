Monday Briefing • Monday, August 3, 2026

US and Japan Stage First Coordinated Yen Defense Since 1998 as the Dollar Retreats. September Rate Hike Now the Market Consensus at 64.5%. Futures Rally on Middle East De-Escalation. ISM Manufacturing and USDA Crop Progress Print This Morning. SpaceX Reports Its First Public Earnings Tuesday.

The yen recovered from 163.73 to the 156 handle after a joint US-Japan currency operation - the first coordinated intervention in 28 years. Five labor market reads culminate in Friday's jobs report, the data point that will test whether the market's 64.5% September hike probability becomes a certainty or collapses.

The Quick Read

The setup entering Monday: S&P 500 futures are up roughly 0.5% before the opening bell, lifted by two weekend developments that have not yet been priced into Friday's close. The first is a confirmed de-escalation in Middle East tensions that removes the near-term supply-risk premium from oil - WTI holds near $85, well below the mid-July peak of $93, and the fear of a rapid re-escalation has receded. The second is a joint US-Japan currency operation that pulled USD/JPY back from 163.73 to the 156 handle over the weekend - the first coordinated foreign exchange intervention between the two nations in 28 years. That move did not involve interest rates; it came from the Treasury and Bank of Japan foreign exchange desks. The distinction matters for Fed policy.

The week's framework is unchanged from Sunday's briefing: five labor market releases culminating in Friday's July jobs report (consensus: +91,000, unemployment 4.3%). What has shifted is the market's baseline for what those numbers mean. Futures markets now price a 64.5% probability of a rate hike at the September 15-16 FOMC meeting - not a hold, a hike. That probability reflects Friday's PCE data for June showing headline inflation at 3.7% year-over-year and core PCE at 3.3%, both well above the Fed's 2% target, and the three July 29 dissents that signaled a growing minority ready to act. A jobs number above 150,000 Friday would likely push that 64.5% above 70%. A miss below 75,000 would reopen the debate but would not eliminate September hike risk by itself.

ISM Manufacturing PMI lands at 10 AM ET this morning. The July reading is the first major economic signal of the week. The June reading came in below 50 - contraction territory - for a second consecutive month. A third straight contraction would add pressure to the dovish case heading into a week that will ultimately be decided by Friday's payroll count. USDA crop progress also releases this morning; corn was at 63% good-to-excellent as of July 27, down four points in one week, with 47.9% of the Lower 48 in moderate drought or worse during grain fill's most heat-sensitive period.

SpaceX reports earnings for the first time as a public company on Tuesday, August 4. There is no comparable precedent for how the market will price guidance from a company that launched its IPO on ambition and is now being asked to account for it in quarterly form. Tuesday's report will set the template for how SpaceX is valued from here.

Monday Morning

  • The US Treasury and Bank of Japan intervened jointly in currency markets over the weekend - the first coordinated action since 1998. USD/JPY fell to 163.73, the weakest yen level in years, before the intervention pulled the pair back to the 156-157 range. The 1998 precedent came during the Russian financial crisis when a collapsing yen threatened Asian financial stability. The current move reflects the interest rate differential between the US (3.75% upper band) and Japan (1.00%) that has been pulling capital relentlessly into dollars and out of yen. Coordination signals both governments see the current trajectory as disorderly. It does not change the rate differential. It changes the confidence with which it plays out. The Nikkei is down 0.9% Monday morning as a stronger yen weighs on Japanese exporters.
  • Middle East de-escalation improved risk appetite heading into the week. Futures markets interpreted the development as reducing the near-term oil supply risk that drove WTI to $93 in mid-July. WTI holds near $85 Monday morning, Brent near $90. The geopolitical risk premium has eased; the underlying production picture has not changed.
  • The September rate hike probability has moved from roughly 50-50 to 64.5%. Futures markets now price a hike as the base case at the September 15-16 meeting. That shift was driven by Friday's PCE data (headline PCE -0.1% MoM but +3.7% YoY; core PCE +0.1% MoM, +3.3% YoY) and the three-dissent July 29 FOMC vote. A 64.5% probability is not a certainty - it is the market saying a hike is more likely than a hold given what we know today. The jobs report Friday is the swing data point.
  • CXMT, a Chinese memory chipmaker, debuted on a Chinese exchange Monday and closed approximately 466% above its IPO price - Asia's largest IPO of 2026. The debut is the most direct market signal yet of China's push to build a domestically independent semiconductor supply chain, arriving one week after South Korea's KOSPI staged an 18% single-day recovery on cloud-driven AI memory demand. The two events are opposite sides of the same competition.
  • Q2 earnings season closes with 85% of S&P 500 reporters beating expectations and aggregate profits tracking +47% year-over-year growth - the strongest earnings season since 2021. The gap between that earnings performance and the market's relatively flat return for July reflects the rate-discount math: even +47% profit growth does not automatically justify higher multiples when the 10-year yield is near 4.75% and a September hike is the probable outcome.
  • June PCE inflation was confirmed Friday: headline PCE -0.1% month-over-month and +3.7% year-over-year (down from +4.1% in May); core PCE +0.1% month-over-month and +3.3% year-over-year. Consumer spending rose 0.3% nominally and 0.4% in real terms. The monthly headline decline was energy-driven - the Iran truce pulled fuel prices lower in June. Strip out energy and the underlying inflation picture changed very little.

Nasdaq Composite (Friday close): 25,373.85 +1.0% Friday; cloud and AI names carried the index

Dow Jones futures: +0.56%

Bottom Line
  • Monday's futures rally is real but narrow in its cause: Middle East de-escalation removes a tail risk, it does not create new upside. The S&P at 7,490 is priced for a soft landing with record earnings and a Fed that holds. One of those three assumptions - the Fed holding - is now less than 36% probable. The market has not resolved that tension; it has priced around it while earnings season ran. ISM Manufacturing at 10 AM ET this morning is the first data point that begins to price the post-earnings reality.
  • This week's key equity events: SpaceX first public earnings Tuesday after market close (no comparable precedent for how the market values a company that was priced on ambition before quarterly reporting began). Palantir, AMD, and Eli Lilly also report this week. If ISM comes in below 49 for a third straight month, the earnings tailwind will have to fight against an explicit manufacturing contraction signal.

Interest Rates

September hike probability at 64.5% is the new rate context. Every labor market release this week is a vote on whether that number moves higher or lower by Friday.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% held July 29; 9-3 vote; three dissents in favor of +25bp hike; next meeting September 15-16
September hike probability (futures market, Aug 3): 64.5% shifted from roughly 50-50 framing last week; market now prices a hike as the base case, not a hold
2-year Treasury: ~4.23% front end priced for tightening; holding near last week's close
10-year Treasury: ~4.75% near the July 31 close; eased modestly from last week's intraday high as PCE printed softer than feared on the monthly read
30-year Treasury: ~5.23% eased slightly from last week's 5.28%; still at levels not seen in 19 years; long-end fiscal premium intact
30-year mortgage (Freddie Mac, July 30): 6.66% last official weekly reading; daily rates as of Monday August 3 running approximately 6.78% as bond market movement since PCE works through
Next Freddie Mac survey: August 7 - publishes the same morning as the July jobs report
Bottom Line
  • A 64.5% September hike probability is not a certainty but it is a directional statement. The market is saying: given PCE at 3.7% YoY, three dissenting FOMC votes, and a labor market that only added 57,000 jobs in June but still has unemployment at 4.2%, the more likely outcome at the next meeting is another quarter-point increase. That calculus changes materially if Friday's jobs report misses badly. A sub-75,000 print would likely push September hike odds below 50%. A print above 150,000 would push them toward 80%.
  • The 30-year at 5.23% is the Treasury market's independent commentary on the fiscal situation. That yield is not only pricing Fed hike risk - it is pricing the supply of new debt that needs to find buyers at a time when foreign central banks (including the Bank of Japan, which just participated in a currency intervention) are running their own balance sheet constraints. The Treasury Quarterly Refunding statement Wednesday sets the actual issuance schedule. That statement is as important to the long end as any Fed speech this week.
  • The spread between the Freddie Mac weekly (6.66%) and Monday's daily mortgage rate (approximately 6.78%) reflects the bond market movement since the July 30 survey closed. The next Freddie Mac reading Friday morning will capture whatever move the jobs number produces. A strong payrolls print could push the next mortgage survey above 6.85%.

Savings & CD Rates

A 64.5% September hike probability strengthens, not weakens, the case for locking in today's available rates.

National avg 1-yr CD (FDIC, July): 1.68% APY what most deposit dollars still earn at the average bank
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.40% APY (E*TRADE, no minimum); a 2.6x premium over the national average on the same FDIC-insured product
Best credit union 1-yr CD: 4.05% APY (NASA Federal CU, $1K minimum)
Bottom Line
  • If September brings a hike to 3.75-4.00%, the 1-year CD market will likely respond with rates in the 4.50-4.65% range. The question for a saver today is whether to lock 4.40% now or wait six weeks for a potentially better number. For money sitting at 1.68% at the average bank, waiting is still better than the status quo either way - but the cost of waiting is now real rather than theoretical, since the rate may not move up as much as the probability implies if the jobs data surprises to the downside.
  • The ladder argument - spreading maturities across 6-month, 1-year, and 2-year CDs - has practical merit this week. The short end is attractive now. The long end may be more attractive in October if the September hike materializes. Splitting is an acknowledgment that you do not know exactly when the best rate arrives, not a hedge against getting it wrong.

Energy & Gas Prices

WTI holds near $85 as Middle East de-escalation removes the spike tail risk. The crude-to-pump lag is still closing against consumers.

WTI Crude (Monday morning): ~$85/barrel holding near Friday's close; de-escalation removed the risk of a rapid return to $90-plus; still well below mid-July's $93 peak
Brent Crude: ~$90/barrel Middle East premium stable; geopolitical risk premium eased but not eliminated
Natural gas (Henry Hub, Monday): ~$2.766/MMBtu slightly above last week's $2.747; trending down from late-July highs near $3.00; summer cooling demand as the driver
National gas avg (EIA, most recent): ~$4.10/gal EIA weekly retail survey publishes today - the July crude spike is still working through to the pump with a 2-4 week lag
West Coast: ~$5.12/gal California near $5.49; widest regional spread in the country
Gulf Coast: ~$3.69/gal cheapest region nationally; 97 cents below California on the same gallon
Bottom Line
  • The Middle East de-escalation removes the fear of a return to $90-plus WTI in the near term, but it does not change the supply math. WTI at $85 is still 28% above where it was before the July escalation began. The EIA's own 3Q26 forecast calls for the national average at the pump to ease toward $3.80 by quarter-end. That relief requires WTI to hold in the $80-85 range through August and September - the de-escalation makes that scenario more likely, not guaranteed.
  • Today's EIA retail gasoline report is the week's first consumer data point. Given the 2-to-4 week lag from crude to pump, and the fact that WTI peaked at $93 roughly two weeks ago, this morning's reading likely shows the tail end of the price spike still working through before relief from the current $85 level starts arriving at the pump. Watch the Gulf Coast reading - it moves first when crude retreats.

Silver / oz: $58.26 +1.10% Monday; recovering some of last week's -2.35% loss; down 6.10% over the past month but +55.68% year-over-year

Corn (Sep) / bu: ~$4.64 grain fill at peak heat sensitivity; USDA crop progress due this morning

Bottom Line
  • Gold at $4,061 is recovering from a weekend dip and holding above $4,000 for the third consecutive week. The structural driver - central bank buying, geopolitical hedging, real-rate positioning - is not a trade that unwinds on a single labor report. The Middle East de-escalation took a small safe-haven premium out of gold Friday; Monday's +0.46% suggests buyers returned at the lower price.
  • Silver's year-over-year number (+55.68%) reflects an industrial demand component that gold does not carry. Silver is used in solar panels and electronics manufacturing, and the AI buildout's demand for data center power has been a sustained tailwind. The monthly decline reflects dollar-strength pressure that hit all commodities when the Fed held hawkishly on July 29.
  • Copper at $6.44 is the commodity to watch against Friday's jobs number. Copper prices raw industrial demand without the safe-haven overlay gold carries. If payrolls disappoint materially Friday, copper will price a growth-slowdown signal faster than any equity index will.

The USDA weekly crop progress report drops this morning. Four consecutive weeks of declining corn ratings would be the food-price alarm ahead of August 12 CPI.

Corn (last reading, July 27): 63% good-to-excellent down 4 points in one week; three straight weeks of decline; grain fill at peak heat sensitivity
Drought (July 27): 47.9% of the Lower 48 in moderate drought or worse Northern Plains deteriorated for a third straight week; Corn Belt heat stress concentrated through a critical growing window
Today's data: USDA Weekly Crop Progress due this morning - will show whether the decline extends to a fourth consecutive week
Key date ahead: August 12 - USDA crop production report (first official yield estimate reflecting July heat stress) lands the same morning as July CPI
Bottom Line
  • A fourth consecutive week of declining corn ratings - which this morning's data could confirm - would be the clearest signal yet that the August 12 crop production estimate will be bearish. Food prices have been the sticky component of core inflation even as energy fell in June. A bad crop season adds an inflation input that neither the Fed nor consumers can engineer away before September.
  • The August 12 double-header (USDA yield estimate + July CPI) is the most consequential single morning between now and the September FOMC. If both prints disappoint - crop conditions lower than expected and CPI holding above 3.5% - the 64.5% September hike probability markets price today could move materially higher in a single morning, 34 days before the meeting.

Household Finance

June PCE confirmed a meaningful monthly improvement driven entirely by energy. The core inflation trend is not moving fast enough for the Fed's dissenters.

PCE inflation (June, Commerce Dept.): -0.1% MoM +3.7% YoY (down from +4.1% in May); monthly decline driven by the Iran truce pulling fuel prices lower; first monthly PCE decline since April 2020
Core PCE (ex-food & energy, June): +0.1% MoM +3.3% YoY; the Fed's preferred inflation measure; still 1.3 percentage points above the 2% target
Consumer spending (June): +0.3% nominal +0.4% real (inflation-adjusted); households kept spending even as savings rate fell
U of Michigan Sentiment (July final): 55.2 near-12% jump from June; five-month high; 1-yr inflation expectations held at 4.2%; 5-10 yr expectations at 3.3%
Conference Board Consumer Confidence (July): 90.8 down 1.4 pts from June; expectations component weakened on job market concerns - opposite direction from Michigan
Personal savings rate (June): 2.7% spending has outrun income; the sentiment bounce needs a jobs foundation to persist
CPI (June, BLS): +3.5% YoY down from 4.2% in May; -0.4% MoM; energy-driven monthly decline; core CPI at 2.6% YoY
Retail sales (June): +0.2% overall +0.7% excluding gasoline; underlying consumer spending holding even as sentiment surveys diverge
Credit card APR (Fed G.19, May): 21.15% the cost of spending on thin savings
Bottom Line
  • The June inflation picture has two honest readings. The headline story - PCE down -0.1% MoM, CPI down -0.4% MoM - shows real improvement driven by falling energy prices after the Iran truce. The core story - PCE core at 3.3% YoY, CPI core at 2.6% YoY - shows that the underlying price pressures in shelter, services, and food are not moving fast enough to satisfy a Fed with three officials already voting to hike. The monthly PCE decline is welcome. It is not a trend until it repeats without the energy driver, and July energy prices (with WTI briefly hitting $93) will not provide that same tailwind for the August reading.
  • Consumer spending rose a real +0.4% in June even as the savings rate fell to 2.7%. That is a late-cycle pattern: households maintain living standards by spending down savings as income growth lags. The credit card APR of 21.15% is the mechanism by which that spending converts to financial stress for households funding it on revolving debt. The K-shape continues: households with assets (home equity, investment accounts) are in a different position than households funding daily expenses on credit cards at 21% while their FHA mortgage approaches delinquency. Friday's jobs number is the most direct input on whether the income side can catch up.
  • The Michigan-Conference Board split persists. Michigan at 55.2 picked up near-term gas-price relief and improved present-condition scores. Conference Board at 90.8 picked up weakening forward-looking job market expectations. Both are measuring real things. The jobs report Friday will say which reading was closer to right.

Spot Bitcoin ETF inflows (all of July): $172.8M modest relative to price recovery; large institutional buyers are waiting for rate-path clarity before adding
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin at $62,865 is inside a descending technical channel, trading below its 20-day moving average with below-neutral RSI - the pattern that follows an asset selling on rate signals rather than buying on fundamentals. The 64.5% September hike probability is a headwind for crypto for the same reason it is a headwind for any asset that benefits from lower rates and more risk appetite: higher rates increase the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset and reduce liquidity across the financial system.
  • Spot Bitcoin ETF inflows of $172.8 million across all of July are muted compared to the flows that drove BTC above $80,000 earlier in the year. The restrained institutional interest reflects the same uncertainty compressing equity multiples and CD ladders: investors who can wait for the September rate outcome are waiting. The jobs report Friday does not resolve the September question definitively, but a decisive print will move the probability and the assets sensitive to it.

Around the World

The US-Japan yen intervention is the biggest international market event of the weekend - and one of the most historically significant foreign exchange actions in 28 years.

USD/JPY: ~156.46 recovered from the weekend low of 163.73 after US-Japan coordinated FX intervention; the yen's largest single-weekend recovery in years; first coordinated US-Japan intervention since 1998
Nikkei 225 (Japan, Monday): -0.9% yen strength weighing on Japanese exporters; a stronger yen compresses yen-denominated profits for Japan's export-heavy industrial companies
DXY (US Dollar Index, Monday): 99.72 -0.19% on the day; dollar softening broadly after the intervention; down 1.13% over the past month
EUR/USD: ~1.1559 euro benefiting from dollar weakness; approaching a two-week high
Shanghai Composite (China, Monday): -0.6% markets digesting the 49.2 July manufacturing PMI and domestic demand weakness
Hang Seng (Hong Kong, Monday): +0.4% modest recovery; CXMT IPO sentiment providing domestic tech lift
CXMT (China chip IPO, Monday debut): +466% above IPO price Asia's largest IPO of 2026; Chinese memory chipmaker; direct market signal of China's semiconductor independence push
STOXX 600 (Europe): ~649 mixed Monday; US futures rising but stronger euro weighing on European exporters
Bottom Line
  • The US-Japan yen intervention is historically significant. The 1998 precedent came during the Russian financial crisis when a collapsing yen threatened to destabilize the Asian financial system. The current intervention reflects a different but related dynamic: the interest rate differential between the US (3.75% upper band) and Japan (1.00%) has been pulling capital relentlessly into dollar assets and out of yen. Coordination signals that both governments see the current trajectory as disorderly. It does not change the rate differential - it changes the speed and market confidence with which that differential plays out. Japan's next move is the Bank of Japan policy meeting, where one vote for a hike already appeared in July. If the BoJ hikes while the Fed also hikes in September, the intervention becomes less necessary and the underlying math reasserts.
  • CXMT's +466% debut is the clearest market signal yet that China's semiconductor ambitions are being priced as credible and near-term rather than aspirational and distant. The AI supply chain debate in the US has focused on whether NVIDIA's dominance can hold against domestic competition; the CXMT debut says China's market believes the competition is arriving from a different direction simultaneously.
  • The yen intervention and the CXMT IPO are unrelated events that share a theme: the global economic architecture that American financial markets have priced for two decades - dollar dominance, US chip leadership, Japanese monetary patience - is being tested from multiple directions at once. None of these tests resolves in a week. They add a layer of structural uncertainty that Friday's payroll count cannot address.
