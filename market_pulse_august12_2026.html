Wednesday Briefing • Wednesday, August 12, 2026

July CPI Lands at 8:30 AM ET - Consensus 3.4% Headline After June's Historic Drop. Brent Approaches $90 as US Forces Disabled a Vessel in Hormuz Overnight. S&P 500 Fell a Further 0.32% Tuesday to 7,728. USDA's First Official Corn Yield Estimate Also at 8:30 AM ET. Bitcoin at $63,588 With $854 Million in Last Week's ETF Inflows. XRP at $1.02 - CLARITY Act Passage Odds Fall to 13%.

Wednesday is the day both markets and the Fed have been building toward all week: July CPI at 8:30 AM ET, with consensus expecting +3.4% headline and core +0.2% monthly, alongside the USDA's first survey-based corn yield estimate. Oil enters the release at its highest sustained level since the Hormuz crisis began, with US forces attacking a vessel overnight and Brent approaching $90.

The Quick Read

Today's 8:30 AM ET double release is a binary event for the September rate decision. July CPI (consensus: +3.4% headline, +0.2% core monthly) arrives against the most hostile oil backdrop of the rate cycle - WTI at $84.17, Brent approaching $90 - and simultaneously with the USDA's first survey-based corn yield estimate of the 2026 season. The two prints together will either preserve the rate-hold narrative or accelerate the repricing that began Monday when Iran rejected the Hormuz framework.

The overnight news does not make the CPI setup easier. US Central Command announced it attacked and disabled a Panama-flagged cargo vessel that attempted to break the US naval blockade of Iranian ports. It was the first direct US military action against a commercial vessel since the Hormuz standoff began, and oil markets responded immediately: Brent surged 2% overnight to approach $90, and WTI rose to approximately $84.17. Between 8 and 15 vessels crossed the Strait on each of August 4, 5, and 6 - compared with approximately 130 transits per day before the conflict. The strait is functionally closed, and the overnight military escalation narrows the diplomatic space for any near-term resolution.

The equity market absorbed Tuesday's continuation of the oil surge but declined for a second consecutive session. The S&P 500 fell 0.32% to 7,728.20 - 29.44 points below Monday's approximate 7,749 close and 29.44 points further below Friday's record 7,757.64. The two-day decline from the record is modest in percentage terms but meaningful in direction: the index has now given back a full week's gains in two sessions. The Dow fell 184 points to 53,791.85. The Nasdaq fell 0.60% to 26,445.45, underperforming as technology stocks bear the highest sensitivity to rate repricing. Wednesday premarket is mildly positive - S&P 500 futures +0.11%, Nasdaq 100 futures +0.25% - but the direction of the session will be decided at 8:30 AM ET, not before.

The CLARITY Act update is the week's most significant change in the crypto narrative: Polymarket passage odds for 2026 have fallen to approximately 13%, sharply below the 30% level cited in prior editions. The September 15 floor vote is still on the Senate calendar, and cloture was filed August 8. But the three unresolved legislative disputes - illicit-finance provisions, stablecoin yield treatment, and government ethics clauses - have not been publicly bridged, and the convergence of a September 15 vote with the September 15-16 FOMC meeting creates a compressed legislative window in which the macro backdrop matters as much as the vote count. XRP on-chain metrics confirm the retail retreat: Daily Active Addresses have fallen to approximately 25,350 - the second-lowest reading of 2026 - and Network Growth at approximately 2,130 new addresses per day is the weakest since November 2024. XRP at $1.02 is holding above the critical $1.00 support level for now, but the on-chain data suggests the retail participation that drove XRP's earlier 2026 gains is substantially absent at current levels.

  • For housing, Wednesday's CPI release matters less in the next 30 days (when mortgage rates will be near 6.75-6.80% regardless of today's print) and more in the next 90 days (when the September-October rate path determines whether rates move toward 7.00% or toward 6.40%).
