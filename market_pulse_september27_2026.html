Market Pulse • Sunday, September 27, 2026 — Weekend Edition

October Hike Odds Stand at 76%. PCE Wednesday Is the Remaining Exit.

The Quick Read

Trump rejected Iran's six-day Hormuz reopening proposal Saturday, telling reporters it was "not acceptable" - Iranian FM Araghchi said Tehran awaits an official response through mediators and has not received one. The rejection reverses Friday's market easing: October 28 FOMC hike odds reset from approximately 67% back toward 73-76%, the 10-year Treasury priced back toward 5.22% in Sunday trading, and Brent held the $104-107 range through the weekend. The mechanism: without a deal, Brent's $100+ floor is structural - every PCE measurement window from August forward already captures $97-107 Brent as an inflation input, and that input does not change with diplomatic inactivity.

September 30 PCE at 8:30 AM ET is the week's only remaining probability pivot. July PCE ran 3.7% YoY headline and 3.3% YoY core; the August reading covers through August 31, capturing early-September $4+ gas and $97-100 Brent but not Friday's $105+ peak. At 73-76% October 28 hike odds, the majority case favors waiting for a post-hike 4.90-5.10% CD ceiling. PCE above 2.5% pushes October odds toward 80%, confirming the wait; only a print below 2.0% pulls odds below 50% and makes locking 4.95% today defensible before banks reprice on a pause signal. October 2 payrolls closes the picture Friday.

The S&P 500 closed the week at 7,741.83 - its first gain in three - on energy and defense composition. Bitcoin gained approximately 10% for the week while gold fell 2%+ before partial recovery; the institutional ETF floor ($2B September inflows) is the difference between the two. Gas is $4.49 nationally, heading toward $4.95-$5.10 mid-October regardless of the Iran outcome - the refinery lag makes the October pump window already set.
