Brandon Turner's Reserves Lesson

Real estate investors who build substantial cash reserves demonstrate discipline and foresight. They understand that survival matters more than any single deal. The real question is whether those reserves should sit idle - or work harder while they wait.

8 min read Share

Brandon Turner - widely known as "Beardy Brandon" - is a prominent real estate investor, author, and co-host of the BiggerPockets Podcast. He has been involved in over $1 billion in real estate transactions and owns interests in more than 14,000 rental units.

Who He Is

Turner has helped shape how a generation of investors thinks about risk management, reserves, and long-term portfolio building. With a large following across platforms and years of practical content focused on rental property investing, he remains one of the most influential voices teaching investors how to build and protect portfolios.

What He Said

Turner consistently stresses the importance of strong cash reserves. He advises keeping ample buffers for vacancies, repairs, evictions, capital expenditures, and market surprises. His core message:

Reserves keep you in the game when things go wrong. The best investors aren't the best predictors - they're the best survivors. "More reserves. Lower leverage."

"The best real estate investors I know are not better at predicting the future. They're better at surviving when they're wrong. More reserves. Lower leverage."

This is practical, battle-tested wisdom that has protected countless real estate investors.

Where We Agree

We agree with Brandon Turner without hesitation.

Reserves are not optional - they are essential. Real estate investing carries real operational risk. Strong cash buffers separate those who thrive over decades from those who get forced out during tough cycles. In today's environment, this discipline is more important than ever.

Where It Gets Complicated

Here is where the nuance appears.

Real estate investors routinely accept significant friction with the capital they set aside. Escrow accounts, impound accounts, earnest money deposits, and operating reserves typically come with:

  • Fees: Transaction escrow fees often run 1-2% of the purchase price (or $500-$2,000+), frequently split between buyer and seller. That's real money paid for protection and administration.
  • Yield: Usually 0% to very low single digits.
  • Insurance: Limited to FDIC coverage of $250,000 per depositor (with pass-through treatment for true escrow accounts). Amounts above that threshold sit uninsured in the event of bank failure.
  • Liquidity: Funds are often locked for 30-90+ days (or longer in disputes). Earnest money refunds after cancellation typically require mutual release and can take days to weeks.

Investors are conditioned to accept these trade-offs because "it's in escrow - it's safe." The protection feels complete, even though it is capped and the capital earns almost nothing while it waits.

Two Answers to the Same Question

Brandon Turner teaches one powerful answer: Build strong reserves and protect your downside.

Totem offers a practical complement: Deploy portions of that short-term reserve or dry powder capital into insured commercial transactions backed by Fortune 100 purchase orders. Earn a contractual 5% every 90 days with full insurance on principal and fee - coverage that applies to the entire amount, not just $250,000.

The timelines often align. Many escrow holds and deal contingencies last 30-90+ days anyway. Why let capital sit at 0-4% earning nothing (or paying fees) when it can complete one productive cycle - or multiple - and return principal plus fee when you need liquidity for the next deal?
