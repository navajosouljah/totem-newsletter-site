Weekly Briefing • Monday, June 15, 2026

FOMC Week Arrives. Iran Peace Text Finalized. Winter Wheat Hits a 60-Year Low.

Chair Warsh's first FOMC meeting begins Monday with inflation running at 4.2% CPI and 6.5% PPI. A finalized US-Iran peace text has sent oil crashing toward $80, offering the first genuine relief valve for energy-driven inflation. Meanwhile, winter wheat production is forecast at its lowest level since 1965 as drought covers 56% of the country.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • US-Iran peace deal text finalized - oil crashes toward $80: Pakistan's prime minister confirmed the US and Iran have agreed on a final peace text that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz. WTI crude fell from $91 early in the week to $84.88 by Friday and continued dropping to $80.23 by Sunday. If signed, it removes the single biggest driver of 2026 inflation.
  • SpaceX IPO shatters every record: SpaceX debuted on Nasdaq at $135, surged 19% to close at $161, and hit $178 by Sunday - a $1.77 trillion valuation. It is the largest IPO in history by a factor of 2.5 times over Saudi Aramco. The company also became the 8th largest corporate Bitcoin holder.
  • FOMC meets June 16-17 - Warsh's first as Chair: Markets price a 99.6% probability of holding at 3.50-3.75%. The dot plot and Summary of Economic Projections are the real event - they will signal whether the committee still sees rate cuts this year or if the inflation data has killed that path. The April meeting was the most divided since 1992 (8-4 vote).
  • New tariffs proposed on 60 economies over forced labor: The Trump administration announced Section 301 investigations into 60 countries with tariffs of up to 12.5%. Temporary 10% tariffs expire July 24. A trade court has ruled the tariffs illegal, but collections continue on appeal.

The Quick Read

The biggest week of the year for markets starts Monday. The FOMC meets with CPI at 4.2%, PPI at 6.5%, and a finalized Iran peace text sending oil toward $80. Stocks finished the week positive - the S&P 500 at 5,431 (+0.7%), the Dow at 51,202 (+0.7%), and the Russell 2000 surging 3.9% on a small-cap rotation trade. But the gains mask a volatile week that included a 4.2% Nasdaq crash on Tuesday before a sharp Friday recovery on Iran peace hopes. Housing distress continues building with Q1 foreclosure starts up 26% year-over-year and FHA delinquencies above 11%. The personal savings rate at 2.6% and 47% of BNPL users paying late confirm the consumer stress story is getting worse, not better. Winter wheat production is forecast at its lowest since 1965 as drought covers 56% of the Lower 48.

The Iran peace deal is the most consequential development since the trade war began. Energy drove 60% of May's monthly CPI increase. If a signed deal reopens the Strait of Hormuz and oil settles in the $75-80 range, the July and August CPI prints could improve meaningfully - giving the Fed cover to cut later this year. WTI has already fallen from $91 to $80 in less than a week, and Brent is following. But "final text" is not a signed treaty. Markets swung wildly this week on Iran headlines - strikes one day, peace talks the next - and positioning around a binary geopolitical outcome remains speculation until ink is on paper.

The FOMC meeting is the mechanism through which Iran peace (or its absence) flows into the real economy. If the dot plot still shows one or two cuts this year, the market will rally and rates will ease. If Warsh's committee acknowledges that inflation is reaccelerating and removes the cutting bias, mortgage rates stay above 6.5%, credit card APRs stay above 21%, and the consumer squeeze continues. The April meeting's 8-4 split - the most divided since 1992 - suggests this will be a contentious debate. The press conference matters more than the statement.

Underneath the headline noise, the food inflation pipeline is building quietly. Winter wheat production at its lowest since 1965 means higher flour and bread prices in the back half of 2026 regardless of what oil or the Fed does. Drought covering 56% of the country with an El Nino forming (87-92% probability through January 2027) threatens the corn crop next. Cattle at record prices with the US herd at its smallest since 1951 keeps beef expensive. Even if energy inflation fades on an Iran deal, food inflation is a structural force that takes longer to resolve.

Stock Market

A volatile week ended positive. Small caps led decisively on rate-cut and rotation hopes.

S&P 500: 5,431 +0.7% wk
Dow Jones: 51,202 +0.7% wk
Nasdaq: 25,889 +0.7% wk
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,944 +3.9% wk
Fear gauge (VIX): 17.68 easing from 19+ midweek
Bottom Line
  • The Russell 2000 outperforming the S&P by more than 5x on the week (+3.9% vs. +0.7%) is the clearest rotation signal in months. Small caps are the most rate-sensitive part of the market, and this move is a bet that Iran peace plus a dovish FOMC will bring rate relief. If that bet is wrong, this gets unwound fast.
  • The Nasdaq crashing 4.2% on Tuesday then recovering to finish the week positive tells you the market is trading on headlines, not fundamentals. SpaceX euphoria on Friday masked what was otherwise a treacherous week. This kind of whipsaw volatility favors the disciplined, not the reactive.
  • The VIX easing to 17.68 from above 19 midweek is a relief signal, but still elevated above the 14-15 range from early June. The FOMC meeting will determine whether volatility compresses further or reignites. Expect the VIX to spike again around Wednesday's rate decision.

Interest Rates

The yield curve is positively sloped and waiting for the FOMC. Mortgages edging higher despite the oil drop.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (effective 3.62%)
2-year Treasury: 4.09% −6 bps wk
10-year Treasury: 4.49% −4 bps wk
30-year Treasury: 4.97% −4 bps wk
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.52% +4 bps wk
Bottom Line
  • Treasury yields dipping across the curve while mortgages tick higher is a divergence worth watching. The bond market is beginning to price in the possibility that Iran peace actually removes the energy inflation premium. But mortgage lenders are not passing that through yet - they are waiting for the FOMC before repricing.
  • The 2-year at 4.09% versus the fed funds rate at 3.62% still implies the market sees at least the possibility of a hike. If the dot plot removes that risk, expect the 2-year to fall toward 3.90% quickly. That single data point on Wednesday will move more money than any earnings report this quarter.
  • The 30-year Treasury slipping below 5% to 4.97% is the first time it has broken below that level in weeks. If it holds below 5% after the FOMC, it signals the bond market believes the inflation peak is in. If it spikes back above 5%, the bond market is telling you otherwise regardless of what the Fed says.

  • The national average CD at 1.97% versus CPI at 4.2% means most savers are losing 2.2% of purchasing power per year in real terms. Even the best credit union rate at 4.65% barely covers headline inflation - and PPI at 6.5% suggests consumer prices have further to rise.
  • If the FOMC signals a cut, CD and savings rates will begin declining before the cut actually happens. Anyone wanting to lock in rates above 4% should do so before Wednesday's press conference, not after. The window for high-yield cash may be closing.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil in freefall on Iran peace. WTI has dropped $11 in a week - from $91 to $80. Gas prices following with a lag.

WTI crude: $80.23/bbl −12.4% from last week
Brent crude: $82.50/bbl −10.2% from last week
National gas avg: $4.17/gal −$0.11 wk
West Coast: $5.39-5.89/gal (CA highest at $5.89)
Cheapest region (South Central): $3.38/gal (Oklahoma)
Bottom Line
  • Oil dropping from $91 to $80 in one week is the most significant inflation development of 2026. Energy drove 60% of May's CPI increase. If crude settles in the $75-80 range on a signed Iran deal, the July CPI print could show the first meaningful deceleration since February. That would give the Fed room to cut and take pressure off the entire economy.
  • Gas at $4.17 nationally has not yet caught up to the oil crash - pumps lag crude by 2-3 weeks. Expect national average gas to fall toward $3.90-4.00 by late June if oil holds below $82. But the $2.51 spread between Oklahoma ($3.38) and California ($5.89) shows that state-level regulatory costs matter as much as crude prices on the West Coast.
  • The US actions in Venezuela, Iran, Cuba, and Greenland, plus oil increasingly settling in USD, all point to more supply and greater American leverage over global energy markets. If the Iran deal sticks, it confirms this trajectory and structurally lowers the floor for oil prices going forward.

Gold / oz: $4,200 steady
Silver / oz: $67.70 −0.1% wk
Copper / lb: $6.43 +2.7% day
Corn / bushel: $4.11 −14% over 4 wks
Soybeans / bushel: $11.14 −9.4% over 4 wks
Live cattle / cwt: $256 record
Bottom Line
  • Gold holding at $4,200 despite oil crashing tells you the geopolitical bid is intact. Gold is pricing in the possibility that Iran peace talks collapse and the conflict resumes. Until a deal is actually signed, gold has a floor. If a deal is signed and sticks, expect a pullback toward $4,000 as the risk premium unwinds.
  • Corn dropping 14% in four weeks and soybeans down 9.4% on weak Chinese demand is a rare bright spot for food prices - but it is offset by cattle at $256 per hundredweight (record) with the US herd at 86.2 million head, the smallest since 1951. Beef prices are not coming down until the herd rebuilds, which takes years.
  • Copper rising 2.7% alongside the stock rally is a constructive industrial demand signal. Copper tracks global economic activity better than any other commodity. Its strength here says the market is not pricing a recession - just an inflation adjustment. Watch whether copper holds above $6.40 through the FOMC.

Crop Weather

Winter wheat production at its lowest since 1965. Drought covers 56% of the Lower 48. El Nino forming.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent, 97% planted, 86% emerged. Solid start, but Illinois 42% abnormally dry and 20% in moderate drought.
Winter wheat: Production forecast 1,048M bushels - down 25% YoY and the lowest since 1965/66. Texas 54% poor/very poor, Oklahoma 48%, Nebraska 47%.
Drought: 56.16% of the Lower 48 in drought. 8.8% in extreme drought (D3), 0.9% exceptional (D4). Record-low snowpack in 8 western states.
El Nino watch: 87-92% probability of El Nino from July 2026 through January 2027 - a major variable for global crop yields and weather patterns.
Bottom Line
  • Winter wheat production at its lowest level since 1965 is a locked-in inflationary force. Higher flour and bread prices are coming in the back half of 2026 regardless of what oil or the Fed does. The harvested-to-planted ratio is at the second-lowest on record, meaning planted acreage was destroyed by drought at a historic rate.
  • The emerging drought in Illinois - 42% abnormally dry, 20% in moderate drought - is the new risk to watch. If it deepens through July and August, the corn crop's 67% good-to-excellent rating could deteriorate rapidly. The corn belt's fate will be determined by weather in the next 8 weeks.
  • El Nino forming at 87-92% probability through January 2027 adds a wildcard. El Nino typically brings wetter conditions to the US South and drier conditions to the Pacific Northwest and parts of the Midwest. Combined with existing drought, it could amplify agricultural stress into 2027.

Real Estate

Foreclosures accelerating sharply. Q1 starts up 26% YoY. REOs up 45%. FHA delinquencies above 11%.

Foreclosure starts Q1 (vs. last yr): +26% YoY
Foreclosure starts May (vs. last yr): 27,304 +13% YoY
Bank repossessions / REOs Q1: +45% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: ~11.5% highest since Q2 2021
Existing-home sales (annual pace): 4.17M +3.2% MoM
Median home price: $429,300 +1.3% YoY, record
% listings cutting price: 35.4% (April, seasonally adj.)
Inventory: 1.55M homes, 4.5 months of supply
30-year mortgage: 6.52%
Bottom Line
  • Q1 foreclosure starts up 26% and REOs up 45% year-over-year is the sharpest acceleration since the pandemic-era moratoriums expired. These are not pre-pandemic levels yet, but the trajectory is what matters. Every quarter the acceleration continues, the narrative that "housing is fine" gets harder to defend.
  • FHA delinquencies above 11% disproportionately affect first-time buyers who purchased with 3.5% down at pandemic prices. The FHA noted 92% of the increase is driven by a policy change requiring Trial Payment Plans before marking loans current - but policy changes that reveal hidden distress are still revealing distress.
  • The median price hitting a record $429,300 while 35% of sellers cut asking prices is the split-market story in its purest form. Low inventory props up the headline number while individual sellers struggle. If Iran peace drops oil and the FOMC signals a cut, mortgage rates could ease toward 6% - but that is two "ifs" stacked on each other.
  • Inventory at 4.5 months is the highest since 2020 and approaching a balanced market (5-6 months). But when REOs are up 45% year-over-year, a growing share of that "balance" is distressed supply. A balanced market driven by foreclosures is not the same as a healthy one.

Household Finance

The consumer is financing daily life on borrowed money. Savings rate at 2.6%. Nearly half of BNPL users are late.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion; avg APR 21.5%
Avg. new-car payment: $748/mo subprime borrowers: $792/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days late: 6.80% (near 32-year high)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (lowest since 2008)
Bottom Line
  • Credit card balances did decline $25 billion in Q1 - a seasonal pattern - but at $1.25 trillion with APRs above 21%, Americans are still paying roughly $270 billion a year in credit card interest alone. That is money not going to savings, investment, or discretionary spending. If the Fed hikes, APRs rise further. If they hold, APRs stay where they are.
  • The personal savings rate dropping from 3.6% in March to 2.6% in April is a cliff, not a trend. At this rate, any unexpected expense - a medical bill, a car repair, a job loss - goes directly onto credit cards at 21.5% interest. There is no buffer left for most households.
  • 47% of BNPL users paying late - with more than half saying they could not make ends meet without installment plans - is the kind of data point that deserves more attention than it gets. When people finance groceries on installment plans and nearly half cannot keep up, consumer stress is not a narrative. It is a math problem.

Crypto

Bitcoin holding above $63K after $1.67B in weekly ETF outflows. Solana leads the recovery. Institutional conviction absent.

Bitcoin (BTC): $63,878 −3-5% wk
Ethereum (ETH): $1,665 −5-6% wk
Solana (SOL): $71.00 +8-10% wk
XRP: $1.15 flat
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin ETFs saw $1.67 billion in outflows this week - the largest weekly drain since the ETFs launched. Institutional money is pulling back while retail dip-buys keep prices from cratering. This divergence is not sustainable. Either institutions come back (needs a dovish FOMC) or retail capitulates (needs another leg down below $60,000).
  • Solana surging 8-10% while Bitcoin and Ethereum fell is a risk-appetite signal from speculative traders, not a fundamental breakout. SOL had extra selling pressure from a 624K token unlock on June 7 that created a base to bounce from. The recovery is technical, not conviction-driven.
  • Crypto's fate this week is the FOMC. A dovish signal (cuts still on the table, no hike language) sends Bitcoin toward $68,000-70,000. A hawkish signal (inflation acknowledged, cuts removed from projections) tests the $60,000 support. Wednesday at 2:30 PM ET is the moment.

Around the World

Japan pulled back from record highs. China posted its first weekly gain in a month. Europe rallied hard on Iran peace.

Japan: Nikkei at 66,020 −0.85% wk. Pulled back from the all-time high of 68,786 set June 3. Still up 27% year-to-date, leading all global indices. Weak yen and AI narrative intact.
Europe: Stoxx 600 at 633 +1.8% Friday. Rallied sharply on falling oil and Iran peace. Banks led (Deutsche Bank +6.6%). ECB at 2.25% after hiking last week.
China: Shanghai Composite at 4,032 first weekly gain in a month. Hang Seng recovered 1.9% Friday after seven straight sessions of losses. US-China tariff framework (30% stays) failing to inspire real confidence.
Emerging: MSCI EM ETF at ~$67.50 −1 to 1.5% wk. Pressured by tariff proposals on 60 economies and a strong dollar. July 24 tariff expiration date is the next catalyst.
Bottom Line
  • Japan's Nikkei pulling back from 68,786 to 66,020 after hitting all-time highs is healthy profit-taking, not a trend change. At +27% year-to-date, Japan remains the best-performing major market in the world. Semiconductor exposure makes it vulnerable if the tech correction deepens, but the weak-yen, AI-buildout thesis is intact.
  • Europe rallying on Iran peace makes sense - Europe is more exposed to energy costs than the US. If oil stays below $85, European margins improve and the ECB's rate hike to 2.25% looks less restrictive. The rate differential between the ECB (2.25%) and Fed (3.50-3.75%) continues supporting the dollar.
  • China's first weekly gain in a month is a low bar. The Shanghai Composite bouncing at 4,032 after nearly breaking below 4,000 shows the government's floor is holding, but domestic demand is not recovering. Until stimulus scales up or trade tensions ease, China remains a drag on global growth expectations.
