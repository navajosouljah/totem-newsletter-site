Market Pulse • Wednesday, September 2, 2026

US Strikes Iranian Tankers Under New 'Tanker for Tanker' Policy - Brent Surges to $94.86. ADP August Payrolls 47K Pre-Signals a Soft Friday NFP. September Rate Hike Odds Climb to 66%.

Gold's collapse to $4,325 is the rate market's verdict - 66% hike odds now override the geopolitical safe-haven bid regardless of the tanker escalation. Friday's jobs report is the last variable before September 15.

The Quick Read

The August ISM Manufacturing PMI read 54.6% on Tuesday - below the 55.2% consensus and down one point from July's 55.6%, but in expansion for the eighth consecutive month. A miss that stays above 50 does not moderate the September hike case; it confirms the case has a foundation that isn't cracking under a slowing expansion pace. The Warsh framework calls for evidence that "underlying trends" are deteriorating before pausing. A 54.6% manufacturing PMI - paired with a Prices Index holding at 71.1% (same as July) and New Orders at 53.7% (down from 56.7%) - is not that evidence. September rate hike odds at approximately 66% on Wednesday morning reflect exactly that read. That is the whole mechanism - sustained expansion above 50, even below forecast, validates the September vote.

ADP's August private payrolls report released this morning at 8:15 ET showed 47K private sector jobs added - three ticks above July's 44K and essentially matching the 48K consensus. This is neither a confirming beat nor a red-flag miss. July's 44K ADP preceded the August 8 NFP that showed a -23,000 print - a 67K divergence in a single month. ADP's 47K sets the expectation midpoint but does not constrain Friday's binary. Three scenarios for the September 4 NFP and what each does to 66% hike odds: a beat above 150,000 with wages 3.5%+ pushes September toward 70-73%; 100,000-150,000 holds at 66%, extending rate-path uncertainty to September 11 CPI; below 100,000 drops September to 45-50% and is the first data argument against the September hike case.

Tuesday's new "tanker for tanker" deterrence doctrine authorized US strikes against Iranian oil tankers directly - approximately 100 targets struck, the first time the US military has struck Iranian vessels rather than launchers or bases. Trump's "we are going to hit them hard" signals this is a policy posture, not a one-time retaliation. Brent absorbing both the ISM miss and the new vessel-strike doctrine at $94.86 entering Wednesday tells you the Hormuz risk premium now has two structural floors: the Larak Island physical escalation and the new vessel-strike precedent. The 10-year Treasury closed Tuesday at 4.81%, crossing the 4.75% threshold that begins pushing Thursday's Freddie Mac PMMS above 6.70% within 2-3 survey cycles.

How Wednesday Opens

  • "Tanker for Tanker" targets Iran's revenue, not just its weapons - and that changes the deterrence shelf life for Brent. The Larak Island strikes hit rocket launchers - military infrastructure. Tuesday's vessel strikes directly hit assets Iran needs to generate oil export revenue. Each Iranian tanker struck reduces Tehran's immediate export capacity and signals that continued mine-laying or corridor interference costs actual income rather than hardware. That calculus changes the deterrence math in a specific way: Iran now faces a choice between restraint and accelerating its own economic deterioration, which Bessent has explicitly called an "acceleration phase" of Iranian bankruptcy. A framework that targets export revenue is harder for Tehran to absorb than one that targets weapons - the asymmetry favors sustained Brent pressure. The risk premium that was $1-2 per barrel for "could be mines" has become $4-6 per barrel for "US strikes tankers directly, not just launchers" - a different risk profile with longer duration. Bessent's secondary sanctions lever remains undeployed and adds a third potential layer above the vessel-strike baseline.
  • ADP's 47K narrows the Friday NFP forecast range without resolving it - and the historical ADP-NFP divergence means Friday is still a live binary. ADP and NFP have historically diverged by 40,000-80,000 in months with labor market inflection points. July 2026 is the cleanest local example: 44K ADP preceded a -23,000 NFP - a 67K divergence. August's 47K ADP is consistent with a continued slow-but-stable expansion. It does not commit to scenario 1 (NFP beat above 150K), scenario 2 (soft NFP between 100K-150K), or scenario 3 (NFP miss below 100K). Each scenario's rate-odds consequence is described in the Quick Read above. The sub-index to track within Friday's report: average hourly wages. July came in at +3.2% YoY against PCE at 3.7% - producing -0.5% real wages. A wage print of 3.5%+ on Friday returns real wages to approximately flat before the FOMC votes and removes one household finance pressure point from the September 15 calculus.
  • Gold at $4,325 through confirmed US vessel strikes against Iranian tankers is the clearest evidence that rate-path pricing has overtaken geopolitical safe-haven demand as the dominant gold force. Gold's 2.86% drop to $4,325 occurred on a day that included new US strikes against Iranian oil tankers, Brent surging to $94.86, and Trump vowing direct retaliation. The geopolitical event that should be the strongest single-session safe-haven trigger of the Hormuz crisis was insufficient to arrest gold's rate-driven decline. Two forces would return gold above $4,400: (1) a Friday jobs miss below 100K that drops September odds from 66% toward 45-50%; or (2) a ceasefire framework restoring the Oman corridor and collapsing the Hormuz risk premium by $3-5. Neither is likely before September 4. The inverse logic also runs: the Warsh pause signal that would produce the largest single-session gold recovery is a bigger catalyst than any geopolitical de-escalation at the current rate baseline. Gold's position at $4,325 is a rate-market signal, not a geopolitical signal.
  • The 10-year Treasury closed Tuesday at 4.81%, crossing 4.75% - Thursday's Freddie Mac PMMS is the first survey cycle above the mortgage-rate acceleration threshold. The September 1 article flagged that "a sustained move above 4.75% this week begins the clock on 6.75%+ mortgage rates within 2-3 weeks." That threshold was crossed on Tuesday. Freddie Mac surveys mortgage rates Wednesday through Thursday each week. Thursday's PMMS (September 3) compiles this week's 10-year range - approximately 4.72-4.81% - and is likely to read 6.68-6.74%, the first above-6.66% print since August 27. A 10-year averaging 4.81%+ through next week pushes the September 11 PMMS toward 6.72-6.78%. The mortgage rate clock started Tuesday. The consumer does not receive relief from this rate move before the FOMC votes September 15-16, and August housing starts data (mid-September release) captures the full Canada lumber tariff impact plus the first above-4.75% 10-year week - both simultaneously.
  • CLARITY Act: four working Senate days remain, and Thursday September 3 is the next legislative session - the best remaining window for ethics divestiture compromise language. The Senate working days before the September 15 cloture vote are September 3, 8, 9, and 10. Wednesday September 2 is not a Senate session day; Thursday September 3 is the next working day. The ethics divestiture provision - requiring officials with $1 million+ in digital assets to divest - remains the primary blocking issue with no reported compromise language as of Wednesday morning. Nine Democratic crossover votes remain unsecured. Polymarket at 30%. Senate bandwidth is compressed by the Iran-Jordan military exchange and now the tanker-strike escalation. A cloture failure on September 15 closes the 2026 legislative window entirely: both chambers are nearly fully out in October before the November 3 midterms, and the Senate's 2026 legislative calendar has no second window. XRP at $1.35 is pricing the four-day countdown with 30% odds.
