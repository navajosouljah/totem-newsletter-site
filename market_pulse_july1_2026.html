Weekly Briefing • Tuesday, July 1, 2026

Q2 Ends with the Best Quarter Since 2020. Alphabet Lifts the Dow Above 52,000. Oil Falls 23% for the Quarter. Jobs Report and FOMC Minutes This Week.

The second quarter is in the books - and it was the best for US indexes since the pandemic. Now the test: FOMC Minutes tomorrow and the June Jobs Report Thursday will set the tone for the second half. ISM Manufacturing and Fed Chair Warsh's remarks at the ECB forum arrive today.

The Quick Read

The quarter that just ended was remarkable for its contradictions. The S&P 500 gained 9.55% YTD and the Nasdaq surged 12.79% - the best quarterly performances since 2020 - even as the tech sector endured a brutal final week that saw the Nasdaq fall 5%. The recovery into quarter-end was sharp: Alphabet's first day in the Dow on Monday saw it jump nearly 5%, pushing the Dow above 52,000 for the first time. The rally was broad enough to paper over the cracks, but those cracks - Apple's price hikes, OpenAI's IPO delay, the global chip selloff - remain structural questions for Q3.

Oil falling 23% for the quarter and 20% for the month alone is the dominant macro story entering Q3. Brent at $72 is cheaper than before the Iran war. Gas near $3.90. This energy disinflation has not yet shown up in inflation data - the PCE at 4.1% still reflects April and May oil prices. The July and August readings should capture the decline. If they do, the hawkish Fed narrative softens considerably. Tomorrow's FOMC Minutes will reveal how many officials see it the same way.

Consumer sentiment rebounded to 49.5 from May's record low. The savings rate improved to 3.0%. But mortgage rates at 6.47% and FHA delinquency at 11.88% keep the housing market frozen. Thursday's Jobs Report is the next major test - strong employment is the one pillar holding the consumer story together.

The holiday-shortened week is data-heavy. ISM Manufacturing today tells us whether the factory sector is expanding or contracting. Warsh at the ECB forum may signal his intentions on rates. FOMC Minutes tomorrow. Jobs Report Thursday with an early close ahead of July 4. These four events in three days set the narrative for Q3.

Stock Market

Best Q2 since 2020. Alphabet's Dow debut pushed the index above 52,000 for the first time. The Dow/Nasdaq divergence from last week reversed sharply into quarter-end on rebalancing flows.

S&P 500: 7,449.36 +9.55% YTD; best Q2 since 2020
Dow Jones: 52,319.20 +8.85% YTD; closed above 52,000 first time; Alphabet debut +5%
Nasdaq: 26,213.72 +12.79% YTD; best Q2 since 2020
Russell 2000 (small caps): ~3,010
Fear gauge (VIX): ~17-18
Today: ISM Manufacturing PMI. ADP Employment. Warsh at ECB forum.
Bottom Line
  • Best quarter since 2020 but the final week exposed fragility in tech. The Dow/Nasdaq divergence from last week reversed sharply into quarter-end as rebalancing flows lifted beaten-down tech names. Alphabet's +5% Dow debut was the headline. The real test is whether Q3 sustains the recovery or whether the AI cost story reasserts itself.
  • The S&P at 7,449 and the Nasdaq at 26,213 came into quarter-end with momentum despite the tech rout. The breadth of the rally across value and industrials matters - it means the market is not entirely dependent on the same five tech names. But those names still dominate index weightings, and their Q3 earnings in late July will be the next major catalyst.
  • The VIX settling into the 17-18 range from the 18.41 print last week suggests the market has digested the tech selloff without panic. Today's ISM Manufacturing number and Warsh's ECB forum remarks are the first data points of Q3. If ISM shows expansion and Warsh signals patience on rates, equities could extend the quarter-end rally into the holiday weekend.

Interest Rates

The 10-year at 4.42% has been stuck at this level for weeks. Tomorrow's FOMC Minutes and Thursday's Jobs Report are the catalysts that break the range. Mortgage rates ticked down to 6.47%.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held)
2-year Treasury: ~4.28%
10-year Treasury: 4.42% bumping against this ceiling since late May
30-year Treasury: ~4.95%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.47% −2 bps WoW (Freddie Mac weekly avg)
Bottom Line
  • The 10-year at 4.42% has been range-bound near this level since late May. It rallied from 4.55% after the PCE data last week but cannot break decisively lower. Tomorrow's FOMC Minutes and Thursday's Jobs Report are the two catalysts that could resolve the range. Dovish minutes plus a soft jobs number pushes the 10-year toward 4.30%. Hawkish minutes plus a strong jobs number sends it back above 4.50%.
  • Mortgage rates at 6.47% - down 2 bps from last week - are moving in the right direction but remain well above the sub-6.25% threshold that housing needs to meaningfully unfreeze. If the 10-year breaks lower on dovish FOMC Minutes and oil disinflation flows into the July PCE, mortgages could drift toward 6.25-6.35% by August. The path is there. The data just needs to confirm it.
  • The 2-year at 4.28% continues to reflect market expectations that the Fed stays on hold at 3.50-3.75% for the foreseeable future. The curve is still inverted but less aggressively so than earlier in the year. A dovish signal from the FOMC Minutes - any acknowledgment that oil disinflation resolves the inflation spike without a hike - would be the most bullish development for rates since the Fed's last cut.

Bottom Line
  • Same rates as last week. The hawkish Fed keeps CD and savings rates elevated for longer. Anyone sitting on cash in a major bank savings account earning 0.01% is leaving real money on the table. A 4.15% CD on $50,000 earns $2,075 in a year. The national average at 1.97% earns $985. That is $1,090 in foregone interest for the same zero-risk product.
  • With Q2 ending on a strong note for equities but the VIX still elevated, CDs and high-yield savings accounts remain an excellent risk-adjusted complement to any portfolio. These promotional rates could get pulled as banks hit deposit targets. Lock in while the hawkish environment supports them.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil down 23% for the quarter - the single most important macro data point entering Q3. Brent at $72 is cheaper than before the Iran war. This disinflation has not hit PCE data yet but will in July and August readings.

WTI crude: ~$70/bbl down 23% for Q2
Brent crude: $72.25 down 20% for June, 23% for Q2
National gas avg: ~$3.90/gal −$0.65 from $4.55 May peak
Hormuz shipping: Normalizing. Strait traffic recovering as peace framework holds.
Bottom Line
  • Oil down 23% for the quarter is the single most important macro data point entering Q3. This disinflation has not hit the PCE yet. When it does - in the July and August readings - the inflation story changes. The PCE at 4.1% still reflects April and May oil prices when WTI was above $85. At $70, the headline inflation number should drop meaningfully. This is the pipeline that could soften the hawkish Fed narrative without requiring a rate hike.
  • Brent at $72.25 is now cheaper than before the US-Iran conflict began. The entire war premium has been unwound. Hormuz shipping is normalizing as the peace framework holds, and OPEC+ production increases continue to add supply. Gas near $3.90 - down $0.65 from the May peak of $4.55 - is saving families $10-15 a week at the pump. Real money flowing back into household budgets.
  • If WTI holds near $70 through July, gas could drift toward $3.75-3.80 by mid-month. The energy disinflation pipeline is loaded and flowing. The question is whether the Fed acknowledges it in tomorrow's FOMC Minutes or waits for the official data to confirm what markets already see.

Commodities

Gold stabilizing near $4,000 after dipping below that level last week. The debasement trade unwind may be pausing. Cattle at records. Silver holding above $58.

Gold / oz: $4,008 stabilizing after dipping below $4,000 last week
Silver / oz: $58.46
Copper / lb: ~$6.30
Corn / bushel: ~$4.13 steady
Soybeans / bushel: ~$11.26
Live cattle / cwt: $255+ record levels
Bottom Line
  • Gold finding support near $4,000 after the $350+ weekly decline suggests the debasement trade unwind may be pausing. The move from $4,081 to briefly below $4,000 and back to $4,008 tells you there is structural central bank demand providing a floor. But the four-week losing streak heading into Q3 is the longest of the year. The next direction depends on whether the FOMC Minutes and Jobs Report shift rate expectations.
  • Silver at $58.46 is holding better than gold on a relative basis, supported by industrial demand alongside the monetary premium. Copper steady near $6.30 reflects stable global manufacturing expectations heading into Q3.
  • Cattle at $255+/cwt remain at record levels on the smallest US herd since 1951 (86.2 million head). This is a multi-year biological cycle - herds take 3-4 years to rebuild, keeping beef prices elevated through 2028-2029. For grocery shoppers, poultry and pork remain more affordable protein alternatives.

Crop Weather

Same structural data. Corn at 68% good-to-excellent. Winter wheat in serious trouble. July pollination is the critical risk window for corn.

Corn: 68% good-to-excellent. 97% emerged, 5% silking. July pollination approaching - the make-or-break period for yield.
Soybeans: 66% good-to-excellent. 93% emerged, 9% blooming. Planting progress ahead of 5-year average.
Winter wheat: 14% good, 0% excellent; 57% poor to very poor. Production forecast 1,030M bushels - down 27% YoY and lowest since 1965. 63% in drought areas. Harvest 11% complete.
Drought: 56% of the Lower 48 in some level of drought. Near-term forecast favors above-normal precipitation in the Corn Belt.
Bottom Line
  • Winter wheat at 57% poor-to-very-poor with 63% of production in drought areas is a genuine supply problem. Production down 27% YoY to the lowest since 1965 will translate to higher flour and bread prices in Q3-Q4. This is already priced into futures, but consumers will feel it at the bakery aisle.
  • Corn at 68% good-to-excellent is adequate but July is the critical month. Pollination determines yield, and the El Nino probability remains elevated through January 2027, which historically dries out the Midwest during this window. Above-normal precipitation forecast is constructive for the Corn Belt, but any shift in the pattern during July would change the outlook quickly.
  • The structural drought (6th year in the West, intensifying in the Plains) constrains long-term agricultural capacity. Not causing acute food-price shocks today, but worth monitoring weekly as we enter the hottest part of summer.

Bottom Line
  • Mortgage rates at 6.47% ticked down slightly but remain well above the sub-6.25% threshold housing needs. If the 10-year breaks lower on dovish FOMC Minutes and the July PCE captures energy disinflation, mortgages could drift toward 6.25-6.35% by August. Homebuyers may need to be patient for 6-8 weeks while the data catches up to the energy reality.
  • FHA delinquency at 11.88% - up 126 bps year-over-year - remains the canary in the housing coal mine. FHA borrowers are first-time buyers with lower down payments and thinner financial cushions. They are the most exposed to any income disruption. Thursday's Jobs Report is critical here - strong employment is the one pillar keeping FHA stress from becoming FHA crisis.
  • Completed foreclosures surging 45% YoY to 14,020 in Q1 tells you the pipeline is converting into actual property seizures. Still well below crisis-era levels (300,000+ per quarter in 2009-2010), but the trend is clearly higher. Housing starts collapsing 15.4% shows builders are pulling back - constraining future supply and supporting prices even as demand weakens. The market is adjusting under stress, not collapsing.

Avg. credit card APR: 21.0% (all accounts); 23.79% on new offers

Subprime auto 60+ day delinquent: 6.90% 32-year high

UMich consumer sentiment (final): 49.5 up from 44.8 May record low; still 19% below a year ago
Inflation expectations: 1-year 4.6% down from 4.8%; long-run 3.3% down from 3.9%
Bottom Line
  • Consumer sentiment rebounding to 49.5 from May's all-time low of 44.8 confirms that falling gas prices are beginning to repair consumer confidence. Still historically weak - 19% below a year ago - but the direction matters. Inflation expectations easing to 4.6% from 4.8% on the one-year horizon and 3.3% from 3.9% on the long-run horizon is particularly constructive. Consumers are beginning to see through the temporary inflation spike rather than extrapolating it forward.
  • The savings rate at 3.0% - up from 2.6% in April - combined with personal income beating expectations at +0.7% suggests consumers are beginning to rebuild small financial cushions. Still less than half the 8.4% long-run average, but the improvement is real. Falling gas prices are contributing directly. Every dollar not spent at the pump is a dollar that can be saved or used to pay down debt.
  • But the stress indicators remain elevated. Credit card delinquencies at 13.1% (16-year high), subprime auto at 6.90% (32-year high), and 20.3% of new-car buyers paying $1,000+/month tell you the bottom third of the income distribution remains maxed out. At 21% APR, anyone carrying the average $7,886 card balance is paying $1,656/year in interest alone. Thursday's Jobs Report is the next critical data point - strong employment is the one thing keeping the consumer story from deteriorating further.

Bottom Line
  • BTC bouncing slightly to $59,587 (+1.98%) from the $59K support zone offers a glimmer of relief after weeks of selling pressure. The Fear and Greed Index at 16 - deep in "Extreme Fear" territory - has historically preceded relief rallies. Readings below 20 have been contrarian buy signals more often than not. But "historically" is not a guarantee, and the macro backdrop remains challenging until the FOMC Minutes and Jobs Report land.
  • The debasement trade unwind continues to weigh across the complex. Gold stabilizing near $4,000, silver at $58, and Bitcoin struggling near $59,500 all reflect the same dynamic - a hawkish Fed, strong dollar, and easing geopolitical risk removing the urgency from inflation-hedge positioning. Until rate expectations shift, crypto remains range-bound at best.
  • Key support levels: BTC $57,000-58,000, ETH $1,500. A dovish read on tomorrow's FOMC Minutes could trigger a sharp relief rally from these extreme fear levels. A hawkish read sends BTC testing that $57K floor. The asymmetry of the setup favors patience - the catalysts arrive in 24-48 hours.
