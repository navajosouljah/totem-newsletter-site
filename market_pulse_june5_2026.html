Weekly Briefing • June 5, 2026

S&P 500 Hits 7,600. SpaceX IPO. Consumer Stress Deepens.

Record highs mask a violent rotation underneath. Stocks cautiously yellow, bonds constructive, housing distress broadening, households under record stress, and crypto selling off hard.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • SpaceX IPO priced at $135/share: The company plans to sell 555.6 million shares in a roughly $75 billion offering, with stock set to debut on Nasdaq around June 12. Elon Musk retains 82%+ voting control, making this the largest IPO in history by a wide margin.
  • Iran-US tensions rattle markets: The House voted 215–208 to limit military operations against Iran, underscoring political divisions over the Strait of Hormuz crisis. The strait has been effectively closed since February 28, driving gas prices up roughly 54% from pre-closure levels.
  • S&P 500 crosses 7,600 for the first time: The index hit an all-time high on June 2 before a major rotation out of tech and into healthcare and financials mid-week, with the Dow surging approximately 900 points on June 3.
  • Andrew Left found guilty of stock manipulation: The famed short seller was convicted of using misleading social media posts to manipulate stock prices. He faces potential decades in prison, a landmark case for social-media-driven market commentary.

The Quick Read

The S&P 500 hit a record above 7,600 before a violent rotation into value names exposed fragility underneath the surface. Stocks are cautiously yellow with stretched valuations, bonds look constructive with rate cuts on the horizon, housing distress is deepening with FHA delinquencies at 11.52%, households are under record stress at $18.8 trillion in debt and a 2.6% savings rate, energy prices remain elevated on the Hormuz closure, and crypto is selling off hard across the board.

Monday’s record gave way to a brutal rotation on Tuesday and Wednesday: the Dow surged 900 points as money poured out of mega-cap tech and into healthcare and financials. The Nasdaq dropped nearly 1% on the week while the Russell 2000 rallied 1.6%. This kind of divergence - blue chips rising, growth lagging - is a classic signal that investors are repositioning defensively. The SpaceX IPO at $135/share is the week’s biggest event on the calendar, and it will test whether risk appetite extends beyond AI-adjacent trades.

The bond market remains the relative bright spot. The yield curve is normalized at +38bps (10Y minus 2Y), the Fed is holding at 3.50–3.75%, and mortgage rates ticked down slightly to 6.48%. But the 30-year Treasury at 4.99% is still one basis point from the psychologically important 5% level. Next week’s FOMC decision on June 11 and the CPI report on June 12 will set the tone for the second half of the year.

Under the surface, consumer distress is intensifying. Subprime auto delinquencies hit a 32-year record at 6.9%, FHA mortgage delinquencies climbed to 11.52% - the highest since Q2 2021 - and the personal savings rate sits at 2.6%, the lowest since June 2022. Meanwhile, gold pushed above $4,500, Bitcoin dropped below $62K, and expanding drought in the central Plains is threatening the summer crop outlook. The Hormuz crisis remains the single biggest swing factor: gas at $4.24 nationally and $5.50–$6.00 on the West Coast is already painful, and any further escalation puts $5 national gas in play.

Stock Market

Record high on Monday, then a sharp rotation out of tech into healthcare and financials. Breadth improving but leadership is narrowing.

S&P 500: 7,584.31 +0.06%
Dow Jones: 51,561.93 +0.50%
Nasdaq: 26,830.96 −0.97%
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,939.41 +1.59%
Fear gauge (VIX): 15.40 −4.1%
Bottom Line
  • The S&P 500 barely moved on the week (+0.06%) while the Nasdaq dropped nearly 1%. That divergence tells you the index-level calm is masking a real rotation underneath. Money is leaving tech and moving into cyclicals and defensives.
  • The Russell 2000 rallying 1.59% while the Nasdaq falls is a broadening signal, but it could also be late-cycle value rotation. The Dow’s 900-point surge on Tuesday was driven by healthcare and financials, not organic growth.
  • VIX falling to 15.40 looks comforting, but complacency at all-time highs with geopolitical risk elevated is exactly when markets tend to surprise. Valuations remain stretched on the S&P at ~22x forward earnings.

Interest Rates

Fed holding steady, curve normalized, mortgage rates ticking down slightly. The constructive setup continues.

Fed policy rate: 3.50–3.75% (effective 3.62%)
2-year Treasury: 4.08%
10-year Treasury: 4.46%
30-year Treasury: 4.99%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.48% −12bps
Bottom Line
  • The curve at +38bps (10Y minus 2Y) remains constructive and supportive of lending. Next week’s FOMC decision on June 11 is widely expected to be a hold, but the dot plot and forward guidance will signal whether cuts are coming in September.
  • Mortgage rates dipping to 6.48% from 6.60% last week is a modest positive, but rates need to break below 6% before the housing market meaningfully thaws. Every 50bps lower unlocks a new wave of refinancing activity.
  • The 30-year at 4.99% is still flirting with 5%. A sustained break above that level would signal term premium repricing and could weigh on rate-sensitive sectors from utilities to REITs.

Savings & CD Rates

Online banks and credit unions still offering significantly more than the national average. The window is narrowing.

National avg 1-yr CD: 1.98% APY
Best online savings: up to 4.40% APY (Pibank)
Best credit unions: up to 5.00% APY (DCU, first $1K)
Bottom Line
  • The spread between the national average CD (1.98%) and the best available online savings (4.40%) is 242bps. Savers at big banks are leaving significant real returns on the table by not shopping around.
  • Credit unions like DCU offering 5.00% on the first $1,000 is a good starter move, but the real opportunity is moving larger balances to high-yield online accounts at 4.40%. If the Fed cuts later this year, these rates will compress further.

Energy & Gas Prices

Hormuz closure since February continues driving elevated prices. Gas up 54% from pre-crisis levels.

WTI oil / barrel: $95.17 elevated
Brent oil / barrel: $96.70 elevated
National gas avg / gallon: $4.24
West Coast gas: ~$5.50–$6.00
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.81
Bottom Line
  • The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since February 28 - over three months now - and gas prices are up 54% from pre-closure levels. The House vote to limit military operations against Iran (215–208) signals political gridlock on resolving this, meaning prices stay elevated.
  • $4.24 national gas is below the psychological $4.50 pain threshold, but the West Coast at $5.50–$6.00 is already in crisis territory. If Brent pushes through $100, the national average follows to $4.50+ within weeks.
  • The structural US advantage is real: domestic production is near records and SPR releases can provide a short-term buffer. But that does not fix the global supply shock. The Gulf Coast at $3.81 vs. West Coast at $5.50+ shows a $1.70 spread that reflects refinery bottlenecks and regulations, not just crude prices.

Commodities

Gold pushing new highs above $4,500. Copper firm on demand signals. Cattle elevated on drought stress.

Gold / oz: $4,503 +1.4%
Silver / oz: ~$74–75 +1.5%
Copper / lb: $6.42–$6.53 firm
Corn / bushel: $4.24½
Soybeans / bushel: $11.29½
Cattle / lb (live): $2.42 +1.7%
Bottom Line
  • Gold above $4,500 reflects persistent geopolitical hedging and central bank buying. The Iran crisis, stretched equity valuations, and rate-cut expectations are all tailwinds. This is not speculative froth - it’s structural demand.
  • Copper holding firm at $6.42–$6.53/lb is a positive demand signal. Copper is the bellwether for global industrial activity, and steady prices here suggest the manufacturing sector is not rolling over despite recession fears.
  • Live cattle at $2.42/lb and climbing is a direct consequence of drought-driven herd liquidation in the Southern Plains. Beef prices at the grocery store will continue rising into 2027 as the breeding herd shrinks.

Crop Weather

Corn emergence strong but expanding drought in the central Plains is the summer wildcard.

Corn: Planting nearly complete with early emergence strong. Good start, but summer heat and moisture will determine yields.
Wheat: Winter wheat heading; spring wheat planting wrapping up. Condition ratings still below average in key states.
Drought: USDA Drought Monitor shows expanding moderate drought across the central Plains. Kansas, Nebraska, and Oklahoma most affected.
Bottom Line
  • Corn planting is nearly done and early emergence looks strong, which is the good news. But expanding drought in the central Plains heading into summer is the variable that could ignite a speculative rally in grains if conditions worsen through July.
  • Winter wheat heading into harvest with below-average condition ratings sets up potential supply tightness. Flour and bread prices could see upward pressure later this year if yields disappoint.
  • The drought expanding into Nebraska is particularly concerning for cattle country - it compounds the herd liquidation already underway and extends the timeline for herd rebuilding.

Real Estate

FHA delinquencies at the highest level since Q2 2021. Foreclosure starts rising. Price cuts increasing. Distress is broadening.

FHA delinquency rate: 11.52% (highest since Q2 2021)
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40bps YoY
Foreclosure starts: 35,000 in Feb (0.24% of loans) +6% YoY
Existing-home sales (annual pace): 4.02M
% listings cutting price: 17.5% (May 2026)
Median home price: $417,800
30-year mortgage rate: 6.48%
NAR membership: ~1.45M −6.5% from 2024 peak
Bottom Line
  • FHA delinquency at 11.52% is the headline number. These are the most vulnerable borrowers - first-time buyers, lower-income households - and one in nine is behind on payments. Pandemic-era forbearance programs expired months ago and the distress pipeline is growing.
  • 17.5% of listings cutting prices in May signals that sellers are beginning to capitulate. Combined with rising foreclosure starts (+6% YoY) and a frozen 4.02M sales pace, the pricing power that held through 2024–2025 is eroding.
  • At 6.48% mortgage rates, affordability remains the core problem. The median home at $417,800 requires roughly $2,800/month in principal and interest alone. Normal sales volume (5–5.5M annualized) requires rates closer to 5.5%.
  • The agent population is thinning fast. NAR membership dropped to roughly 1.45 million from a 2024 peak near 1.55 million, and could fall to 1.2 million by year-end if volume stays frozen. About 15% of agents turn over annually - but in a 4M-sale market, there are roughly 2.8 agents for every transaction, meaning the shakeout still has room to run.

Household Finance

Record debt, record-low savings, and record subprime auto delinquencies. The consumer stress story is not improving.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion (record)
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion; avg household balance $11,507
Avg. credit card APR: 21.52%
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days late: 6.9% (32-year record high)
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (lowest since June 2022)
Bottom Line
  • $18.8 trillion in total household debt at a 2.6% savings rate is the definition of a stretched consumer. The average household is carrying $11,507 in credit card debt at 21.52% APR - that is roughly $2,475 per year in interest alone on cards.
  • Subprime auto delinquencies at 6.9% (60+ days late) is a 32-year record. The average new-car payment has climbed to $770/month, which is unaffordable for a large share of borrowers who financed at the top of the rate cycle.
  • The savings rate at 2.6% is the lowest since June 2022 and near the all-time low. Every prior period at this level preceded either a recession or a significant market correction. Consumers are spending everything they earn and then some.
  • This is a K-shaped economy in sharp relief: prime borrowers with equity and fixed-rate mortgages are fine, while subprime and near-prime borrowers are in genuine distress across auto, credit card, and mortgage categories simultaneously.

Crypto

Broad risk-off selling across every major token. Bitcoin below $62K, altcoins hit harder.

Bitcoin (BTC): $61,924 −2.74%
Ethereum (ETH): $1,657 −6.76%
Solana: $65.66 −6.26%
XRP: $1.12 −5.14%
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin below $62K and falling is technically weak. The failure to hold $65K support opens the door to a test of $58–60K. Risk-off flows from the Iran crisis and sector rotation in equities are pulling capital out of speculative assets first.
  • Ethereum at $1,657 (down 6.76%) and Solana at $65.66 (down 6.26%) are getting hit materially harder than Bitcoin, which is typical in risk-off environments. Altcoins amplify the move in both directions.
  • The institutional vs. retail divergence continues: ETF flows remain relatively stable while on-chain retail activity is declining. Crypto is not leading risk appetite this cycle - it is a lagging indicator of equity market sentiment, and that sentiment is turning cautious.

Around the World

Japan at record highs, Europe flat, China slipping. The global picture is diverging.

Japan: Nikkei 225 at 66,856.50 +0.79%. AI and semiconductor stocks continue driving the rally. Yen weakness providing a tailwind for exporters.
Europe: Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,079.94 −0.38%. Flat as the market weighs the ECB rate decision next week and Iran-driven energy cost pressures.
China: Shanghai Composite at 4,028 −0.74%. Continued drift lower on property sector headwinds and weak domestic demand signals.
Bottom Line
  • Japan remains the standout global market with the Nikkei above 66,800, but the rally is heavily concentrated in AI and semiconductor names. Any reversal in yen weakness or global chip demand would hit Japan disproportionately.
  • Europe is caught between the ECB rate decision next week and the direct impact of Iran-driven energy inflation. European consumers and manufacturers absorb Hormuz-related oil price shocks more directly than the US, compressing margins.
  • China at 4,028 and drifting lower is going nowhere. Property, demographics, and US tech restrictions remain structural headwinds. EM ex-China (Taiwan, Korea, India) continues to be the more compelling opportunity set.
