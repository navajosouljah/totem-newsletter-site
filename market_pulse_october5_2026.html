Savers Enter a December Decision Window. October Hike Fell to 17%.

The Quick Read

September nonfarm payrolls came in at 29,000 - a two-year low and 55,000 below the 84,000 consensus - completing the first dual-softening signal of this rate cycle. August PCE core printed 3.0% on September 30, below the 3.4% forecast; Friday’s payrolls miss sealed the combination. October 28 FOMC hike probability collapsed from 73% Wednesday to 17% by Friday, the largest single-session swing in October hike odds this cycle. Stocks responded with a relief rally: S&P 500 +0.7% to 7,722.72 Friday, Nasdaq led at +1.2% to 27,190.86. The mechanism is the dual-softening pivot - when both the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge (PCE core 3.0%) and the labor gauge (29K jobs) miss simultaneously, the case for near-term tightening narrows sharply. December at 75% is now the market’s replacement hike date.

ISM Services for September arrives at 10:00 AM ET this morning - the first read on whether service-sector activity softened alongside the labor market in September. Prior was 55.4; consensus is 55.1. A print below 53.0 would extend the dual-softening thesis into a third data point and begin testing December’s 75% probability. Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET, the FOMC September 16 meeting minutes reveal the committee’s internal conditions for October - and whether any member named labor-market data as a precondition that Friday’s 29K print now satisfies. Also today at 3:00 PM ET: the USDA Crop Progress report for the week ending October 4 - the first harvest pace reading of October’s primary acceleration window.

Below the week’s data headlines, two structural reversals are building. Oil crossed back above $100 over the weekend as the US reportedly deployed a third aircraft carrier to the Middle East, restoring the Hormuz geopolitical risk premium that the supply-recovery narrative had been compressing since September 29. Brent at $101.31 Monday morning reverses the November pump-relief window that had been forming at $97 Brent - that window is now less certain. Mortgage rates confirmed a second consecutive above-7% week (PMMS 7.28% October 1) - the first such streak since 2007 - while the top 1-year CD has begun repricing from 4.95% toward approximately 4.40% as banks absorb the October rate-path shift.
