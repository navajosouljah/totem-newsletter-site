CD Rates at 4.50%. Japan Joined the Rate Hike Cycle.

The Quick Read

Friday's quad witching session closed mixed - S&P +0.17% to 7,650.50, Nasdaq +0.39% to 26,522.55, Dow -0.18% to 51,682.64 - as the week's catalysts settled into price. The 10-year Treasury drifted back to approximately 4.94% on Friday after Thursday's 4.84% close. The session's other headline: the Bank of Japan hiked to 1.25% - a 31-year high - narrowing the US-Japan rate differential to approximately 2.5-2.75%. Despite the hike, the yen weakened to approximately 157 per dollar: the carry-trade margin still overwhelmingly favors the dollar.

Three releases in the week of September 21-25 set the November FOMC path. Monday September 21 USDA crop progress (3:00 PM ET) delivers the first full harvest-week read. Tuesday September 23 Conference Board consumer confidence is the first post-hike household-sentiment data point. Friday September 25 PCE - the Fed's preferred inflation measure - is the most consequential release: a core reading at or above +0.3% MoM holds November hike odds at 60-65%; below +0.2% MoM starts softening them.

Two slow pressures are compressing simultaneously. The CD window opened at 4.45-4.50% APY this week and gas is at $4.4759 today - with the Saudi partial restart pointing the September 22-30 pump window toward $4.35-4.55. On the other side: credit card APRs moved to approximately 22.40% against a 15-year delinquency high. The global rate cycle accelerated this week - the Fed and BOJ both tightened in the same seven-day window.

Bank of Japan (September 18 decision): 1.25% ▲ 25-bps hike · 7-2 vote · 31-year high since 1995The BOJ's September 18 hike to 1.25% is the highest policy rate since 1995. The vote was 7-2, with board members Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato dissenting. The BOJ cited upside inflation risks driven by rising import costs - a direct reference to energy prices following the Saudi pipeline disruption. The pace is quickening: three months between hikes versus six months previously. The US-Japan rate differential at approximately 2.5-2.75% is still wide enough to sustain yen carry trades, but each BOJ hike erodes the margin. A BOJ rate above 1.5% - not priced for 2026 - would begin to materially compress the carry incentive.
Nikkei 225 (Friday September 18 close): 65,019 ▲ +1.38% · Best session of the post-hike weekThe Nikkei's 1.38% gain to 65,019 on the same day as the BOJ hike reflects the market's read that the US-Japan differential remains wide enough to keep export competitiveness intact. Toyota, Sony, and Mitsubishi Heavy - the primary yen-weakness beneficiaries - led the advance. At yen 157 per dollar, Japanese exporters receive approximately 3% more yen revenue per dollar of overseas sales than at last month's 151-152 level. The BOJ hike is good news for Japanese savers and banks; the yen's muted response is good news for exporters.

Monday September 21 - USDA crop progress at 3:00 PM ET: First full harvest week · corn from 8% to ~13–15% expectedMonday's report captures the first full week of material corn harvest activity. The 8% harvested share from September 13 will likely advance to approximately 13-15%. A third consecutive corn G/E reading at 57% confirms stabilization; a renewed decline reintroduces bearish pressure on December corn near $5.30. Soybean harvest (6%, double the five-year average) will also advance materially. Monday's data is the directional signal ahead of the October 9 WASDE.
Tuesday September 23 - Conference Board consumer confidence: First post-hike consumer read · August baseline was 103.3The Conference Board survey captures mid-September sentiment - the first full survey period with the September 16 hike, the CLARITY failure, and the BOJ hike in the data. The August baseline was 103.3. A reading above 100 suggests consumers absorbed the September catalysts without deterioration; below 95 would signal the compound effects of 22.40% card APRs, $4.47 gas, and 13.12% delinquency are weighing on forward expectations. This is also the first read since the BOJ's hike, which could affect business-sector confidence through the dollar-strengthening channel.
Friday September 25 - PCE inflation (BEA, August data): ▲ Fed's preferred measure · most consequential release of the weekPCE is the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation metric and the primary input to the November 4-5 hike decision path. CME FedWatch prices approximately 60-65% November hike odds. A core PCE at or above +0.3% MoM keeps those odds intact or raises them toward certainty; a reading below +0.2% MoM begins softening the November probability. August CPI came in at +0.4% MoM on September 10 - if PCE aligns with or exceeds that read, the November hike is essentially locked. This is the single most consequential release of the week for rates, equities, and crypto.
Bottom line

Three releases drive the week: Monday September 21 crop progress (3:00 PM ET), Tuesday September 23 Conference Board consumer confidence, and Friday September 25 PCE. PCE is the single most consequential - the number that shifts November hike odds before October 2 payrolls. A sustained core PCE at or above +0.3% MoM locks the November hike path.
