Friday Briefing • Friday, August 14, 2026

S&P 500 Hits All-Time Record Close 7,798.99 as Thursday Rally Surpasses August 8 High. CLARITY Act Formally Shelved Before Senate Recess - Prediction Markets Shift to 30%. Oil Retreats to WTI $81.27. XRP On-Chain Activity Surges 84% Despite Price Near $1.00. July Retail Sales + UMich Sentiment at 8:30 and 10 AM ET Today.

Thursday carried what Wednesday's CPI started: the S&P 500 hit a new all-time intraday high of 7,816.70 and closed at a record 7,798.99, surpassing the August 8 high of 7,757.64 for the first time. The Nasdaq and Dow also set records. Oil's continued retreat removed another headwind. The Senate formally shelved the CLARITY Act before its August recess, removing the September 15 floor vote and shifting prediction market odds to approximately 30% for 2026 passage. Friday brings July retail sales at 8:30 AM ET (consensus +0.3%) and University of Michigan August preliminary consumer sentiment at 10:00 AM ET (consensus 54.1).

The Quick Read

Thursday delivered the week's most important number: a new all-time S&P 500 record. The index closed at 7,798.99 - the first close above 7,800 in history - after touching an intraday high of 7,816.70. The 0.65% gain from Wednesday's 7,748.50 post-CPI close extended the relief rally into record territory and confirmed that the market's interpretation of the week's data - benign CPI, retreating oil, stable labor market - was durable enough to absorb and exceed the prior August 8 record of 7,757.64. The Nasdaq reached 26,803.03 (+0.81%) and the Dow closed at 53,839.99 (+0.13%). All three indices set records on a single session - a rare simultaneous multi-index record in the context of a week that had opened with oil near $90 Brent and September hike odds at 50%.

The Senate provided the week's other major development, and it was negative for crypto: the CLARITY Act was formally shelved before the August recess as the Senate prioritized a Russia sanctions bill. The September 15 floor vote - the date that had defined the act's legislative calendar since cloture was filed August 9 - is now off the calendar. Prediction markets have recalibrated: from approximately 13% when the September 15 vote was expected to fail, to approximately 30% now that the act has been pushed beyond the recess to a longer and more uncertain timeline. The higher odds reflect the removal of the near-term failure scenario, not new momentum. For XRP, ETH, and the broader crypto legislative complex, the act's shelving represents the loss of the only near-term catalyst that had been priced into institutional positioning.

XRP's on-chain activity presented a sharp divergence from price: Daily Active Addresses jumped from approximately 23,642 (August 1) to 43,543 (August 11) - an 84% increase in network usage over ten days even as price holds near $1.00. This divergence - rising on-chain activity, flat price, near-zero ETF inflows - describes a network where activity is building on infrastructure and development grounds rather than speculative or institutional demand. Whether that activity converts to price pressure depends on the CLARITY Act's eventual resolution. Friday's setup: July retail sales at 8:30 AM ET and the University of Michigan August preliminary consumer sentiment at 10:00 AM ET are the week's final data inputs. If retail sales at +0.3% MoM and sentiment near 54 confirm that consumers are absorbing the oil shock without pulling back spending, the rate-hold narrative gets another data point. A miss on either introduces the stagflationary concern the Fed most wants to avoid before September.
