Gas Falls to Lowest Since July. CPI Tomorrow Decides the December CD Call.

The Quick Read

Gas fell 15 cents overnight to $4.02 (AAA, November 9) – its lowest reading since July – as Trump said renewed Iran strikes are “highly unlikely” if talks progress, deflating the crude risk premium. Simultaneously, $1.04 billion in crypto liquidations drove Bitcoin to approximately $74,300 from Friday’s $75,750, with BTC ETFs bleeding $1.28 billion overnight. The mechanism connecting both moves is the dollar: approximately 81% December hike odds hold dollar strength, which pressed risk assets lower while the Iran peace-talks thesis cut crude supply-risk pricing. Both moves carry into tomorrow’s October CPI at 8:30 AM ET, the final inflation read before the December 9 FOMC decision.

Today’s USDA Crop Progress (releasing this afternoon) shows Iowa’s harvest pace through November 8 – the freeze verdict after the weekend. Iowa stood at 66% harvested through November 1, 13 points behind the five-year average; a reading above 75% today clears the supply-confirmation threshold. Tomorrow’s October CPI is the week’s decisive release: at ~81% hike odds, a core above 3.0% closes the December hold debate entirely. A reading near September’s 2.6% core holds the one-in-five hold probability through December 9. CD savers treating tomorrow’s 8:30 AM ET as a decision deadline have the right instinct.

The Nikkei recovered 2.5% to approximately 58,800 overnight – the dollar-strength trade working as designed, with yen weakening benefiting Japanese exporters. China’s October PPI surged to +5.7% year-over-year, a 4.5-year high, signaling upstream commodity-price pressure building toward US producers. The NY Fed Q3 consumer credit report – covering the first full post-September-hike quarter – is due this month; card delinquency 90+ was 12.9% in Q2.
