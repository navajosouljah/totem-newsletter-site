Tech Futures Rebound After the AI Selloff. Savers Can Still Lock 4.75%.

The Quick Read

Thursday split the market in two. A Financial Times report, confirmed by CNBC, put OpenAI's annualized revenue near $50 billion, about $20 billion below the figure widely reported last month, and chip stocks fell 3.4%. The Nasdaq lost 1.25% while the Dow finished up 0.10%, because energy led as Brent rose 4% on Gulf attacks and Hurricane Isaias. The mechanism: one revised number repriced the most crowded trade, while oil and a softer 10-year moved in opposite directions underneath it.

Friday opens calmer. Nasdaq futures were up about 0.8% before the bell and Brent eased toward $103 after President Trump said the US would not strike Iran before the November 3 midterms. The University of Michigan's preliminary October sentiment arrives at 10:00 AM ET (consensus 47.6 against September's 48.1), and USDA's WASDE crop report follows at noon ET.

Underneath, the rate picture is steady rather than easing. The 10-year closed at 5.22% after touching a 24-year high on Wednesday, the 30-year mortgage averaged 7.40% for a seventh straight weekly rise, and futures price roughly a 78% chance of another hike by December. For savers, the best 1-year CD now pays 4.75%. Curated from Reuters · CNBC · Financial Times · US Treasury · Freddie Mac · CME FedWatch · Bankrate · Dow Jones

Friday's 1.3% dip is that premium easing on the midterm pledge, while shipping risk in the Strait of Hormuz remains.

Operators shut in about 1.28 million barrels a day, roughly 63% of Gulf of Mexico oil production, ahead of Isaias, which is forecast to make landfall late Friday or early Saturday near the Mississippi-Alabama-Florida coast.
National gas average (AAA, October 8): $4.36/gallon ▼ down 5 cents in a week · record high for autumnPump prices are falling week over week but remain the highest ever for this time of year. Diesel moves with crude, which matters for freight and farm costs.
Bottom line

Thursday's spike reads as temporary: the Iran pledge and restarting Gulf platforms both point to supply returning. The structural picture still favors more US supply and lower prices once storm and shipping disruptions clear.
