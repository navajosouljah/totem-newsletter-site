Sunday Briefing • Sunday, August 2, 2026

Consumer Confidence Hits a Five-Month High Even as the Fed's Dissenters Sharpen Their Case - The Week That Decides the September Rate Path Begins Monday. Bitcoin Recovers to $63,454. July Jobs Report Friday Is the Number Everyone Is Watching.

Households are more confident than they were in February, the Fed's three hawks are unmoved, and five labor market releases over the next five days will determine which side of that tension writes the September 15-16 story.

The Quick Read

The setup entering the week: markets closed Friday at the same levels they closed Thursday - the S&P 500 at 7,490 - and the weekend has added one surprise and one confirmation. The surprise is that consumer sentiment for July came in at 55.2 on Friday's final reading, a nearly 12% jump from June and a five-month high, with broad-based gains across income groups and political affiliations. The confirmation is that Bitcoin has recovered to $63,454 Sunday morning while Ethereum sits at $1,867, both above their Friday closes, as crypto is the only major asset class still trading. Gold has eased slightly to $4,045 from Friday's close.

The market framework for this week is entirely about labor. Three FOMC members voted to hike on July 29; what it would take to move a fourth is exactly what the next five days will try to answer. JOLTS job openings land Tuesday, ADP private payrolls Wednesday, initial claims Thursday, and then the full Bureau of Labor Statistics July jobs report on Friday morning - consensus expects +91,000 new jobs and an unemployment rate that edges up to 4.3%. One strong print does not flip three committed dissents into a majority. Three or four weak prints might.

The consumer sentiment number is worth understanding before the labor data starts arriving. At 55.2, the index is a five-month high and a meaningful bounce from June, but it is still 11% below where it was a year ago and the current-conditions component (54.8) and expectations component (55.4) are nearly identical - households have no more clarity on where things are going than they do on where things stand now. One-year inflation expectations held at 4.2%, which means the sentiment improvement is about fewer immediate fears, not a belief that the inflation problem is solved. The Fed's dissenters will read that as continued cover for their position.

This Weekend's Updates

  • University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment hit 55.2 for the final July reading (released Friday, August 1), a five-month high and a near-12% jump from June. Current Conditions rose to 54.8 and Expectations climbed to 55.4 - the narrowest gap between the two components in months. Gains were broad-based across income, age, education, wealth, and political party. One-year inflation expectations held at 4.2% and 5-10 year held at 3.3%, meaning the improved mood is about easing near-term anxiety, not a belief that inflation is defeated. (University of Michigan)
  • Bitcoin recovered to $63,454 early Sunday morning after closing Friday around $62,689. The weekend move is modest but directionally positive, reversing part of the weekly decline that saw BTC fall roughly 1.9% when equity markets rallied on earnings. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs saw only $172.8 million in inflows over July - a divergence worth watching as the price recovered but institutional interest stayed muted. (CoinDesk, Bloomberg)
  • No new geopolitical flash points broke over Saturday and Sunday. The Middle East situation that kept Brent above $90 mid-July remains active but contained at current levels, with WTI holding near $84-85. The relief from the $93 mid-July crude peak is still the dominant energy story heading into the week.
  • China's July manufacturing PMI final reading confirmed the contraction signaled by the flash: 49.2, below the 50.0 neutral line and the 50.3 reading in June. Analysts cited domestic demand weakness and typhoon disruptions to factory activity. The number is relevant to the U.S. because it reinforces that global demand is slowing even as U.S. inflation remains sticky - a combination that historically argues for Fed patience, not additional hikes. The dissenters disagree.
  • The June jobs context, a reminder heading into Friday: the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on July 2 that nonfarm payrolls rose just 57,000 in June - the slowest month since early 2021 and well below the 115,000 consensus. The unemployment rate fell to 4.2% only because labor force participation dropped 0.3 points to 61.5%, a five-year low. Professional and business services led gains (+36,000); manufacturing shed jobs for a third straight month. Average hourly earnings rose 0.3% to $37.64. Friday's July report (consensus: +91,000, unemployment 4.3%) will show whether June was an anomaly or a trend. (BLS, July 2)

Stock Market

Markets are closed Sunday. The Friday close stands as the last trade; the week ahead opens Monday with the first of five labor market reads.

S&P 500: 7,489.72 +1.0% for the week; -0.9% for July overall
Dow Jones Industrial Average: 52,485.03 +1.0% for the week; cloud earnings and not much else
Nasdaq Composite: 25,373.85 +1.6% for the week; Microsoft and Amazon did the work
Russell 2000: 2,931.34 flat on the week; small caps excluded from the cloud rally on rate sensitivity
VIX: 15.99 fell from 18.67; the fear premium unwound with earnings
Bottom Line
  • The S&P's weekly gain was the product of two earnings reports, not a broad economic re-rating. At 7,490 with a hawkish three-dissent Fed, the index is priced for a soft landing with no room for the labor market to disappoint. Friday's jobs number is the most consequential single data point the market has seen since the July 29 FOMC decision. A strong print (above 150K) would validate the dissenters and likely send the 10-year back toward 4.85%. A weak print (below 75K) would give the doves oxygen - but also raise recession questions the bulls have been successfully dismissing.
  • The consumer sentiment bounce to 55.2 is a mildly constructive input for equities if it holds. Sentiment was running well below the level that typically accompanies a recession and the broad-based improvement across demographics suggests the July gas-price anxiety may have peaked. The pass-through from $84 WTI to the pump will determine whether August sentiment holds that gain.

Interest Rates

The 10-year enters this week at 4.75%. Every labor print this week is an argument in the hike-vs-hold debate that three Fed officials already answered on July 29.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% held July 29; 9-3 vote; three dissents in favor of +25bp hike; next meeting September 15-16
2-year Treasury: 4.29% July 31 close; front end priced for tightening risk, not cuts; up from 4.23% on July 30 after FOMC digestion
10-year Treasury: 4.75% +10 bps last week; rose through a GDP miss and the first monthly PCE decline since 2020
30-year Treasury: 5.28% +15 bps last week; long-end fiscal premium intact
30-year mortgage (Freddie Mac, July 30): 6.66% up from 6.58% prior week; next reading August 7 - the day of the jobs report
Bottom Line
  • The 10-year at 4.75% and the 30-year mortgage at 6.66% are the two numbers with the most direct household impact this week. The Freddie Mac weekly survey publishes the same day as the July jobs report (Thursday/Friday, August 7), which means the two biggest rate signals of the week land simultaneously. A stronger-than-expected jobs number could push the next mortgage reading toward 6.80%.
  • For CD savers: the case for locking a 1-year CD at 4.40% has improved, not weakened, since the hawkish FOMC. If the dissenters are right and September brings a hike, the 4.40% you lock this week is the floor, not the ceiling.

  • The September meeting is 44 days away. A 1-year CD locked this week at 4.40% captures one full year regardless of what happens in that meeting or the one after it. The ladder argument - spread maturities rather than lump-summing into a single term - has more merit now that the rate path is genuinely uncertain in both directions.

Natural gas (July 31): $2.747/MMBtu -$0.011 on the day; summer cooling demand is the primary driver; flat on the week

Bottom Line

Given the two-to-four week lag from crude to pump, and the fact that WTI peaked at $93 two weeks ago, this week's reading is likely to show further upward pressure before relief arrives from the current $84-85 level. EIA's own 3Q26 forecast calls for the national average to ease toward $3.80 by quarter-end - that relief is two to three months out, not this week.

Silver / oz: ~$57.79 -2.35% Friday as the dollar firmed post-FOMC; little additional weekend movement; industrial demand in focus with the week's macro data
Copper / lb: ~$6.47 the cleanest forward-looking industrial demand read; rose 2% last week on the cloud-earnings beat

The metal holding above $4,000 through a strong equity week and a hawkish Fed says the structural bid is geopolitical and central bank driven, not just a rates trade. That bid does not reverse on a single labor report.
  • Copper at $6.47 is the commodity to watch against Friday's jobs number. If payrolls disappoint materially, copper will price a growth-slowdown read faster than any equity index will.

Crop Weather

The drought map expanded through a third straight week. Corn yield estimates will be reset in the August 12 USDA report.

Corn: 63% good-to-excellent (USDA, week ending July 27) down 4 points in one week; grain fill at peak heat sensitivity; next USDA reading this Monday
Drought: 47.9% of the Lower 48 in moderate drought or worse; Northern Plains deteriorated for a third straight week
Key date: August 12 - USDA crop production report. The first official yield estimate that fully reflects July's heat stress.
Bottom Line
  • The USDA crop condition report drops this Monday morning and the crop production report with the first full yield estimate lands August 12 - the same day as July CPI. If corn ratings fall a fourth consecutive week, the futures market will price additional food-price risk into a CPI print that the Fed's dissenters are already watching as their primary justification. Two pressure points landing on the same morning.

Foreclosure starts (H1 2026, ATTOM): 164,566 +18% YoY; June alone +20% YoY
Median existing-home price (June): $440,600 a record, even as 20.2% of listings carry a price cut

A jobs beat that sent the 10-year toward 4.85% would cement a 6.80-plus mortgage heading into fall's buying season. The Friday number is a housing market event as much as a labor market one.

Household Finance

Consumer sentiment surprised to the upside Friday. The underlying balance sheet pressures are unchanged.

U of Michigan Sentiment (July final): 55.2 near-12% jump from June; five-month high; broad-based across income, age, wealth, and party
Current Conditions component: 54.8 households slightly more positive on the present
Expectations component: 55.4 roughly equal to current conditions; no clear optimism about the forward path
1-yr inflation expectations (July final): 4.2% held flat; improvement in mood is not driven by a belief inflation is solved
5-10 yr inflation expectations: 3.3% held; long-run inflation anchoring is not deteriorating, a key Fed input
Conference Board Consumer Confidence (July): 90.8 down 1.4 points from 92.2 in June; present situation held but expectations component weakened on job market concerns — the opposite direction of the Michigan read
CPI (June, BLS): +3.5% YoY down from 4.2% in May; -0.4% MoM, the largest monthly decline since April 2020; energy fell 5.7%; core (ex-food & energy) at 2.6% YoY, flat MoM
Retail sales (June, Commerce Dept.): +0.2% overall +0.7% excluding gasoline price effects; the underlying consumer is spending, just not energetically
Personal savings rate (June): 2.7% spending has outrun income; the sentiment bounce needs a jobs foundation to last
Credit card APR (Fed G.19, May): 21.15% on interest-bearing accounts; the cost of spending on thin savings
Bottom Line
  • The Michigan-Conference Board split is the most interesting consumer data point this week. Michigan (55.2, up nearly 12%) picks up near-term mood, gas prices, and present conditions — gas fell, mood improved. The Conference Board (90.8, down 1.4) weights forward-looking job market expectations more heavily, and those expectations deteriorated. Same consumer, same month, opposite directions. The divergence usually resolves toward wherever the next jobs report lands, which is Friday.
  • The June CPI read (3.5%, down from 4.2%) is the most constructive inflation data since January, but it was almost entirely energy-driven. Core at 2.6% is the cleaner signal, and it is still a full point above the Fed's 2% target. The sentiment bounce needs lower gas prices to persist and a labor market that does not roll over. Friday's jobs number is the sentiment report's most important near-term input.
  • The K-shaped balance sheet: the savings rate at 2.7%, credit card balances at $1.25 trillion growing at 5.9% annually at a 21% APR, and FHA delinquency at 11.88% are all worsening even as the headline sentiment index improves. Sentiment surveys measure mood; the balance sheet data measures facts. The households whose delinquency rates are rising are also among those reporting better sentiment — because June gas prices fell from May's highs. That is a real improvement in real life. It does not change the balance sheet math.

The institutional signal is more cautious - spot Bitcoin ETF inflows were a modest $172.8 million across all of July even as the price recovered, suggesting large institutional buyers are waiting for clarity on the rate path before adding. Friday's jobs report is as relevant to crypto as it is to bonds.

Around the World

China confirmed factory contraction. Europe and Japan enter the week with their own rate debates running.

China: Official manufacturing PMI 49.2 (July final) contraction; missed 50.0 expected; domestic demand slump and typhoon disruption cited; Shanghai Composite near 3,832
Japan: Nikkei near 64,362 BoJ held at 1.00% on July 31 with one vote for a hike; core inflation warning; Asia's central bank divergence from the Fed's direction is narrowing
Europe: STOXX 600 at 649 a fourth straight monthly gain; July inflation ticked up; ECB rate path uncertain
South Korea (KOSPI): still processing last week's 17.9% single-day recovery the index remains down roughly 1.4% for the week after July's 22% slide; AI memory demand validated by cloud earnings
Bottom Line
  • China's contraction is the most consequential international number of the weekend. A 49.2 manufacturing PMI out of the world's largest goods producer sends two signals simultaneously: global demand is weaker than the AI-earnings narrative implies, and commodity prices (particularly copper and industrial metals) have a ceiling. That ceiling is relevant to the inflation thesis that is keeping three FOMC officials in the hike camp.
  • The BoJ's one dissenting vote toward a hike mirrors the structure at the Fed. The global cheap-money era is not reopening from any direction - central banks from Tokyo to Frankfurt to Washington are all leaning the same way, just at different speeds.
