Dow’s First 50,000 Close. CD Leaders Rise to 4.80% Before Today’s PCE.

The Quick Read

The tech-value split crystallized overnight. The Nasdaq fell 1.48% to 23,733.54 while the Dow crossed 50,000 for the first time in its history - closing at 50,018.70 as Consumer Confidence beat sharply at 103.3 versus 92.2 expected, driving a value rotation that left AI hardware names behind. By Wednesday morning, Asian markets confirmed the split: the Nikkei fell 2.8% as Tokyo Electron shed 12.5% and Advantest lost 12.9%; Taiwan’s Taiex fell 4.8%; South Korea’s KOSPI reversed Monday’s full 6.32% gain. The AI hardware short position, partially cleared Monday, re-entered at Tuesday’s close and found fresh entry points in Asia. That is the whole mechanism.

Today at 8:30 AM ET brings PCE, the Q3 2026 GDP second estimate (advance: 3.4%), Initial Claims for the week ending November 22, and New Home Sales at 10:00 AM ET - then the FOMC media blackout begins. With December 25bp now at 97%, PCE’s role today has narrowed: a hot print confirms the base case; a cool print narrows what little uncertainty remains. After the 8:30 AM ET release, 14 days of FOMC silence begin before December 9. CD savers note: the best 1-year rate rose to 4.80% (Eagle Bank, Forbes, November 24) - PCE today confirms whether the 25bp delivery in 14 days is real.

Copper holds at $5.44 - its China-demand signal unchanged by this week’s equity volatility, still at a five-month-plus low. Gas reached $3.5866/gallon (AAA, Wednesday November 25), a new month’s low 52 cents below November 1. Card delinquency at 5.0% (NY Fed Q3) holds at its highest since 2019. Bitcoin slipped below the $60,000 floor to approximately $59,000 as Asia’s selloff extended to crypto. Curated from Federal Reserve · CME FedWatch · BEA · BLS · AAA · CNBC · Bloomberg
