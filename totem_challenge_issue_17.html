The Guarantee Illusion: MYGAs Advertising 7.10%, If You Can Wait Seven Years

As of November 5, 2026, the top multi-year guaranteed annuity on AnnuityJournal.org - sourced from AnnuityRateWatch, A- or better - is Knighthead Life's Staysail 7 at 7.10% simple interest for seven years, with a $100,000 minimum and 0% free withdrawal. The same carrier's rate sheet shows a 5.93% compound equivalent. We open with the reader's worry (rate lock, no market risk), then run MYGAs and Totem through the same six questions.

Every issue, we analyze the published strategies of prominent voices in the wealth-building space. Where we agree, we say so. Where we differ, we run both through our six-question Totem Scorecard and publish the comparison. This week the one-word challenge is the industry's favorite marketing word: guaranteed.

This Week's Pitch

The version we hear most goes like this: you are tired of watching markets swing. You want a rate you can lock, for years, without equity risk. A multi-year guaranteed annuity - a MYGA - offers a fixed rate for a term, usually two to ten years, from an insurance company. Interest grows tax-deferred until you take it out. The brochure leans hard on one word: guaranteed.

That word is their word, not ours. We quote it because it is how the products are sold. What it means in practice is a contractual promise from a specific insurer for a specific term, backed by that insurer's claims-paying ability - and, in many states, by a state guaranty association up to statutory limits. It is not a government guarantee of the rate you see on a rate board.

The challenged piece: the MYGA rate-lock pitch - a headline rate near 7%, no market risk, and the comfort of the word "guaranteed" - if you can leave the money alone for the full term.

The MYGA Playbook

Strip away the brochures and most versions run the same five steps:

  1. Lead with the worry. Markets are noisy. You want a rate lock and no equity drawdown.
  2. Quote a headline rate. Today that headline often starts with a 6 or a 7, and the top rates are frequently marked SI - simple interest.
  3. Pick a term. Longer terms usually pay more. Seven years is where the board's top number lives right now.
  4. Describe the exit. Hold to the end of the surrender period, or pay a declining surrender charge - and, on many top-rate contracts, accept 0% free withdrawal.
  5. Put the fine print in the illustration. MVA, rider trade-offs, and "guarantees backed by the claims-paying ability of the issuing company."

What the Rate Board Shows Today

We re-checked AnnuityJournal.org's rates page on November 5, 2026. The page says rates are updated from AnnuityRateWatch, limited to carriers rated A- or better, and notes that products marked SI use simple interest - "the effective compound yield is lower than the stated rate."

Knighthead Life Staysail 7 (Simple Interest), A-: 7.10%, 7 years, $100,000 minimum, 0% free withdrawal. California has a parallel listing at the same stated rate. Knighthead's own non-liquid rate flyer (rates effective October 5, 2026) shows 7.10% for the seven-year term at $100,000 or more. The carrier's State Availability Guide for the same effective date lists 7.10% simple interest with a 5.93% compound equivalent for the high band ($100,000+).

Ibexis MYGA Plus 7 SI, A-: 6.85%, 7 years, $100,000 minimum, 10% free withdrawal. Same source page, same day.

Aspida Synergy Choice 7, A-: 6.35%, 7 years, $100,000 minimum, 0% free withdrawal on the base listing. OhioInsurePlan's product page (rate effective October 1, 2026) lists the surrender schedule as 9%, 8%, 7%, 6%, 5%, 4%, 3%, then 0%, with an MVA provision and optional free-withdrawal riders that reduce the annual rate.

Athene Max Rate 7, A+: 6.15%, 7 years, $100,000 minimum, interest-only free withdrawal, per the same November 5 rates table.

Three Leaks Behind the Word

Leak 1 - Stated SI rate is not the compound keep. On $100,000, 7.10% simple interest for seven years credits interest on the original premium only. That path ends at $149,700. The same carrier prints 5.93% as the compound equivalent. Compounding at 7.10% would end near $161,600 - which is not what the SI contract pays. AnnuityJournal.org says this out loud on the rates page. Compare dollars at maturity, not headline rates.

Leak 2 - Top rates often buy illiquidity. The board's top 7.10% listing carries 0% free withdrawal. Aspida's Synergy Choice 7 pairs a competitive rate with a seven-year surrender schedule that starts at 9% and steps down to 3% in year seven. Optional liquidity riders exist on many products - and they cut the rate. You are not comparing 7.10% to a liquid 7.10%. You are comparing a locked rate to a lower liquid rate, or to a surrender charge.

Leak 3 - The "guarantee" is the insurer's promise for the term. Knighthead's own flyer states that guarantees are backed by the claims-paying ability of the issuing company. Behind the marketing word sit carrier credit, possible market-value adjustments on early exit, state guaranty association limits that vary by state, and a surrender calendar measured in years - not weeks.

How Big Is the Category?

LIMRA's U.S. Individual Annuity Sales Survey puts fixed-rate deferred annuities - the category that includes MYGAs - at $165.3 billion in full-year 2025 (Q4 2025 sales estimates PDF). In the second quarter of 2026, LIMRA reported fixed-rate deferred sales of $41.8 billion, with $77.4 billion through the first half of 2026 - about 10% below the first half of 2025, per LIMRA's September 8, 2026 release. This is a large, liquid product category. Size is not the same as fit.

Where We Second Them

Rate lock without equity risk is a real buyer need. For someone who wants contractual interest and is willing to leave the money alone for the full term, a MYGA from an A- or better carrier is a coherent product. We do not pretend otherwise.

Tax deferral inside the contract is a real feature. Interest that is not taxed until withdrawal is a legitimate design point for the right account type and the right tax situation. That is their win to claim.

Principal protection for the term is their strongest row. On our scorecard, MYGAs take Principal Protection outright. A contractual accumulation value for the guarantee period, subject to the insurer's claims-paying ability and (in many states) guaranty-association limits, is a cleaner principal story than most yield pitches we review.

The Guarantee Question

Now run the money. Put $100,000 into the top SI listing at 7.10% for seven years. If every credit posts as simple interest, you end at $149,700. The same sheet's compound equivalent of 5.93% ends near the same neighborhood once you stop comparing apples to oranges. Need a large withdrawal in year two on a 0% free-withdrawal contract? You are in surrender-charge and possibly MVA territory - arithmetic from the product terms, not a prediction of ours.
