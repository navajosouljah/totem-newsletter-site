Savers Hold 70% Odds on Tomorrow's PCE. Saudi Pipeline Adds 3.5M Barrels.

The Quick Read

Three US indices closed lower Monday as the 10-year rose to 5.24% and gold shed approximately $137 - rate geometry overrode Saudi Arabia's pipeline supply boost, which eased Brent only $0.79 from Monday's $106+ open to $105.31 by the close. October hike odds settled near 70% (CME FedWatch), modestly below Monday morning's 73-76% as the Saudi supply signal slightly reduced the energy inflation premium. The mechanism: a 10-year at 5.24% is the primary headwind for rate-sensitive equities, the dominant force compressing gold's opportunity-cost advantage, and the yield level that frames how markets will interpret Wednesday's PCE reading - that is the whole setup for Tuesday.

Wednesday August PCE at 8:30 AM ET is the sole remaining probability pivot for October 28. July PCE ran 3.7% YoY headline and 3.3% core. At 70% October hike odds, three outcomes: above 2.5% YoY pushes odds toward 78% and confirms the wait for a post-hike 4.90-5.10% CD ceiling; between 2.0-2.5% holds the 70% majority case; only below 2.0% flips October below 50% and makes locking 4.95% today defensible. Saudi Arabia's pipeline addition eases forward Brent but does not reach August's PCE measurement window, which closed August 31.

Monday's USDA Crop Progress report (week ending September 27) showed corn harvest advancing to 17% from 13% - above the five-year average pace of approximately 14%. Bitcoin held at $83,587 through Monday's stress session, institutional floor intact. Credit card delinquencies near 13% at 22.40% APR and gas at $4.49 heading toward mid-October's $4.95-$5.10 continue building Q4 household pressure regardless of Wednesday's PCE outcome.

Nikkei 225 (Tuesday September 29): 64,988 ▼ -1.35% Tue · US 10-year and equity carry-through · full US session pricedThe Nikkei's -1.35% Tuesday decline reflects the overnight carry-through of Monday's US session: the 10-year rising to 5.24%, equity indices down 0.67-0.92%, and Brent holding above $105 despite the Saudi supply addition. Japan's energy-import exposure and yen at approximately 158 maintain the currency-amplification headwind. The Nikkei's -1.35% Tuesday move is significantly larger than Monday's -0.1% opening adjustment to the deal rejection - Tuesday's session is pricing the full US market session rather than a single geopolitical event.
Hang Seng (Tuesday September 29): 24,409 ▼ -0.95% Tue · US rate carry-through · less direct oil-import exposure than TokyoThe Hang Seng's -0.95% Tuesday decline is less severe than the Nikkei's -1.35%, consistent with Hong Kong's lower direct energy-import exposure relative to Japan. China's positioning as the world's largest crude importer means the Saudi supply addition is constructive for China's forward energy cost picture over the next two to three weeks. Wednesday's PCE at 8:30 AM ET will drive the next significant rate signal that Asian markets will price in their Thursday sessions.
Yen / Dollar (September 29): approximately 158 ▼ US-Japan rate differential · yen amplifies Japan's Brent exposureThe yen at approximately 158 reflects the persistent US-Japan interest rate differential - Fed funds at 3.75-4.00% versus the Bank of Japan's near-zero policy rate. The 10-year's rise to 5.24% Monday widens the US-Japan rate differential further. At 158 yen per dollar, Japan's $105 Brent import cost is approximately 16,590 yen per barrel - near the top of the current cycle's yen-denominated energy cost. The Saudi pipeline supply addition reduces the dollar-denominated Brent price modestly; the yen-denominated benefit is partly offset by continued yen weakness.
Bottom line

Nikkei 64,988 (-1.35%), Hang Seng 24,409 (-0.95%). Tuesday's Asian session carried through US Monday declines and the 10-year's rise to 5.24%. Yen approximately 158 amplifies Japan's energy exposure. PCE Wednesday at 8:30 AM ET is the next global repricing catalyst.
