Mortgage Rates Hit 7.24%. ECI at 8:30 AM ET Closes the Week.

The Quick Read

Amazon fell approximately 7% and Intel fell 23% after Thursday’s close - Intel’s Q4 revenue guidance of $15–16 billion missed the $17.1 billion estimate by more than $1 billion. Together with Alphabet’s 12% drop Wednesday, three consecutive AI-era tech earnings misses have shifted the market’s question from who is winning AI to when AI spending produces returns. The mechanism running the other direction: the same flight to safety moving investors from equities into Treasuries has sent the 10-year yield to approximately 4.90% - a seven-week low. That Treasury bid is reinforcing Thursday’s Freddie Mac PMMS of 7.24%, down from 7.32% the prior week, which is the best mortgage-rate reading since before October’s Fed meeting. That is the mechanism Friday opens pricing.

The Q3 Employment Cost Index releases at 8:30 AM ET today - the wage-inflation input that feeds directly into PCE forecasts. Yesterday’s Q3 GDP beat at 3.4% and September PCE at 3.6% have December hike odds at approximately 87%. Consensus expects the ECI at 0.8% Q/Q (prior: 0.9%). A hot print above 0.8% pushes December odds above 90% and could partially reverse the Treasury rally. A soft print at 0.7% or below is the first disinflationary wage signal since August - it would trim December odds and potentially extend the yield decline that is already helping mortgage rates. The BoJ held at 1.25% overnight and for the first time said underlying inflation has reached its 2% target, opening the door to a December hike in Japan.

Midterm elections are Tuesday, November 3. The Senate composition that results determines the lame-duck session’s agenda for three items that touch reader finances directly: the CLARITY Act (failed cloture 49–50 September 15), energy deregulation, and the Canada softwood lumber tariff (Day 80). Iowa corn at 47% harvested sits 20 points behind its 67% five-year average with northern freeze risk rising materially within 7–10 days. Monday’s USDA Crop Progress (week ending November 1) is the final read before that window closes. Curated from BEA · BLS · Freddie Mac · CME FedWatch · BoJ · USDA NASS
