Four Banks Report Before the Bell. Savers Have 5.00% Until CPI Decides.

The Quick Read

JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo reported third-quarter earnings before the US open Tuesday - the first institutional read on how consumers and businesses are absorbing 7.40% mortgage rates and 12.9% card delinquencies. Stocks retreated in early trade as bank shares slid, adding to the Nasdaq's third consecutive session of semiconductor-led pressure. Bank management commentary on Q4 credit-loss provisions is the number analysts carry out of earnings calls.

September CPI arrives tomorrow at 8:30 AM ET. The consensus is 3.6% headline - up from August's 3.4% - and 2.5% core. That is the last inflation print before the October 27-28 FOMC meeting. The October hold is priced at 82% and does not move on a 3.6% print. What moves is December: at 78% hike odds, a reading above 3.7% pushes December from live to near-certain. A miss below 3.3% would be the first downside inflation surprise since June.

Japan's Nikkei fell 2.64% to 67,207 overnight as AI-related names sold off and Middle East geopolitical concerns persisted. Brent crude held near $106 - the hurricane supply-shock has resolved, and the floor is now Hormuz risk, which does not clear on a weather timeline. Top 1-year CD rates remain at 5.00% APY, unchanged by this morning's bank earnings. Tomorrow's CPI is the rate signal that matters for savers making decisions now. The decision is about which scenario you want protection against.

Tomorrow's CPI changes the December path but does not change this spread, which reflects institutional inertia rather than rate-cycle direction.
Bottom line

Top CDs pay 5.00% APY today. Waiting 24 hours for tomorrow's CPI costs nothing if the rate holds - but a surprise miss could begin to soften the rate environment by December. Locking today eliminates that uncertainty; waiting preserves the option to catch a December hike if one materializes at 78% probability.

Brent remained near $105.76 Tuesday morning - essentially unchanged from Monday despite the domestic supply restoration. The price floor has shifted from hurricane-related supply disruption to Middle East tanker risk in the Strait of Hormuz, which does not resolve on a weather timeline and holds the risk premium independent of Gulf production levels.

Brent crude (Tuesday October 13 morning): ~$105.76/barrel Nov contract · Gulf restart complete · Hormuz risk premium holdsThe Gulf restart removed the domestic supply-shock scenario that briefly added $2-3 to Brent Thursday and Friday. The Middle East component - tanker-attack risk in the Strait of Hormuz - is the remaining floor. That risk is not weather-dependent and does not clear on any near-term timeline. Brent's 72% year-to-date gain is primarily a Hormuz and OPEC story, not a hurricane story.
WTI crude (Tuesday October 13 morning): ~$93.31/barrel Tue morning · Brent-WTI spread ~$12.45 reflects Middle East premiumWTI typically trades at a discount to Brent reflecting US domestic supply dynamics. The $12.45 spread is wider than the typical $3-5 historical range, reflecting a Middle East risk premium that accrues directly to Brent (the international benchmark) without the same magnitude on domestic WTI. Gulf production resuming keeps the domestic supply picture stable.
National gas average (AAA, October 11): $4.36/gallon -$0.01 from prior week · 10-14 day lag between crude and pumpGas prices held near $4.36 last week despite crude's volatility - the typical 10-14 day lag between wholesale crude and pump prices means Friday's Brent spike arrives at the pump around next week. The Gulf restart holds down the domestic crude component, limiting how much of the Brent premium passes through to US pump prices in the near term.
Bottom line

The hurricane supply risk is resolved. Oil's remaining elevation is Middle East risk premium, which does not have a weather-forecast equivalent. Gas at $4.36 is more likely to hold or drift lower this week as Gulf production normalizes - unless a new Hormuz incident changes the Brent calculus.

30-year fixed (Freddie Mac PMMS, October 8): 7.40% ▲ from 7.28% Oct 1 · 15-year at 6.73% · year-ago 6.30%At 7.40%, the monthly principal-and-interest payment on a $400,000 loan is approximately $2,770 - about $290 more than at last year's 6.30%. That math constrains buyer demand and traps existing homeowners who financed at 3-4% - selling into a 7.40% market means trading into an identical payment on a smaller house.
10-year Treasury (Friday October 9 close · bond mkt reopened Tuesday): 5.24% off 5.349% Monday Oct 6 high · Freddie Mac survey window open through ThursdayThe 10-year is the primary benchmark for mortgage rate pricing. If CPI tomorrow arrives below consensus and yields fall through Wednesday, next week's Freddie Mac reading could show the first decline since August.
Canada softwood lumber tariff (Day 63): 50% US duty on Canadian softwood · no relief or negotiation announcedThe tariff adds framing cost to new home construction. Builders absorb it through buyer incentives and lot discounts rather than price increases in a demand-constrained market. September housing starts data is due later this month - the July figure showed a 12.4% decline that largely reflected mortgage-rate affordability rather than lumber costs alone.
Bottom line

Seven consecutive weekly rate increases have pushed mortgage affordability to the worst level of this survey cycle. The fastest path to relief is a lower CPI tomorrow - not because rates respond immediately, but because a surprise downside print would be the first real signal the Fed's October hold could extend into December.
