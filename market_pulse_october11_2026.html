Market Pulse • Sunday, October 11, 2026

Stocks End a Winning Week Near Records. Savers Can Lock 5.00%.

The Quick Read

The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,811.54, just below all-time highs, after a week that bent without breaking. Chip stocks fell 3.4% mid-week on a revised OpenAI revenue figure, but the Dow gained 423 points on Friday alone as energy and value stocks held. The mechanism: narrow selling in one crowded theme, not a broad retreat, while bonds found buyers and oil eased from its Thursday spike after the White House pledged no Iran strike before the November midterms.

The week ahead delivers its two biggest decisions Tuesday and Wednesday. Major banks report before the open on Tuesday - JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo - followed by Morgan Stanley and Bank of America plus September CPI at 8:30 AM ET on Wednesday. The consensus for September CPI is 3.6% headline (from 3.4%) and 2.5% core. That is the last inflation read before the Fed's October 28 decision, with futures pricing an 82% chance the Fed holds.

Underneath, Hurricane Isaias made landfall near the Mississippi coast late Friday with more than 60% of Gulf oil production shut in and refineries in Pascagoula, MS and the Mobile, AL area facing storm exposure. Platforms are expected to resume once inspected, but the restart timeline is unknown heading into Sunday. Also registered Friday: UMich consumer sentiment fell to 46.3, a five-month low and only 1.5 points above the May record, as year-ahead inflation expectations ticked up to 4.7%. Bottom line

Isaias is the dominant energy story heading into Monday. A clean restart keeps Brent near $104 and lets pump prices continue their gradual weekly decline. Refinery damage changes that calculus.

Friday's October WASDE was the biggest agricultural surprise of the year. USDA raised its corn yield estimate to 181.2 bushels per acre, up 2.7 bpa from September and well above the pre-report consensus of 177.7 bpa, sending corn futures briefly limit down. Soybean yield hit a record 53.1 bpa.

Corn (WASDE October 9): 181.2 bpa yield ▲ +2.7 from September · production 16.03B bushels · ending stocks 1.849B · 282M above SeptemberThe consensus was near 177.7 bpa. The 3.5-bpa miss sent December corn futures limit down briefly before recovering. Larger ending stocks mean more corn available for feed, ethanol, and export - containing cost pressures in those supply chains.
Soybeans (WASDE October 9): 53.1 bpa yield (record) ▲ +0.3 from September · production 4.562B bushels (record) · ending stocks 315MRecord soybean output pushes ending stocks above pre-report estimates by about 10 million bushels. Soybean meal is a key input in poultry and pork production, so a larger crop caps feed costs for both.
Harvest pace (USDA Crop Progress, week ending October 4): Corn 23% · Soybeans 25% ▼ both behind five-year averages · wet Midwest September delayed progressHarvest is running behind pace due to a historically wet second half of September across Iowa and Nebraska, but the WASDE confirms the crop is large despite the delay. Quality and size are separate questions.
Bottom line

A bigger-than-expected corn crop and record soybeans contain feed costs even as diesel runs high. The limit-down reaction in corn futures shows how much smaller crop was already priced in before Friday's report.

Consumer sentiment fell to its worst reading in five months on Friday. The October preliminary UMich index came in at 46.3, below the 47.8 consensus and only 1.5 points above the May 2026 record low. Year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.7%. Layoffs, however, remain low: initial jobless claims held at 197,000 for the week ending October 3.

University of Michigan sentiment (preliminary October, released October 9): 46.3 ▼ vs 47.8 expected · vs 48.1 September final · five-month lowCurrent conditions fell to 44.7 (versus 51.0 expected), while expectations came in at 47.3. Year-ahead inflation expectations ticked up to 4.7% from 4.6%, and five-year expectations rose to 3.5% from 3.4%. Gas above $4 and 7.40% mortgages are the dominant drags on how households feel.
Initial jobless claims (DOL, week ending October 3): 197,000 ▼ -2,000 · fifth straight weekly decline · four-week average 198,000Continuing claims rose 17,000 to 1.716 million, a sign that job searches are taking longer even as layoffs stay low. The labor market is low-hire, low-fire: those with jobs are mostly protected; those without face extended searches.
Credit card balances 90+ days delinquent (NY Fed, Q2 2026): 12.9% ▲ from 12.2% in Q2 2025 · Q3 report due NovemberThe New York Fed notes the rising share partly reflects charged-off debt carried on credit reports, not entirely new delinquencies. The stress is concentrated in lower-income households and shows up most clearly in the rising continuing-claims trend.
Bottom line

Sentiment is close to a record low with jobs still holding. The split tells the story: those employed are under price pressure but not layoff pressure; those between jobs are taking longer to land. Bank earnings Tuesday give the next read on whether credit stress is spreading.
