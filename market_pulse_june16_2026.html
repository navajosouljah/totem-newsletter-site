Weekly Briefing • Monday, June 16, 2026

Iran Deal "Now Complete." FOMC Begins. Bank of Japan Hikes to 1%.

President Trump declared the US-Iran peace deal "now complete" Sunday night, with a signing ceremony expected in Switzerland on June 19. Oil continued its freefall - WTI hit $77.31, down from $91 less than two weeks ago. The FOMC convenes today for Chair Warsh's first meeting, and the Bank of Japan just raised rates to 1.0% for the first time since 2008.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • Iran deal declared complete - signing expected June 19 in Switzerland: President Trump announced Sunday night that the US-Iran peace text is "now complete." A formal signing ceremony is expected Thursday in Switzerland. WTI crude fell to $77.31 - down nearly $14 from its $91 peak less than two weeks ago. If signed, it reopens the Strait of Hormuz and removes the single biggest driver of 2026 inflation.
  • FOMC meeting begins today - Warsh's first as Chair: The two-day FOMC meeting starts Monday with the rate decision, new dot plot, and Summary of Economic Projections due Tuesday at 2:00 PM ET. Markets price a 99% probability of holding at 3.50-3.75%. The press conference at 2:30 PM is the real event - Warsh's first public remarks on the path forward with oil crashing and inflation data still hot.
  • Bank of Japan hikes to 1.0% - first time since 2008: The BOJ raised rates 25 basis points to 1.0% on Monday, ending the last vestige of the global zero-rate era. The Nikkei shrugged it off and pushed toward 70,000 for the first time in history, reading the move as a confidence signal rather than a headwind.
  • SpaceX hits $206 - up 53% from IPO in three trading days: SpaceX shares surged to $206.19 by Friday's close, up from the $135 IPO price on June 12. The company's valuation has blown past $2 trillion, making it the most valuable IPO in history and the fastest to double in market cap from debut.

The Quick Read

The Iran deal going from "final text" to "now complete" in one week is the fastest-moving geopolitical development of 2026. Oil has crashed from $91 to $77.31 in less than two weeks - a 15% decline that, if it holds, rewrites the inflation outlook for the second half of the year. Stocks rallied broadly with the Nasdaq leading at +3.1% on the week, the Dow adding 0.9% to 51,671, and the Russell 2000 pushing above 2,990. The VIX dropped to 16.20 - the lowest fear reading in weeks. Gold surged past $4,300 even as oil fell, signaling that geopolitical uncertainty is not resolved, just shifting. Bitcoin recovered to $66,287 as risk appetite returned. Housing distress continued building with Q1 foreclosure starts up 26% year-over-year. The personal savings rate remains at 2.6% and 47% of BNPL users are paying late.

The FOMC meeting starting today is the mechanism through which the Iran deal either flows into the real economy or gets stuck at the policy level. If oil settling below $80 gives Warsh's committee confidence that inflation is peaking, the dot plot could preserve one or two cuts for 2026 - and mortgage rates, credit card APRs, and the consumer squeeze begin to ease. If the committee takes a wait-and-see stance, arguing that CPI at 4.2% and PPI at 6.5% are still too high regardless of oil, rates stay where they are and the consumer continues to burn through savings at the fastest pace since 2008.

The Bank of Japan hiking to 1.0% is the last major central bank to exit the zero-rate era. It matters because it signals the global end of the cheap-money framework that held since 2008. The Nikkei rallying toward 70,000 on the news says the market reads it as confidence, not restriction - but combined with the ECB at 2.25% and the Fed at 3.50-3.75%, global credit conditions are the tightest they have been in nearly two decades. This is the backdrop against which US consumers are carrying $18.8 trillion in household debt at record APRs.

The NY Empire State Manufacturing Index falling to 5.7 from 19.6 (versus 13.9 expected) is a data point worth watching. Manufacturing was one of the bright spots in the spring. A sharp slowdown heading into the FOMC meeting gives the doves ammunition to argue that the economy is already cooling without further tightening. But one regional survey is not a trend - the national ISM number matters more.

Stock Market

Broad rally on Iran peace and risk-on sentiment. Nasdaq led as tech rebounded from last week's crash.

S&P 500: 5,530 +1.8% wk
Dow Jones: 51,671 +0.9% wk
Nasdaq: 26,684 +3.1% wk
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,995 +1.7% wk
Fear gauge (VIX): 16.20 −8.4% wk
Bottom Line
  • The Nasdaq leading at +3.1% after crashing 4.2% the previous Tuesday is a classic whipsaw recovery. SpaceX alone added billions in market cap. But the rally is built on two "ifs" - an Iran deal that has not been signed yet and an FOMC that has not spoken yet. If either disappoints this week, the gains are vulnerable.
  • The VIX dropping to 16.20 from 17.68 is the market pricing in a best-case scenario: Iran signs, the Fed stays dovish, and oil keeps falling. That complacency is a setup for a volatility spike if anything deviates from the script. Expect the VIX to move sharply on Tuesday's rate decision.
  • Small caps outperforming again (+1.7%) confirms the rotation trade is intact. Rate-sensitive stocks are betting on lower rates ahead. If the FOMC kills that narrative, the Russell gives back its recent gains quickly. If the dot plot confirms cuts, small caps could outperform for the rest of the summer.

Interest Rates

Yields flat ahead of the FOMC. Mortgages crept higher even as oil crashed. The 30-year Treasury holding just below 5% is the line to watch.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (effective 3.62%)
2-year Treasury: 4.09% steady wk
10-year Treasury: 4.48% −1 bp wk
30-year Treasury: 4.97% steady wk
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.58% +6 bps wk
Bottom Line
  • Yields barely moved on the week despite oil crashing 15% - the bond market is waiting for the FOMC before repricing. The 10-year at 4.48% is holding in a tight range that will break decisively after Tuesday's statement. A dovish surprise sends it toward 4.30%. A hawkish hold pushes it back above 4.55%.
  • Mortgages rising to 6.58% while oil crashes is the disconnect that frustrates homebuyers. Lenders price off MBS spreads and volatility, not crude oil. Until the FOMC provides clarity on the rate path, mortgage rates will drift higher on uncertainty alone. A dovish dot plot Tuesday could snap them back toward 6.40%.
  • The 30-year Treasury holding just under 5% for a second straight week is the bond market's line in the sand. Below 5% means the long end believes the inflation peak is in. If it breaks above 5% after the FOMC, the bond market is telling you it does not believe the Fed regardless of what Warsh says at the podium.

  • The national average at 1.97% versus CPI at 4.2% means the typical saver loses 2.2 percentage points of purchasing power per year. Only those actively shopping for the best rates are keeping pace with inflation - and even then, PPI at 6.5% suggests consumer prices have further to climb.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil in freefall. WTI has dropped nearly $14 in two weeks on Iran peace. Gas prices lagging but beginning to follow.

WTI crude: $77.31/bbl −3.6% from last week, −15.1% from $91 peak
Brent crude: $81.23/bbl −1.5% wk
National gas avg: $4.06/gal −$0.11 wk
West Coast: $5.30-5.80/gal (CA highest at $5.80)
Cheapest region (South Central): $3.32/gal (Oklahoma)
Bottom Line
  • WTI crashing from $91 to $77 in less than two weeks is the most consequential price move of 2026. Energy drove 60% of May's CPI increase. If crude settles in the $72-77 range after a signed deal on June 19, the July and August CPI prints could show meaningful deceleration for the first time since February. That changes the entire rate conversation.
  • Gas at $4.06 nationally is finally catching up to the oil crash - pumps lag crude by 2-3 weeks. If oil holds below $80, expect national average gas to fall toward $3.75-3.85 by early July. The decline from the $4.55 peak in late May would be nearly 20%, the fastest drop since the pandemic. The $2.48 spread between Oklahoma ($3.32) and California ($5.80) still shows state-level costs dominate on the West Coast.
  • The risk is a deal collapse. "Now complete" is not "signed." If the June 19 ceremony falls through or gets delayed, oil snaps back above $85 in hours and gas reverses course. Markets are pricing in completion, so the downside surprise from a collapse is much larger than the upside from a signing.

Commodities

Gold surges past $4,300 even as oil crashes. Cattle pulling back from records. Grains finding a floor.

Gold / oz: $4,318 +2.8% wk
Silver / oz: $69.91 +3.3% wk
Copper / lb: $6.49 +0.9% wk
Corn / bushel: $4.16 +1.2% wk
Soybeans / bushel: $11.19 +0.4% wk
Live cattle / cwt: $243.25 −5.0% from $256 record
Bottom Line
  • Gold surging to $4,318 while oil crashes is a signal worth reading carefully. If Iran peace were fully priced in, gold would be falling on reduced geopolitical risk. Instead, gold is rising - which means the market is either pricing in FOMC uncertainty, dollar weakness ahead, or the possibility that the Iran deal falls apart. Gold above $4,300 is a hedge, not a celebration.
  • Cattle pulling back 5% from the $256 record to $243.25 is healthy profit-taking, not a trend reversal. The US herd at 86.2 million head - its smallest since 1951 - is a structural constraint that takes years to resolve. Beef prices are coming down temporarily on seasonal demand shifts but will remain elevated through 2027 at minimum.
  • Corn and soybeans bouncing after their 14% and 9.4% respective four-week declines suggests a floor may be forming. If drought conditions in Illinois and the Corn Belt worsen through July, these prices reverse sharply higher. The next USDA Crop Progress report is the catalyst to watch.

Crop Weather

Drought still covering 56% of the Lower 48. Kansas wheat only 15% good-to-excellent. El Nino probability rising.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent. Planting essentially complete. Illinois 42% abnormally dry with 20% in moderate drought - the key risk zone.
Winter wheat: Kansas only 15% good-to-excellent. Oklahoma 13%. Harvest 23% complete in Oklahoma. Production still forecast at 1,048M bushels - lowest since 1965.
Drought: 56% of the Lower 48 in drought. 75% of US counties affected at some level. Record-low snowpack in 8 western states continues depleting reservoirs.
El Nino watch: 87-92% probability through January 2027. NOAA expects development by late summer - historically brings drier Midwest conditions that could stress the corn crop during pollination.
Bottom Line
  • Kansas wheat at 15% good-to-excellent and Oklahoma at 13% are catastrophic ratings. The 1,048 million bushel production forecast is locked in as the worst since 1965. This means higher flour, bread, and cereal prices in Q3 and Q4 regardless of what oil or the Fed does. Food inflation is structural and slower to resolve than energy inflation.
  • The Illinois drought is the emerging risk that could turn a bad crop year into a crisis. If drought deepens during the critical July pollination window, the 67% good-to-excellent corn rating could deteriorate rapidly. The next 6-8 weeks determine whether 2026 is a poor crop year or a historically bad one.
  • El Nino forming at 87-92% probability adds a multi-year dimension. El Nino typically dries the Midwest and wets the South - exactly the wrong combination for corn-belt agriculture. Combined with existing drought and depleted reservoirs, agricultural stress could extend well into 2027.

Real Estate

Foreclosure acceleration continues. Mortgages ticking higher. The split market deepens as prices hit records while distress builds underneath.

Foreclosure starts Q1 (vs. last yr): +26% YoY
Foreclosure filings April: 42,430 properties +18% YoY
Bank repossessions / REOs Q1: +45% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: ~11.5% highest since Q2 2021
Existing-home sales (annual pace): 4.17M +3.2% MoM
Median list price: −2.2% YoY nationally
% listings cutting price: 36% nationally; up to 44% in pandemic boom markets
Inventory: ~4.5 months of supply (highest since 2020)
30-year mortgage: 6.58%
Bottom Line
  • April foreclosure filings at 42,430 - up 18% year-over-year after Q1 starts rose 26% - confirms the acceleration is not slowing. Each month brings more data points showing the same trajectory. REOs up 45% means banks are completing the foreclosure process, not just starting it. Distressed supply is entering the market.
  • Median list prices falling 2.2% nationally while 36% of sellers cut asking prices (44% in pandemic boom markets) is the headline number finally catching up to what individual sellers have experienced for months. The record median sale price story from last week reflected closed sales on contracts signed weeks earlier. The list-price data is more forward-looking - and it is pointing down.
  • Mortgages at 6.58% are now 6 basis points higher than last week despite oil crashing 15%. If the FOMC signals a cut on Tuesday, mortgage rates could ease toward 6.30-6.40% by month end. That would be the first meaningful relief for buyers since spring. But if the Fed holds a hawkish tone, mortgages stay above 6.50% through the summer and the housing freeze continues.
  • Inventory reaching 4.5 months of supply - the highest since 2020 - is approaching the 5-6 month balanced-market threshold. But a growing share of that inventory is distressed (REOs up 45%). A balanced market driven by foreclosures and price cuts is not the healthy normalization that the industry spin suggests.

Household Finance

Consumer stress data continues to build. Auto delinquencies at record highs. Credit card delinquencies approaching 2008 levels.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: ~$1.18 trillion; avg APR 21.5%
Credit card delinquency: approaching 2008 crisis levels (NY Fed)
Avg. new-car payment: $748/mo subprime: $792/mo
Auto loan 60+ days delinquent: 1.54% record high (NY Fed)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (lowest since 2008)
Bottom Line
  • Auto loan delinquencies hitting a record 1.54% (NY Fed) alongside credit card delinquencies approaching 2008 levels is the consumer stress story moving from anecdotal to systemic. These are not subprime-only problems anymore. The delinquency wave is broadening across credit tiers as the savings buffer at 2.6% provides no cushion for any disruption.
  • Credit card debt at ~$1.18 trillion with APRs at 21.5% means American households are paying roughly $250 billion a year in credit card interest alone. If the FOMC holds rates steady Tuesday, APRs stay where they are. If they signal a hike is possible, APRs rise further. Only a cut path provides relief - and even then, APR reductions lag the fed funds rate by months.
  • 47% of BNPL users paying late - up from 41% a year ago - with more than half saying they cannot make ends meet without installment plans is a leading indicator that traditional credit metrics are understating real consumer distress. BNPL debt does not always appear on credit reports, meaning the $18.8 trillion household debt figure is conservative.

Bitcoin (BTC): $66,287 +3.8% wk
Ethereum (ETH): $1,795 +7.8% wk
Solana (SOL): $73.78 +3.9% wk
XRP: $1.23 +7.0% wk
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin recovering from $63,878 to $66,287 after last week's $1.67 billion in ETF outflows is a sign that retail buying absorbed the institutional selling. But a 3.8% bounce does not erase the structural concern. BTC is still down 48% from its $128,198 all-time high in October 2025. The gap between retail enthusiasm and institutional conviction remains wide.
  • Ethereum's 7.8% weekly gain to $1,795 is the strongest relative performance in weeks, driven by speculative rotation into altcoins ahead of the FOMC. ETH at $1,795 is still down from $4,953 in August 2025 - a 64% decline. The rally is positioning, not fundamentals.
  • The entire crypto complex is a leveraged bet on the FOMC outcome. A dovish signal Tuesday (cuts still on the table) sends Bitcoin toward $70,000 and lifts the whole market. A hawkish signal (inflation acknowledged, cuts removed) tests $62,000-63,000 support. Wednesday morning will tell you whether this week's gains stick or evaporate.

Around the World

Japan nears 70,000 on BOJ hike. Europe rallies on oil. China treads water. Emerging markets squeezed by tariff proposals.

Japan: Nikkei at 69,841 +5.8% wk. Approaching 70,000 for the first time in history. BOJ hiked to 1.0% - markets read it as confidence. Now up 30% year-to-date, far outpacing every global index.
Europe: Stoxx 600 rallying on falling oil and Iran peace. Banks leading (Deutsche Bank +6.6% last week). ECB at 2.25%. The rate differential with the Fed continues supporting the dollar.
China: Shanghai Composite holding above 4,000. Government floor intact but domestic demand not recovering. US-China 30% tariff framework failing to inspire confidence.
Emerging: MSCI EM under pressure from tariff proposals on 60 economies and strong dollar. July 24 temporary tariff expiration is the next catalyst. Brazil IBOV at 170,415 −0.6%. Mexico IPC at 68,208 +1.8%.
Bottom Line
  • Japan's Nikkei approaching 70,000 while the BOJ raises rates to 1.0% is the most remarkable story in global markets. The market is reading the hike as the BOJ finally normalizing from an era that ended everywhere else years ago - a sign of economic health, not restriction. With the yen still weak and AI investment accelerating, Japan has been the best major-market trade of 2026 by a wide margin.
  • Europe benefits more from falling oil than the US does. European industry is more energy-dependent, and the Stoxx 600 rally on Iran peace reflects that directly. If oil stays below $80, European corporate margins improve meaningfully and the ECB's 2.25% rate looks increasingly appropriate rather than restrictive.
  • Emerging markets are caught in a vise between tariff proposals on 60 economies and a strong dollar that makes dollar-denominated debt more expensive to service. The July 24 temporary tariff expiration is the next binary event - if tariffs become permanent, EM underperformance accelerates. If they expire or are reduced, capital flows reverse.
