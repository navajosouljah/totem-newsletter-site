Daily Briefing • June 12, 2026

PPI Surges to 6.5%. SpaceX Goes Public at $1.77 Trillion. Oil Crashes on Iran Peace Hopes.

Producer prices stunned markets at 6.5% year-over-year - the worst wholesale inflation reading since early 2023 - just one day after CPI printed 4.2%. But stocks rallied sharply on Iran peace deal optimism that sent oil crashing below $86, and SpaceX became the most valuable company to ever go public.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • PPI shocks at 6.5% - wholesale inflation far hotter than expected: Producer prices surged 1.1% in May versus 0.7% expected, pushing the 12-month rate to 6.5%. Coming one day after CPI at 4.2%, it confirms inflation is reaccelerating from both ends of the supply chain. The FOMC meeting next week just became the most important in years.
  • SpaceX debuts as the largest IPO in history: Priced at $135 per share with a $1.77 trillion valuation, raising $75 billion. Trading opens today on Nasdaq under ticker SPCX with a 30% retail allocation. It shatters Saudi Aramco's $29.4 billion IPO record by 2.5 times.
  • Iran peace deal hopes crush oil, lift stocks: Reports of a potential agreement that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz sent WTI crashing below $86 - down from $91.56 just yesterday. Stocks rallied broadly, with the S&P 500 gaining 1.75% and the Russell 2000 surging over 3%.
  • ECB hikes rates to 2.25% on war-driven inflation: The European Central Bank raised rates Thursday as Iran conflict and energy costs push eurozone inflation higher. Europe is now tightening while the Fed stays on hold - a policy divergence that strengthens the dollar.

The Quick Read

Two inflation reports in two days, and neither is good. PPI at 6.5% on top of CPI at 4.2% means inflation is reaccelerating from the factory floor to the checkout line. Oil crashing below $86 on Iran peace hopes gave stocks a sharp relief rally - the S&P bounced 1.75% and the Russell surged 3% - but wholesale prices are already locked in and will flow through to consumers in the months ahead. Housing distress keeps building with foreclosure starts up 13% and FHA delinquencies above 11%. Consumer stress remains severe - $18.8 trillion in debt, a 2.6% savings rate, and 47% of BNPL users paying late. Gold rebounded to $4,222 as geopolitical bidding returned after yesterday's crash.

The PPI report is the more important development today. CPI tells you what consumers are paying. PPI tells you what producers are paying - and at 6.5%, those costs have not reached the consumer yet. This is the pipeline problem: companies absorbing 6.5% input cost increases will either pass them through (more CPI inflation ahead) or eat the margin (earnings downgrades ahead). Neither outcome is good for stocks at current valuations.

Oil's crash is the one piece of genuine good news. If Iran peace talks produce a deal that reopens the Strait of Hormuz, crude could fall significantly further, which would take the single biggest driver out of the inflation equation. Energy drove 60% of May's monthly CPI increase. Remove that, and the inflation picture improves quickly. But "peace hopes" is not a deal, and this week has shown how fast the narrative can swing - strikes one day, peace talks the next.

The Fed meets June 16-17 in new Chair Kevin Warsh's first meeting. The dot plot will signal whether the committee still sees any rate cuts this year or if the inflation data has killed that path entirely. With CPI at 4.2%, PPI at 6.5%, and the 30-year Treasury above 5%, the market is doing the tightening for them. Mortgage rates at 6.52% and credit card APRs above 21% are already squeezing consumers. The question is whether the Fed acknowledges that inflation is reaccelerating or maintains the "one more cut" narrative the market wants to hear.

Stock Market

Sharp relief rally on Iran peace hopes and SpaceX euphoria. Broad-based, with small caps leading.

S&P 500: 7,394 +1.75% day
Dow Jones: 50,849 +1.86% day
Nasdaq: 25,810 +2.54% day
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,921 +3.02% day
Fear gauge (VIX): 19.24 −1.03 (easing)
Bottom Line
  • The Russell 2000 surging 3% and leading all indices is the most interesting signal. Small caps are rate-sensitive and the most beaten-down group - this rally reads as short-covering and a bet that Iran peace will take the inflation heat off rates. Whether that bet pays depends entirely on whether a deal materializes.
  • The VIX easing to 19 from 22 yesterday shows fear receding, but it is still well above the 14-15 range from early June. The market is not complacent yet, which is actually healthy. The bigger test comes next week with the FOMC.
  • SpaceX going public at $1.77 trillion on the same day PPI prints 6.5% is a fitting snapshot of this market - massive liquidity chasing big names while inflation eats the foundation. Peak-cycle IPOs have historically marked inflection points.

Interest Rates

Rates barely budged despite the PPI shock. The market is holding its breath for next week's FOMC and dot plot.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (effective 3.62%)
2-year Treasury: 4.15% −1 bp
10-year Treasury: 4.53% −2 bps
30-year Treasury: 5.01%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.52% −3 bps wk
Bottom Line
  • The 30-year Treasury holding above 5% despite an oil crash and stock rally tells you the bond market is not buying the relief narrative. PPI at 6.5% reinforces the inflation problem. Until inflation data actually turns, long rates are not coming down.
  • Mortgages ticking down 3 bps to 6.52% is noise. The trajectory since May has been up, and the FOMC meeting next week will determine whether 6.5% is the floor or the ceiling. The dot plot under new Chair Warsh is the single most important signal for rates.
  • The 2-year at 4.15% versus the fed funds rate at 3.62% tells you the market is pricing in at least one more hike as a possibility. That was unthinkable two weeks ago. The inversion between the 2-year and 10-year has narrowed, which historically signals the economy is approaching a transition point.

Savings & CD Rates

With CPI at 4.2% and PPI at 6.5%, even the best savings rates are underwater on a real basis. Cash is losing purchasing power.

National avg 1-yr CD: 1.98% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.10-4.11% APY (Popular Direct, E*TRADE, Live Oak)
Best credit union CD: 5.00% APY (Consumers CU, Digital FCU)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • The national average CD at 1.98% versus CPI at 4.2% means most savers are losing 2.2% of purchasing power per year. Even the best rates at 5.00% barely beat inflation. PPI at 6.5% suggests consumer prices have further to rise, which will widen this gap.
  • If the Fed signals a hike next week, CD and savings rates could rise - but so will the inflation that erodes them. The only winning move for cash right now is keeping durations short and rolling into higher rates as they appear.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil crashes on Iran peace hopes. WTI drops from $91 to below $86 in 24 hours - the sharpest daily move since March.

WTI crude: $86/bbl −6.1% from yesterday
Brent crude: $88/bbl −7.5% from yesterday
National gas avg: $4.17/gal −$0.11 wk
West Coast: $5.36/gal
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): $3.64/gal
Bottom Line
  • Oil crashing from $91 to $86 overnight on Iran peace hopes is the single best thing that could happen for the inflation picture. Energy drove 60% of May's monthly CPI increase. If a deal materializes and crude drops toward $75-80, the July and August CPI prints could improve meaningfully.
  • But the whipsaw this week - up $3 on strikes Monday, down $6 on peace hopes Thursday - shows how fragile the situation is. Until a deal is signed, oil trades on headlines, not fundamentals. Positioning around a binary geopolitical outcome is speculation, not investing.
  • Gas at $4.17 nationally and $5.36 on the West Coast is still painful for consumers despite the weekly decline. The $1.72 spread between the West Coast and Gulf Coast ($3.64) reflects California's regulatory premium as much as crude prices. Even if oil falls further, West Coast prices may stay above $5.

Gold rebounds to $4,222 as geopolitical bid returns. Cattle near records. Agricultural commodities slip on crop progress.

Gold / oz: $4,222 +2.9% day
Silver / oz: $67.00 +3.8% day
Copper / lb: $6.38 +1.4% day
Corn / bushel: $4.12 −1.7% wk
Soybeans / bushel: $11.15 −0.6% wk
Live cattle / cwt: $242.67 near record
Bottom Line
  • Gold bouncing from $4,101 to $4,222 in a single day after yesterday's 9% crash shows the geopolitical bid has not disappeared - it was just overwhelmed by rate-hike fears. The competing forces are clear: Iran tension supports gold, but higher real rates undermine it. Until one narrative wins decisively, expect volatility to stay elevated.
  • Copper rising 1.4% alongside stocks is a constructive demand signal. Copper tracks global economic activity, and its recovery suggests the market is not pricing a recession - just an inflation scare. Watch whether copper holds above $6 in the coming weeks.
  • Cattle at $242.67 per hundredweight near record highs means beef prices are not coming down anytime soon. Combined with winter wheat at 26% good-to-excellent, the food inflation pipeline is building even as energy-driven inflation may ease on peace hopes.

Crop Weather

Drought covers 56% of the Lower 48. Winter wheat in crisis. Corn looks fine but summer is the test.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent, 97% planted, 86% emerged. Solid start but summer heat has not arrived yet.
Winter wheat: Only 26% good-to-excellent; 44% poor or very poor. Production forecast down 25% from 2025.
Drought: 56% of the Lower 48 in drought. Record-low snowpack in 8 western states. Severe drought expanding into Northeast and Minnesota.
New risk: Drought developing in parts of the Midwest - the corn belt. Sierra Nevada snowpack below 20% of normal threatens Western irrigation.
Bottom Line
  • Winter wheat production forecast down 25% from last year is a locked-in inflationary force. Higher flour and bread prices are coming in the back half of 2026 regardless of what oil or the Fed does. This will show up in CPI with a lag.
  • The drought expanding into the Midwest is the new development to watch. If it reaches the core corn belt in July and August, the 67% good-to-excellent rating could deteriorate rapidly. The corn crop's fate will be determined by weather in the next 8 weeks.
  • Record-low snowpack across 8 western states means irrigation water is severely constrained. This is not a one-season problem - it compounds into reduced planting acreage and higher commodity prices going into 2027.

Foreclosures accelerating. FHA delinquencies above 11%. Median price hits a record while one in five sellers cuts asking price.

Foreclosure starts May (vs. last yr): 27,304 +13% YoY
Foreclosure starts Q1 (vs. last yr): +20% YoY
Bank repossessions / REOs May (vs. last yr): 4,092 +6% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.52% +126 bps YoY
Existing-home sales (annual pace): 4.17M +3.2% MoM
Median home price: $429,300 record
% listings cutting price: 20.2% (Redfin, May)
Inventory: 1.55M homes, 4.5 months of supply
30-year mortgage: 6.52%
Bottom Line
  • FHA delinquencies above 11% and rising 126 bps year-over-year is the clearest distress signal in housing. Pandemic-era FHA relief options fully expired in September 2025 and the pipeline from delinquency to foreclosure is accelerating. First-time buyers who bought with 3.5% down at pandemic prices are the most exposed group.
  • The median home price hitting a record $429,300 while 20% of sellers cut asking prices is the same split-market story: low inventory props up the headline number while individual sellers struggle to find buyers. If Iran peace drops oil and the FOMC signals a cut, mortgages could ease - but we are not there yet.
  • Foreclosure starts up 13% in May and 20% in Q1 are still below pre-pandemic norms, but the trajectory is what matters. Every month the acceleration continues, the narrative that "housing is fine" gets harder to defend. Watch Q2 numbers for confirmation.
  • Inventory at 4.5 months of supply is the highest since 2020 and approaching a balanced market (5-6 months). But distressed properties - REOs up 6% YoY - are an increasing share. A balanced market driven by distressed supply is not the same as a healthy one.

Household Finance

PPI at 6.5% means higher costs are coming for everything consumers buy. The 2.6% savings rate offers no buffer.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion; avg APR ~21.5%
Avg. new-car payment: $773/mo; avg financed: $43,899 record
Subprime auto 60+ days late: 6.80% (near 32-year high)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (lowest since 2008)
Bottom Line
  • PPI at 6.5% is a leading indicator for consumer prices. Producers absorbing 6.5% cost increases will either pass them through (higher prices at the register) or cut costs (layoffs, reduced quality). Either way, the consumer gets squeezed further on top of a 2.6% savings rate that already provides zero buffer.
  • Credit card debt at $1.25 trillion with APRs above 21% means Americans are paying roughly $270 billion a year in credit card interest alone. That is money not going to savings, investment, or discretionary spending. If the Fed hikes, APRs rise further.
  • 47% of BNPL users paying late and nearly a quarter using installment plans for groceries is the kind of data point that deserves more attention than it gets. When people finance food purchases and nearly half cannot keep up, the consumer stress story is not a narrative - it is a math problem.
  • The average amount financed for a new car hitting a record $43,899 with 22.9% of loans stretching past 7 years means consumers are taking on more debt for longer to afford the same goods. This is the definition of declining purchasing power.

Bottom Line

  • XRP and Solana leading the recovery with 2.5% gains while Bitcoin ETFs see outflows points to a retail-driven bounce, not institutional conviction. Sustainable recovery requires macro clarity - specifically, a Fed that is not about to hike.
  • Crypto needs oil to keep falling and the FOMC to signal dovishness. If both happen, Bitcoin could reclaim $65,000-70,000 range. If either fails, the $60,000 support gets tested again.

Around the World

Europe rallies on ECB clarity. Japan holding near records. China slips below key support. Emerging markets pressured.

Japan: Nikkei at 64,217 +0.06%. Up 27.2% year-to-date - leading all major global indices. Weak yen and AI infrastructure narrative providing support.
Europe: Stoxx 600 at 632 +1.57%. Rallied on ECB rate hike providing policy clarity. FTSE 100 at 10,304.
China: Shanghai Composite at 3,987 −0.16%. Slipped below the 4,000 psychological level. US-China tariff framework (30% stays, higher duties paused 60 days) failing to inspire confidence.
Emerging: MSCI EM ETF at 64.66 −1.76%. Proposed 10-12.5% tariffs on 60 economies over forced labor adding a fresh overhang ahead of July 7 hearings.
Bottom Line
  • Japan's Nikkei up 27% year-to-date and holding near records while most other markets struggle tells you the weak-yen, AI-buildout narrative is intact. But semiconductor exposure makes it vulnerable if the global tech correction deepens further.
  • China breaking below 4,000 despite the US-China tariff framework is a negative signal. The market wanted de-escalation and got a pause at best. Until domestic demand recovers or stimulus scales up, China remains a drag on global growth expectations.
  • The ECB hiking to 2.25% while the Fed holds at 3.50-3.75% creates a widening rate differential that supports the dollar and pressures emerging-market currencies. Combined with new tariff proposals on 60 economies, the EM outlook is getting more complicated.
