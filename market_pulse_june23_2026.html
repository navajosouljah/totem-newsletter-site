Weekly Briefing • Monday, June 23, 2026

Gas Falls Below $4 as the Iran Deal Delivers. Japan Hits Record Highs. A Chip Selloff Tests Markets. The Savings Rate Drops to 2.6%.

The US-Iran peace framework is doing what it was designed to do - oil dropped to $73 and gas fell to $3.93, saving families $12-15 a week at the pump. Japan's Nikkei hit fresh all-time highs on surging exports. Europe touched records. But a sharp chip selloff out of South Korea rattled tech stocks Monday, and the American savings rate fell to 2.6% - a number that deserves attention. Wednesday's PCE inflation reading is the most important data point of the month.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • Gas prices fall below $4 as the Iran deal delivers real consumer relief. The 60-day framework reopened the Strait of Hormuz and Iran shipped 30+ million barrels last week. WTI dropped to $73, Brent to $77. Goldman Sachs cut its year-end forecast to $80. Families are saving $12-15 a week at the pump compared to last month.
  • Japan hits fresh all-time highs; Europe touches records. The Nikkei reached 71,661 on surging exports (+17% YoY). Euro Stoxx 50 touched 6,320 before pulling back. Cheaper oil is lifting manufacturing economies worldwide. China's CSI 300 quietly hit its highest level since December 2021.
  • A sharp chip selloff in South Korea rattled global tech. The KOSPI fell 9.99% after regulators flagged the AI rally as overheated. US semis followed - Micron sank 11.4%, Nvidia dropped 3.5%. Micron reports earnings Wednesday, which will test whether this is a healthy reset or something deeper.
  • The American savings rate dropped to 2.6%. Down from 4.0% in Q1 and 6.2% in early 2024. Households are spending nearly everything they earn. Employment remains solid, but the cushion is thin. A story worth watching alongside falling gas prices, which provide the most direct relief.

The Quick Read

The Iran deal is delivering exactly what consumers need most right now - cheaper energy. Gas fell to $3.93 nationally, down $0.62 from the May peak, saving families $12-15 a week at the pump. WTI crude dropped to $73, its lowest since March. Japan's Nikkei hit fresh all-time highs. Europe touched records. Meanwhile, a chip selloff out of South Korea rattled tech stocks, the S&P 500 pulled back to 7,403, and the American savings rate dropped to 2.6%. Wednesday's PCE inflation report is the most important data point of the month.

Start with what is working. Oil at $73 is down 20%+ from its April peak above $91 because the Iran framework is doing exactly what it was designed to do - bringing supply back to the market and deflating the geopolitical risk premium. Iran shipped 30+ million barrels last week under its new license. Goldman Sachs cut its year-end Brent forecast to $80. For consumers, this translates directly: cheaper gas, lower heating costs ahead, and downward pressure on inflation. If oil holds in the low $70s through summer, gas could reach $3.75 by early July. That is real money back in people's pockets, and it flows into inflation data within 4-6 weeks - making Wednesday's PCE reading a test of whether the hawkish Fed stance still has legs.

Globally, the picture is stronger than most Americans realize. Japan is having its best year in decades - the Nikkei up 32% YTD on export growth of 17%, driven by a weak yen and AI-related semiconductor demand. Europe touched record highs on the Euro Stoxx as cheaper energy lifts manufacturing margins. China's CSI 300 quietly reached its highest level since December 2021, up 30% in twelve months. Emerging markets are near 52-week highs. For American savers, CD rates at 4.10-4.30% are delivering positive real returns above inflation for the first time in over a decade. These are tangible, measurable tailwinds.

Now the challenges. South Korea's KOSPI fell 9.99% on Monday after regulators flagged the AI chip rally as overheated. The KOSPI had surged 28% since April, and when the correction came, it came all at once - circuit breakers tripped twice, SK Hynix and Samsung lost 12%, and US semiconductor names followed. Micron reports earnings Wednesday, which will determine whether this was a healthy valuation reset or something that spills into broader markets. The VIX spiked 13% to 19.60, which is elevated but still below the 20 level that signals real fear.

At home, the savings rate dropping to 2.6% is the consumer number that deserves the most attention. Down from 4.0% in Q1 and 6.2% in early 2024, it means Americans are spending nearly everything they earn. Credit card debt at $1.25 trillion with a 21% APR is where the gap between income and spending lands. Subprime auto delinquencies at 6.90% just hit a 32-year high, and 47% of buy-now-pay-later users are paying late. This is not a recession signal - employment remains solid at 4.3% - but it is a vulnerability. The good news is that falling gas prices are the most direct relief available, and they are arriving at exactly the right time.

Stock Market

South Korea's crash drove a global risk-off session. Tech and semis led losses. The Dow held up on rotation into industrials and value.

S&P 500: 7,403 −0.93% on the day
Dow Jones: 51,820 +0.21% on the day
Nasdaq: 25,789 −1.44% on the day
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,992 −0.42% on the day
Fear gauge (VIX): 19.60 +13.4% on the day
US Dollar Index: 101.36 13-month high
Bottom Line
  • The VIX spiking from 16.40 to 19.60 in a single session is the sharpest one-day move in weeks and tells you this is more than routine profit-taking. The KOSPI crashing 10% overnight while US semis fell 6-11% signals genuine repositioning, not just noise. But the VIX is still under 20 - fear is elevated, not panicked. A VIX above 25 would signal a broader contagion trade. We are not there.
  • The Dow outperforming the Nasdaq by 1.65 percentage points on the day is a clear rotation signal. Money is leaving high-multiple tech and moving into industrials, value, and defensives. This is consistent with the hawkish Fed posture - rate-sensitive growth stocks get punished first. If Micron earnings Wednesday disappoint, the rotation accelerates.
  • The dollar at a 13-month high above 101 is a headwind for multinational earnings and commodity prices. A strong dollar makes US exports more expensive and compresses margins for companies that earn overseas. Gold and silver selling off sharply this week is partly a dollar-strength story. For consumers, a strong dollar means cheaper imports, which helps at the grocery store and gas pump.

Interest Rates

Yields pulled back Monday as risk-off flows pushed money into Treasuries. The hawkish dot plot set the tone last week, but today's flight to safety is bending the curve back.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held, 4th consecutive meeting)
2-year Treasury: 4.18% −5 bps from Friday
10-year Treasury: 4.48% −3 bps from Friday
30-year Treasury: 4.94% steady
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.47% −5 bps from prior week (Freddie Mac)
2s/10s spread: +30 bps normally sloped
Bottom Line
  • Yields fell Monday as the Korea crash pushed safe-haven buying into Treasuries. The 2-year dropping 5 bps tells you the market is backing off the rate-hike trade for the moment. If Wednesday's PCE comes in cool, the 2-year falls further and the hike narrative fades. If it comes in hot, the 2-year goes back above 4.25% and hike pricing intensifies.
  • Mortgages at 6.47% ticked lower last week despite the hawkish dot plot because falling oil prices are doing the Fed's work on inflation expectations. The housing market needs sub-6.25% to meaningfully unfreeze, but the direction is favorable. If WTI stays in the low $70s, energy disinflation feeds through to mortgage rates within 4-6 weeks.
  • For savers, the hawkish Fed remains a tailwind. CDs at 4.10-4.30% and the 2-year Treasury at 4.18% mean cash is earning a real positive return above inflation. The Fed's reluctance to cut guarantees these rates hold through summer. A 12-month CD at 4.30% today remains a straightforward opportunity.

Bottom Line
  • With the savings rate at 2.6%, most Americans are not saving at all - but those who can should be taking advantage of these rates while they last. A 4.30% CD beats inflation over the term and locks in yield regardless of what the Fed does next. The risk of waiting is that the best promotional rates get pulled, not that rates fall.
  • The 2-3 point gap between the national average CD (1.34-1.97%) and the best online options (4.30%) remains the single easiest financial move most people can make. On $50,000, that gap is $1,150+/year in foregone interest. Opening an online savings account takes 15 minutes and requires zero market knowledge.

Oil's structural decline continues. The Iran framework is bringing supply back to global markets. Gas below $4 nationally.

Brent crude: $77.50/bbl −2.2% from Friday; three-month low
National gas avg: $3.93/gal −$0.62 from $4.55 May peak
West Coast: $5.56/gal (CA highest)
Cheapest region: $3.32/gal (Indiana)
Bottom Line
  • Iran shipping 30+ million barrels in the past week under the new license is a tangible supply increase, not a promise. Goldman cutting its year-end Brent forecast to $80 reflects the consensus view that the geopolitical risk premium is structurally deflating. Every week the Hormuz stays open and Iranian oil flows, the floor price moves lower.
  • Gas at $3.93 is down $0.62 from the May 21 peak. A family filling up twice a week saves roughly $12-15/week compared to a month ago. If WTI holds in the low $70s through summer, gas drifts toward $3.75 by early July. This is the most direct form of middle-class relief available and it feeds into inflation data quickly.
  • The West Coast premium ($5.56 in CA vs. $3.32 in Indiana) remains extreme but trending down at both ends. State taxes and refinery structure create a permanent $2+ gap, but the direction is favorable nationwide. Gas below $4 nationally is a psychological milestone.

Commodities

A broad commodity selloff this week. Gold down 5%, silver down 12%, copper off 5%. Dollar strength and risk-off positioning are driving the rout.

Gold / oz: $4,136 −5.0% from last week's $4,353
Silver / oz: $62.11 −11.8% from last week's $70.39
Copper / lb: $6.15 −5.4% from last week's $6.50
Corn / bushel: $4.09 −1.2% (lowest since October 2025)
Soybeans / bushel: $11.16 −1.3%
Live cattle / cwt: $255.46 near record levels
Bottom Line
  • Gold dropping $217 in a week from $4,353 to $4,136 is the sharpest weekly decline since March. The dollar at a 13-month high is the primary driver - gold and the dollar trade inversely. But gold above $4,100 still reflects structural demand from central banks and institutions. This is a healthy correction after an extraordinary run, not a reversal. A soft PCE reading Wednesday could send it back above $4,200.
  • Silver's 12% weekly decline is violent but characteristic - silver overshoots in both directions because of its dual industrial-precious role. The hawkish Fed plus the Korea chip crash hit silver's industrial component while the strong dollar hit the precious component. Silver tends to recover faster than gold on good news.
  • Cattle at $255/cwt remain near all-time highs on the smallest US herd since 1951 (86.2 million head). This is a multi-year biological cycle - herds take 3-4 years to rebuild, keeping beef prices elevated through 2028-2029. Poultry and pork remain more affordable protein alternatives.

Crop Weather

Corn holding at 68% good-to-excellent. Winter wheat in serious trouble - production forecast down 27% YoY, lowest since 1965. July pollination is the next critical risk window.

Corn: 68% good-to-excellent (up 1 pt from first rating). 97% emerged, 5% silking. Slightly below analyst expectations of 70%. July pollination window approaching - the make-or-break period for yield.
Soybeans: 66% good-to-excellent (unchanged). 93% emerged, 9% blooming. Planting progress ahead of 5-year average.
Winter wheat: 14% good, 0% excellent; 57% poor to very poor. Production forecast 1,030M bushels - down 27% YoY and lowest since 1965/66. 63% of total US winter wheat is in drought areas, up from 15% a year ago. Harvest 11% complete.
Drought: D0-D1 drought spans Iowa, southern Minnesota, Wisconsin, Illinois. Patches of D2 severe drought in northern Minnesota. Forecasts favor above-normal precipitation through end of June, which should limit expansion.
Bottom Line
  • Winter wheat at 57% poor-to-very-poor with 63% of production in drought areas is a genuine supply problem. Production down 27% YoY to the lowest since 1965 will translate to higher flour and bread prices in Q3-Q4. This is already priced into futures, but consumers will feel it at the bakery aisle. The scale of the shortfall makes this a multi-quarter story, not a one-week data blip.
  • Corn at 68% good-to-excellent is adequate for now but July is the critical month. The El Nino probability remains elevated through January 2027, which historically dries out the Midwest during pollination. If corn conditions deteriorate below 60%, futures react sharply. Worth monitoring weekly.
  • The near-term forecast is constructive - above-normal precipitation through late June should limit drought expansion in the Corn Belt. But the structural drought (6th year in the West, intensifying in the Plains) is a slow-moving water-infrastructure challenge that governments are actively managing.

Real Estate

May existing-home sales improved 3.2%. Foreclosures continue climbing from pandemic lows. Completed foreclosures surged 45% YoY. The market is adjusting under stress, not collapsing.

Existing-home sales (May): 4.17M SAAR +3.2% MoM
Median home price (May): $429,300 +1.3% YoY (35th consecutive month of gains)
Housing starts (May): 1.177M SAAR −15.4% MoM
Foreclosure starts (Q1): 82,631 +20% YoY
Total foreclosure filings (Q1): 118,727 +26% YoY
Completed foreclosures (Q1): 14,020 +45% YoY
Foreclosure starts (Apr): 28,414 +12% YoY
850,000+ borrowers 90+ days past due +104,000 from last year
Mortgage delinquency rate (Q1): 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: ~11.6% +126 bps YoY
% listings cutting price (May): 20.2%
Inventory: 1.48M units / ~4.5 months supply
30-year mortgage: 6.47%
Bottom Line
  • Completed foreclosures surging 45% YoY is the most alarming number in housing right now. Foreclosure starts rising 20% tells you filings are building; completions surging 45% tells you the pipeline is now converting into actual property seizures. At 14,020 completions in Q1, we are still well below crisis-era levels (300,000+ per quarter in 2009-2010), but the acceleration rate warrants attention.
  • FHA delinquency at 11.6% and rising 126 bps YoY is the canary in the coal mine. FHA borrowers are first-time buyers with lower down payments and thinner cushions - exactly the demographic most exposed to a savings rate at 2.6%. Part of the increase reflects a reporting change (expiration of pandemic-era relief reclassifying borrowers), but the trend is real underneath.
  • Pending sales have fallen for five consecutive weeks and new listings posted one of the sharpest weekly drops of 2026 in early June. This is a market freezing up as mortgage rates near 6.50% deter both buyers and sellers. If rates decline on softer PCE data, activity thaws. If rates hold or rise, the freeze deepens into late summer.
  • Housing starts falling 15.4% reflects builders pulling back rationally in response to soft demand. Fewer starts tighten supply 12-18 months out but prevent overbuilding. The median price holding at $429,300 with 4.5 months of inventory reflects a market slowly normalizing under pressure.

Household Finance

The savings rate collapsed to 2.6% in April - down from 4.0% in Q1 and 6.2% in early 2024. Americans are running on fumes. Subprime auto delinquencies hit a 32-year high.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion all-time high (Q1 2026)
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion −$25B QoQ seasonal dip; +5.9% YoY
Avg. credit card APR: 21.0% (all accounts); 23.79% on new offers
Avg. credit card balance (with balance): $7,886
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo record; 20.3% of financed purchases now $1,000+/mo
Subprime auto 60+ day delinquent: 6.90% 32-year high (Jan 2026, Fitch)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY; 62% have paid late at some point
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (April 2026) down from 4.0% in Q1; 6.2% in early 2024
Bottom Line
  • The savings rate plunging from 4.0% to 2.6% in a single month is the most important consumer data point we have tracked this year. Americans are spending virtually everything they earn. At 21% APR, anyone carrying the average $7,886 card balance is paying $1,656/year in interest alone. New card offers at 23.79% APR are even worse. The math is punishing and it is getting worse, not better.
  • Subprime auto delinquencies hitting 6.90% - a 32-year record - and 20.3% of new-car buyers now paying $1,000+/month tells you the auto market has stretched borrowers to their limit. These are real families choosing between car payments and groceries. The average amount financed ($43,899) is also a record. Dealers extended terms and pushed larger loans during 2021-2023, and those chickens are coming home to roost.
  • The BNPL data is striking: 47% paying late this year, up from 34% in 2024, and more than half of users say they couldn't make ends meet without it. BNPL has become a lifeline for stretched consumers, not a convenience. These delinquencies don't show up in traditional credit reports, which means the standard consumer credit metrics are understating real financial stress.

Crypto

Crypto sold off with the broader risk-off move. Bitcoin fell below $64,000. Solana led losses at -7%. Wednesday's PCE is the next catalyst.

Bitcoin (BTC): $63,170 −4.9% from last week's $66,449
Ethereum (ETH): $1,699 −5.8% from $1,803
Solana (SOL): $71.47 −7.0% from $76.85
XRP: $1.12 −1.8% from $1.14
Bottom Line
  • Crypto selling off alongside stocks on a risk-off day is exactly what you would expect. Bitcoin falling from $66,449 to $63,170 looks like it is testing the $60,000 support level. If that floor holds through the Korea contagion and Micron earnings, it is constructive. If it breaks, the next support is $55,000-57,000. The correlation between crypto and rate expectations remains the dominant trading dynamic.
  • Solana's 7% decline was the sharpest among the majors, consistent with its higher beta to risk appetite. SOL at $71.47 and ETH at $1,699 are both well below their cycle highs. XRP's relative resilience (-1.8%) reflects its more institutional holder base. Wednesday's PCE data is the next binary catalyst - soft reading lifts all risk assets, hot reading sends BTC toward $60,000.
  • The $1.1 billion in liquidations triggered by the broader selloff shows that leveraged positions in crypto remain elevated. Deleveraging events like this clear out speculative excess and typically set up healthier rallies. The institutional infrastructure continues to build while retail participation stays subdued.

Around the World

South Korea's crash dominated the global session. Japan pulled back from record highs. Europe retreated from its own records. China held steady on policy support.

South Korea: KOSPI crashed 9.99% - circuit breakers triggered twice. SK Hynix −12%, Samsung −12%. The worst single session since the August 2024 yen-carry unwind. Regulatory signals that the chip rally was overheated.
Japan: Nikkei 71,661 - pulled back −0.96% Monday after hitting fresh all-time highs last week. Up ~32% YTD. Exports +17% YoY. Remains the standout global equity market of 2026.
Europe: Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,236 - pulled back −1.25% Monday after touching record 6,320 mid-week. ECB holding at 2.25%. Benefiting from cheaper energy.
China: Shanghai Composite 4,154 - flat. CSI 300 at 5,031, highest since December 2021, +30.3% over 12 months. Government support keeping index above 4,000.
Emerging: MSCI EM (EEM) at $71.18 - near 52-week high ($71.57). Watching July 24 tariff expiration on 60 economies and July 1 USMCA review date.
Bottom Line
  • The Korea crash is a valuation correction, not a fundamental crisis. The KOSPI had rallied 28% since April on an AI chip frenzy that pushed price-to-earnings ratios to levels Seoul regulators publicly flagged as excessive. When the correction came, it came all at once because leveraged retail positions amplified the move. This is similar to the August 2024 yen-carry unwind - violent, scary, and largely self-correcting once positions are cleaned out.
  • Japan at 71,661 with exports surging 17% remains the strongest macro story in global markets. The pullback on Monday was modest (-0.96%) relative to Korea's crash, which shows the Nikkei has fundamental support from earnings and currency dynamics. The weak yen keeps Japanese goods competitive globally.
  • China's CSI 300 quietly reaching its highest level since December 2021 (+30% over 12 months) is an underreported story. Government support is working to keep the floor in place, and the July 24 tariff expiration is a potential catalyst if the temporary 10% tariffs on 60 economies get reduced or expire.
