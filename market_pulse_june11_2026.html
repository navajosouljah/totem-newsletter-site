Daily Briefing • June 11, 2026

Inflation Hits Three-Year High at 4.2%. Oil Spikes on Fresh Iran Strikes. Rate Hike Odds Surge to 50%.

May CPI shocked markets at 4.2% - the hottest reading since April 2023 - driven by energy costs up 23.5% and shelter up 3.4%. Fresh US military strikes on Iran reversed oil's pullback, pushing WTI back above $91. The market is now pricing a coin-flip chance of a rate hike in 2026 for the first time.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • May CPI surges to 4.2% - a three-year high: Headline inflation blew past expectations, driven by energy (+23.5% YoY) and shelter (+3.4% YoY). Core CPI ticked up to 2.9%. Rate-cut bets collapsed overnight and rate-hike odds jumped to roughly 50% for the first time since 2023.
  • Fresh US strikes on Iran spike oil, kill deal hopes: Trump announced new military strikes on Iranian targets with more planned for Wednesday evening. WTI jumped to $91.56 and Brent to $95.15 - erasing the pullback from deal optimism just 24 hours earlier.
  • SpaceX prices largest IPO in history at $135/share: The $1.77 trillion valuation makes SpaceX the most valuable company to ever go public. Trading begins Thursday on Nasdaq under ticker SPCX. OpenAI also filed confidentially for an IPO this week.
  • Oracle AI cloud backlog hits $638 billion: Q4 revenue surged 21% to a record $19.2 billion with cloud infrastructure revenue nearly doubling (+93% YoY). Capital spending hit $55.7 billion for the fiscal year, raising profitability questions even as the AI buildout accelerates.

The Quick Read

Inflation just made everything harder. CPI at 4.2% - a three-year high - killed rate-cut hopes and pushed rate-hike odds to a coin flip for the first time since 2023. Oil reversed sharply higher on fresh US strikes on Iran, erasing yesterday's deal-driven pullback. Stocks are down 4-7% from last week's records with the VIX at its highest since April. Housing distress continues to accelerate - foreclosure starts up 13% YoY, FHA delinquencies at 11.88%, and the median home price hit a record $429,300 while 20% of sellers are cutting asking prices. Consumer stress is unchanged at severe levels - $18.8 trillion in debt, a 2.6% savings rate, and 47% of BNPL users paying late.

The CPI report changes the macro calculus. At 4.2%, inflation is reaccelerating - not decelerating. Energy costs up 23.5% year-over-year are the primary driver, but shelter at +3.4% means the stickiest component is not cooperating either. The market has gone from pricing two rate cuts in 2026 to pricing a potential hike. This is a dramatic shift in just two weeks and explains why the Nasdaq is down nearly 7% from its early-June record.

The Iran situation adds fuel - literally. Yesterday's narrative was "Iran deal in two or three days, oil easing, gold crashing." Today's narrative is "fresh military strikes, oil spiking, deal dead." WTI jumped from $89 to $91.56 overnight, and Brent pushed back above $95. If strikes continue, oil goes higher, which feeds directly back into CPI. It is a reflexive loop: higher oil means higher inflation means fewer rate cuts means more market pain.

Bonds are in a difficult spot. The 30-year Treasury is at 5.01% and the 30-year mortgage jumped to 6.55%. The constructive case for bonds assumed the new Fed chair would pursue lower rates and easier credit. But 4.2% CPI ties the Fed's hands - they cannot cut into accelerating inflation without destroying credibility. The June 16-17 FOMC meeting and the new dot plot will be the most important in years.

Gold's continued weakness despite fresh Iran strikes is the most telling signal. At $4,101, it has barely bounced from yesterday's crash despite escalating geopolitical risk. The message: rate-hike fears now outweigh safe-haven demand. Higher real rates are kryptonite for gold, and the market is recalculating that scenario in real time. Crypto is stabilizing near its lows - Bitcoin at $62,574, Ethereum at $1,642 - as the worst week since FTX digests.

Stock Market

CPI shock extends the selloff. Nasdaq down nearly 7% in a week as rate-hike fears replace rate-cut hopes.

S&P 500: 7,300 −4.0% wk
Dow Jones: 49,670 −2.8% wk
Nasdaq: 25,235 −6.8% wk
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,815 −1.1% wk
Fear gauge (VIX): 22.22 +45% wk (highest since April)
Bottom Line
  • The Nasdaq down 6.8% in a week and the VIX at 22 - its highest since April - is the correction we have been warning about. CPI at 4.2% provided the catalyst for a rally that was already stretched and narrowly led by a handful of AI names.
  • Small caps continued to underperform with the Russell down 1.1% on the day despite large-cap partial recovery. Rate-hike fears pressure all equity classes, not just tech.
  • SpaceX pricing at $1.77 trillion is remarkable but also a peak-liquidity signal. The largest IPO in history pricing into a 4.2% CPI print and a rising VIX is a market running on momentum, not fundamentals.

Interest Rates

CPI at 4.2% reshuffles the rate outlook. The 30-year mortgage jumped to 6.55% and the dot plot next week is now the most important in years.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (effective 3.62%)
2-year Treasury: 4.13%
10-year Treasury: 4.50%
30-year Treasury: 5.01%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.55% +7 bps day
Bottom Line
  • The 30-year mortgage at 6.55% - up 7 bps in a single day - is a direct consequence of the CPI shock. Anyone who was waiting for rates to fall before buying just got the opposite signal.
  • The Fed will hold next week at 3.50-3.75%, but the dot plot is now the most important communication tool. If dots shift toward fewer cuts or a possible hike, expect another wave of selling in rate-sensitive assets.
  • With CPI at 4.2% and the 30-year Treasury above 5%, the bond market is telling the Fed that inflation is not under control. The constructive case for bonds requires the Fed to prioritize growth over inflation - that just got much harder.

Savings & CD Rates

With inflation at 4.2%, even the best savings rates barely keep pace. Most savers are losing purchasing power.

National avg 1-yr CD: 1.98% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.10-4.30% APY (Popular Direct, E*TRADE)
Best credit union CD: 5.00% APY (A+ Federal Credit Union)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo)
Bottom Line
  • With inflation at 4.2% and the best savings rates at 4.10-5.00%, real returns on cash are barely positive to negative. The national average of 1.98% means most savers are losing purchasing power rapidly.
  • If the Fed signals a hike, CD rates could actually rise. For now, locking in 4%+ on a 1-year CD is still reasonable, but the calculus is shifting toward shorter durations given rate uncertainty.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil reverses sharply higher on fresh Iran strikes. Yesterday's deal optimism is gone. Energy drove 23.5% of the CPI surge.

WTI crude: $91.56/bbl +2.9% day
Brent crude: $95.15/bbl +1.3% day
National gas avg: $4.12/gal −$0.16 wk
West Coast: $5.84/gal
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.55/gal
Bottom Line
  • Oil's whipsaw - down $3 on Iran deal hopes Monday, up $2.56 on strikes Wednesday - shows how binary the geopolitical situation is. Until the Strait of Hormuz is resolved, energy markets will trade on headlines, not fundamentals.
  • Energy drove 23.5% of the CPI increase. As long as oil stays above $90, inflation cannot return to target. The Iran situation is now directly feeding the inflation problem in a reflexive loop.
  • Gas at $4.12 is slightly lower than last week but the West Coast at $5.84 is approaching summer 2022 levels. The $2.29 spread between the West Coast and Gulf Coast ($3.55) remains historically extreme.

Corn / bushel: $4.19 −5.6% wk
Soybeans / bushel: $11.22 −5.0% wk

Bottom Line
  • Gold at $4,101 - barely bouncing despite fresh military strikes on Iran - tells you rate-hike fears now outweigh geopolitical safe-haven demand. This is a meaningful regime shift for the metal. Higher real rates are kryptonite for gold.
  • Silver holding at $64.52 after last week's 14% plunge suggests the industrial demand story (solar, AI data centers) is providing a floor that gold does not have.
  • Cattle at $241.50/cwt - near record highs - means beef prices are not coming down. Combined with wheat conditions at 26% good-to-excellent, food inflation is a building concern that CPI has not yet fully reflected.

Crop Weather

Winter wheat in crisis. Drought expanding into the Upper Midwest. Corn looks fine for now but summer heat is the risk.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent, 97% planted, 86% emerged. Strong start but summer heat has not arrived.
Winter wheat: Only 26% good-to-excellent. Production forecast down 25% from last year to 1.05 billion bushels.
Drought: 49% of the US in drought; 58% of the Lower 48. Extreme/exceptional drought entrenched in the Plains and West.
New risk: Drought expanding into Upper Midwest and Great Lakes. Sierra Nevada snowpack below 20% of normal.
Bottom Line
  • Winter wheat production forecast down 25% from last year means higher flour and bread prices are locked in for the back half of 2026. This will show up in CPI later this year.
  • Corn at 67% good-to-excellent looks fine but 49% of the country is in drought and summer heat has not started. The corn crop's fate will be determined in July and August.
  • Drought expanding into the Upper Midwest and Great Lakes is a new development worth watching. Combined with Sierra Nevada snowpack below 20% of normal, Western irrigation is in crisis.

Real Estate

Foreclosures still accelerating. FHA delinquencies approaching 12%. Median price hits a record while sellers keep cutting.

Foreclosure starts May (vs. last yr): 27,304 +13% YoY
Foreclosure starts Q1 (vs. last yr): +20% YoY
Bank repossessions / REOs May (vs. last yr): 4,092 +6% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.88% +126 bps YoY
Existing-home sales (annual pace): 4.17M +3.2% MoM
Median home price: $429,300 record
% listings cutting price: ~20% (Redfin, May)
Inventory: 1.55M homes, 4.5 months of supply
30-year mortgage: 6.55%
Bottom Line
  • FHA delinquencies at 11.88% - up 126 bps year-over-year and the highest since Q4 2018 - are the clearest distress signal. Pandemic-era relief options fully expired in September 2025 and the pipeline to foreclosure is accelerating.
  • The median home price hitting a record $429,300 while 20% of sellers cut asking prices is a split market. Headline prices are propped up by low inventory while individual sellers are struggling to find buyers at current levels.
  • Mortgage rates jumping to 6.55% on the CPI shock makes affordability worse overnight. The "wait for lower rates" strategy that sidelined millions of buyers just got pushed further out.
  • Inventory at 4.5 months of supply is the highest since 2020 and moving toward a balanced market. But normal is 5-6 months, and distressed properties (REOs up 6% YoY) are becoming a growing share of that supply.

Household Finance

CPI at 4.2% makes everything worse. Inflation erodes the 2.6% savings rate even further while debt and delinquencies stay at record levels.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion; avg APR ~21.5%
Avg. new-car payment: ~$770/mo; avg financed: $43,899 record
Subprime auto 60+ days late: 6.80% (near 32-year high)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (lowest since 2008)
Bottom Line
  • The CPI report makes consumer stress objectively worse. Inflation at 4.2% erodes the 2.6% savings rate even further - real savings are deeply negative. Every prior reading this low preceded a downturn.
  • If the Fed hikes instead of cuts, consumer stress gets materially worse. Higher rates flow directly into credit card APRs (already 21.5% on $1.25 trillion), auto loan costs, and housing affordability.
  • 47% of BNPL users paying late and 25% now using BNPL for groceries. When people need installment plans for food, that is not a healthy consumer economy - and 4.2% inflation just made groceries even more expensive.
  • Subprime auto delinquencies at 6.80% - near the 32-year high set in January - show no signs of improving. The average amount financed for a new car hit a record $43,899.

  • Crypto needs a macro catalyst to recover - a dovish Fed surprise or a geopolitical de-escalation. Neither is likely in the near term with CPI at 4.2% and Iran strikes escalating.
  • Solana at $65 and XRP at $1.11 are at levels that would have been unthinkable two weeks ago. The institutional ETF outflows driving the selloff may slow, but retail confidence is broken.

Around the World

Japan holding up. Europe soft on ECB hike and tariff fears. China slips below the 4,000 psychological level.

Japan: Nikkei at 64,217 +0.06%. Weak yen and domestic AI investment narrative providing support. Up 31.8% year-to-date - leading all major global indices.
Europe: Euro Stoxx 50 at 5,954 −0.97%. Caught between ECB rate hike, hot US CPI data, and tariff uncertainty. DAX at 24,223.
China: Shanghai Composite at 3,987 −0.16%. Slipped below the 4,000 psychological level. US-China tariff deal (30% stays, higher duties paused 60 days) failed to generate enthusiasm.
Emerging: MSCI EM at 1,661 −0.33%. Tariff proposals targeting 60 economies over forced labor add a fresh overhang.
Bottom Line
  • Japan's Nikkei holding near 64,000 despite the global selloff reflects its unique position - a weak yen and AI infrastructure buildout are providing support that other markets lack. But semiconductor exposure makes it vulnerable if the tech correction deepens.
  • China slipping below 4,000 despite the US-China tariff framework (30% stays, higher duties frozen 60 days) is a negative signal. The market was hoping for de-escalation and got a pause at best.
  • The proposed 10-12.5% tariffs on 60 economies over forced labor - with hearings starting July 7 - is a new headwind that is barely priced into emerging markets.
