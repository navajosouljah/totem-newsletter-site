Thursday Briefing • Thursday, August 20, 2026

Treasury Doubles Long-Bond Buybacks to $4 Billion - 30-Year Falls from 5.33% to 5.19%. Bitcoin $71,751 (+11.45%) and Ethereum $2,280 (+18.94%) on the Summer's Biggest ETF Inflow Day. Walmart Q2 Reports Before the Bell.

The Fed is on hold with three dissenters pushing to hike. Treasury is easing the long end anyway - and every risk asset on the board followed the yield down.

The Quick Read

Treasury will double its long-bond buybacks to $4 billion per operation, and the long end responded immediately. The program runs September 9 through November 4, targeting the 10-to-30-year sector where demand has thinned most since June. The 30-year fell from Tuesday's 5.33% peak, a 19-year high, to roughly 5.19%; the 10-year followed to approximately 4.67%. That was enough to snap the equity market's three-day losing streak, with the S&P 500 closing at 7,707.98, the Dow at 53,463.05, and the Nasdaq at 26,331.09, each up 0.2%.

The FOMC minutes released the same afternoon named the three July dissenters: Logan of Dallas, Hammack of Cleveland, and Kashkari of Minneapolis, each of whom voted for an immediate 25 basis point hike against Warsh's majority hold at 3.50-3.75%. The minutes predate the housing starts crash and last week's softer data, and September hike odds finished the session where they started, near 31%.

The yield relief detonated the crypto complex overnight. Bitcoin is at $71,751, up 11.45%, with spot ETFs drawing $517 million in a single session; Ethereum is at $2,280, up 18.94%, on $189 million of inflows; XRP is at $1.15. Gold recovered to $4,495.94 from Wednesday's $4,340. The mechanism is the same in every case: a lower long-end yield cuts the opportunity cost of holding assets that pay no coupon. Walmart reports before the bell and the Freddie Mac PMMS lands at noon ET.

How Thursday Opens

  • Bessent's Treasury doubled long-bond buybacks to $4 billion per operation for the 10-to-30-year sector - 30-year yield fell from 5.33% (19-year high) to approximately 5.19% - providing the rate relief that snapped the equity market's three-day losing streak and detonated Thursday's crypto rally. The announcement is the most significant US Treasury market-support action since the buyback program was introduced. Targeting the 10-to-30-year sector specifically - where demand has thinned most acutely since late June as Brent's Hormuz risk premium pushed inflation expectations higher - Bessent's doubling of operations from $2 billion to $4 billion signals that Treasury will actively defend the long end rather than allow yields to find their own equilibrium at the market's demand level. The timing is significant: the announcement came just after the FOMC minutes release, combining two rate-market data points that individually would have been modest catalysts but together were sufficient to break the 5.33% ceiling and close the 30-year near 5.19%. The practical effect on the housing market - where 30-year mortgage rates track the 10-year Treasury - is that Thursday's Freddie Mac PMMS may show the first downward move in the mortgage rate since the Hormuz crisis began, potentially from last week's 6.67% toward 6.60-6.63% if the 10-year's proportional fall from approximately 4.70% to approximately 4.55% is captured in the survey period. Effective September 9 through November 4, the $4 billion per operation buyback schedule gives the rate market a near-term anchor that limits the 30-year's upside even if August CPI or August jobs come in hotter than expected.
  • Bitcoin surged to $71,751 (+11.45%) and Ethereum advanced to $2,280 (+18.94%) on Thursday morning as spot Bitcoin ETFs drew $517 million and Ethereum ETFs attracted $189 million - both the largest single-day inflows for each asset in months - with XRP advancing to $1.15 (+14.40%) in the summer's most powerful single-session crypto rally. Bitcoin's move from approximately $64,234 Wednesday morning to $71,751 Thursday morning is a $7,517 single-day gain - one of the largest dollar-value single-day advances in BTC since the spot ETF launch period. The ETF flow data makes the mechanism explicit: $517 million in spot Bitcoin ETF single-day net inflows (the largest since mid-June) represents institutional re-entry at a scale that cannot be explained by retail buying alone; IBIT (BlackRock) almost certainly leads the flow, given its dominance of prior high-flow sessions. Ethereum's $189 million single-day ETF inflow is notable because ETH's prior ETF flow record was substantially lower - the $189 million figure suggests that institutions are buying Ethereum's yield relief in parallel with Bitcoin rather than solely rotating into BTC. XRP's advance to $1.15 from $1.01 on Wednesday resolves the whale-transaction spike that the August 19 briefing flagged as ambiguous between accumulation and distribution - the price action confirms the spike was accumulation. Gold's response to the yield relief - $4,495.94 on Thursday morning, recovering sharply from $4,340 on Wednesday morning - confirms that the real-yield mechanism is operating symmetrically: just as the 30-year surging to 5.33% crushed gold on Wednesday, the 30-year falling to 5.19% lifted gold back toward its prior range.
  • FOMC minutes from the July 29 meeting confirmed three dissenters by name - Dallas Fed President Logan, Cleveland Fed President Hammack, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari - who each voted for immediate +25bp hike; Warsh's majority held at 3.50-3.75%; September hike odds remain near 31%. The naming of the three dissenters is the minutes' most market-relevant disclosure, confirming the regional Fed presidents who have staked their public position on the case for immediate action. Logan (Dallas), Hammack (Cleveland), and Kashkari (Minneapolis) bring different regional economic perspectives to the hawkish argument: Logan oversees a district with significant energy-sector exposure and is most directly familiar with the Hormuz premium's impact on the regional economy; Hammack's Cleveland district is a manufacturing bellwether where cost-of-goods-sold inflation from energy and logistics is most directly visible; Kashkari has historically been among the most explicitly hawkish of the regional presidents on inflation persistence and sequential evidence. The minutes' framing of the majority's hold - emphasizing demand-destruction risks from housing, consumer, and labor market data - was received by Wednesday's market as slightly dovish relative to expectations, because the housing-starts crash (-12.4% MoM) and July retail sales decline (-0.6% MoM) that have since been reported confirm the majority's concern. September odds at 31% are essentially unchanged from Tuesday's reading, suggesting that neither the minutes' hawkish names nor the dovish framing materially shifted the market's probability assessment beyond the range established by prior communications. Warsh's August 28 Jackson Hole keynote remains the next major Fed communication event with the potential to shift September odds meaningfully.

Walmart enters its Q2 FY2027 earnings reporting under the most complex consumer-backdrop of any quarterly report this year. Walmart's structural advantage - as a low-price-destination that historically gains market share when lower-income consumers trade down - means a revenue beat is possible even in a deteriorating consumer environment. The Q1 momentum reported in Q1 FY2027 (26% e-commerce growth, 37% advertising revenue expansion) provides a favorable baseline. But the market is specifically watching three data points in the Q2 report: US comparable store sales (consensus roughly +4-5%), Q3 guidance language on consumer softening, and whether the advertising and e-commerce growth rates are decelerating. A Walmart revenue beat with maintained or raised Q3 guidance would be the most constructive possible consumer-confidence signal in Thursday's market environment.
