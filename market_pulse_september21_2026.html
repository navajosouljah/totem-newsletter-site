CD Window Holds at 4.50% APY. Oil Below $100 for First Time.

The Quick Read

WTI crude slipped below $100 per barrel Monday morning for the first time since the Saudi East-West pipeline was disrupted on September 11 - the supply-risk premium that held oil above $100 for 10 days is now repricing. The trigger: Trump signaled willingness to meet Iran's president on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly in New York this week - not a deal, not a ceasefire, just an opening of the diplomatic track. The mechanism: oil's post-disruption bid rested on a no-diplomatic-path assumption, and any credible signal of engagement reprices that assumption lower before a barrel of supply actually changes. Houthi drone attacks on Saudi Arabia on Saturday did not reverse Monday's direction.

Two events shape the rest of the week. Xi Jinping arrives in Washington around Wednesday September 23 for a bilateral summit with Trump on Thursday September 24 - their first face-to-face on American soil since 2015, with a 90-day tariff truce clock running down. PCE on Friday September 25 is the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation measure and the primary gate on November hike odds (currently 60-65%): core PCE at or above +0.3% month over month and November becomes near-certain; below +0.2% softens the path. Before both: USDA crop progress at 3:00 PM ET today delivers the first full harvest-week read - corn expected to advance from 8% to 13-15% harvested.

Two slow pressures are building. Savers hold the best 1-year CD window in two years at 4.50% APY - Day 5 of a 7-10 day reprice cycle that closes approximately September 26-28, with a second ceiling of 4.60-4.75% still live if November delivers. On the other side: the 10-year Treasury crossed 5.01% on Friday - the first close above 5% since the September 16 hike - and holds above 5% this morning, meaning the September 24 PMMS will likely cross 7% for the first time since October 2023.
