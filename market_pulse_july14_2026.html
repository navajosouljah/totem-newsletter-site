Weekly Briefing • Monday, July 14, 2026

CPI Drops to 3.5% - Best Print in a Year. Iran Pushes Oil Past $79. The Good Number Meets a Bad Setup.

June inflation came in at 3.5% - well below the 3.8% Wall Street expected and the sharpest one-month decline since early 2025. Falling energy prices did the heavy lifting, with gasoline down 10% in June. But those savings are already being reversed: the U.S. struck Iranian targets for a fourth straight night, the IRGC disabled two supertankers, and Brent crude climbed to $85. The number the Fed wanted arrived just as the conditions that produced it disappeared.

This Week in the News

  • CPI dropped to 3.5% - the best inflation print in over a year. June consumer prices fell 0.4% month-over-month, driven by a 10% decline in gasoline and 6% drop in the broader energy category. Core inflation fell to 2.6%, below the 2.9% consensus. Shelter rose just 0.1%.
  • Iran strikes entered a fourth consecutive night. The U.S. hit Abadan - home to the oldest oil refinery in the Middle East - and the port city of Mahshahr. Iran's IRGC disabled two supertankers in the Strait of Hormuz. The June 17 ceasefire memorandum is dead. Brent climbed to $85.
  • IBM fell 22% on disappointing preliminary results. The Dow component's worst single-day drop in years dragged the index flat on what should have been a strong CPI-reaction day. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq rose on the inflation data regardless.
  • Existing home sales fell 2.4% in June to a 4.09 million pace. The median price hit a record $440,600. Inventory edged to 4.6 months. Higher rates and elevated prices continue to freeze buyer activity despite modestly improving supply.

The Quick Read

The June CPI at 3.5% is the number the market has been waiting for - and it arrived on the same day it started to expire. Gasoline fell 10% in June, dragging headline inflation from 4.2% to 3.5% in a single month. Core hit 2.6%. Shelter - the stickiest component - rose just 0.1%. On paper, this is the clearest path to a rate cut since the cycle began. But the conditions that produced the print are already gone. Brent crude is at $85 today, up from $72 when the June CPI data was collected. The U.S. is striking Iranian coastal targets for the fourth straight night, Iran has disabled supertankers in Hormuz, and war risk premiums on shipping are climbing. The energy deflation that made June's number possible is reversing in real time.

The stock market tried to celebrate anyway. The S&P 500 rose 0.4% to 7,544, and the Nasdaq gained 0.9% on renewed rate-cut hopes. But the Dow went nowhere - IBM's 22% collapse on weak earnings absorbed the CPI tailwind entirely. The VIX fell to 16.50, which reads as complacency given the Hormuz situation. Gold held flat near $4,070; Bitcoin edged up to $62,600. The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.56%, essentially unchanged, because the bond market is doing the same math: a great backward-looking number meets a deteriorating forward-looking energy picture.

Housing keeps deteriorating. Existing home sales fell 2.4% in June to a 4.09 million annualized pace even as the median price hit a record $440,600. Foreclosure starts are running 12-13% above year-ago levels. FHA delinquency remains near 12%. The consumer below the median is stretched: $18.8 trillion in total household debt, 47% of BNPL users paying late, subprime auto delinquency at a 32-year high. The savings rate at 3.0% is holding but fragile - if gas prices climb toward $4.00 on the Iran escalation, that margin disappears.

Stock Market

The CPI beat lifted tech and rate-sensitive names. IBM's collapse kept the Dow flat. The market is pricing in rate relief while ignoring the oil spike happening in real time.

S&P 500: 7,544 +0.4%
Dow Jones: ~52,900 flat (IBM −22% offset CPI gains)
Nasdaq: 26,107 +0.9% (tech and chips led)
Russell 2000 (small caps): ~3,004 +0.4%
Fear gauge (VIX): 16.50 −3.9%
Bottom Line
  • The market's selective hearing is on full display. The S&P rallied on a backward-looking CPI print while Brent crude rose to $85 on the same day. If oil stays above $80 through mid-July, the July CPI (reported in August) will not repeat this performance. The VIX at 16.50 with an active shooting war in the Strait of Hormuz is the kind of complacency that precedes sharp corrections - not because it must, but because it prices in nothing going wrong from here.
  • IBM falling 22% on preliminary earnings is a reminder that index-level calm can mask single-stock violence. The Dow absorbed a ~200-point drag from IBM alone. If Q2 earnings season delivers more misses at large-cap industrials, the narrow leadership problem (mega-cap tech carrying everything) becomes more acute.

Interest Rates

The CPI beat revived rate-cut expectations. Traders cut the odds of a July hike from 35% to 15%. But the bond market barely moved - it sees the oil problem coming.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held since December 2025)
2-year Treasury: 4.21% +2 bps from last week
10-year Treasury: 4.56% +1 bp from last week
30-year Treasury: 5.06% holding above 5%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.49% −1 bp from last week's 6.50%
Bottom Line
  • The 10-year barely moved on a CPI beat that would normally send it down 10+ basis points. That tells you the bond market is looking through the June data and pricing in what oil at $85 means for July and August inflation. A 3.5% CPI is excellent; a 3.5% CPI followed by a $15 crude spike is a one-month anomaly, not a trend.
  • The 30-year holding above 5% for two straight weeks is the market's way of saying the structural inflation story is not resolved. The FOMC meets July 28-29. Today's print makes a hike unlikely at that meeting, but it does not make a cut likely either. The Fed is stuck until the oil picture clarifies - and right now, Hormuz is in control of that timeline.

Best CD rates ticking up slightly as rate-cut expectations fade. The CPI print helps savers by keeping the Fed on hold longer.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.99% APY

Best credit union CD: 5.00% APY (A+ Federal Credit Union, 12-month)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • The Iran escalation is paradoxically extending the window for savers. If oil keeps the next CPI elevated, the Fed stays on hold and these 4-5% CD rates persist. Newtek Bank at 4.30% for a 12-month CD and A+ Federal Credit Union at 5.00% both remain well above the 3.5% headline CPI - meaning savers are earning real positive returns for the first time in this cycle.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil surging on Iran strikes. The June energy deflation that drove CPI to 3.5% is being reversed this week. Gas prices beginning to catch up at the pump.

WTI crude: $79.34/bbl +6.9% from last week's $74.20
Brent crude: $84.73/bbl +12.2% from last week's $75.53
National gas avg: $3.86/gal +$0.06 from last week
West Coast: $4.83/gal −$0.35 from last week's $5.18
California: $5.20/gal steady
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): $3.42/gal
Bottom Line
  • Brent at $85 today versus $72 when the June CPI data was collected tells the whole story. The 10% gasoline decline that drove CPI from 4.2% to 3.5% is a snapshot of a month that no longer exists. If crude holds above $80 through late July, the pump catches up within two weeks and the August CPI report (covering July) will show energy as a headwind, not a tailwind. The Fed knows this, which is why the bond market shrugged at the print.
  • The Strait of Hormuz is now an active conflict zone. Iran disabling two supertankers changes the insurance math for every tanker transit. War risk premiums on Hormuz shipping are climbing, and some shipowners are pausing transits. If this continues, the oil price increase is not just about supply disruption - it is about the cost of moving oil through the world's most important chokepoint.

Commodities

Gold flat despite the CPI beat. Copper rallied on demand signals. Cattle pulling back while corn and soybeans hold steady.

Gold / oz: $4,070 flat from last week's $4,075
Silver / oz: $58.64 +1.6% from last week's $57.70
Copper / lb: $6.31 +4.8% (demand signal)
Corn / bushel: $4.38 +1.4%
Soybeans / bushel: $12.02 +1.4%
Live cattle / cwt: $234.73 −$2.99 from last week
Bottom Line
  • Gold sitting flat at $4,070 despite both a CPI beat and an active Iran war is the second week it has failed to rally on a clear catalyst. Last week it ignored the geopolitical escalation; this week it ignored the inflation progress. The safe-haven bid appears fully priced at current levels. A move in gold from here likely requires a clear Fed rate-cut signal, not more headline risk.
  • Copper rising 4.8% to $6.31 is the constructive data point in an otherwise uncertain week. Copper follows real economic activity, and this move suggests underlying demand has not collapsed despite the geopolitical noise. Cattle at $234.73 - down $3 on the week - is a welcome pullback but remains elevated by historical standards with the US herd near multi-decade lows.

Crop Weather

Corn holding steady. Winter wheat remains near 60-year lows. Drought coverage improving but still affecting tens of millions.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent (steady wk/wk). Critical pollination window continues through mid-July.
Winter wheat: 26% good-to-excellent - down 22 points from a year ago. Harvest progressing at 59% complete, ahead of the 5-year average.
Soybeans: 64% good-to-excellent (down 1 pt from last week).
Drought: ~47% of Lower 48 in drought, down 1 pt from last week. Improving slightly but 38% of the country remains in active drought (D1 or worse).
Bottom Line
  • Corn holding at 67% through pollination is the most important crop data point of the summer. The heat wave that threatened the western Corn Belt did not deliver the worst-case damage, but the next two Crop Progress reports remain high-stakes. If ratings hold above 64%, the supply outlook stays intact and corn-driven food inflation stays contained.
  • Winter wheat at 26% good-to-excellent - compared to 48% a year ago - is locked in as the worst quality season in decades. Higher flour, bread, and cereal costs in Q3-Q4 are a near-certainty. This food inflation operates on a separate clock from the oil-driven CPI story and will persist regardless of what happens in Hormuz.

Real Estate

Home sales fell again. Prices hit a record. Foreclosures climbing steadily. The CPI beat did nothing for the housing market because mortgage rates barely moved.

Existing-home sales (June): 4.09M ann. −2.4% MoM
Median existing-home price: $440,600 record high
Inventory: 4.6 months (up from 4.5 in May)
Foreclosure starts (Apr): 28,414 +12% YoY
Foreclosure starts (May): 27,304 +13% YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.88% highest since Q2 2021
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
30-year mortgage: 6.49% −1 bp wk
Bottom Line
  • The CPI beat should have been good news for housing - but the 30-year mortgage barely moved because the bond market sees oil coming. A 1-basis-point decline in rates does nothing to unfreeze a market where the median home costs $440,600 and the buyer needs $88K down at 20%. Sales at 4.09 million annualized are historically weak, and rising prices alongside falling volume is a sign of a locked market, not a healthy one.
  • Foreclosure starts running 12-13% above year-ago levels in April and May confirm the distress pipeline continues to build. These are not 2008 numbers - they remain well below crisis levels - but the direction is consistently upward. FHA delinquency at 11.88% means roughly 1 in 8 first-time buyers with government-backed loans is behind on payments. That cohort bought at peak prices with low down payments, and no CPI print fixes their balance sheet.

Household Finance

Consumer balance sheets still stretched. Credit card debt dipped seasonally in Q1 but total household debt hit another record. The savings rate holds at 3.0% - fragile if gas prices rise.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record (Q1 2026)
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion down from $1.28T in Q4 (seasonal); avg APR 21.0%
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo 20.3% of buyers now pay $1,000+/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days delinquent: 6.90% 32-year high (Fitch)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 3.0% holding (May 2026)
Bottom Line
  • The credit card balance dipping to $1.25 trillion from $1.28 trillion is a seasonal pattern - consumers pay down holiday spending in Q1 - not a structural improvement. The APR at 21.0% means even the reduced balance costs roughly $263 billion in annualized interest. The K-shaped split persists: upper-income households benefiting from record stock prices and 4-5% savings yields, while lower-income households service record debt at punitive rates.
  • The savings rate at 3.0% is the fragile bright spot. June's falling gas prices likely helped consumers save a bit more. But if the Iran oil spike pushes gas back toward $4.00 nationally, that recovery reverses. For the consumer below the median, a $20-per-week increase in gas is the difference between building a small buffer and running on fumes.

Crypto

Bitcoin edged higher on the CPI beat. Crypto continues to trade as a liquidity asset, rallying on rate-cut hopes and ignoring geopolitical risk.

Bitcoin (BTC): $62,611 +0.6% wk
Ethereum (ETH): $1,786 +3.1% wk
Solana (SOL): $75.12 −2.5% wk
XRP: $1.07 −0.9% wk
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin nudging higher to $62,600 on a CPI beat while ignoring an active Hormuz conflict confirms the pattern from last week: crypto is trading purely on rate-cut expectations, not as a geopolitical hedge. Ethereum gaining 3.1% on the week shows renewed interest in the broader ecosystem, though ETH remains far below its 2024 highs. The SEC's "Regulation Crypto" agenda expected this month is the next structural catalyst.
  • Solana dropping 2.5% and XRP falling 0.9% while BTC and ETH rose suggests capital rotation toward the top two assets. In uncertain macro environments, institutional money concentrates in Bitcoin first and Ethereum second. The smaller tokens tend to underperform until there is a clear risk-on signal from the Fed.

Around the World

Asia sold off on Iran risk. Europe under pressure from oil. China holding steady on domestic stimulus. The July 24 tariff deadline approaches.

Japan: Nikkei fell 1.3% on the day. The index is pulling back from its 70,000 highs as export-heavy Japanese industrials face higher input costs from oil.
Europe: Under pressure as Brent at $85 raises input costs across the continent. European industry is more energy-dependent than the US, so sustained oil above $80 compresses margins and complicates the ECB's rate path.
China: Shanghai Composite mixed, trading near 3,970. Domestic stimulus continues to offset global risk-off sentiment. The property sector remains fragile but government support is preventing a broader selloff.
Emerging: Under pressure from the strong dollar, rising oil, and the approaching July 24 tariff expiration on 60+ economies. A double squeeze - higher energy costs plus potential permanent tariffs - is the risk scenario.
Bottom Line
  • Europe is the most exposed developed market to the Iran escalation. Brent at $85 versus $72 two weeks ago translates directly to higher manufacturing costs, compressed margins, and a harder ECB rate decision. The STOXX 600's record high from early July now looks like it priced in an energy deflation story that no longer holds.
  • The July 24 tariff expiration on 60+ economies is now 10 days away. If temporary 10% tariffs become permanent while oil is elevated, emerging markets face capital flight to the dollar and higher import costs simultaneously. This remains underpriced relative to the binary nature of the outcome.
