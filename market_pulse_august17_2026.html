Monday morning's primary overnight development is a marginal oil pullback - Brent to $88.31 from Sunday's $89.60 - driven by US-Iran back-channel signals through Oman intermediaries that surfaced without any formal agreement or change in Iran's stated conditions for Hormuz reopening. The Quick Read

The primary overnight development is an oil pullback that looks like diplomatic noise more than genuine resolution. Brent fell from Sunday's $89.60 to $88.31 as Monday's Asian session opened - WTI from approximately $83.90 to $81.74 - after reports surfaced that US and Iranian officials have been exchanging messages through Oman intermediaries. Iran's Foreign Minister Araghchi repeated this weekend that until the United States remedies its violations of the June 2026 memorandum of understanding, "the possibility of resuming negotiations does not exist." No Hormuz agreement has been announced, no Iranian conditions have been modified, and Lloyd's of London marine underwriters have not indicated any change to their transit coverage position. The $88-89 Brent range remains approximately $12-13 above pre-crisis levels of $75-76. The pullback from Sunday's $89.60 is less than two dollars and almost certainly reflects profit-taking after weekend escalation headlines rather than any substantive diplomatic progress.

Gold at approximately $4,414 Monday morning is the market's cleaner signal: it is higher than Sunday's $4,389.07, not lower, which is the response one would expect if a genuine Hormuz resolution were being priced. Gold at $4,414 is instead pricing the confluence of sustained energy risk premium, 4.3% consumer inflation expectations from last Friday's UMich reading, and declining Federal Reserve hike urgency. September hike odds have slid from 25% on Friday toward approximately 15-20% as markets continue digesting July's retail sales contraction (-0.6% MoM) as the dominant near-term Fed signal. At 15-20% hike odds, the market is pricing approximately one hold and a potential cut path into 2027 rather than any additional tightening in 2026 - a framework that is directly constructive for gold and explains the continued advance despite marginally lower oil.

The crypto complex opens Monday with XRP recovering to approximately $1.06 - reversing Sunday's breach of the $1.00 psychological floor that had been the structural support level throughout August. The recovery is technical: CLARITY Act Polymarket odds remain at approximately 15-18% for 2026 passage, XRP ETF monthly flows collapsed 79% between May and July, and the Senate does not reconvene until after Labor Day with no confirmed schedule for a September floor vote. Bitcoin holds near $63,000, Ethereum near $1,876, and the Hashdex Bitcoin ETF (DEFI) - the smallest US spot product by net assets - closes today after the fund announced it would sell its remaining Bitcoin holdings and return cash to shareholders. Today's data schedule: Empire State Manufacturing Index at 8:30 AM ET (estimate 10.2, prior 15.6), NAHB Housing Market Index at 10:00 AM ET (estimate 35.0), and the USDA weekly crop progress report at 4:00 PM ET showing whether the 61% good-to-excellent corn rating from August 10 held through the first half of August.
