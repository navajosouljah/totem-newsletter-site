Forbes Advisor on Why CDs Are a Top Safe Option

With top rates still near 4.30% APY, Forbes Advisor and similar outlets are highlighting Certificates of Deposit as a reliable choice for conservative capital while rates remain elevated. We run the same six questions we always do.

7 min read Share

Forbes Advisor's Banking Team is one of the most respected institutional voices in consumer finance. They consistently publish data-driven roundups such as "Best CD Rates of July 2026," comparing hundreds of options and highlighting the strongest offers - up to 4.30% APY. Their work reaches millions of mainstream readers and financial advisors seeking clear, trustworthy guidance on where to park safe money. They actively recommend CD ladders as a smart way to balance yield and liquidity.

Who They Are

Forbes Advisor operates as an editorial team separate from Forbes magazine, focused on actionable personal finance guidance. Their CD rate roundups are among the most-read banking content on the internet, updated weekly with verified rates from hundreds of institutions. NerdWallet, Investopedia, and Bankrate publish similar coverage, all showing top 1-year and multi-year rates in the 4.00-4.30% range as of early July 2026.

What They Said

Forbes Advisor positions CDs as a top-tier safe and predictable option in today's environment. With FDIC insurance up to $250,000 per depositor and fixed rates locked in for the term, they offer peace of mind while rates are still attractive.

They emphasize building CD ladders - staggering maturities across 6-month, 1-year, and 2-year terms - to capture yield without tying up all capital long-term. In a world of market volatility and uncertain rate cuts, CDs provide contractual returns without principal risk.

Key Highlights from Forbes Advisor

  • Top 1-year CD rates range from 4.10% to 4.30% APY from online banks like Popular Direct, E*TRADE, and Bask Bank.
  • Credit union specials reach as high as 6.00% APY for shorter terms (Financial Partners CU, 8-month).
  • The national average 1-year CD sits at roughly 1.99% - meaning most savers earn less than half of what is available if they shop.
  • CD laddering is recommended as the best strategy to balance yield, liquidity, and rate-change risk.
"In a world of market volatility and uncertain rate cuts, CDs provide contractual returns without principal risk."

Where We Agree

We agree with Forbes Advisor on several key points.

Safety matters. FDIC insurance provides strong principal protection up to the coverage limit. For capital that truly needs to stay ultra-safe and predictable, CDs are solid, accessible tools used by millions of Americans. There is nothing wrong with a guaranteed return backed by the full faith and credit of the United States government.

Locking in rates now makes sense. If the Fed shifts from holding to cutting later this year, today's 4.30% CDs will look better in hindsight than whatever replaces them. The window to earn a real positive return on cash may be closing - Forbes is right to flag the urgency.

CD ladders are a thoughtful strategy. Staggering maturities so that capital comes available at regular intervals solves the liquidity problem that single long-term CDs create. It is a disciplined approach to managing cash reserves.

Where It Gets Complicated

The trade-off is clear: attractive safety comes with meaningfully lower yields and longer lockups.

First, 4.30% APY still leaves significant ground uncovered after inflation and opportunity cost. With CPI running above 4%, a 4.30% CD barely keeps pace with inflation - and after taxes on interest income, most savers are losing purchasing power. The "safe yield" is safe, but it is not building wealth. It is treading water.

Second, once capital is committed, early withdrawal penalties erode returns. Most CDs charge 3-12 months of interest for early withdrawal. A 12-month CD at 4.30% that gets broken at month 6 may return less than a standard savings account would have. The locked structure that provides certainty also removes flexibility.

Third, FDIC coverage caps at $250,000 per depositor per institution. For investors with larger reserves - the kind of capital that needs to stay productive - the protection does not scale without spreading accounts across multiple banks. That creates administrative complexity and still caps the yield at 4.30%.

Fourth, there is no short-cycle flexibility or independent insurance on the full compensation. A CD locks you in for the term. You cannot redeploy after 90 days based on how conditions change. And the insurance covers principal only - it does not independently protect the yield itself.

Our Challenge

Forbes Advisor makes a strong case for CDs as safe, predictable income while rates last. That is one legitimate answer for capital that prioritizes maximum safety and simplicity. We respect it.

Another answer exists for the portion of capital that can deploy into documented, insured commercial execution on short cycles: higher contractual compensation with principal and fee protection through insurance arrangements, visible 90-day turns, and verifiable Fortune 100 counterparties.

Consider the math. On $50,000 deployed for one year:

  • A standard savings account at the national average earns roughly $190.
  • The best 1-year CD at 4.30% earns $2,150 - with the capital locked for the full 12 months.
  • Four 90-day Totem cycles at 5% each earn $10,000 - with capital returned and re-deployed every quarter.

Both approaches value safety. Only one delivers it with materially higher compensation and shorter duration.
