4.75% CDs Hold Into FOMC Week. WTI at $103 - Highest Since May.

The Quick Read

The week ended with WTI crude closing Friday above $103 - its first close above $100 since May - as Houthi strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities in Yanbu and Shaybah drove the fourth and fifth supply disruptions of October. The Dow fell 652 points on energy cost fears while the Nasdaq fell a more modest 0.21% as semiconductor names partially recovered from Thursday's AI-capex rout. October FOMC hike odds fell from 17-18% at the week's open to approximately 7-9% by Friday's close - a 10-point compression driven by Thursday's 17-19 basis-point Treasury yield plunge, the largest single-day move since March 2023. That is the mechanism: oil spikes hit the Dow through industrial and consumer cost exposure; AI capex uncertainty reprices the Nasdaq; and both together compress the Fed's room to move into next week.

The five-day window that begins Monday is the year's most consequential. Monday brings the USDA Crop Progress report - Iowa corn harvest at 26% against the five-year average of 52% is the key number. Tuesday-Wednesday brings the FOMC decision at 2:00 PM ET October 28 at 7-9% hike odds - the first Fed communication since the blackout began October 17. Thursday October 29 at 8:30 AM ET brings Q3 GDP and September PCE simultaneously - the morning after the FOMC. The ECB also decides Thursday. Friday October 30 brings the Employment Cost Index and the BoJ.

Two pump transmission waves are building simultaneously. The October 16-17 Brent spike arrives at the pump approximately October 26-31 - right inside FOMC week - and Friday's WTI surge above $103 adds a second wave arriving approximately November 1-5. A household paying $4.29 today faces both. Against that backdrop, 4.75% 1-year CDs remain available this weekend - before next week's yield-moving data window has a chance to reduce what banks offer.
