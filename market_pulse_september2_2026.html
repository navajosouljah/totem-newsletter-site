Savers Are 66% Likely to Get a Raise. Gold Just Got Cheaper.

The Quick Read

Gold fell 2.9% to $4,325 on the day the US struck Iranian tankers, which is not how a safe haven behaves. It is the rate market's verdict. The August ISM Manufacturing PMI came in at 54.6% on Tuesday, under the 55.2% consensus but in expansion for an eighth straight month, and under Warsh's framework a hike pauses only when "underlying trends" deteriorate. A factory sector still growing, with its prices index stuck at 71.1%, is not deterioration. That is why September hike odds moved to 66%, and that is the whole mechanism.

ADP's 47K this morning matched consensus and settles nothing. July's 44K ADP was followed by a jobs report showing a 23,000 loss. Friday's payrolls at 8:30 ET is the number that moves September. Above 150,000 with wages at 3.5% or better pushes hike odds past 70%. Below 100,000 pulls them toward 45 to 50% and hands savers, gold, and crypto their first relief of the month.

Underneath, two slower pressures. The US struck roughly 100 Iranian targets Tuesday under a new "tanker for tanker" policy, the first strikes on vessels rather than bases, and Brent is holding $94.86 with two floors under it now. And the 10-year closed at 4.81%, through the 4.75% line that starts pushing mortgage rates above 6.70% within two or three Freddie Mac surveys. Neither resolves before the Fed votes September 15.
