Weekly Briefing • Saturday, August 1, 2026

Cloud Earnings Outrun a Divided Fed - S&P 500 Ends a Turbulent July at 7,490, Up 1.0% for the Week. Three FOMC Dissents Put a September Hike in Play. Q2 GDP +1.5%, First Monthly PCE Decline Since 2020. Gas Crosses $4.10 While Foreclosure Starts Rise 18%.

July threw a chip rout, a divided Fed, and an active Middle East conflict at this market, and the week still ended higher. The recovery is real, and so is the cost side of the ledger building underneath it.

The Quick Read

The week's story in one paragraph: stocks closed a violent month roughly where they started it - the S&P 500 finished Friday at 7,489.72, up 1.0% for the week and about 0.9% lower for July - because cloud earnings from Microsoft and Amazon answered the AI-demand fear that had driven three weeks of selling. The Fed held at 3.50-3.75% with three members voting to hike, the first triple dissent in the direction of tightening this cycle, and the bond market took the hint: the 10-year Treasury ended the week at 4.75% and the 30-year mortgage at 6.66%, both higher despite a soft Q2 GDP print of +1.5% and the first monthly decline in headline PCE since April 2020. Gas crossed $4.10 nationally while foreclosure starts run 18% above last year, the savings rate sits at 2.7%, and 47% of buy-now-pay-later users have paid late in the past year.

The tension worth understanding is between the two halves of that paragraph. The earnings half says the AI buildout is generating measurable demand - AWS grew 37%, Azure 43%, and Seoul's chip complex staged the largest single-day recovery in its history on the news. The macro half says the household sector is absorbing higher fuel, higher borrowing costs, and thinner savings at the same time three Fed officials have concluded rates need to go up, not down. Both halves are true simultaneously, which is why the equity rally and the rate pressure coexist.

For a saver or borrower, the practical read is unchanged from Wednesday: cheap money is not coming back on the schedule markets assumed a month ago. The best 1-year CDs at 4.40% now look durable rather than fleeting, and the 6.66% mortgage is more likely to rise than fall before the September 15-16 meeting. The K-shaped divide runs through everything below - record median home prices over rising foreclosures, record cloud revenue over a 2.7% savings rate.

This Week in the News

  • The FOMC held rates at 3.50-3.75% on Wednesday in a 9-3 vote, with Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan dissenting in favor of a hike. It is the most hawkish committee split of the cycle, delivered with inflation above target for a fifth straight year. Market-implied odds shifted from a September cut to a meaningful probability of a September hike within hours. Next meeting: September 15-16. (Federal Reserve statement, July 29)
  • Big Tech earnings split into two tiers. Microsoft rose 15% after Azure grew 43% and cloud revenue topped $100B annually for the first time. Amazon crossed $200B in quarterly revenue with AWS up 37%, its fastest growth in 18 quarters, though the headline EPS was inflated by a $53.4B gain on its Anthropic stake. Meta fell 9% on weak guidance and a free-cash-flow collapse. Apple beat on revenue ($109.4B, +16%) but guided below consensus for Q4, citing memory-price inflation. (Company IR releases, CNBC)
  • Q2 GDP came in at +1.5% annualized, below the +2.1% expected, while June headline PCE fell 0.1% on the month - the first monthly price decline since April 2020. Headline PCE eased to 3.7% YoY from 4.1%; core held at 3.3%. Slower growth and decelerating inflation give the Fed a case for patience that three of its members clearly no longer find persuasive. (BEA, July 30)
  • The tariff regime was rebuilt after the Supreme Court struck the IEEPA tariffs: a new 10-12.5% Section 301 baseline is replacing the voided duties, the administration used Section 338 for the first time to impose 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, and Beijing says Washington pledged a 20% cap on China rates. The pass-through into fall goods prices is the number to watch in the August and September CPI prints. (Bloomberg, CNBC)
  • Seoul's KOSPI rose 17.9% on Friday - the largest single-day gain in its history - after the Amazon and Microsoft results validated AI memory demand. SK Hynix closed at its 30% daily limit and Samsung gained 27%. The rebound follows a July decline of roughly 22%, the index's worst month since 1997, and still leaves the KOSPI down 1.4% for the week. (CNBC, BoJ/exchange data)

Stock Market

A weekly gain that hides a 7% intramonth drawdown. The recovery arrived in the final three sessions, powered almost entirely by two cloud earnings reports.

S&P 500: 7,489.72 +1.0% for the week; roughly -0.9% for July
Dow Jones Industrial Average: 52,485.03 +1.0% for the week
Nasdaq Composite: 25,373.85 +1.6% for the week; led by Microsoft and Amazon
Russell 2000: 2,931.34 flat on the week; small caps sat out the cloud rally on rate sensitivity
VIX: 15.99 down from 18.67 last Friday; fear premium unwound as earnings landed
Bottom Line
  • The index-level calm is doing a lot of work. The S&P's 1.0% weekly gain is the product of a two-tier market: companies that can show AI-driven revenue acceleration were rewarded heavily, and everything rate-sensitive - small caps flat on the week - was left behind. Narrow leadership at stretched valuations remains the setup, and a market carried by two earnings reports is a market with concentrated downside if the next two disappoint.
  • The VIX at 16 with three Fed officials voting to hike and an active Middle East conflict is a low price on a long list of open questions. That is not a prediction; it is a description of how much good news is already in the price at 7,490.

Interest Rates

The bond market spent the week deciding the dissenters matter more than the data.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% held July 29; 9-3 vote, three dissents in favor of a +25bp hike
2-year Treasury: 4.23% as of July 30; front end pricing tightening risk, not cuts
10-year Treasury: 4.75% +10 bps on the week; rose through soft GDP and PCE data
30-year Treasury: 5.28% +15 bps on the week; long end carrying a fiscal premium
30-year mortgage (Freddie Mac, July 30): 6.66% up from 6.58% the prior week; first reading to fully reflect the post-FOMC repricing
Bottom Line
  • A 10-year that rises 10 basis points in a week containing a GDP miss and the first monthly PCE decline in six years is telling you the committee's distribution - three votes to hike, zero to cut - is the dominant signal. The constructive case for bonds runs through exactly this: yields at 4.75% on the 10-year and 5.28% on the long bond now pay real money to wait, and a Fed determined to keep credit conditions honest implies more pressure on stretched equities than on high-grade fixed income.
  • Every release between now and September 15 is now a referendum on the hike case: JOLTS Tuesday, the July jobs report Friday, and CPI on August 12. One soft print is unlikely to flip three committed dissents. Two might.

Savings & CD Rates

The savers' window that was supposed to be closing has been propped back open.

National avg 1-yr CD (FDIC, July): 1.68% APY the average bank still pays a fraction of what the best offers do
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.40% APY (E*TRADE, no minimum); Bankrate's top-ranked pick at 4.17%
Best credit union 1-yr CD: 4.05% APY (NASA Federal CU, $1K minimum)
Bottom Line
  • The gap between the 1.68% national average and the 4.40% best offer is a 2.6x difference on the same insured product. Most deposit dollars are still parked at the average. Moving money remains the highest-certainty return available to a household this year.
  • Before Wednesday, locking a 1-year CD was framed as a race against September cuts. With a hike now the live scenario, 4.40% is more likely a floor than a ceiling through year-end - the lock-in urgency has eased, and the case for laddering instead of lump-summing has improved.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil has come down from its mid-July spike. The pump has not gotten the message yet.

WTI Crude: $84.67 +2.5% on the week; well below the $93 mid-July peak
Brent Crude: $90.12 +2.0% on the week; the Iran premium keeps a floor under it
National gas avg (EIA, July 27): $4.10/gal +10 cents on the week; up 97 cents from a year ago; third straight weekly rise
West Coast: $5.12/gal California at $5.49, the highest in the nation
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): $3.69/gal
Bottom Line
  • Retail gas lags crude by two to four weeks, so the July spike to $93 WTI is still working its way into pump prices even though crude has retreated to the mid-80s. The structural view holds: near-term Middle East spikes keep fading rather than sticking, and US supply leverage points lower over time. The next month of pump prices was already written in July.
  • "Gas is around $4" is true on average and false where a lot of members live. A California driver at $5.49 is paying $1.80 over the Gulf Coast - the widest practical spread in the country, and a recurring reminder that national averages flatten a K-shaped map.

Commodities

Metals held their safe-haven bid through a risk-on week; the grain complex split on drought math.

Gold / oz: $4,107 +0.8% on the week; held above $4,000 even as equities rallied
Silver / oz: $57.79 -1.2% on the week
Copper / lb: $6.47 +2.0% on the week; the cleanest industrial-demand signal of the week
Corn (Sep) / bu: $4.64 +2.7% on the week as heat trimmed crop ratings
Soybeans (Nov) / bu: $11.88 -1.7% on the week
Live cattle / lb: $2.27 +0.9%; beef costs keep grinding higher
Bottom Line
  • Gold holding $4,100 during a strong equity week says the geopolitical floor under the metal is structural, not a trade. Copper up 2% alongside the cloud-earnings beat is the more encouraging read - actual industrial demand confirming the AI buildout is pulling real materials, not just narratives.
  • Corn up 2.7% on a 4-point drop in crop ratings is the food-price channel opening in real time. With 29% of corn acres in drought and grain fill at peak sensitivity, the crop-condition reports over the next three weeks will set the fall food-inflation trajectory that lands in the same CPI prints the Fed's dissenters are watching.

Crop Weather

Heat is winning the second half of the growing season so far.

Corn: 63% good-to-excellent (USDA, week ending July 27) down 4 points in a week on Corn Belt heat; grain fill at peak sensitivity
Winter wheat: harvest 81% complete, ahead of the 79% five-year average pace harvest-side risk mostly behind
Drought: 47.9% of the Lower 48 in moderate drought or worse; 29% of corn acres affected; Northern Plains and Upper Midwest deteriorated for a third straight week
Bottom Line
  • A 4-point weekly drop in corn ratings during grain fill is how yield estimates get cut in August. The drought map expanding through the Northern Plains for a third consecutive week means the next USDA reads carry real downside risk - and the futures market moved 2.7% this week saying exactly that.

Real Estate

Record prices on top, building distress underneath. Look at the starts and the price cuts before the median.

Foreclosure starts (H1 2026, ATTOM): 164,566 +18% YoY; June alone 26,217, +20% YoY
Completed foreclosures (H1 2026): 27,983 +33% YoY; still 26% below the H1 2020 level
Mortgage delinquency (MBA, Q1): 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency: 11.88% +126 bps YoY; roughly 9 points above conventional loans
Existing-home sales (NAR, June): 4.09M annualized -2.4% MoM
Listings with a price cut (Redfin): 20.2% rising; sellers meeting the market first
Median existing-home price (June): $440,600 +1.8% YoY, a record for any month
30-yr fixed mortgage: 6.66% +8 bps this week
Bottom Line
  • Distress is building at the edges even as the median sets records. Foreclosure starts up 18% and completions up 33% describe households who stretched at peak prices or exited pandemic-era relief - though the absolute levels remain well below 2008-2010, this is a repricing at the margins, not a systemic break.
  • FHA borrowers are the tell. At 11.88% delinquency - nine points above conventional - first-time and lower-credit buyers are slipping first, and the post-FOMC mortgage at 6.66% removes the refinancing escape hatch they were waiting for. The record $440,600 median is not one market; it is an average of increasingly divergent ones.

Household Finance

The aggregate consumer looks fine. The margins of the consumer do not.

Total household debt (NY Fed, Q1): $18.8 trillion a record
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion +5.9% YoY; the $25B Q1 dip is the usual seasonal pattern, not deleveraging
Avg card APR (Fed G.19, May): 21.15% on interest-bearing accounts
Avg new-car payment (Edmunds, Q2): $777/mo a third straight record; 20.3% of borrowers now above $1,000/mo
Subprime auto 60+ day late (Fitch): 6.11% (March) off January's 32-year record of 6.90% on tax-refund season; Fitch expects renewed deterioration
BNPL users paying late (LendingTree): 47% up from 41% a year ago
Personal savings rate (June): 2.7% spending has outrun income all year
Bottom Line
  • The honest split: aggregate spending held up in June and headline prices actually fell for the month, yet the funding source is a savings rate at 2.7 cents per earned dollar and card balances growing 5.9% at a 21% APR. That is a consumer running on thinner ice, not a consumer in crisis - the distinction matters and both halves belong in the sentence.
  • The subprime auto tell repeats the FHA tell: January's 6.90% was a 32-year record, the spring improvement was tax-refund seasonality, and Fitch expects the deterioration to resume. Stress keeps showing up first exactly where the least cushion exists. A $4.10 gas average and a possible September hike land on those households before they land anywhere else.

Crypto

Crypto sat out the equity recovery. The rate environment is the reason.

Bitcoin (BTC): $62,689 -1.9% on the week
Ethereum (ETH): $1,837 -4.3% on the week
Solana (SOL): $71.65 -2.8% on the week
XRP: $1.06 -1.0% on the week
Bottom Line
  • Equities rallied on earnings; crypto has no earnings to rally on, so it traded the other input - a hawkish Fed and a 10-year at 4.75%. The institutional-retail divergence continues: the asset class that was supposed to be a hedge is behaving like a high-beta expression of rate expectations, and this week rates said no.

Around the World

A historic Friday in Seoul, a fourth monthly gain in Europe, and a contraction print out of Beijing.

Japan: Nikkei 64,362 -0.9% on the week; BoJ held at 1.00% Friday with one vote for a hike and a warning that core inflation may exceed 2%
Europe: STOXX 600 at 649 +0.7% on the week, a fourth straight monthly gain; July inflation ticked up, keeping another ECB hike in the conversation
China: Shanghai Composite 3,832 -0.7% on the week; official manufacturing PMI at 49.2, an unexpected contraction
Emerging markets: MSCI EM near 1,567 up roughly 25% over 12 months; Hong Kong's Hang Seng +3.7% this week on the chip rebound
Bottom Line
  • The KOSPI's record 17.9% Friday still left it down for the week and roughly 22% for July - the cleanest illustration anywhere of forward fear detaching from fundamentals and then partially snapping back. Meanwhile the policy direction abroad rhymes with Washington: the BoJ warning on inflation, the ECB leaning tighter, and a Chinese factory sector back in contraction. The global cheap-money era is not re-opening from any direction.

Upcoming Events

August opens with the first post-FOMC labor data. Every print between now and September 15 is an argument in the hike debate.

Aug 4 (Tue) - June JOLTS job openings (BLS). The first labor read since the 9-3 hold.
Aug 5 (Wed) - Treasury Quarterly Refunding statement. Sets the size of the long-end supply that has been pressuring 30-year yields.
Aug 7 (Fri) - July jobs report (BLS, 8:30 AM ET). A strong print validates the three dissenters; a soft one opens the release valve.
Aug 11-13 - Treasury refunding auctions (3-, 10-, and 30-year, per the tentative schedule). Long-end demand is the market's live vote on fiscal supply.
Aug 12 (Wed) - July CPI (BLS). First full read on July's $90+ Brent and the new tariff baseline.
Aug 13 (Thu) - July PPI (BLS). Pipeline pressure check on the same inputs.
Sep 15-16 (Mon-Tue) - Next FOMC meeting, with a fresh dot plot showing whether the three-dissent hike camp is growing.
