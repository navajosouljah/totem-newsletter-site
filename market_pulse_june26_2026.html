Weekly Briefing • Friday, June 26, 2026

The Dow Hits an All-Time High While the Nasdaq Posts Its Longest Losing Streak Since February. Apple Drops 6% on Price Hikes. Oil Steadies Near $70. Consumer Sentiment Final Today.

A tale of two markets. The Dow Jones hit a new all-time intraday high on Thursday, lifted by value and industrial names, while the Nasdaq fell for its fourth consecutive day - its longest losing streak since February. The split? Apple dropped 6% after raising MacBook and iPad prices by up to $200 to offset skyrocketing AI memory costs - wiping $265 billion from its market cap. Micron surged 17% on blowout earnings but couldn't save the broader tech index. Yesterday's hot PCE at 4.1% continues to weigh on rate-sensitive assets. Oil steadied near $70 after a cargo ship was struck off the Omani coast. The University of Michigan final consumer sentiment reading arrives today.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • The Dow hit a new all-time intraday high while the Nasdaq posted a 4-day losing streak. Classic rotation from tech to value. The Dow gained 0.14% to 51,920. The Nasdaq fell 0.46% to 25,358. Money is leaving high-multiple tech for industrials and defensives.
  • Apple dropped 6% - its worst day in a year - after raising MacBook and iPad prices by up to $200. The memory chip crunch from AI demand is now flowing through to consumer electronics. Apple wiped $265 billion from its market cap. CEO Tim Cook said "price increases are unavoidable."
  • Micron surged 17% on blowout earnings but couldn't lift the broader Nasdaq. Revenue $41.5B vs $35.1B expected. The AI chip thesis is validated. But the Nasdaq still fell because Apple's pain outweighed Micron's gain - showing how concentrated the tech index has become.
  • A cargo ship was struck by an unknown projectile off the Omani coast. The incident renewed safety concerns about the Strait of Hormuz, briefly pushing Brent up 2% to $74.70 before settling. The peace framework remains intact but fragile.

The Quick Read

The Dow hit an all-time high. The Nasdaq fell for a fourth straight day. Apple dropped 6% on price hikes. Micron surged 17% on AI demand. PCE inflation at 4.1% is the hottest in 3 years but reflects old oil prices that have since collapsed. WTI near $70. 30-year mortgage at 6.49%. Gold at $4,020. Bitcoin at $59,299. Consumer sentiment final today - preliminary was 48.9, up from May's all-time low.

The Dow/Nasdaq divergence is the story of the week. Value is outperforming growth because the hot PCE strengthens the rate-hike narrative, which hits long-duration growth stocks hardest. Apple's price increases show the cost side of the AI boom - memory chips are so expensive that consumer products are getting repriced. Micron benefits (seller), Apple suffers (buyer). The irony: the same AI demand that made Micron's quarter also caused Apple's worst day in a year.

Oil steadying near $70 with the cargo ship incident is a reminder that the Hormuz peace is fragile. But the structural trend is clear - oil down 25%+ from April. Consumer sentiment improving from record lows on falling gas prices. The University of Michigan final reading arrives today - preliminary was 48.9, up from May's all-time low of 50.8. If the final confirms the improvement, it validates the thesis that falling energy costs are beginning to repair consumer confidence.

The broader picture: we are watching the AI boom create winners and losers simultaneously. Micron's 17% surge and Apple's 6% drop are two sides of the same coin. Memory chips are the bottleneck - whoever sells them wins, whoever buys them pays. That dynamic will define tech earnings through the rest of 2026. Meanwhile, the hot PCE at 4.1% is backward-looking data reflecting oil prices that have since fallen 25%. The inflation picture will improve dramatically in the July and August readings when WTI below $70 flows through the data. Friday futures are pointing sharply higher - S&P +0.7%, Nasdaq +1.9%, Dow +0.2% - suggesting markets see the Apple selloff as overdone.

Russell 2000 (small caps): ~3,005
Fear gauge (VIX): ~18

Bottom Line
  • The Dow/Nasdaq divergence is the clearest rotation signal this year. Money leaving high-multiple tech for value and industrials. The Dow hitting an all-time intraday high while the Nasdaq posts its worst streak since February tells you the market is not weak - it is rotating. Investors are not selling equities, they are selling growth and buying value.
  • Apple's 6% drop shows the cost side of AI demand. Memory chips are so expensive that Apple had to raise MacBook and iPad prices by up to $200, wiping $265 billion from its market cap. Micron's 17% surge shows the benefit side - $41.5B in revenue versus $35.1B expected. Same force, opposite effects. The AI boom has winners and losers, and they trade in opposite directions on the same day.
  • Friday futures pointing sharply higher - especially Nasdaq +1.9% - suggest the market sees Apple's selloff as overdone and the Micron-driven AI thesis as intact. If the session confirms the bounce, the 4-day Nasdaq losing streak ends and the rotation narrative softens. Consumer sentiment at 10:00 AM is the key catalyst for the session.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held)
2-year Treasury: ~4.28%
10-year Treasury: 4.41% pulled back from 4.55% post-PCE
30-year Treasury: ~4.95%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.49% +2 bps from 6.47% last week (Freddie Mac weekly avg)
Bottom Line
  • The 10-year pulling back from 4.55% to 4.41% despite yesterday's hot PCE at 4.1% is a significant signal. The bond market is looking through the backward-looking inflation data and pricing in the energy disinflation that is coming. WTI at $70 versus $85+ when the May PCE data was collected means the July and August readings should show a meaningful decline. Bond traders are ahead of the data.
  • Mortgage rates at 6.49% - up just 2 bps from 6.47% last week - are essentially flat despite the PCE volatility. The Freddie Mac weekly average smooths out the intraday spikes. Housing still needs sub-6.25% to meaningfully unfreeze, but the stability suggests rates are finding a ceiling rather than continuing to climb. If the 10-year holds near 4.40% and the July PCE shows energy disinflation, mortgages could drift toward 6.25-6.35% by August.
  • For savers, the hawkish Fed posture continues to support elevated CD and savings rates. The 2-year at 4.28% and best online CDs at 4.10-4.15% mean cash earns a solid real return. The hot PCE makes it unlikely that the Fed cuts anytime soon, which keeps the savings tailwind intact through at least Q3.

With the savings rate improving from 2.6% to 3.0%, more Americans are setting money aside - those who can should be locking in these rates while the hawkish environment supports them.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil steadying near $70 after a cargo ship was struck off the Omani coast. Brent briefly spiked 2% before settling. The structural trend remains intact - oil down 25%+ from April. But any Hormuz escalation could reverse consumer relief quickly.

WTI crude: ~$70/bbl in $69.65-73.17 range this week
Brent crude: $74.43/bbl −1.11%; briefly rose 2% Thursday on cargo ship incident off Oman
National gas avg: $3.93/gal −$0.62 from $4.55 May peak
West Coast: $5.49-5.71/gal (CA highest)
Cheapest region: ~$3.40/gal (Indiana)
Bottom Line
  • The cargo ship struck off Oman is a reminder that Hormuz peace is fragile. Brent spiked 2% to $74.70 briefly before settling back to $74.43. The market's quick reversal tells you traders view this as an isolated incident, not a pattern. But the speed of the spike shows how quickly oil can reprice if the peace framework breaks down. Any escalation near Hormuz would reverse the consumer relief from falling gas prices almost immediately.
  • The structural trend remains intact despite the incident. Oil is down 25%+ from April. WTI has traded in a $69.65-73.17 range this week - consolidating near $70 rather than breaking lower or spiking higher. Gas at $3.93 continues saving families $12-15 a week at the pump compared to the May peak. If WTI holds near $70 through the end of June, gas could drift toward $3.75-3.85 by mid-July.
  • The critical disconnect remains: yesterday's PCE at 4.1% reflects oil prices from April and May, when WTI was above $85. The 25%+ decline has not shown up in the inflation data yet. When it does - in the July and August PCE readings - headline inflation should drop significantly. The energy disinflation pipeline is loaded. It just needs time to flow through to the official data.

Commodities

Gold down $333 in a week - the sharpest weekly decline of 2026. The debasement trade is unwinding as dollar strength and hawkish Fed expectations crush precious metals. Cattle still at records.

Gold / oz: $4,020 −$333 from $4,353 last week; sharpest weekly decline of 2026
Silver / oz: $57.25 below $60; down from $70+ last week
Copper / lb: ~$6.10 down from $6.50
Corn / bushel: ~$4.10 steady
Soybeans / bushel: ~$11.15 steady
Live cattle / cwt: $255+ near record levels
Bottom Line
  • Gold dropping $333 in a single week - from $4,353 to $4,020 - is the sharpest weekly decline of 2026. The "debasement trade" that drove gold above $4,300 is unwinding as the hot PCE strengthens the dollar and reinforces hawkish Fed expectations. A stronger dollar and higher real yields are hostile to gold, which pays no interest. But gold holding above $4,000 suggests structural central bank demand is providing a floor. The question is whether the July PCE shows enough disinflation to reverse the hawkish narrative - if it does, gold rebounds sharply.
  • Silver falling from $70+ to $57.25 in a week is an even more dramatic decline than gold, consistent with silver's higher volatility. Silver's dual industrial-precious nature means it gets hit from both sides when the macro turns hostile - hawkish Fed pressures the precious component while growth concerns pressure the industrial component. Below $57 would be significant technical support. Silver tends to overshoot in both directions and recover faster than gold when the narrative shifts.
  • Cattle at $255+/cwt remain at record levels on the smallest US herd since 1951 (86.2 million head). This is a multi-year biological cycle - herds take 3-4 years to rebuild, keeping beef prices elevated through 2028-2029. For grocery shoppers, poultry and pork remain more affordable protein alternatives.

Crop Weather

Corn at 68% good-to-excellent. Winter wheat in serious trouble - production forecast down 27% YoY to the lowest since 1965. July pollination is the next critical risk window.

Corn: 68% good-to-excellent (up 1 pt from first rating). 97% emerged, 5% silking. July pollination approaching - the make-or-break period for yield.
Soybeans: 66% good-to-excellent. 93% emerged, 9% blooming. Planting progress ahead of 5-year average.
Winter wheat: 14% good, 0% excellent; 57% poor to very poor. Production forecast 1,030M bushels - down 27% YoY and lowest since 1965. 63% in drought areas. Harvest 11% complete.
Drought: 56% of the Lower 48 in some level of drought. Near-term forecast favors above-normal precipitation through end of June in the Corn Belt.
Bottom Line
  • Winter wheat at 57% poor-to-very-poor with 63% of production in drought areas is a genuine supply problem. Production down 27% YoY to the lowest since 1965 will translate to higher flour and bread prices in Q3-Q4. This is already priced into futures, but consumers will feel it at the bakery aisle. Colorado and Montana have seen the sharpest condition declines.
  • Corn at 68% good-to-excellent is adequate but July is the critical month. The El Nino probability remains elevated through January 2027, which historically dries out the Midwest during pollination. Above-normal precipitation forecast through end of June is constructive for the Corn Belt, but excess moisture is disrupting fieldwork in some areas.
  • The structural drought (6th year in the West, intensifying in the Plains) is a slow-moving water infrastructure challenge. It is not causing acute food-price shocks today, but it constrains long-term agricultural capacity. Worth monitoring weekly rather than reacting to any single reading.

Bottom Line
  • Mortgage rates at 6.49% - up just 2 bps from 6.47% last week - are essentially flat despite the hot PCE volatility. The Freddie Mac weekly average smoothed out the intraday spike to 6.60% that followed yesterday's PCE report. The market needs sub-6.25% to meaningfully unfreeze. If the 10-year holds near 4.41% and the July PCE captures energy disinflation, mortgages could drift toward 6.25-6.35% by August. Homebuyers may need to be patient for 6-8 weeks while the data catches up to the energy reality.
  • FHA delinquency at 11.88% - up 126 bps year-over-year - remains the canary in the housing coal mine. FHA borrowers are first-time buyers with lower down payments and thinner financial cushions. The savings rate improving from 2.6% to 3.0% helps at the margins, but FHA stress is structural, not cyclical. These borrowers are the most exposed to any income disruption.
  • Completed foreclosures surging 45% YoY to 14,020 in Q1 tells you the pipeline is converting into actual property seizures. Still well below crisis-era levels (300,000+ per quarter in 2009-2010), but the trend is clearly higher. Housing starts collapsing 15.4% shows builders are pulling back on new construction as rates rise - which constrains future supply and supports prices even as demand weakens. The market is adjusting under stress, not collapsing.

UMich consumer sentiment (preliminary): 48.9 up from May all-time low; final reading today
Bottom Line
  • The University of Michigan final consumer sentiment reading at 10:00 AM today is the key data point for the session. The preliminary reading was 48.9 - up from May's all-time low. If the final confirms or improves on that number, it validates the thesis that falling gas prices are beginning to repair consumer confidence. A surprise decline would undercut the recovery narrative and pressure equities heading into the weekend.
  • The savings rate at 3.0% - up from 2.6% in April - combined with personal income beating expectations at +0.7% suggests consumers are beginning to rebuild small financial cushions. Still less than half the 8.4% long-run average, but the direction matters. Falling gas prices - down $0.62 from the May peak - are contributing directly. Every dollar not spent at the pump is a dollar that can be saved or used to pay down debt.
  • But the stress indicators remain elevated. Credit card delinquencies at 13.1% (16-year high), subprime auto at 6.90% (32-year high), and 20.3% of new-car buyers paying $1,000+/month tell you the bottom third of the income distribution remains maxed out. At 21% APR, anyone carrying the average $7,886 card balance is paying $1,656/year in interest alone. The savings-rate improvement is real but needs to continue for several more months before it represents a genuine turning point.

Crypto

Bitcoin below $60K for a second day. The debasement trade unwind is hitting crypto alongside gold and silver. Hot PCE and hawkish Fed continue to pressure rate-sensitive assets.

Bitcoin (BTC): $59,299 −2.26% on the day; below $60K for second day
Ethereum (ETH): $1,562 −2.75% on the day
Solana (SOL): $65.68 −2.28% on the day
XRP: ~$1.02 under pressure
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin below $60,000 for a second consecutive day at $59,299 confirms the break of that psychological support level. The hot PCE at 4.1% and hawkish Fed expectations are the primary drivers. When inflation runs hot and rate-hike talk intensifies, crypto sells off - regardless of what is happening in the equity market. The Dow hitting an all-time high did nothing to support Bitcoin because the asset class is pricing rate expectations, not equity rotation.
  • The debasement trade is unwinding across the board. Gold down $333 in a week, silver down from $70+ to $57.25, Bitcoin from $61K+ to $59,299. These assets all benefited from the same narrative - currency debasement, fiscal deficits, inflation hedging. When the dollar strengthens and real yields rise on hawkish Fed expectations, the entire complex reverses. Crypto is the highest-beta expression of this trade, which means it falls fastest and will also recover fastest when the narrative shifts.
  • The path forward runs through inflation data. If the July and August PCE readings show energy disinflation flowing through - which they should, given oil's 25%+ decline - rate-hike expectations will fade and crypto will benefit. But until that data arrives, Bitcoin's next meaningful support is $57,000-58,000. ETH around $1,500 is a key level to watch. The correlation between crypto and rate expectations remains the dominant trading dynamic.

Around the World

KOSPI recovering on Micron earnings. Nikkei pulling back from highs. Europe steady on cheaper energy. Watching the July 24 tariff deadline on 60 countries.

South Korea: KOSPI recovering from Monday's crash. Micron's blowout earnings lifted chip names - SK Hynix and Samsung rallying on validated AI memory demand thesis.
Japan: Nikkei ~69,800, pulled back from 71,661 highs. BOJ at 1.0%. Semiconductor demand supportive but profit-taking after the recent run.
Europe: DAX steady. ECB holding at 2.25%. Benefiting from cheaper energy as Brent continues its decline from $90+ April highs.
China: CSI 300 ~4,920. Government support keeping a floor in place. July 24 tariff deadline increasingly important.
Emerging: MSCI EM watching July 24 tariff expiration on 60 economies and July 1 USMCA review date. Binary events ahead.
Bottom Line
  • KOSPI recovering from Monday's crash on Micron earnings validates that the selloff was a leveraged retail blowout, not a fundamental reckoning. SK Hynix and Samsung are rallying as Micron's $41.5B revenue and $31.3B in DRAM sales confirmed the AI memory demand thesis. The KOSPI crash-to-recovery arc in a single week is a textbook example of sentiment-driven volatility in a market with heavy retail leverage.
  • Cheaper oil benefits Europe disproportionately because European industry is more energy-intensive. Brent at $74.43 - down from $90+ in April - is a direct tailwind for manufacturing economies like Germany. The ECB holding at 2.25% while the Fed talks hikes creates a widening rate differential that weakens the euro and strengthens European export competitiveness. Watch for European earnings upgrades in Q3.
  • The July 24 tariff expiration on 60 countries and the July 1 USMCA review date are the two upcoming binary events for global trade. If the temporary 10% tariffs are extended or made permanent, emerging markets face significant headwinds. If they expire or are reduced, it removes a major overhang. China's CSI 300 near 4,920 with government support suggests Beijing is positioning defensively ahead of the deadline. These dates deserve more attention than they are getting.
