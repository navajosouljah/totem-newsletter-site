Savers Now Have 4.75% CDs. Stocks Face October Hike Decision at 55%.

The Quick Read

The S&P 500 fell 0.75% Wednesday to 7,706 - the Dow shed 351 points to 51,512 and the Nasdaq gave back 1.13% to 26,936, pulling back from an earlier intraday record near 27,244, as the 10-year Treasury surged to 5.12%: its highest level since 2007. Three simultaneous inputs drove the move - stronger-than-expected economic data, $101 Brent, and a weak Treasury auction each reinforcing the same case. Markets now price 55.1% odds of a second consecutive hike at October 28 FOMC - to 4.00-4.25%. The mechanism: a resilient economy keeps inflation elevated, the Fed keeps hiking, long rates keep rising, and rate-sensitive assets face compounding pressure.

Thursday's Freddie Mac PMMS is the first mortgage-rate reading since September 17's 6.95%, and the first to capture this week's 5.12% 10-year; the expected range is 7.00-7.08% - potentially the cycle's first above-7% print. Top 1-year CDs moved to 4.75% following the September 16 hike to 3.75-4.00%. At 55.1% October odds, the expected-value math on locking 4.75% now versus waiting for a post-hike 4.90-5.00% is the tightest it has been since the hike. The Xi-Trump bilateral also runs today in Washington - the first US-China meeting since the September 16 hike.

Brent holds near $101 as US-Iran talks in New York and a partial Saudi East-West pipeline restart pull crude slightly below last week's high without resolving the Hormuz supply structure. Corn harvest is 13% complete at 57% good-to-excellent - ahead of the five-year average pace but below year-ago quality. The CLARITY Act failed Senate cloture September 15 at 49-50; crypto is repricing to a post-legislative baseline with ETF inflows still positive. Q2 GDP (third estimate) and August PCE arrive September 30 - the last major reads before October 28.
