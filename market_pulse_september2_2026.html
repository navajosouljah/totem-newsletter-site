Market Pulse • Wednesday, September 2, 2026

Yields Ease and Stocks Snap a Three-Day Slide. Gold Bounces 1.1%.

The Quick Read

ADP printed 38,000 private jobs this morning, the smallest gain since January and under the 47,000 consensus, and the market rallied anyway. The reason is the bond market. The 10-year eased to 4.79% from a high of 4.81%, its loftiest since October 2023, and stocks took the breather. Warsh's test is "underlying trends," and a weak private payroll is the first data point since Jackson Hole that says the trend is softening. Hike odds still sit near 66%, and that is the whole tension: the labor data says pause, the ISM prices index at 71.1% says hike.

Friday's jobs report at 8:30 ET is now the tiebreaker. ADP has undershot the official number before; July's 46,000 ADP preceded a 23,000 loss. Below 100,000 Friday pulls September toward 45 to 50%. Above 150,000 with wages at 3.5% pushes it past 70%. Thursday brings Freddie Mac's survey and jobless claims first.

Underneath, two things did not move the way the headlines said they should. Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed until further notice" and hit US facilities in Jordan, Kuwait, and Bahrain after Tuesday's tanker strikes, and Brent closed up 0.2% at $94.86, with Bessent noting 17 million barrels transited Monday. And the Beige Book at 2 PM described modest growth, very slight hiring, and moderate price increases in eight of twelve districts, with energy the top uncertainty. Neither resolves before the Fed votes September 15.
