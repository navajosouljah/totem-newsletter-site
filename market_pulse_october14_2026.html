Savers Now Have 95% Odds of a Raise. Gold Got 2% Cheaper.

The Quick Read

September CPI landed hot at 8:30 AM ET: 3.7% headline against a 3.6% consensus, with core at 2.6% against 2.5%. Gasoline rose 9.1% in the month and delivered half the increase, but core also ran 0.3% for a second straight month, so the Fed cannot file this as a fuel story. Rate markets repriced within minutes: the 2-year jumped 12 basis points to 4.92% and the 10-year climbed to 5.29%. Higher yields raise the discount rate on every growth stock and every mortgage quote at the same time. That is the whole mechanism.

The print turned October 28 from a settled hold into a live meeting. CME FedWatch puts the odds of a quarter-point hike this month at 43.7%, up from 22.1% on Tuesday, and the chance of at least one hike by year-end above 95%. Thursday's PPI and retail sales at 8:30 AM ET are the last prints that can cool that number before the Fed's blackout begins Saturday.

Top 1-year CDs still pay 4.75% APY this afternoon, and the odds now favor that rate moving higher, not lower. Bank of America missed on a $1.2 billion regulatory settlement while Morgan Stanley beat. The USDA's Monday report put corn at 33% harvested, ahead of the five-year average for the first time this season. Wednesday October 14 - September CPI (released 8:30 AM ET) + Morgan Stanley & Bank of America earnings: ▲ headline 3.7% vs 3.6% consensus · core 2.6% vs 2.5% · gasoline +9.1% in the month · October 28 hike odds 43.7%The print came in hot on both lines. Gasoline supplied half of the headline increase, but core ran 0.3% for a second straight month, which is why the market moved October 28 from an 82% hold to a 43.7% hike chance and pushed year-end hike odds above 95%. The bank results completed the institutional read on Q3 consumer health: Morgan Stanley beat, Bank of America missed on a $1.2 billion settlement.
Thursday October 15 - PPI, Retail Sales, Jobless Claims (8:30 AM ET) + Freddie Mac PMMS: producer price index · advance September retail sales · weekly jobless claims · mortgage rate surveyThursday's four releases complete the week's data picture in a single morning. PPI is a leading indicator for future CPI - a hot PPI after a hot CPI is the doubly-bad scenario for December hike expectations. Retail sales confirm or contradict whether the intact labor market is still translating into consumer spending against $4.35 gas and 12.9% card delinquency. Freddie Mac PMMS captures this week's 10-year yield movement, including today's CPI reaction, in Thursday's mortgage-rate reading.
Friday October 16 - Import/Export Prices (8:30 AM ET) + Industrial Production (9:15 AM ET): trade price data with Brent at $106 · September manufacturing outputImport prices with Brent above $106 will reflect the September energy surge. Industrial production captures whether manufacturing held up under higher energy and financing costs - a read on Q4 capacity utilization entering the final quarter. Both complete the pre-FOMC-blackout data sequence. After Friday, no additional economic data lands before October 18's blackout period; the full picture for October 28 is assembled by end of this week.
Bottom line

Wednesday's CPI answered the December question and reopened October. Thursday's four data points are the last that can move the October 28 meeting before the FOMC blackout begins Saturday October 18. A hot PPI on top of a hot CPI makes an October hike the base case; a soft one keeps it a coin flip.
