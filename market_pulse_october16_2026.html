Thursday Closed Higher Despite Hot PPI. Hike Odds Fall to 13%.

The Quick Read

September PPI rose 1.3% month over month Thursday morning - the largest single-month jump since March 2022 - and markets closed up. The S&P gained 0.3%, the Dow jumped 1.1%, and the Nasdaq edged up 0.1%. The reason: energy drove over 80% of the headline gain, with final demand energy up 10.8%. Core PPI (less foods, energy, and trade services) rose 0.5% - elevated but not a shock. The Fed has signaled through multiple speeches that it responds to persistent core inflation, not commodity spikes with a direct external cause. Markets read Thursday's print as an energy event, not a policy event. That is the whole mechanism.

Friday's import prices (8:30 AM ET) and industrial production (9:15 AM ET) are the final two releases before the FOMC blackout begins Saturday. October 28 hike odds sit at 13% this morning - down from 31% Thursday morning before the data, reflecting the market's energy-versus-core read. For import prices to move October 28 back toward 30%, they would need to land far above the 0.9% forecast on top of PPI. A soft or in-line print cements the hold narrative heading into Saturday's blackout.

Underneath the rate story, Thursday's data showed a labor market tighter than the rate cycle has touched. Initial jobless claims came in at 199,000 - nearly 30,000 below the 229,000 consensus and the lowest four-week average since spring. Retail sales beat at 0.8%, though gasoline drove 71% of that gain and the core control group added only 0.1%. Top 1-year CDs still pay 4.50% APY this morning. Bitcoin is holding near $82,400 and gold is near $4,180 after Thursday's recovery. Curated from BLS · CME FedWatch · US Treasury · Freddie Mac · Bankrate · CoinMarketCap · Reuters
