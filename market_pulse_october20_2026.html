Market Pulse • Tuesday, October 20, 2026

Auto and Defense Answer Before Open. Tesla and Meta Set Wednesday's Direction.

The Quick Read

Monday's brief relief in yields and oil did not hold overnight. The 10-year Treasury climbed from 5.04% Monday morning to approximately 5.15% by Tuesday's pre-market; Brent crude rebounded from $105.2 back to near $110, restoring both pressures that markets had begun to unwind. GM reports before the open, giving the first answer on auto demand at 7.40% financing rates. RTX reports alongside it, testing whether defense revenue is tracking the geopolitical spending cycle. Those two results set the floor for Wednesday, when Tesla and Meta face the question the October Nasdaq selloff was really asking: whether AI-infrastructure and EV demand justify current borrowing costs. That is the whole mechanism.

Tuesday's morning anchor is September housing starts, due at 8:30 AM ET - arriving one day after NAHB's October builder confidence index landed at 29, a 14-year low and four points below consensus. A starts print below 1.35 million SAAR would confirm that sentiment and ground-breaking are moving together. GM's earnings consensus stands at approximately $3.63 EPS on $48.70 billion in revenue; a beat sets the consumer-demand baseline that Tesla will either confirm or complicate Wednesday evening. Netflix reports after Tuesday's close with consensus at $0.82 EPS and $12.87 billion in revenue - a streaming-consumer read on whether households are holding up under cost pressure.

The 4.75% CD available this morning - confirmed on Bankrate - pays 50 basis points more than the top HYSA's 4.25% floating rate. Yields rising to 5.15% overnight do not close the CD case; they extend it: a locked 12-month CD at 4.75% captures a yield the HYSA trails regardless of the rate direction from here. Iowa corn harvest at 26% against a five-year average of 50% means the acceleration confirmed in Monday's national 44% reading has not yet reached the state that produces more corn than any other.
