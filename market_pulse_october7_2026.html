S&P, Nasdaq at Records on Ceasefire Relief. FOMC Minutes at 2:00 PM ET.

The Quick Read

Tuesday’s US-Iran two-week ceasefire announcement drove both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq to fresh all-time records – the S&P closed at 7,818.93 (+0.58%) and the Nasdaq at 27,599.79 (+0.45%) – as oil fell from its Hormuz-premium high and yields pulled back from Monday’s ISM-driven spike. The mechanism: a supervised Hormuz reopening (vessels must coordinate with Iranian armed forces, but transit is permitted) removes the geopolitical supply-risk premium that had pushed Brent above $100, which compresses the near-term inflation pipeline from energy and lets the market price a modestly less aggressive Fed path into December. That is the whole mechanism: ceasefire relief + yield pullback = equity re-rating at record levels.

Wednesday’s FOMC minutes at 2:00 PM ET are now the session’s defining event, releasing the verbatim record of the September 15-16 committee discussion that voted 12-0 to hike to 3.75-4.00%. The key question the minutes settle: did the committee frame the 4.10% terminal rate as a ceiling or a floor? The ISM Services Prices Index at 74.0 percent – its highest reading since July 2022, a data point released after the September vote – is the context the minutes do not carry but the market will use to read them. Consumer Credit (August) follows at 3:00 PM ET as the week’s first household-finance read after the jobs miss. Thursday’s Freddie Mac PMMS closes the housing rate story, now carrying a lower 10-year baseline than last week’s survey.

Bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.2 billion in net inflows on Tuesday – the second-largest single-day haul since launch – as BlackRock’s IBIT led institutional capital into the ceasefire rally. Asian markets extended gains on Wednesday with the Nikkei adding to Tuesday’s advance. The Corn Belt is entering its most critical dry-weather window of the season; USDA Crop Progress for the week ending October 11 releases next Monday.
