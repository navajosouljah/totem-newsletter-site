Weekly Briefing • Tuesday, July 21, 2026

Tariffs Expire Thursday. Alphabet and Tesla Report Tuesday. Gas Crosses $4. Chip Stocks Enter Bear Market.

The densest week of the year begins. Section 122 tariffs expire Thursday at midnight with no extension in sight. Alphabet and Tesla report Tuesday - the first real test of AI spending at scale. Gas crossed $4.00 nationally as oil held above $82. The PHLX Semiconductor Index officially entered a bear market, down 20% from its June record. Iran signaled possible diplomacy over the weekend and futures rallied Tuesday morning.

This Week in the News

  • Section 122 tariffs expire Thursday at midnight with no congressional extension. The 10% universal import surcharge hits its 150-day statutory limit. USTR is finalizing Section 301 replacements - 12.5% on 46 countries, 10% on 14 others - with no rate cap and no expiration date.
  • Chip stocks officially entered a bear market. The PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 20% from its June record of 14,655, erasing $3.3 trillion in market value. A new open-source Chinese AI model raised questions about demand for expensive proprietary hardware.
  • National gas average crossed $4.00 for the first time since spring. Oil held above $82 as the Iran conflict continued through a ninth day of strikes. Brent touched $91.42 Tuesday morning before easing on diplomacy signals.
  • Alphabet and Tesla report Tuesday after close. The first major AI spending test of earnings season. Goldman posted record trading revenue of $7.42 billion last week. Netflix hit a 52-week low on weak guidance.

The Quick Read

Three binary events in four days define this week. Section 122 tariffs expire Thursday at midnight - either the effective U.S. tariff rate drops from 13% to 7%, or USTR's Section 301 replacement kicks in with rates as high as 12.5% on 46 economies and no sunset clause. Alphabet and Tesla report Tuesday - the first real test of whether AI capital spending is delivering returns at scale. And gas just crossed $4.00 nationally as oil held above $82 on the Iran conflict. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,458, down 1.6% for the week. The Nasdaq fell 2.9% as chip stocks entered a bear market. Mortgage rates ticked up to 6.55%.

The chip bear market is the structural story. The PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 20% from its June record, erasing $3.3 trillion in value, after a Chinese open-source AI model raised questions about whether the proprietary hardware buildout is overbuilt. This is not a one-day selloff - it is a reassessment of the AI thesis that powered markets all year. Alphabet's earnings Tuesday will either confirm the spending trajectory or accelerate the unwind.

Iran signaled possible mediator involvement over the weekend and Tuesday futures rallied - Nikkei +3%, KOSPI +4.5%. But gas at $4.00 nationally and Brent touching $91 Tuesday morning show the conflict is already flowing into consumer costs. The FOMC meets July 28-29 with oil elevated and the tariff regime in flux. The GDP advance estimate drops July 30. Five major catalysts in ten days.

Stock Market

Chip selloff dominated the week. Nasdaq fell 2.9%. Tuesday futures rallied on Iran diplomacy signals.

S&P 500: 7,458 −1.6% wk
Dow Jones: 52,146 −1.0% wk
Nasdaq: 25,520 −2.9% wk
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,962 −0.5% wk
Fear gauge (VIX): 18.77 elevated
Bottom Line
  • The chip bear market is the headline but the divergence underneath matters more. The Nasdaq fell 2.9% while the Russell lost only 0.5% - money is rotating out of AI and mega-cap tech into smaller, more domestically oriented companies. Apple was the only Mag 7 name in the green last week, gaining 5.8%.
  • Tuesday futures rallied on Iran diplomacy hopes - Nikkei +3%, KOSPI +4.5% overnight. But with Alphabet and Tesla reporting Tuesday and tariffs expiring Thursday, the market has to navigate two more binary events before the week is over. The VIX at 18.77 suggests traders are not yet positioned for the volatility this calendar demands.

Interest Rates

Yields held steady despite the CPI beat. The bond market is watching oil and tariffs, not backward-looking inflation data.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held; 89% probability of hold at July 28-29 FOMC)
2-year Treasury: 4.18%
10-year Treasury: 4.55%
30-year Treasury: 5.06% still above 5%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.55% highest in almost a year per Fox Business
Bottom Line
  • Mortgage rates at 6.55% - the highest in almost a year - despite June CPI falling to 3.5% tells you everything about the disconnect between inflation data and borrowing costs. The bond market is pricing in oil risk and tariff uncertainty, not the backward-looking CPI improvement.
  • The FOMC on July 28-29 faces an unusual setup: the best CPI print in two years, oil above $82, and a tariff regime that may restructure entirely three days before the meeting. The statement language on "transitory" energy versus "persistent" trade policy will set the rate outlook for Q3.

Top CD rates holding near 4.30%. With CPI at 3.5%, savers are earning a real positive return for the first time in months.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.65-2.00% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.30% APY (Newtek Bank, Popular Direct 4.17%, E*TRADE 4.10%)
Best credit union CD: 6.00% APY (Financial Partners CU, 8-month)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • A 4.30% CD with CPI at 3.5% means savers are earning 80 basis points of real return above inflation - the first meaningfully positive real yield on cash in over two years. If the FOMC holds and oil keeps CPI elevated through Q3, these rates persist. The case for locking in remains strong.

Energy & Gas Prices

Gas crossed $4.00. Brent touched $91 Tuesday morning. The Iran conflict is now directly hitting consumer wallets.

WTI crude: $82.40/bbl held above $82 since last week
Brent crude: ~$88/bbl touched $91.42 Tuesday morning before easing
National gas avg: $4.02/gal crossed $4.00 threshold
West Coast (California): $5.50/gal elevated
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.50/gal
Bottom Line
  • Gas crossing $4.00 nationally is the threshold where consumers start changing behavior - fewer discretionary trips, more price comparison, tighter household budgets. At $4.02, the average household is spending roughly $200 more per month on fuel than they were when gas was $3.34 at the Gulf Coast. The K-shaped split widens: California at $5.50 is paying 57% more than the Gulf.
  • Iran signaling diplomacy over the weekend eased Brent from $91 back to $88, but the Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea remain disrupted. Even a diplomatic resolution takes weeks to flow through to pump prices. The July CPI (reported in August) will capture this oil spike - the June disinflation story is already behind us.

Commodities

Gold recovered above $4,000. Cattle fell sharply. Grains firming on heat wave pressure.

Gold / oz: ~$4,030 recovered above $4,000 on geopolitical risk
Silver / oz: $58.55 flat
Copper / lb: $6.29 demand signal
Corn / bushel: ~$4.38 steady
Soybeans / bushel: ~$11.97 steady
Live cattle / cwt: $237 −$10/cwt from prior week - relief for beef prices
Bottom Line
  • Gold recovering above $4,000 after falling below it last week shows the safe-haven bid is back - but driven by Iran diplomacy uncertainty rather than rate-cut positioning. If the conflict resolves, gold gives back the bounce. If it escalates, $4,100+ is in play.
  • Cattle falling $10/cwt to $237 is the most meaningful grocery relief of the month. But the structural floor remains: the US herd at 86.2 million head is the smallest since 1951. Every pullback in cattle is a temporary reprieve, not a trend change.

Crop Weather

Corn conditions stable at 68% good-to-excellent. Heat wave pressure building in northern Plains. Winter wheat harvest continues.

Corn: 68% good-to-excellent +1 pt. Iowa leading at 78%. Silking 38% complete, 5 points ahead of last year.
Soybeans: 65% good-to-excellent. Steady improvement.
Drought: Heat wave hit northern production areas week of July 14-18. Irrigation stress increasing in Plains and western areas. Nebraska and western Kansas remain under pressure.
Bottom Line
  • Corn at 68% good-to-excellent and running 5 points ahead on silking is the best crop news in weeks. If the heat wave that hit the northern Plains last week does not persist through pollination, the supply picture stabilizes. The next two USDA reports are the ones that matter.

Real Estate

ATTOM's mid-year report confirms foreclosure acceleration. Median price hit a record. Housing starts surged on multifamily but permits fell.

Foreclosure filings H1 2026: 227,548 +21% YoY, +28% from two years ago
Foreclosure starts: +18% YoY
REO completions: +33% YoY
Avg time in foreclosure: 563 days (shortest since 2013)
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.88% highest since Q2 2021
Existing-home sales: 3.73M ann. −2.4% MoM
Median existing-home price: $440,600 all-time record, +1.8% YoY
Housing starts: 1.427M +19% MoM - but multifamily; single-family flat
30-year mortgage: 6.55% elevated
Bottom Line
  • ATTOM's mid-year report is definitive: foreclosure filings up 21% year-over-year with the fastest time-to-foreclosure since 2013 means the distress pipeline is accelerating. Florida leads at 1 in 373 homes with a filing. This is not 2008 - volumes remain well below crisis levels - but the direction is clear and consistent across every metric.
  • The median existing-home price hitting $440,600 while sales fall and foreclosures rise is the K-shaped housing market at work. Prices hold because supply is tight (4.6 months), but the stress underneath is building among FHA borrowers and first-time buyers who purchased at peak rates.

Household Finance

New-car payments hit another record at $777/month. Nearly a quarter of auto loans now stretch past 7 years. Savings rate at 3.0%.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.252 trillion; avg APR 20.94%
Avg. new-car payment: $777/mo Q2 record - third consecutive record quarter
84+ month auto loans: 23.9% of new financings record
Subprime auto 60+ days delinquent: 6.65% highest since 1990s
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 3.0% (May 2026)
Bottom Line
  • New-car payments hitting $777/month with nearly a quarter of buyers stretching past 7 years is the consumer finance story in one data point. The average financed amount is $44,156 at 7.0% APR. These are not luxury purchases - they are transportation costs that compress every other budget line item.
  • The savings rate recovering to 3.0% from 2.6% was driven by falling gas prices in May. With gas now at $4.02 and rising, that cushion is already under pressure. The June savings rate (due July 30 with the GDP report) will tell us whether the consumer held or gave back the gains.

Bitcoin (BTC): $65,000 +1.0%
Ethereum (ETH): $1,940 +4.2%
Solana (SOL): $78.41 +2.6%
XRP: $1.13 +3.7%
Bottom Line
  • All four major cryptos rallying on a Tuesday when equity futures are also green suggests crypto is trading as a risk-on asset tied to Iran diplomacy hopes. Ethereum's 4.2% jump leads the group - likely positioning ahead of Alphabet and Tesla earnings, which carry AI narrative weight for the entire digital asset space.
  • Crypto ETFs drawing $271 million in inflows extends the institutional buying streak. The structural demand floor from ETF inflows is now a persistent feature of the market, adding buying pressure regardless of short-term sentiment swings.

Around the World

Japan bounced 3% Tuesday after its worst week of the year. Europe flat. China under pressure.

Japan: Nikkei at 63,674 −6.4% last wk, worst week of 2026. Tuesday bounced +3% on Iran peace signals.
Europe: STOXX 600 flat for the week (+0.07%). DAX −0.9%. European banks fell on gas inflation risk.
China: Shanghai Composite at 3,765 −3.0% on Friday alone. Down 1.9% YTD. Domestic stimulus not offsetting global risk-off.
Emerging: Under pressure from strong dollar and oil surge. Tariff expiration Thursday is the key catalyst.
Bottom Line
  • Japan's Nikkei falling 6.4% then bouncing 3% Tuesday on Iran headlines shows how sensitive global markets are to a single geopolitical variable right now. The Tuesday rally is positioning, not conviction. If Alphabet earnings disappoint or the tariff transition is messy, the bounce reverses.
  • The tariff expiration Thursday reshapes trade for every economy on this list. If Section 301 replaces Section 122 at higher rates with no sunset, emerging markets face a permanent cost increase rather than a temporary surcharge. The structure of the replacement matters more than the headline rate.
