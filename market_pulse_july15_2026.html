Weekly Briefing • Tuesday, July 15, 2026

Back-to-Back Inflation Beats. PPI Confirms the CPI Story. Iran Escalates - Jordan Intercepts Missiles, Brent Hits $86.

Two days, two inflation beats. The PPI fell 0.3% in June - the second straight below-consensus print after yesterday's CPI dropped to 3.5%. Core producer prices rose just 0.2%. The rate-cut case is building. But Iran's war is building faster: the IRGC targeted U.S. bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan. Jordan shot down three Iranian ballistic missiles at dawn. Brent crude climbed to $86.

This Week in the News

  • PPI fell 0.3% in June - the second straight inflation beat. Producer prices for goods dropped 1.4%, driven by falling fuel and energy costs. Core PPI rose 0.2%, below the 0.3% consensus. Back-to-back sub-consensus prints strengthen the case for the Fed to hold rather than hike at the July 28-29 meeting.
  • Iran targeted U.S. military bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan. The IRGC launched retaliatory strikes after four nights of U.S. attacks on Iranian coastal targets. Jordan's military intercepted three ballistic missiles at dawn. The conflict is expanding beyond Iran-U.S. into a regional confrontation.
  • Brent crude climbed to $86 as the Hormuz conflict widens. Oil has risen 20% in two weeks from $72 pre-escalation. Two supertankers remain disabled in the Strait. War risk premiums on shipping continue to climb. The energy deflation that drove the CPI beat is being erased in real time.
  • Crypto rallied on the inflation data. Bitcoin jumped 3.6% to $64,751, Ethereum surged 4.9% to $1,874. The market is pricing in rate-cut expectations more aggressively than equities.

The Quick Read

The PPI confirmed what the CPI started. Producer prices fell 0.3% in June, with goods prices dropping 1.4% on falling fuel costs. Core PPI at 0.2% landed below consensus. Two straight inflation beats in two days give the Fed its clearest window to hold rates - or even signal a cut path - at the July 28-29 meeting. But the data tells a story about June, not July. Brent crude is at $86 today, up 20% from the $72 level when the June data was collected. Jordan intercepting Iranian ballistic missiles at dawn confirms the conflict is widening beyond the Strait of Hormuz.

Stocks edged higher on the PPI data, with the S&P 500 near 7,560 and the Nasdaq climbing 0.5% on tech strength. Crypto moved faster - Bitcoin jumped 3.6% to $64,751 and Ethereum surged 4.9%, pricing in rate-cut expectations more aggressively than equities. Gold fell to $4,032, continuing its puzzling failure to rally on either inflation progress or geopolitical risk. The 10-year Treasury yield pulled back to 4.57% from its 4.62% high, acknowledging the inflation data but not yet pricing in cuts.

The tension between the backward-looking data and the forward-looking energy picture defines the next two weeks. The July 24 tariff deadline on 60+ countries is nine days away. The FOMC meets July 28-29. If oil stays above $85 through month-end, the August CPI report will reverse the progress that June delivered. Housing remains frozen at a 4.09 million sales pace with a record $440,600 median price. The consumer below the median is running on a $20-per-week gas margin that is shrinking as Brent climbs.

Stock Market

Back-to-back inflation beats lifted tech and rate-sensitive names. The Dow lagged as defensive sectors sold off.

S&P 500: ~7,560 +0.2%
Dow Jones: ~52,550 flat to +0.1%
Nasdaq: ~26,250 +0.5% (tech and chips leading on ASML earnings)
Russell 2000 (small caps): ~2,975 +0.4%
Fear gauge (VIX): ~16.0 down slightly
Bottom Line
  • Two straight sub-consensus inflation prints and the market is barely moving. The S&P rising 0.2% on a PPI beat while Brent trades at $86 shows a market that cannot decide whether to celebrate the data or fear the oil. The VIX near 16 with a widening Middle East conflict and a tariff deadline nine days away is pricing in a best-case scenario.
  • Tech leadership continues as rate-cut expectations firm. But the narrow breadth problem persists - health care, consumer staples, and real estate all fell on both CPI and PPI days.

Interest Rates

The PPI reinforced the CPI story. The 10-year pulled back from its two-month high. But mortgage rates ticked higher - the bond market and the mortgage market are telling different stories.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held since December 2025)
2-year Treasury: ~4.18% −3 bps
10-year Treasury: 4.57% −6 bps from 4.62% high
30-year Treasury: ~5.02% slipping below 5% threshold
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.57% +8 bps from last week's 6.49%
Bottom Line
  • The 10-year falling 6 basis points from its 4.62% high is the bond market acknowledging the inflation data. But it is still above 4.55%, which means the market sees one good CPI-PPI combo as progress, not a trend. The FOMC needs two or three clean prints before moving.
  • Mortgage rates at 6.57% - up from 6.49% last week despite the inflation beat - show the disconnect. Mortgage spreads have widened as oil risk reprices the long end. Falling yields and rising mortgage rates can happen simultaneously when credit risk increases.

Savings & CD Rates

CD rates holding steady. The back-to-back inflation beats make a near-term rate hike less likely, which means these yields persist.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.99% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.17-4.30% APY (Newtek Bank 4.30%, Popular Direct 4.17%)
Best credit union CD: 5.00% APY (A+ Federal Credit Union, 12-month)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • With headline CPI at 3.5% and these CDs paying 4.30-5.00%, savers are earning a meaningful real return. The inflation data makes a rate cut more plausible in Q4, so the window to lock in at these levels may be shorter than it looked a week ago.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil climbed again as the Iran conflict widened to Jordan. The pump is starting to catch up - national average now $3.89, up $0.09 in a week.

WTI crude: ~$80/bbl up from $79.34 yesterday
Brent crude: $86.19/bbl +14% from last week's $75.53
National gas avg: $3.89/gal +$0.03 from yesterday
California: ~$5.25/gal rising
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.45/gal
Bottom Line
  • Brent at $86 is now 20% above where it was when the June CPI data was collected. Every dollar of the 10% gasoline decline that drove the CPI to 3.5% is being given back. If crude holds above $85 for another week, expect the national average to cross $4.00 by late July - erasing the consumer tailwind that made June's inflation print possible.
  • The conflict expanding to Jordan changes the risk calculus. This is no longer just a Hormuz shipping story - it is a regional conflict involving U.S. ally military infrastructure. Each escalation step raises the floor under oil prices and shortens the timeline for pump prices to catch up.

Commodities

Gold fell despite the PPI beat. Cattle dropped sharply. Grains steady.

Gold / oz: $4,032 −0.9% from yesterday's $4,070
Silver / oz: ~$58 flat
Copper / lb: $6.33 flat
Corn / bushel: $4.39 flat
Soybeans / bushel: $11.93 −0.8%
Live cattle / cwt: $231.42 −$3.31 from Monday, −$6.30 in two days
Bottom Line
  • Gold falling to $4,032 on a day with both a PPI beat and expanding Middle East conflict is the third consecutive session where gold failed to rally on a clear catalyst. The safe-haven trade appears exhausted at current levels. A move lower toward $3,900 is more plausible than a move to $4,200 unless the Fed explicitly signals cuts.
  • Cattle dropping $6.30 in two days to $231.42 is the sharpest pullback since May. But with the U.S. herd near 86 million head - the smallest since 1951 - the structural floor under beef prices remains. This looks like profit-taking, not a trend reversal.

Crop Weather

Crop conditions holding. Wheat quality remains near 60-year lows. Drought improving but still widespread.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent (steady). Pollination window continues.
Winter wheat: 26% good-to-excellent - down 22 points from a year ago. Harvest 59% complete.
Soybeans: 64% good-to-excellent (down 1 pt).
Drought: ~46% of Lower 48 in drought, improving slightly.
Bottom Line
  • Corn at 67% through mid-July pollination is the best-case scenario for supply. If ratings hold here through the next USDA report, corn-driven food inflation stays contained through Q4.
  • Winter wheat quality at 26% good-to-excellent versus 48% a year ago is locked in. Higher flour and cereal costs in Q3-Q4 are a certainty regardless of what oil does.

Real Estate

No new housing data today. The June picture remains - frozen sales, record prices, and rising distress at the edges.

Existing-home sales (June): 4.09M ann. −2.4% MoM
Median existing-home price: $440,600 record high
Inventory: 4.6 months
Foreclosure starts (May): 27,304 +13% YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.88% highest since Q2 2021
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
30-year mortgage: 6.57% +8 bps wk
Bottom Line
  • Mortgage rates at 6.57% - now higher than last week despite the CPI-PPI double beat - are the housing market's problem in a single number. Rates are not following Treasury yields lower because mortgage spreads are widening on oil-driven uncertainty. The housing market needs rates below 6% to meaningfully unfreeze, and that is not happening soon.
  • The PPI beat theoretically supports the case for lower rates eventually, but "eventually" does not help the buyer facing a $440,600 median price and a $2,900/month payment at 6.57%.

Household Finance

Consumer balance sheets unchanged from yesterday. The inflation beat is good news for purchasing power, but gas prices climbing toward $4 threaten to erase the benefit.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record (Q1 2026)
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion down from $1.28T in Q4 (seasonal); avg APR 21.0%
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo 20.3% of buyers now pay $1,000+/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days delinquent: 6.90% 32-year high (Fitch)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 3.0% holding (May 2026)
Bottom Line
  • The CPI-PPI double beat is constructive for the consumer in theory - falling producer prices should eventually flow through to lower shelf prices. But that transmission takes 2-3 months, and gas prices are rising now. The consumer below the median feels the pump before the grocery shelf.
  • The savings rate at 3.0% remains the fragile indicator. If gas crosses $4.00 nationally, the savings recovery stalls and credit card balances - which dipped seasonally in Q1 - resume climbing.

Crypto

Crypto rallied hard on the back-to-back inflation beats. Bitcoin and Ethereum pricing in rate cuts more aggressively than equities.

Bitcoin (BTC): $64,751 +3.6%
Ethereum (ETH): $1,874 +4.9%
Solana (SOL): $77.54 +2.9%
XRP: $1.10 +3.5%
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin jumping 3.6% to $64,751 - its highest level since early June - shows crypto trading as a pure rate-cut proxy. Two sub-consensus inflation prints in two days triggered the sharpest crypto rally in a month. If the July 28-29 FOMC signals any dovish lean, BTC could test $70,000.
  • Ethereum surging 4.9% to $1,874 is the strongest single-day move since May. ETH is more rate-sensitive than BTC because DeFi activity accelerates when borrowing costs decline. The SEC's "Regulation Crypto" agenda expected this month adds a structural catalyst.

Around the World

Global markets mixed. Asia pulled back on oil. Europe faces the sharpest energy cost pressure.

Japan: Nikkei 66,981 (−0.4%). Pulling back on oil costs and yen strength.
Europe: Under pressure as Brent at $86 raises input costs. ECB rate path increasingly complicated by energy-driven inflation.
China: Shanghai Composite near 3,960. Property sector fragile, government stimulus preventing broader selloff.
Emerging: Double squeeze risk - rising oil plus July 24 tariff expiration (9 days away).
Bottom Line
  • The Iran conflict expanding to Jordan - with ballistic missiles intercepted over Jordanian airspace - changes the regional risk map. This is no longer a bilateral U.S.-Iran Hormuz dispute. If Gulf allies face direct Iranian missile attacks, the oil risk premium moves from temporary disruption pricing to sustained conflict pricing.
  • The July 24 tariff expiration is nine days away and still underpriced. The combination of Brent above $85 and potential permanent tariffs on 60+ economies creates a scenario where emerging market currencies and equities face simultaneous pressure from energy costs and trade barriers.
