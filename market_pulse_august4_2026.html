Tuesday Briefing • Tuesday, August 4, 2026

ISM Manufacturing Surges to 55.6 - Best in Four Years, vs. an Expected Third Straight Contraction - as Trump Cancels Iran Strikes, Oil Falls 7%, and the Dow Closes at a Record 53,178. SpaceX Reports Its First Public Earnings This Evening. Iran's Foreign Ministry Disputes the Peace-Talks Framing. JOLTS Job Openings at 10 AM ET.

Monday produced two simultaneous surprises that reversed the week's opening narrative in a single session: the factory sector printed its strongest reading since May 2022 and Trump announced he had canceled planned Iran military strikes in favor of diplomacy, cutting oil by more than $6 per barrel and lifting the Dow to its first record close in months. The resolution is incomplete. Iran's foreign ministry says no peace talks are scheduled, leaving oil's 7% drop resting on a claim the other side has not confirmed.

The Quick Read

What Monday changed: The S&P 500 closed at 7,600.50, up 1.48% - its best single-session gain in weeks. The Dow added 693 points to a record close of 53,178. Both moves were driven by two data prints that arrived within hours of each other and pulled in the same direction: ISM Manufacturing jumped to 55.6 for July - the highest reading since May 2022, well above the 49.5 consensus, and a decisive exit from the two consecutive months of sub-50 contraction the June and May readings had confirmed - and WTI crude oil fell more than 7% to approximately $79 per barrel after Trump announced he had called off planned military strikes on Iran and was pursuing diplomatic negotiations.

The timing matters. Monday's Market Pulse went out describing ISM as the first test of whether the factory sector would post a third straight contraction - a signal that would have strengthened the dovish case ahead of Friday's jobs report. Instead, 55.6 delivered the opposite message: manufacturing is not the weak link in this economy. It is the strong link. Production jumped 6.3 points to its highest level in nearly five years. The employment index broke into expansion territory for the first time in 33 months. The ISM's own historical model maps a 55.6 PMI to approximately +2.8% annualized real GDP growth. That reading does not soften the September rate hike argument. It strengthens it.

The Iran development is more complicated. Trump said he called off strikes after receiving a request from Tehran and announced the "perimeters of a deal" had been agreed to. Iran's foreign ministry said no such diplomatic outreach has been agreed to and no talks are scheduled. Oil's 7% single-day drop is predicated on a de-escalation the other side has explicitly contradicted. WTI at $79 is priced for a resolution that may not arrive. That distinction matters for oil prices, for gas prices, and for the Fed's inflation calculus heading into the August 12 CPI print.

Today's calendar is consequential: JOLTS June job openings at 10:00 AM ET (consensus around 7.3 million, down from May's 7.7 million - the first post-ISM labor read of the week), SpaceX Q2 2026 earnings after market close at 4:30 PM ET (the company's first report as a public company, with a $116 billion insider lock-up expiration approaching), and AMD, Palantir, Caterpillar, McDonald's, and Merck also reporting today.

Tuesday Morning

  • ISM Manufacturing PMI hit 55.6 in July - a four-year high and a decisive reversal of the contraction trend that had been building all summer. The consensus was 49.5, which would have marked a third straight month below 50. The actual print was 6.1 points higher and firmly in expansion territory. Production surged to 58.5, the highest in nearly five years. Employment hit 52.8, the highest since August 2022 and the first expansion reading for factory employment in 33 months. New orders came in at 56.7 and new export orders at 53.0 - both expanding. The ISM's historical mapping puts a 55.6 reading at approximately +2.8% annualized real GDP growth. This is not a soft-landing number. It is a hard-expansion number, and it makes the September rate hike case more difficult to dismiss.
  • Trump announced Monday he canceled planned military strikes on Iran and is pursuing diplomatic negotiations. He said the "perimeters of a deal" had been agreed to. WTI crude fell more than 7% to approximately $79 per barrel - from roughly $85 before the announcement. Brent fell from approximately $90 to $83. The Dow surged 693 points to a record close of 53,178. The S&P 500 gained 1.48% to 7,600.50. The Nasdaq gained 2.1% to 25,913.
  • Iran's foreign ministry disputed Trump's characterization of the situation. A spokesperson said Iran had not agreed to any diplomatic engagement and no peace talks are scheduled. The statement arrived after the US market close, meaning Tuesday opens with an unresolved question: oil's 7% drop is priced on a claim the other party has explicitly contradicted. WTI at $79 is the first test of that discrepancy.
  • Amazon crossed $3 trillion in market capitalization on Monday. The milestone came on the heels of its strongest post-earnings trading performance in years. AWS has been the engine - AI infrastructure demand has reaccelerated cloud revenue growth at a pace that most analysts did not model going into 2026. Amazon was one of several mega-cap names that contributed to Monday's Nasdaq rally.
  • USDA Crop Progress released Monday confirmed a fourth consecutive week of declining corn ratings. Good-to-excellent ratings fell to approximately 61% - down from 63% the prior week - as heat stress continued through grain fill's most critical window. 47.9% of the Lower 48 remains in moderate drought or worse. The August 12 USDA crop production report, which will be the first official yield estimate incorporating July's heat stress, is now 8 days away.
  • SpaceX reports its first public earnings this evening at 4:30 PM ET. The company posted a $4.28 billion net loss in Q1 2026 on $4.69 billion in revenue. Starlink had 10.3 million subscribers as of March 31, up 105% year-over-year. Analysts are focused on Starlink's path to profitability, AI capital spending levels, launch economics, and management guidance for the second half of 2026. A $116 billion insider lock-up expiration is approaching, adding a separate pressure to how the market responds to any guidance that disappoints.

Stock Market

Monday's Dow record and S&P 500 gain were real and driven by real data - but one of the two catalysts (Iran peace talks) has already been disputed by one of the two parties involved.

S&P 500 (Monday Aug 3 close): 7,600.50 +1.48% on the day; best single-session gain in weeks; nearing all-time record territory
Dow Jones Industrial Average (Monday close): 53,178.41 +693 pts, +1.32% on the day; first record close in months
Nasdaq Composite (Monday close): 25,913.9 +2.10% Monday; mega-cap tech led; Amazon, Nvidia, Meta among top contributors
S&P 500 futures (Tuesday premarket): +0.20% to +0.30% - modest follow-through after Monday's gains; Iran denial limiting the upside
Q2 earnings season: ~86% beat rate; aggregate S&P 500 profits +47% YoY strongest earnings season since 2021; earnings and macro data both pointing to expansion
VIX (Monday close): eased on risk-on session; below 15; fear premium at lowest level in weeks
Bottom Line
  • The ISM 55.6 print reframes the week's narrative in a way that is harder to walk back than a geopolitical headline. Going into Monday, markets were weighing a factory sector in its third potential contraction against a still-elevated September hike probability. ISM at 55.6 says the factory sector is not the weak link. It is the strongest link in the economic chain right now. That changes how the jobs report Friday is interpreted - a strong factory sector implies a labor market that may still be adding manufacturing jobs, which means the risk to Friday's number is more upside than down.
  • Monday's Dow record carries a qualification: part of the 693-point gain is oil's 7% decline on a peace-talks claim Iran's foreign ministry has since rejected. If Tuesday's early trading reflects Iran's position in crude prices, some of the equity market's risk-premium relief will need to be repriced. The ISM foundation is solid. The Iran foundation is contested.

Interest Rates

ISM Manufacturing at 55.6 is not a number that gives the Fed cover to pause. September hike probability moved higher Monday evening on the data.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% held July 29; 9-3 vote; three dissents in favor of +25bp hike; next meeting September 15-16
September hike probability (futures market, Aug 4 morning): ~66% moved up from 64.5% after ISM 55.6; market now pricing the hike as more likely than the hold with fresh economic strength as the driver
2-year Treasury: ~4.25% front end holding; ISM strength offsetting some of the oil-driven disinflation relief
10-year Treasury: ~4.70% eased from ~4.75% on Monday's risk-on session; lower oil expectations reduced near-term inflation premium slightly; ISM strength partially countered that relief
30-year Treasury: ~5.18% eased slightly from Monday's open; long-end fiscal premium intact
30-year mortgage (Freddie Mac, July 30): 6.66% last official weekly reading; next reading August 7
30-year mortgage (daily, Aug 4, Bankrate): ~6.80% daily surveys running above the weekly Freddie Mac; bond market movement since July 30 survey has not fully worked through
Bottom Line
  • ISM Manufacturing at 55.6 is the most direct argument the hawks on the Federal Reserve have received in months. The three FOMC members who voted to hike on July 29 were arguing that the economy is still strong enough to absorb another quarter-point increase without breaking growth. Monday's factory data - the strongest in four years, with employment breaking into expansion for the first time in 33 months - validates that argument more directly than any Fed speech this week will. A 66% September hike probability reflects the market's updated read: strong economy, still-elevated inflation, three active dissenters. The probability moves again with Friday's jobs number.
  • The 10-year eased slightly on Monday's Iran-driven oil drop. If oil partially reverses Tuesday on Iran's denial, that easing gives back. The mortgage rate path this week depends almost entirely on whether WTI holds near $79 or bounces back toward $83-85. Every $5 sustained change in WTI has historically correlated with approximately 5-10 basis points on the 10-year over 2-3 weeks. The next Freddie Mac reading Friday morning will capture whatever move the jobs report produces - and potentially whatever oil does between now and Thursday.

Savings & CD Rates

ISM 55.6 strengthens the September hike case. The window for locking in today's 4.40% before that hike materializes is now a defined timeline.

National avg 1-yr CD (FDIC, July): 1.68% APY what most deposit dollars still earn at the average bank
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.40% APY (E*TRADE, no minimum); a 2.6x premium over the national average on the same FDIC-insured product
Best credit union 1-yr CD: 4.05% APY (NASA Federal CU, $1K minimum)
Bottom Line
  • If the Fed hikes to 3.75-4.00% in September, the 1-year CD market's best available rates will likely move toward 4.50-4.65% shortly after the meeting. The question for a saver who can access 4.40% today is whether the expected 10-25 basis point improvement in six weeks is worth leaving money at 1.68% at the average bank in the meantime. For money already in a high-yield account, the case to lock is about certainty, not yield maximization. For money still sitting at the national average, the current 4.40% is already 2.72 percentage points better than doing nothing - the September hike is upside, not the baseline.
  • The manufacturing strength in Monday's ISM adds to the rate hike confidence but does not change the practical timeline. September 15-16 is still 42 days away. A CD ladder across 6-month, 12-month, and 18-month maturities - opened this week before the jobs report - captures today's rates on the short end and positions for potentially better rates on the longer end if the September hike arrives and proves not to be the last.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil fell 7% Monday on Trump's Iran announcement. Iran's denial of any talks means the price drop is built on contested ground.

WTI Crude (Tuesday morning): ~$79/barrel down from ~$85 before Monday's Trump-Iran announcement; Iran foreign ministry has since denied any talks are scheduled; price may be fragile
Brent Crude: ~$83/barrel down from ~$90; both benchmarks priced for resolution that Tehran has not confirmed
Natural gas (Henry Hub): ~$2.80/MMBtu trending near recent range; summer cooling demand tapering slightly
National gas avg (AAA/EIA, Aug 3): $4.09/gal still pricing the ~$85 WTI range from last week; pump relief from crude's fall will arrive with the usual 2-4 week lag
West Coast: ~$5.12/gal California near $5.49; widest regional spread in the country
Gulf Coast: ~$3.69/gal cheapest region nationally; will show crude relief first when it arrives
OPEC+ context: Seven OPEC+ members approved a production increase of approximately 188,000 barrels per day from September - an independent supply factor that adds modest downward price pressure separate from the Iran situation
Bottom Line
  • WTI at $79 is the lowest price since mid-July, and it carries a footnote: Iran said there are no talks. The 7% Monday drop was driven almost entirely by one geopolitical announcement from one side - not by supply changes, OPEC decisions, or demand data. The OPEC+ production increase does provide some independent downward pressure (approximately 188,000 barrels per day from September), but that is a modest figure relative to global supply. If Iran's foreign ministry position holds and Tuesday confirms it in the market, crude could recover $3-5 per barrel before the pump price adjustment the Monday drop implied has had time to begin.
  • The path from crude to pump still matters for household budgets. WTI peaked at $93 in mid-July. At $79 today that is a $14 per barrel decline over three weeks. The 2-to-4 week pump lag means the relief from the July-to-mid-July decline was just beginning to arrive at the pump this week. If $79 holds, consumers will get the relief. If crude bounces back to $83-85 on the Iran dispute, the pump will have barely moved before the relief reverses. EIA's own 3Q26 forecast projects the national average easing toward $3.80 by quarter-end - that assumes $80-85 WTI, which is where today's price sits.

Commodities

Gold held above $4,050 through a risk-on rally - a structural signal that the safe-haven bid is not simply a fear trade. Copper validates the ISM manufacturing reading directly.

Gold / oz: $4,054 +0.27% Monday; holding above $4,050 through a Dow record session - typically a headwind for safe-haven assets; structural buying absorbing the equity competition
Silver / oz: $58.26 +0.81% Monday; solar and electronics demand supporting the industrial component of silver's dual role
Copper / lb: $6.47 +0.55% Monday; ISM Manufacturing's production surge at 58.5 is exactly the kind of demand signal copper prices on; +5.10% over the past month; +45.74% year-over-year
Corn (Sep) / bu: ~$4.62 fourth consecutive week of crop rating decline; USDA first official yield estimate August 12
Live cattle / lb: ~$2.27 food-price channel open; beef costs holding elevated into fall grilling season
Bottom Line
  • Gold at $4,054 is the data point that does not fit the standard risk-on playbook. When equities rally 1.5%, money typically rotates out of safe-haven assets. Gold gained Monday anyway - modest gain, but a gain. The structural drivers that have kept gold above $4,000 for three consecutive weeks - central bank buying, real-rate positioning, geopolitical hedging that does not go away when one side announces talks the other side denies - are providing a floor that one Dow record session cannot displace.
  • Copper at $6.47 and the ISM 55.6 reading are the most consistent pair of data points in Tuesday's economic picture. Copper prices raw industrial demand without the safe-haven overlay that complicates gold's signal. A manufacturing sector at 55.6 with production at 58.5 is the kind of factory activity that moves copper. The year-over-year copper gain of +45.74% was not a 2025 number - it has been continuously validated by 2026 industrial data. ISM Monday confirmed that the manufacturing demand is real.

Crop Weather

A fourth consecutive week of declining corn ratings arrives 8 days before the USDA's first official yield estimate. The August 12 crop report is now the food-price alarm to watch.

Corn (USDA Crop Progress, Aug 3 reading): ~61% good-to-excellent fourth consecutive weekly decline; down from 63% (July 27) and 67% (July 13); heat stress during grain fill's most sensitive window
Drought (July 27): 47.9% of the Lower 48 in moderate drought or worse Northern Plains deteriorated for a third straight week; Corn Belt heat stress concentrated through grain fill
Key date ahead: August 12 - USDA crop production report (first official yield estimate incorporating July heat stress) lands the same morning as July CPI
Development status: corn now in dough-to-dent transition; heat sensitivity window has not fully closed
Bottom Line
  • Four consecutive weeks of declining corn ratings during grain fill is the setup for a below-trend yield estimate on August 12. The USDA's first official production report will incorporate July's heat stress data across the Corn Belt and Northern Plains - the same drought conditions that drove the four-week rating decline. A below-consensus yield estimate arriving simultaneously with July CPI creates a specific scenario the Fed's dissenters are watching: food prices re-accelerating while overall inflation is still above 3%, on the same morning that the USDA confirms a supply shortfall in the commodity most broadly embedded in the American food chain.
  • Oil's drop Monday temporarily eased the commodity inflation picture. Food prices do not move with oil on a 24-hour timeline - they move on crop yields, processing costs, and seasonal demand patterns. A bad corn crop in 2026 is not an oil story. It is a core-inflation story, and it does not reverse when Iran and the US start (or deny starting) diplomatic talks.

Real Estate

Oil's drop could ease mortgage rates modestly if the 10-year holds its Monday gains. But the June housing data shows an affordability crisis the rate table alone cannot solve.

30-yr fixed mortgage (Freddie Mac, July 30): 6.66% last official weekly reading; next reading August 7 (same morning as jobs report)
30-yr fixed mortgage (daily, Aug 4, Bankrate): ~6.80% daily surveys running above the Freddie Mac weekly; bond market movement since July 30 has not yet run through the weekly average
Existing home sales (June): 4.09M annualized -2.4% month-over-month; buyers hitting affordability limits even at record median prices
Existing-home median price (June): $440,600 all-time record high; 20.2% of active listings carrying a price cut simultaneously
New home sales (June): 628,000 annualized -5.6% year-over-year; new home median at $398,300 - below the existing-home median for the fourth consecutive quarter
FHA delinquency: 11.88% nine points above conventional; first-time buyers under the most stress
Foreclosure starts (H1 2026, ATTOM): 164,566 +18% YoY; June alone +20% YoY
Bottom Line
  • The gap between the existing-home median ($440,600, record) and the new-home median ($398,300) tells the builder's story: new homes are cheaper because builders have been systematically reducing square footage and accepting thinner margins to stay within reach of rate-constrained buyers. That gap - $42,300 - represents the affordability concession the new-home market has made to compete. It does not represent oversupply or discount selling. It represents an industry adapting to a buyer who cannot afford the product as it was originally configured.
  • Monday's 10-year Treasury easing to ~4.70% provides a potential pathway to modest mortgage rate relief. Every 10-15 basis points of sustained 10-year yield decline typically produces 10-12 basis points of mortgage rate relief over a few weeks. If WTI holds near $79 and oil's de-escalation trade sticks, the next Freddie Mac reading Friday could show a slight decline from 6.66% - the first since early July. That would be directionally meaningful for confidence, even if 6.55% is not materially different from 6.66% on a $400,000 purchase.

Household Finance

The June data confirmed a consumer who is spending, but spending on borrowed time and borrowed money. ISM 55.6 is the first sign that the income side might catch up.

PCE inflation (June, Commerce Dept.): -0.1% MoM +3.7% YoY (down from +4.1% in May); monthly decline driven by the Iran truce pulling fuel prices lower in June; first monthly PCE decline since April 2020
Core PCE (ex-food & energy, June): +0.1% MoM +3.3% YoY; the Fed's preferred inflation measure; still 1.3 percentage points above the 2% target
CPI (June, BLS): -0.4% MoM +3.5% YoY (down from 4.2% in May); energy drove the monthly decline; core CPI +2.6% YoY unchanged MoM
Consumer spending (June): +0.3% nominal +0.4% real (inflation-adjusted); fifth consecutive month of retail sales gains; consumer holding through the rate environment
U of Michigan Sentiment (July final): 55.2 near-12% jump from June; 1-yr inflation expectations held at 4.2%; 5-10 yr expectations at 3.3%
Conference Board Consumer Confidence (July): 90.8 below 92.4 consensus; expectations component weakened on job market concerns
Personal savings rate (June): 2.7% spending has outrun income; late-cycle pattern of maintaining living standards by drawing down savings
Credit card APR (Fed G.19, May): 21.15% the cost of spending on thin savings
Bottom Line
  • ISM Manufacturing's employment component hitting 52.8 - the first expansion reading in 33 months - is the closest thing this week has produced to a leading indicator that Friday's jobs report could beat the +91,000 consensus. Manufacturing employment turning positive after nearly three years in contraction is a directional change, not a rounding error. If it shows up in Friday's non-farm payrolls, the Michigan-Conference Board divergence - Michigan bouncing on gas prices, Conference Board staying weak on job market concerns - begins to resolve. The manufacturing employment signal from ISM says the Conference Board's job-market pessimism may be about to be tested.
  • The 2.7% personal savings rate paired with 21.15% credit card APRs is the K-shape in a single sentence. Households with savings are watching ISM 55.6, a Dow record, and lower gas prices and feeling meaningfully better. Households funding daily expenses on revolving credit at 21.15% are paying interest that compounds faster than any wage gain the current labor market is producing for lower-income workers. The economy that ISM 55.6 describes and the economy that a 21.15% credit card rate describes are occupying the same country. Friday's jobs report will say something about which of those two households has more members.

Crypto

Bitcoin gained 1.05% Monday but underperformed the Nasdaq by more than a full percentage point. The ISM data that strengthens the September hike case is a direct headwind for rate-sensitive assets.

Bitcoin (BTC): ~$63,776 +1.05% Monday; lagged the Nasdaq's 2.1% gain on the same session; below $65,000 resistance
Ethereum (ETH): ~$1,857 near Monday's close; immediate supports at $1,807 and $1,717; neutral momentum
Spot Bitcoin ETF inflows (all of July): $172.8M muted relative to the price recovery; institutional buyers waiting for rate-path clarity
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin's 1.05% gain Monday is directionally correct but structurally weak. On a session where the Dow hit records and oil fell 7%, BTC gained roughly half what the Nasdaq did. The gap reflects the rate-sensitivity problem: crypto benefits from the same risk appetite that drove Monday's equity rally, but it is also directly penalized by the September hike probability that ISM 55.6 just moved from 64.5% to approximately 66%. Higher rates mean higher opportunity cost for a non-yielding asset, reduced liquidity across the financial system, and reduced incentive for institutional allocators to add positions before rate clarity arrives. ISM 55.6 made all three of those conditions worse for crypto, while simultaneously giving equities a manufacturing tailwind they do not have to share with BTC.
  • The $172.8 million in July ETF inflows represents institutional patience, not institutional absence. The allocators who were expected to drive a new BTC high post-ETF-approval are watching the same September hike probability the rest of the market is watching - and holding back proportional to how confident they are in the rate outcome. A Friday jobs report that pushes hike probability below 60% would be the most direct short-term catalyst for ETF inflows to accelerate. A print above 150,000 jobs would likely do the opposite.

Around the World

The US-China manufacturing gap widened on Monday - ISM at 55.6 versus China's PMI at 49.2, contraction for the first time in five months. The divergence is not coincidental; it reflects where the AI buildout's demand is landing.

China Manufacturing PMI (July official): 49.2 first contraction in 5 months; down from 50.3 in June; weak domestic demand and typhoon disruption; US-China trade tensions a persistent backdrop
USD/JPY: ~156-157 holding in the range established by last weekend's US-Japan coordinated intervention; yen stability maintained so far
Nikkei 225 (Japan): modestly positive Tuesday; stronger yen less of a headwind as currency settled in the 156-157 range
Bank of Japan: policy rate held at 1.00% (8-1 vote, July 31); one dissent in favor of a hike; September BoJ hike signaled as possible given core inflation running "clearly above 2%"
Eurozone Q2 GDP: +0.4% (euro area); +0.5% (EU) solid quarter; lagging the US expansion pace
Eurozone inflation (July flash): 2.9% YoY up from 2.8% in June; energy +10.0% YoY the lead driver; ECB re-acceleration concern
UK CPI (June): +2.6% YoY down from 2.8% in May; Bank of England managing a gentler disinflation than the US
Bottom Line
  • The US-China manufacturing divergence on Monday is the most structurally significant data point for the medium term. US ISM at 55.6 - the highest in four years. China official PMI at 49.2 - contraction for the first time in five months. These two readings are measuring the same global manufacturing cycle from opposite sides of the same trade architecture. The AI buildout's demand for semiconductors, data center hardware, and power infrastructure is concentrated in the US, Taiwan, and South Korea. Chinese manufacturers are facing a domestic demand weakness and tariff headwind simultaneously. The gap between 55.6 and 49.2 is partly a trade-policy artifact and partly a demand-composition story - the industries growing fastest globally are currently growing in the West, not in China.
  • Japan's BoJ dissent for a hike at the July 31 meeting - one member already voted yes on a hike while the Fed had just had three dissenters calling for a hike in the US - creates a scenario where both the Federal Reserve and the Bank of Japan are moving in the same direction (toward higher rates) at different speeds. If the Fed hikes in September to 3.75-4.00% and the BoJ follows in October or November from 1.00%, the yen-dollar interest rate differential narrows. The coordinated intervention last weekend bought time. Narrowing rate differentials would provide the structural fix that intervention cannot.
