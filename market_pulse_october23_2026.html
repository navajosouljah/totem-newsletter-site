Mortgage Rate Falls to 7.32% - Nine-Week Low. Claims at 194K.

The Quick Read

Thursday's session ended with the Nasdaq down 2.07% and Meta down nearly 7% - not because the company missed, but because the beat disclosed what it cost. Q3 revenue came in at $64.6 billion (+26%), above consensus, but AI capital expenditure guidance rose to $145 billion for 2026 - a figure large enough to push free cash flow negative in the quarter. Investors saw the mechanism they had been trusting - AI investment drives returns - rerun as a risk: when spending scales faster than revenue can follow, near-term value flows backward. Tesla fell 3.08% on a capex cut to $20 billion, completing the picture: the market punished more spending and less spending in the same session. The Nasdaq closed at 26,150. That is the mechanism.

Friday's calendar is light. The FOMC begins its two-day meeting October 27-28; hike odds sit at 17-18% after Thursday's 194K claims came in below the 200K consensus - the last pre-FOMC labor read. Freddie Mac's PMMS released at noon ET Thursday at 7.32%, down from 7.40% - the first decline in nine straight weeks, tied to the 10-year Treasury falling 17 basis points to 4.96%. The window for October homebuyers is open for the first time since September.

Brent crude sits near $113 Friday morning on four supply disruptions in two weeks - the pump transmission from Thursday's event arrives approximately November 1-5, one week after the FOMC. Bitcoin lost $635 million in ETF outflows Thursday, the most in three weeks. Q3 GDP advance and September PCE land together on Thursday October 29 at 8:30 AM ET - the morning after the FOMC decision - in what is likely the most consequential 48-hour data window of 2026.
