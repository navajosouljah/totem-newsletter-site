Hike Odds Drop to 35% on PCE. Jobs at 8:30 AM ET.

The Quick Read

August PCE core landed at 3.0% on Wednesday - 40 basis points below the 3.4% consensus - cutting October hike odds from 73% to approximately 35% in a single session. The Bureau of Economic Analysis simultaneously revised its methodology for measuring spending on software, investment management, and legal services, retroactively lowering the inflation series by enough to amplify the market’s dovish interpretation. Goldman Sachs moved their hike forecast to December. The mechanism: a 3.0% core reading at the September 30 analysis’s own boundary condition, plus a BEA revision that raised questions about the measurement’s continuity, gave the market sufficient ambiguity to move October from near-certainty to a minority case of approximately 35%.

September payrolls at 8:30 AM ET is the final data input before October 28. Consensus: 84,000 jobs, 4.1% unemployment - the weakest expected monthly gain since the cycle’s soft patch began. Thursday’s initial claims for the week ending September 26 came in at 197,000 - below the 201,000 estimate and lower than the prior week’s 198,000 - suggesting labor conditions held through late September. Three paths: above 150,000 jobs pushes October odds back toward 50%; between 84,000 and 150,000 keeps odds near 35%; below 84,000 combined with PCE at 3.0% delivers the dual-softening signal that could push October below 25% and establish December as the clear majority case.

Two structural shifts built through October’s first session. The Freddie Mac PMMS jumped to 7.28% on Thursday - 25 basis points above last week’s 7.03% and potentially the first consecutive above-7% mortgage rate streak since 2007 - calculated on the survey week that closed before PCE’s soft reading could influence rates downward. And the 5.00% 1-year CD window that 73% October odds made an obvious hold decision is now a genuine coin flip: with October at 35%, locking before banks potentially reprice for a December majority case is defensible today in a way it was not one week ago.

September payrolls at 8:30 AM ET today closes Q3’s labor picture and sets the directional context for October 28. At October hike odds near 35% after PCE, today’s report is the last data of the week with the power to move November’s Fed funds futures materially. Claims at 197,000 enter the report as the most recent labor signal: strong claims with weak payrolls would be the cycle’s clearest labor-market paradox and most likely to confirm December as the sole remaining hike this year. Next week opens with Monday’s USDA Crop Progress at 3:00 PM ET - the first acceleration-week harvest read - and Tuesday’s ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET, the first services-sector read of Q4.

Today October 2: September Jobs Report at 8:30 AM ET · Q3 labor close · consensus 84,000 jobs, 4.1% unemploymentSeptember payrolls covers the first full month of post-September-16-hike borrowing conditions, $97-107 Brent, and the consumer confidence deterioration to 12-year lows. Consensus at 84,000 is the weakest expected monthly gain since August 2025. ADP September private payrolls came in at 90,000 - the most in three months - providing a mild upside signal. Claims at 197,000 entering today are consistent with 84,000-120,000 payrolls. Three scenarios: (1) Above 150,000 - October hike odds revive toward 50%, rate-path uncertainty re-opens; (2) 84,000-150,000 - October holds at ~35%, December majority intact; (3) Below 84,000 with PCE at 3.0% - dual-softening signal, October falls below 25%, December becomes dominant case.
Monday October 5: USDA Crop Progress at 3:00 PM ET · week ending October 4 · first harvest acceleration-week readMonday’s Crop Progress covers October 1-4 - the primary harvest acceleration window for both corn and soybeans. Corn entered the week at 18% harvested (five-year average); the October 5 report should show 30-40% if weather cooperated in the Corn Belt. Soybeans entered at 17% harvested; 30-35% is expected. A harvest pace above the five-year average would confirm the positive Q3 crop quality picture and keep futures prices in a stable range.
Tuesday October 6: ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET · Q4’s first services read · prior September: not yet releasedThe ISM Services PMI is Q4’s first read on the services sector - which has driven the labor market’s above-consensus performance through 2025 and into 2026. A services PMI above 52 entering Q4 with payrolls below 84,000 would be the clearest signal that services hiring is intact even as goods and manufacturing soften. A reading below 50 (contraction) combined with today’s soft payrolls would constitute the broadest simultaneous softening signal of the cycle.
Bottom line

Today’s September payrolls at 8:30 AM ET closes Q3 labor and sets October 28 odds from their current 35% base. Monday: USDA harvest acceleration check. Tuesday: ISM Services PMI opens Q4’s first services read. October 28 is the remaining high-stakes FOMC date; today’s payrolls determines whether December consolidates as the majority case or October stays in play.
