Yields Ease Overnight. PPI and Retail Sales Land at 8:30 AM ET.

The Quick Read

The S&P 500 fell 1.11% Wednesday to 7,718.94 after September CPI printed hot at 3.7% headline and 2.6% core, pushing the 10-year yield to 5.19% at Wednesday's close. Overnight, yields reversed: the 10-year fell 9 basis points to 5.10% and the 2-year dropped 6 basis points to 4.74% as international bond buyers absorbed the rate spike - and CME FedWatch moved October 28 hike odds from 43.7% back down to 31%. A falling 10-year is the input that moves mortgage quotes, growth-stock valuations, and gold's inflation-hedge bid at the same time. That is the whole mechanism.

Thursday's PPI and retail sales at 8:30 AM ET are the last numbers the Fed hears before its blackout begins Saturday. A soft PPI could pull October 28 hike odds below 25%; a hot print pushes them back toward 40% and extends Wednesday's selling into the bond and equity markets together. Freddie Mac publishes its next mortgage rate survey at noon ET - the 10-year at 5.10% today is the reading it will capture, the first in seven weeks that offers any chance of relief.

Top 1-year CDs still pay 4.75% APY this morning. Bitcoin is near $80,700 and defending the $80,000 level for the first time since August, with Ethereum near $2,362 and XRP near $1.30. The Nikkei fell nearly 5% overnight - the global growth-stock selloff from Wednesday's rate spike did not stop at the New York close.
