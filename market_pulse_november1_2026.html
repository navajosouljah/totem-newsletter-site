Savers Lock 4.65% Before Tuesday’s Vote. Mortgage Rates Target a Spring Low.

The Quick Read

AI earnings week closed Friday with Intel falling 30% – its worst session since 1974 – completing a three-day sweep that repriced when AI returns arrive, not whether they will. The S&P 500 closed at 7,580.07 (-1.82%), the Nasdaq at 25,437 (-2.86%), the Dow at 51,770.31 (-1.09%). A Binance liquidation event of approximately $1.5–2 billion simultaneously pushed Bitcoin from $80,000 to approximately $76,800. Q3 ECI matched consensus at 0.8% Q/Q, but wages rose just 3.1% year-over-year – the slowest in five years – trimming December hike odds from 87% to approximately 80%. Soft wages and the AI selloff’s flight-to-safety Treasury bid are both pressing yields lower: the CD window stays open, and the 10-year enters the mortgage survey week at approximately 4.93%. That is the mechanism.

Three catalysts open the five-day window. Monday’s USDA Crop Progress (week ending November 1) shows whether Iowa corn at 47% – 20 points behind its 67% five-year average – advanced another 15 or more points before freeze risk rises within days. Tuesday’s midterm elections set the Senate composition for three lame-duck finance items: the CLARITY Act (failed cloture 49–50, September 15), the Canada softwood lumber tariff (Day 82), and energy deregulation. Thursday’s Freddie Mac PMMS reflects the survey window where the 10-year enters at 4.93% – below the average that produced 7.24% – making a third consecutive improvement possible and a spring-low test realistic.

Gas wave #2 – from October 23’s WTI surge through $100 – arrives at retail pumps this week (November 1–5) on top of the $4.24 Friday average; wave #3, from Thursday October 28’s $115.32 Brent close, arrives approximately November 8–12. Bitcoin’s 200-day moving average near $76,000 is the structural floor that held Friday despite the Binance event; it is the number to watch as the week opens. The Q3 NY Fed consumer credit report – the first full quarter after the September 16 rate hike – arrives this month and will show whether the combined pressure of negative real wages and elevated rates is registering in household credit quality.
