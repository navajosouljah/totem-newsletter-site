Manufacturing Expansion Hits Ten Months. Today’s Senate Vote Shapes Crypto’s Path.

The Quick Read

ISM Manufacturing for October came in at 55.5 – beating the 54.6 consensus, the tenth consecutive expansion month – confirming that last week’s AI-hardware selloff was tech-specific, not economy-wide. Apple and Palantir both beat their Q3/Q4 estimates after Monday’s close, yet both fell in Tuesday’s premarket: Apple posted fiscal Q4 revenue of $114.5 billion (+12%) and EPS of $2.10, while Palantir hit $2.31 billion in revenue (+96% year-over-year) and EPS of $0.44. The pattern – beats and still down – signals the market is holding positions ahead of election results rather than trading on data. That is the mechanism entering Tuesday.

Election Day is today. The Senate composition that emerges tonight determines three lame-duck items with direct reader implications: the CLARITY Act (digital asset regulation, failed cloture 49–50 on September 15), the Canada softwood lumber tariff (Day 84 today), and energy deregulation measures. A net Senate shift of two or more votes toward CLARITY Act supporters could provide a lame-duck floor vote – the catalyst Ethereum and XRP ETF flows have priced in absence of since September. AMD reports after the close today, the next major AI-chip data point after last week’s Intel and Amazon misses.

Oil rebounded to approximately $105 Brent Tuesday morning after Monday’s 8% peace-talk-driven drop, on reports of US strikes on Iran’s Qeshm Island – reversing roughly half the Monday decline. Gas wave #2 (from October 23’s WTI surge) is still arriving at pumps this week regardless. Bitcoin slipped to approximately $76,540, testing the 100-day moving average for the first time since August – the structural floor the market will watch against tonight’s Senate outcome.
