The Quick Read

July housing starts delivered the summer's worst data miss - 1.239 million SAAR against a 1.39 million consensus, a -12.4% MoM collapse and 11% undershoot that confirms the 30-year mortgage rate at 6.67% has frozen new construction. The magnitude of the miss is significant not merely because of the gap versus consensus but because of what it reveals about the transmission of rate policy to the real economy: with the 30-year mortgage rate at 6.67% - the highest since 2007 - builders have sharply curtailed starts even as permits remained more resilient at 1.443 million SAAR (+5.0% beat). The permits-starts divergence is the housing data's most analytically important signal: builders are maintaining their entitlement pipeline (permits) while pulling back on capital outlays (starts), suggesting they expect rate relief eventually but are not willing to commit construction capital at current mortgage-rate levels. The 1.239 million SAAR reading is the lowest housing starts print of 2026 and materially below the approximately 1.35-1.40 million range that supported the housing sector's Q1 2026 strength. For the Fed's demand-destruction argument - the framework underlying Chair Warsh's majority hold at 3.50-3.75% in July - the housing starts crash is confirmatory evidence that the prior rate hike cycle is still delivering demand destruction, supporting the hold-at-September case against the three dissenters (Logan, Hammack, Kashkari) who voted for immediate action.

The 30-year Treasury's surge to 5.33% - a 19-year high - is the rate market's most consequential development of the week and reflects the stagflation framework operating in full force: persistently elevated oil inflation (Brent approaching $92 on Day 6 of the Hormuz standoff) combined with deteriorating economic data (housing starts crash) creates a configuration where long-duration bonds are simultaneously pressured by inflation expectations from the oil channel and by the sustained credit issuance required to finance stimulus in a weakening economy. The 10-year Treasury rose to 4.84%. Chip stocks led the equity decline, with the Nasdaq closing -1.33% at 26,289.71 and the S&P 500 falling to 7,691.76; the mechanism is direct - as the 30-year rises toward 5.33%, the discount rate applied to high-multiple technology valuations increases, compressing the present value of future earnings estimates. FOMC minutes at 2:00 PM ET today are the session's single most important event: the minutes will name the three July 29 dissenters, reveal the deliberative framework that produced the 7-3 hold decision, and provide the market's first detailed read on whether the majority's hold language has room to shift toward hike if August data (CPI September 11, jobs September 4) comes in above expectations.

Brent approaches $92 on Day 6 of the Hormuz standoff as no successor framework to the expired US-Iran MOU has emerged. Trump's August 13 statement that he was "not interested in extending" the MOU remains the US's public posture, and Iran's conditions for Hormuz reopening - sanctions remediation and war reparations - are unchanged. The national gas average has reached approximately $4.08/gallon. Walmart Q2 FY2027 reports tomorrow morning at 6:00 AM CDT against a consensus of $0.74 EPS and $186.8 billion revenue - the week's most anticipated consumer-health data point. Crypto markets reflect the rate pressure: Bitcoin trades near $64,234, Ethereum near $1,890, and XRP at $1.01 as the 30-year's surge to 5.33% increases the real opportunity cost of holding non-yielding digital assets.
