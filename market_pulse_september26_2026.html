Stocks Closed the Week at 7,742. Iran Proposed a Six-Day Hormuz Deal.

The Quick Read

Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi laid out a six-day phased Hormuz reopening timeline Friday at the UN General Assembly sidelines - pulling Brent from a near-$108 intraday high (driven by the Yanbu strikes) to a NYMEX settlement of $104.32 and sending the 10-year Treasury to 5.17% in Saturday trading. The mechanism: the US takes previously agreed steps in four to five days; the strait reopens on day six and negotiations resume the day after. October 28 FOMC hike odds pulled from 73% to approximately 67% as the oil-inflation link temporarily softened. Trump declined Iran's proposed seven-day ceasefire but aides said the diplomatic channel stays open. The Yanbu military situation continues - France has deployed defense assets to the site. These two facts run in parallel: a possible diplomatic off-ramp and a live escalation at the East-West pipeline terminus.

September 30 PCE at 8:30 AM ET is the week's most consequential data point - and it arrives on the same day as the Hormuz deal's window closes. PCE above 2.5% plus a failed deal pushes October 28 odds back toward 73-80%, confirming the majority case for waiting on a post-hike 4.95-5.10% CD. PCE at or below 2.0% plus a deal that holds is the one scenario that pulls October odds below 50% and makes locking 4.95% today the defensible call before banks potentially reprice lower on a pause signal. The two scenarios arrive simultaneously Wednesday morning.

The S&P 500 closed the week at 7,741.83, +0.49% Friday - the first winning week in three - as energy and defense names absorbed the oil shock and carried the index. Bitcoin gained approximately 10% for the week while gold declined 2%+ before recovering $30 Saturday to $4,301. Gas is $4.49 nationally - the mid-October pump window already carries $103-105 Brent to retail regardless of how quickly any Hormuz deal materializes. October 2 September payrolls and the Hormuz outcome are the two additional variables before October 28.
