Savers Keep Their 4.65% Window. December Hike Odds Ease to 80%.

The Quick Read

Intel fell 30% Friday – its worst single session since 1974 – on Q4 revenue guidance of $15–16 billion against the $17.1 billion estimate. With Alphabet down 12% Wednesday and Amazon down 7% after Thursday’s close, three consecutive AI-era earnings misses in two sessions repriced the question from who is winning AI to when AI spending produces returns. The S&P 500 closed at 7,580.07 (-1.82%), the Nasdaq at 25,437 (-2.86%), the Dow at 51,770 (-1.09%). The AI hardware selloff compressed gold (-3%), silver (-6.22%), and copper (-2.03%) simultaneously: reduced near-term data-center demand projections hit industrial metals while flight-to-safety money bid Treasuries and sent the 10-year to approximately 4.93%. That is the whole mechanism.

Q3 ECI landed at 0.8% Q/Q – matching consensus exactly – but the composition is the story: wages rose only 0.7% Q/Q (3.1% year-over-year, the slowest in five years), while benefits held at 0.9%/3.9%. Real wages are running at -0.6% year-over-year. The soft wage print trimmed December hike odds from 87% to approximately 80% – the first disinflationary wage signal since August. Top 1-year CDs at 4.65% APY are the direct beneficiary: the window stays open at four-to-one odds, with the Fed no longer near-certain to hike in December.

Iowa corn stands at 47% harvested, 20 percentage points behind its 67% five-year average, with northern freeze risk rising within 7–10 days. Monday’s USDA Crop Progress (week ending November 1) is the last read before that window may close. Tuesday’s midterms set the Senate’s composition for three lame-duck items: the CLARITY Act (failed cloture 49–50, September 15), the Canada softwood lumber tariff (Day 81), and energy deregulation.
