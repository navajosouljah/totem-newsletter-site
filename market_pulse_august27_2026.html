Market Pulse • Thursday, August 27, 2026

Nvidia's $108B Q3 Guide Confirms AI Capex Is Accelerating - Chip Derating Ends as Nasdaq Recovers. July PCE Comes in Hot at 3.7% - September Hike Odds Tick Back to 38% the Day Before Warsh Keynotes.

The consumer is still at $4.14 gas and 13.1% delinquency. Nvidia resolved the chip question Thursday; Warsh's 10am keynote tomorrow answers the inflation one.

The Quick Read

Nvidia guided Q3 at $108 billion against Wall Street's $104.2 billion consensus - a 4% upside surprise, the largest guidance beat of the AI cycle. Jensen Huang named Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta by name as customers with accelerating demand; Blackwell architecture is supply-constrained, not demand-moderated. The AI cloud and enterprise segment delivered $40.3 billion in Q2, up 138% year-over-year. Nvidia jumped approximately 6% in premarket Thursday, pulling Micron, AMD, and Broadcom broadly higher - ending the two-week chip derating that had pulled the Nasdaq roughly 600 points off its August 14 all-time high. The hyperscaler capex question is answered.

Thursday also delivered July PCE at 3.7% year-over-year, above the 3.6% consensus, with the monthly reading at 0.2% versus 0.1% expected. That single number pushed September FOMC hike odds from approximately 33% back toward 38-42% - meaning Warsh arrives at tomorrow's 10am ET Jackson Hole keynote with a hot inflation print in hand rather than a cooling one. Jackson Hole opened this morning with approximately 120 central bankers and economists from more than 70 countries. The Freddie Mac PMMS also released this morning - 6.62%, a 3-basis-point improvement from last week's 6.65% as the prior week's 10-year yield decline compiled into the weekly average.

The Pro Farmer Crop Tour concluded field surveys with a 2026 US corn yield estimate of 173.2 bushels per acre - 7.5 below USDA's August WASDE estimate of 180.7. If Pro Farmer is right, the September 11 WASDE delivers a downward shock to corn futures already trading near $4.90 per bushel on new contract highs. That food-channel CPI input lands the same week as Canada's retaliatory tariffs (September 8) and August CPI itself - three concurrent inflation signals in four days, all arriving before the FOMC meets September 15. The crop tour finding is not yet in any equity or rates price.

How Thursday Opens

  • What Nvidia's $108B guide locks in for the AI capex cycle - not just today's rally. The $108 billion Q3 guide is not a one-quarter statement; it is a signal about the pace at which Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta are committing capital through the end of their fiscal years. The combined Q2 capex from those four hyperscalers was $166 billion - up 87% year-over-year, the largest single-quarter hyperscaler build in history. Huang's guidance language on Blackwell ("supply-constrained" rather than "demand-moderated") means the bottleneck is on Nvidia's side, not the buyers' side - which is a structurally different signal from the one the market had feared since August 14. The chip derating was pricing a demand-moderation scenario. That scenario is now off the table through Q3. What it does not resolve: the rate-sensitivity of hyperscaler capex commitments at 4.67-4.70% Treasury levels - the 30-year still runs above 5.22%, and that cost of capital remains present in every hyperscaler CFO's capex model for 2027 planning.
  • July PCE at 3.7% changes Warsh's calculus at 10am tomorrow - the specific framework he needs to deliver. The hot PCE print is not the same as a September hike decision - it is the data that changes the weight of Warsh's conditional framework. At 33% hike odds on Wednesday, the optimal keynote was: name the specific data thresholds that restore September to 50% (August CPI above 3.8% plus August jobs above 150,000). At 38-42% odds Thursday after PCE, the calculus shifts: Warsh now arrives with the Fed's preferred inflation measure running 0.1 percentage points above forecast, which is a smaller number than the market fears but a real one. His most important task tomorrow is to frame what July PCE's 3.7% means in the context of the Hormuz energy peak (a one-time distortion that is already unwinding as Brent falls toward $87) versus Canada's tariff-driven second inflation channel (structural, beginning September 8). One is transitory by construction; the other is not. A keynote that distinguishes them preserves November as the measured action window. A keynote that treats them identically invites the market to price a September hike at 50%+ before the September 4 jobs number is even published.
  • The Iran-Oman temporary maritime corridor - what it buys and what it doesn't change for Brent's floor. Reports Wednesday evening indicated Iran and Oman reached an understanding on a "temporary joint maritime corridor" in the Strait of Hormuz. The word "temporary" is the operative term: it is not a sanctions relief agreement, not a ceasefire, and not a reopening of the Strait to normal commercial traffic. What it likely provides is a narrow, monitored passage for specific vessel categories - humanitarian cargo, potentially medical supplies - that reduces the closure scenario probability without restoring the 130-vessel-per-day pre-conflict pace. Markets interpreted it as resolution-positive, and Brent fell to approximately $87 as a result - down roughly $6 from Monday's $93.09 in three sessions. The floor question: Brent below $87 sustained requires an Oman-mediated formal sanctions-relief agreement, not just a corridor. Iran's central bank governor confirmed exports remain effectively zero; even a corridor does not change that revenue calculation until a formal agreement restores commercial transit. The next threshold that matters is the secondary sanction trigger - targeting China and India as Iran's largest buyers - which Bessent has not yet deployed. If crossed, the resolution premium currently embedded in $87 Brent inverts quickly.
  • CLARITY Act Polymarket odds collapsed to 14% - what the drop from 27-30% means and what it doesn't for September 15. The Polymarket 2026 passage odds have fallen from 27-30% on Wednesday to approximately 14% Thursday morning - a significant single-session move that reflects two compounding developments: first, Senate Majority Leader Thune has confirmed there will be no August vote, and the September 15 floor date is a cloture vote (requiring 60 votes to proceed), not a passage vote; second, the ethics dispute blocking Democratic support - specifically the divestiture requirement for officials holding more than $1 million in digital assets - has not moved toward resolution. A 14% Polymarket probability means the market assigns only a one-in-seven chance the bill passes in 2026. What this does NOT change: the September 15 date for the cloture vote still holds. The distinction matters for XRP positioning: the CLARITY Act premium in XRP is unwinding (XRP at approximately $1.42 versus Bitcoin's $79K), but the vote remains a real event that could reset the premium in a single session if a compromise on the ethics provisions emerges. At 14% odds, the XRP CLARITY premium is largely unwound - leaving XRP trading primarily on the institutional ETF flow dynamics that continue to benefit BTC.
  • The Pro Farmer 7.5-bpa gap is the September WASDE revision that isn't priced yet - and the specific math that makes it matter. The Pro Farmer Crop Tour is the most widely followed independent yield survey in the industry; in prior drought years when Pro Farmer came in more than 4 bpa below USDA, the September WASDE revised downward by an average of 3-5 bpa. A 7.5-bpa Pro Farmer gap is the largest since 2012. If USDA revises from 180.7 to approximately 176-177 bpa on September 11, corn at $4.90 likely moves toward $5.20-5.50 before the report - because futures markets price the revision probability in the two weeks leading to WASDE, not the day it publishes. The timing intersection is what makes this structurally different: September WASDE on September 11 + Canada retaliatory tariffs September 8 + August CPI September 11 + FOMC September 15 means four high-impact inflation inputs converge in a single week. The food channel, the energy channel, the trade-policy channel, and the rate decision all arrive simultaneously. No single one of those inputs is individually decisive; their convergence makes November 19 the more honest rate-action window regardless of what each individual print says.
