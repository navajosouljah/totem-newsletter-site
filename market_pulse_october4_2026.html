Credit Unions Still Show 5.00%. Monday’s ISM Services Decides What Comes Next.

The Quick Read

September payrolls came in at 29,000 - a 55,000 miss below the 84,000 consensus - and the bond market responded in two moves: the 2-year fell 10 basis points to 4.78%, the largest single-day drop since the September 16 hike, while the 10-year closed at 5.281%, above Thursday’s 5.24%. The mechanism: with October hike odds falling from 35% to 18% (CME FedWatch), the market priced a longer runway to December rather than removing the inflation risk premium from the long end. Equities read the repricing as a tailwind and closed higher - S&P +0.73%, Nasdaq +1.35%, Dow +0.49%.

Monday’s ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET is the week’s first decisive input. September’s payrolls miss was concentrated in goods-sector employers - healthcare added only 17,000 against its 33,000-per-month average, manufacturing added 9,000. Services drove 18 months of above-consensus hiring. ISM Services above 52 means the September miss is sectoral and the household income floor holds; below 50 is the broadest simultaneous softening signal of the cycle. Wednesday’s FOMC minutes from the September 15-16 meeting (released at 2:00 PM ET) will show how divided the committee was at the hike decision, framing the December path.

Credit unions - A+ Federal Credit Union and Apple Federal Credit Union - still show 5.00% APY on 1-year CDs as of Sunday morning. Major online banks have already repriced to 4.40-4.45%. The 60-basis-point gap closes further as the business week opens Monday. Brent closed Friday at $99.68 (-2.57%), WTI at $91.24. Gas at $4.41 (AAA October 1) reflects crude from two to three weeks ago; Friday’s Brent feeds the November window. Bitcoin at $84,570 and XRP at $1.48 (+3.49%) reflect the PCE-to-payrolls double-softening macro tailwind.

FOMC minutes Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET will show how the September 15-16 committee described the terminal rate path.

At 4.78%, the 2-year implies the Fed funds rate will average near 4.78% over the next two years - down from 4.88%, incorporating a near-certain October hold and a slightly lower December probability than before. For savers comparing CD options: the 2-year’s fall is the rate-market signal that the post-October rate ceiling of 5.15-5.25% is no longer the majority expected outcome, which is precisely why banks have begun repricing their 1-year CDs.

The mechanism is the distinction between the near-term rate path and the long-end risk premium: with October hiked out, the bond market extended its runway to December, keeping the inflation risk premium and fiscal supply concern in place rather than pricing a rate cycle peak. Monday’s ISM Services PMI is the first input that could shift the 10-year: a below-50 reading would likely push it toward 5.15-5.20% and begin pricing a genuine cycle ceiling.

That context matters for December odds, which CME FedWatch currently shows as the majority next-action scenario.
Bottom line

Monday October 5: ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET · USDA Crop Progress at 3:00 PM ET · forecast ISM Services ~58.7ISM Services is the week’s most consequential number. Above 52 means the household income floor is intact: services hiring continued in September and the payrolls miss is a goods-sector story; Friday’s equity rally has a fundamental foundation. Below 50 with 29,000 payrolls is the broadest simultaneous labor softening of the cycle - that combination would push the 10-year lower, challenge Friday’s equity rally, and begin pricing whether December remains the majority case. At 50-52: a deceleration signal without contraction, the most ambiguous outcome for markets. The forecast is approximately 58.7; any significant miss below 55 would be noteworthy given the payrolls context. USDA Crop Progress at 3:00 PM ET covers October 1-4 - the primary harvest acceleration week for corn and soybeans.
Wednesday October 7: FOMC minutes from September 15-16 meeting at 2:00 PM ET · terminal rate discussion keyThe FOMC minutes will provide the first detailed view of how the September 15-16 committee discussed the terminal rate path.
