Single-Family Starts Rose 7.6% in August. Mortgage Rates Stopped Short of 7%.

The Quick Read

Thursday erased Wednesday's selloff. The S&P rose 1.14% to 7,637.76, the Nasdaq 1.69% to 26,418.30, and the Dow added 316 points to 51,778. Yields fell - the 10-year retreated from 5.016% to approximately 4.84% - as markets absorbed the dot plot and moved on. The permission came from August housing data: total starts came in at 1,275,000 SAAR, above the 1.18-1.22 million consensus, with single-family construction rising 7.6% to 918,000 despite 6.95% mortgage rates and bilateral lumber tariffs stacking simultaneously. The Freddie Mac PMMS landed at 6.95% - stopping short of the 7% threshold where purchase applications historically step down.

Today is quadruple witching - the quarterly simultaneous expiration of stock index futures, index options, and stock options. It is the heaviest volume session of the quarter. Pre-market futures are relatively flat after Thursday's bounce. The Saudi pipeline story also evolved overnight: Saudi Arabia is now seeking to restore roughly half the East-West pipeline's capacity within days, with full capacity in approximately six weeks. The partial-restart signal is pushing WTI toward $101.

The week's CD window is open. Top 1-year CDs are already at 4.45-4.50% APY from early-mover online banks, repricing within 48-72 hours of Tuesday's hike. The dot plot's 12-of-18 November hike signal means the ceiling could reach 4.60-4.75% APY if November delivers. Friday September 25 PCE - the Fed's preferred inflation measure - is next week's most consequential release and the first post-hike read the November decision path depends on.
