Tuesday Briefing • Tuesday, August 18, 2026

US-Iran June Ceasefire Expired Without a Replacement Deal - Trump Says He Is 'Not Interested in Extending' - Brent Surges to $90.97, September Hike Odds Climb Back Toward 28-30% - Empire State Manufacturing 20.6 Crushes the 10.2 Estimate in August's Biggest Data Beat - Gold $4,429, 10-Year at 4.73%. July Housing Starts 8:30 AM ET Today. FOMC Minutes Tomorrow 2:00 PM ET. Walmart Thursday.

The US-Iran June 2026 MOU expired Monday without a replacement deal as President Trump publicly stated he is "not interested in extending" it - a position that reversed Sunday's Oman back-channel optimism and drove Brent from $88.31 to $90.97, surpassing Sunday's $89.60 peak and fully validating the August 17 "noise not resolution" framework. Empire State Manufacturing delivered Tuesday's positive surprise - 20.6 against a 10.2 estimate and prior 15.6 reading, the strongest print in over four years - a result that modestly complicates the Fed's hold case heading into tomorrow's FOMC minutes at 2:00 PM ET. September hike odds repriced upward to approximately 28-30% on the combined signal of Brent above $90 and manufacturing strength; the 10-year Treasury rose to 4.73%. Today's primary scheduled release: July housing starts at 8:30 AM ET (consensus 1.39 million SAAR).

The Quick Read

The US-Iran June 2026 MOU is gone, and so is Sunday's diplomatic optimism. The memorandum of understanding - the June ceasefire that temporarily reduced Hormuz tension and allowed the brief oil pullback to $88.31 Monday morning - expired without a replacement agreement. President Trump stated publicly Monday that he is "not interested in extending" the MOU, removing the ambiguity that had supported the Oman back-channel narrative. The response was immediate and directional: Brent rose from $88.31 to $90.97, surpassing Sunday's $89.60 peak and establishing a new high for the post-ceasefire period. The August 17 Market Pulse characterized Sunday's back-channel signals as "noise not resolution" - the MOU expiry confirms that assessment. Hormuz transit traffic remains at approximately 25 vessels per day, down from the July 7 peak of 49. No new formal diplomatic framework has been proposed by either side. The structural Hormuz premium is now reset at $90+ Brent with no near-term resolution mechanism visible.

Empire State Manufacturing delivered the week's positive surprise and one of the most significant upside data beats of the summer. The August reading of 20.6 arrived against a consensus of 10.2 and a prior reading of 15.6 - the largest positive deviation from estimate in the manufacturing series in over four years. A reading above 0 indicates expansion; 20.6 represents not merely an expansion but an acceleration. The Northeast manufacturing sector's strength in August - despite $4.07 gas, 22.18% credit card APR, and consumer spending contracting in July - suggests that business investment and industrial production have not followed the consumer pullback into contraction. That manufacturing signal complicates the Fed's hold narrative in one specific way: the three July FOMC dissenters who voted for an immediate hike cited sequential inflation evidence as their threshold; an accelerating manufacturing sector adds one more constructive data point to that argument. September hike odds repriced from 15-20% Monday morning to approximately 28-30% Tuesday on the combined weight of $90.97 Brent and Empire State's 20.6. The 10-year Treasury rose to 4.73%, up from approximately 4.60-4.64% Monday. Gold continued to advance to $4,429.49, up 0.30% from Monday.

The crypto complex shows differentiated performance Tuesday morning: Bitcoin recovered to approximately $64,212, up 1.39% in 24 hours after holding the $63,000 institutional cost basis through Monday's light volume session; Ethereum advanced to approximately $1,920, up from Monday's $1,895; XRP holds approximately $1.003, barely sustaining the $1.00 psychological floor that Sunday's session had temporarily breached. NAHB Housing Market Index came in at 35 Monday - meeting the consensus estimate and up 1 point from July's 34, a modest improvement that does not change the housing market's fundamental constraint at 6.67% mortgage rates. The USDA weekly crop progress confirmed corn slipped one point to 60% good-to-excellent (from 61% the prior week), adding marginally to the food-channel CPI risk ahead of the September 11 August CPI report. Today's primary scheduled data: July housing starts and building permits at 8:30 AM ET (consensus 1.39 million SAAR, prior 1.427 million). Tomorrow: FOMC minutes from the July 29 meeting at 2:00 PM ET. Thursday: Walmart Q2 earnings at 6:00 AM CDT ($0.74 EPS and $186.8 billion revenue consensus).

Savings & CD Rates

Monday's rate repricing - September hike odds rising from 15-20% to 28-30% and the 10-year moving from 4.60-4.64% to 4.73% - has introduced a new element of uncertainty into the CD-rate calculus that was not present 48 hours ago. The case for locking in current CD rates (4.25-4.40%) as cycle-high has weakened modestly: if September hike odds sustain at 28-30% and the FOMC actually hikes, CD rates could move incrementally higher. The case for acting now remains, however - the hold is still 70-72% probable and the 28-30% hike odds represent a tail, not a base case.

Bottom Line
  • Monday's rate repricing has made the CD decision more genuinely uncertain without changing the fundamental calculus for most savers. The 28-30% September hike odds represent a meaningful tail risk - not trivial, but still a minority probability. For savers who were planning to lock in rates, the question is whether the expected value of waiting for a potential hike-driven rate increase (roughly 30% x 10-15 additional basis points) exceeds the certainty of current 4.25-4.40% rates. At a $50,000 deposit, the expected value of waiting is approximately $15-22 in additional annual interest against the certain capture of current rates. Tomorrow's FOMC minutes and August 28's Warsh keynote are the two events that could materially shift the probability distribution; savers who want to wait for those signals before deciding have a legitimate case, and those who want the certainty of today's rates also have a legitimate case. This is one of the few genuine two-sided rate decisions of the current cycle.
