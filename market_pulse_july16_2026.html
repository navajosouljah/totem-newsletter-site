Weekly Briefing • Wednesday, July 16, 2026

Chips Lead the Selloff. TSMC Raised the Bill. Gold Breaks $4,000. Retail Sales Flat.

TSMC posted a 77% profit jump but raised capital spending to $60-64 billion - and the market punished the whole sector. The Nasdaq fell 1.47% as chip stocks dragged global indices lower. Gold broke below $4,000 for the first time since November. The U.S. struck northern Iran overnight while Jordan downed eight missiles at dawn. Retail sales came in at +0.2% for June - the consumer is spending, but barely.

This Week in the News

  • TSMC posted a 77% profit surge but raised its capital expenditure forecast to $60-64 billion. The market read the spending increase as margin pressure ahead. Chip stocks sold off globally - Kioxia fell 15% in Tokyo, SoftBank shed 6.3%, and the Nasdaq dropped 1.47% on the day. The entire AI supply chain traded lower.
  • The U.S. expanded strikes into northern Iran and disabled a ship trying to run the Hormuz blockade. Targets included command centers, air defense sites, and cruise missile installations on Greater Tunb Island. Iran's health ministry reports at least 35 killed and 300 injured. Strait traffic fell to 3 ships in 24 hours versus a normal 110.
  • Jordan downed eight Iranian missiles early Thursday. The conflict has expanded beyond a bilateral U.S.-Iran dispute into a regional confrontation involving Gulf-state ally military infrastructure. Oil held near $85 but did not spike further - the market may be absorbing war risk as a new baseline.
  • Gold broke below $4,000 for the first time since November 2025. The safe-haven trade continues to fail despite escalating geopolitical risk and favorable inflation data. Selling pressure appears linked to margin calls in the tech selloff and profit-taking after a prolonged rally.

The Quick Read

The chip selloff drove today's story. TSMC's 77% profit jump was overshadowed by a capex raise to $60-64 billion that spooked investors about future margins. The Nasdaq dropped 1.47%, the Nikkei fell 2.8%, and gold broke below $4,000 as margin calls forced selling across asset classes. Meanwhile, the U.S. expanded strikes into northern Iran while Jordan intercepted eight missiles - the conflict is widening but oil barely moved, suggesting the market is pricing war as permanent rather than temporary.

Retail sales for June came in at +0.2% month-over-month - enough to avoid contraction but not enough to signal strength. The consumer is spending on necessities while pulling back on discretionary. With gas now at $3.94 nationally and climbing, the tailwind from lower producer prices (last week's PPI beat) is being erased at the pump before it reaches the grocery shelf. The 10-year Treasury held at 4.58%, unmoved by the risk-off session.

The next eight days are loaded. Housing starts for June drop tomorrow after May's collapse to the lowest level since 2020. The Clarity Act field hearing in New York tomorrow sets the tone for crypto regulation. The tariff deadline on 60+ countries arrives July 24. The FOMC meets July 28-29. And Q2 GDP prints July 30. The market is trading risk-off into a gauntlet of catalysts that could confirm or reverse the direction.

Stock Market

A global semiconductor selloff dragged tech lower. Small caps held flat. The VIX jumped 6% as volatility returned.

S&P 500: 7,533.77 −0.51%
Dow Jones: 52,552.97 −0.20% (−106 pts)
Nasdaq: 25,881.95 −1.47%
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,974.57 flat (−0.06%)
Fear gauge (VIX): 16.73 +6%
Bottom Line
  • The Nasdaq losing 1.47% on a day TSMC posted a 77% profit increase tells you everything about this market's vulnerability. When the best earnings in the sector produce a selloff because the capex number was too high, the trade is crowded and priced for perfection. Any deviation from the script gets punished.
  • The VIX jumping 6% to 16.73 with a tariff deadline eight days away, an FOMC meeting in twelve days, and Hormuz at 3 ships per day suggests volatility is still underpriced relative to the catalyst calendar. The Russell holding flat while the Nasdaq dropped 1.47% shows this is a rotation story, not a broad panic - yet.

Interest Rates

Yields held steady despite the equity selloff. The bond market is not panicking - it is waiting for the FOMC in twelve days.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held since December 2025)
2-year Treasury: 4.18% flat
10-year Treasury: 4.58% flat
30-year Treasury: ~5.00% holding at threshold
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.55% −2 bps from last week's 6.57%
Bottom Line
  • The 10-year at 4.58% after a 1.47% Nasdaq drop and an expanding Middle East war shows the bond market is in wait-and-see mode. Last week's CPI-PPI double beat built the case for a dovish FOMC signal on July 29, but the market wants to hear it before pricing it.
  • Mortgage rates ticking down 2 basis points to 6.55% is directionally positive but functionally irrelevant. The buyer facing a $440,600 median price needs rates below 6% - not 6.55% instead of 6.57%. The housing market remains frozen at these levels.

Savings & CD Rates

Rates holding. With a rate cut more plausible after last week's inflation data, the window to lock in may be narrowing.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.99% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.17-4.30% APY (Newtek Bank 4.30%, Popular Direct 4.17%)
Best credit union CD: 5.00% APY (A+ Federal Credit Union, 12-month)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • CDs paying 4.30-5.00% with headline CPI at 3.5% deliver a meaningful real return. Back-to-back inflation beats plus a risk-off equity session make the case for locking in before the FOMC signals any dovish shift. If a cut materializes in Q4, today's 5% CD rate becomes the high-water mark.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil dipped slightly despite the expanded Iran strikes. The market may be treating the conflict as priced in. Gas keeps climbing toward $4.

WTI crude: $79.60/bbl flat to slightly lower
Brent crude: $84.63/bbl −0.4% from yesterday's $86
National gas avg: $3.94/gal +$0.05 from Monday
California: $4.76/gal still elevated
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.48/gal
Bottom Line
  • Brent slipping from $86 to $84.63 on a day the U.S. struck northern Iran and disabled a blockade runner is the most interesting signal this week. The oil market may be shifting from "each escalation spikes the price" to "the war is the new normal." That is constructive for the near term but it means any de-escalation produces a sharper move down than any new strike produces a move up.
  • Gas at $3.94 nationally - up from $3.89 on Monday - is still on track to test $4.00 by late July. California at $4.76 means West Coast drivers are paying 37% more than the Gulf Coast. The K-shaped fuel burden persists.

Commodities

Gold broke the $4,000 floor. Silver and copper held. Cattle continued their slide.

Gold / oz: $3,998 −1.5% - broke below $4,000
Silver / oz: $57.38 flat
Copper / lb: $6.34 +0.8%
Corn / bushel: $4.37 steady
Soybeans / bushel: $11.95 −0.6%
Live cattle / cwt: ~$229 −$2.50 on day, down $10 from last week
Bottom Line
  • Gold breaking $4,000 on a day with expanding military conflict and a tech selloff is a clear signal that the safe-haven trade is exhausted at these levels. When gold cannot rally on its two primary catalysts - geopolitical risk and rate-cut expectations - the path of least resistance is lower. Watch $3,900 as the next support.
  • Cattle dropping $10 from last week to $229/cwt is the sharpest multi-day decline since spring. But with the U.S. herd near 86 million head - the smallest since 1951 - this remains profit-taking against a structural floor. Beef prices at the consumer level will not follow cattle futures lower in any meaningful way.

Crop Weather

Corn conditions improved slightly. Wheat quality remains near 60-year lows. Silking progress is on schedule.

Corn: 68% good-to-excellent (up 1 pt). Silking at 34%, on pace with last year.
Winter wheat: 26% good-to-excellent - down 22 points from a year ago. Harvest advancing.
Soybeans: 64% good-to-excellent (steady).
Drought: ~46% of Lower 48 in drought. Improving slightly from peak.
Bottom Line
  • Corn at 68% good-to-excellent through mid-July pollination is the supply story the market needs. If ratings hold here through the next USDA report, corn-driven food inflation stays contained through Q4. The improvement from 67% to 68% is modest but directionally right.
  • Wheat at 26% versus 48% a year ago is locked in. The harvest is advancing but quality is already determined. Higher flour and cereal costs in Q3-Q4 are a certainty. This does not require a weather event to hit the consumer - it is already in the pipeline.

Real Estate

No new housing data today. Tomorrow's June housing starts report follows May's collapse to the lowest level since 2020.

Existing-home sales (June): 4.09M ann. −2.4% MoM
Median existing-home price: $440,600 record high
Housing starts (May): 1.177M ann. −15.4% MoM - lowest since May 2020
Foreclosure starts (May): 27,304 +13% YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.88% highest since Q2 2021
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
30-year mortgage: 6.55% still elevated
Bottom Line
  • Housing starts collapsing 15.4% in May to the lowest level since 2020 - with multi-family plunging 41.6% - means the supply pipeline is drying up precisely when demand is frozen. Tomorrow's June report will show whether that was a one-month anomaly or the start of a builder pullback. Given mortgage rates still above 6.5%, a sustained recovery is unlikely.
  • The FHA delinquency rate at 11.88% - highest since Q2 2021 - remains the early-warning signal. These are first-time buyers and lower-credit borrowers. When this cohort slips, it tends to spread. The distress at the edges is building while the headline median price posts records, which is the definition of a K-shaped housing market.

Household Finance

Retail sales landed at +0.2% for June - spending but not strongly. The consumer is navigating between falling producer prices and rising gas costs.

Retail sales (June): $768.6B +0.2% MoM, +6.7% YoY
Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record (Q1 2026)
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion down from $1.28T Q4 (seasonal); avg APR 21.0%
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo 20.3% of buyers now pay $1,000+/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days delinquent: 6.90% 32-year high (Fitch)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 3.0% holding (May 2026)
Bottom Line
  • Retail sales at +0.2% is the consumer treading water. The YoY gain of 6.7% looks healthy until you adjust for inflation - real spending growth is closer to 3%. The consumer below the median is making trade-offs: gas is taking a larger share of the weekly budget, which leaves less for discretionary spending.
  • The subprime auto delinquency rate at a 32-year high (6.90%) alongside 47% of BNPL users paying late shows the stress is concentrated in credit-dependent households. The seasonal dip in card balances from $1.28T to $1.25T provides a temporary buffer, but if gas crosses $4.00 nationally, that buffer evaporates and Q3 card balances resume climbing.

Crypto

Crypto gave back yesterday's gains as the broader risk-off session pulled leveraged positions lower. The Clarity Act hearing tomorrow adds a regulatory catalyst.

Bitcoin (BTC): $63,733 −1.3%
Ethereum (ETH): $1,862 −2.7%
Solana (SOL): $75.24 −2.1%
XRP: $1.09 −2.1%
Bottom Line
  • Crypto pulling back 1-3% after yesterday's 3-5% rally shows the asset class is trading as a leveraged risk proxy, not a safe haven. Bitcoin giving up gains on the same day gold broke $4,000 confirms neither is functioning as a traditional store of value in this environment - both are trading on liquidity and positioning.
  • The Clarity Act field hearing in New York tomorrow is the near-term catalyst. The House passed the bill in 2025; the question is whether the Senate moves it before summer recess. A constructive hearing could provide a floor under crypto prices even in a risk-off equity tape. But it needs 60 votes in the Senate and currently has about 55 - the seven-vote gap is the real story.

Around the World

The chip selloff went global. Tokyo fell 2.8%. Shanghai dropped 1.9%. Europe followed lower. Hong Kong was the sole winner.

Japan: Nikkei 66,836 (−2.8%). Kioxia −15%, SoftBank −6.3%. The chip selloff hit Tokyo hardest.
Europe: DAX 24,847 (−0.6%), FTSE 10,494 (−0.2%). Chip weakness plus oil costs pressured the region.
China: Shanghai 3,882 (−1.9%). Property fragile, stimulus preventing a broader selloff.
Hong Kong: Hang Seng 25,009 (+1.3%) - the sole outlier, possibly on mainland fund inflows.
Emerging: Under pressure from oil costs, chip selloff contagion, and the July 24 tariff deadline 8 days away.
Bottom Line
  • The Nikkei dropping 2.8% - double the Nasdaq's decline - shows Japan's outsized exposure to the semiconductor supply chain. When TSMC's capex guidance spooks the market, the damage radiates outward through memory (Kioxia), equipment, and materials suppliers concentrated in Tokyo and Seoul.
  • The tariff expiration on July 24 is eight days away and still underpriced in emerging markets. If the temporary 10% tariffs on 60+ countries become permanent, it creates a simultaneous hit from trade barriers and elevated oil costs. The combination is worse than either alone.
