Lock 5.00% Before Monday. September’s 29,000 Cut October Hike Odds to 18%.

The Quick Read

September payrolls came in at 29,000 - a miss of 55,000 below the 84,000 consensus that arrived alongside a two-month revision cutting 60,000 additional jobs from July and August. The unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, though participation climbed to 61.8%, a four-month high. Wage growth hit 3.0% year-over-year, the slowest since 2021. October hike odds fell from 35% to 18% (CME FedWatch), with Kalshi and Polymarket settling near 17%. Equities rallied on the “no October hike” read: S&P +0.73%, Nasdaq +1.35%, Dow +0.49%.

Monday’s USDA Crop Progress at 3:00 PM ET and Tuesday’s ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET are the week’s first post-shock data inputs. The ISM question is the more important: whether September’s weakness is contained in goods and manufacturing - healthcare added only 17,000 versus its 33,000-per-month average, manufacturing gained 9,000 - or has spread into services, which drove the labor market’s above-consensus performance for 18 months. Services above 52 means the miss is sectoral; below 50 is the broadest simultaneous softening of this cycle.

The structural tension building through Q4: the 10-year yield ended Friday at 5.281% - above Thursday’s 5.24% close despite the weakest monthly payrolls in 18 months. The yield initially fell to 5.18% on the data, then reversed through the session. With October at 18%, the bond market priced a longer runway to December rather than a definitive rate peak. The 2-year at 4.78% was the one unambiguous move - a 10-basis-point decline, the sharpest since the September 16 hike.

The bond market extended its runway to December rather than pricing a rate cycle peak.

10-year Treasury (Friday October 2 close): 5.281% ▲ closed higher than Thursday despite 29,000 jobs · initial fall to 5.18% reversed · December runway pricedThe 10-year’s reversal - falling to 5.18% on the jobs release, then closing at 5.281% above Thursday’s 5.24% - is the week’s most counter-intuitive move. The mechanism: with October hiked out at 18%, the bond market repriced for a longer path to December. A softer October probability does not remove the inflation risk premium from the term structure; it extends the timeline over which that risk must be held. Additionally, the weak jobs print raised questions about whether fiscal deficits will require sustained Treasury supply absorption at a time when the Fed is not a buyer - keeping the term premium elevated even as the near-term rate path softened. Tuesday’s ISM Services PMI is the next input: if services are also softening, the long-end could finally price a genuine cycle peak.

The 2-10 spread at 5.281% versus 4.78% = ~50 basis points represents a steepening from the ~52-basis-point spread on Thursday - a flatter-than-usual curve entering Q4 that reflects the market’s uncertainty about where the cycle peaks.

Three catalysts govern the week following Friday’s jobs shock. Monday’s USDA Crop Progress at 3:00 PM ET is the first harvest acceleration-week read - covering October 1-4. Tuesday’s ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET is the week’s most important data point: whether services held through September determines whether the payrolls miss is a sectoral story (goods only) or a systemic deceleration reaching the employment base that has driven household income stability. Thursday’s Freddie Mac PMMS release will show whether Friday’s 10-year reversal to 5.281% extends the consecutive above-7% mortgage rate streak to a third week.

Monday October 5: USDA Crop Progress at 3:00 PM ET · week ending October 4 · first harvest acceleration-window readMonday’s report covers October 1-4 - the primary harvest acceleration window for corn and soybeans. Corn entered the week at 18% harvested (at the five-year average); the report should show 30-40% if weather cooperated. Soybeans entered at 17%; 30-35% is expected. A pace above the five-year average confirms quality is intact entering the bin; a below-pace read signals weather disruption or crop quality concerns that could affect Q4 futures pricing. This is the first definitive measure of whether the September crop-quality ratings (corn 57%, soybeans 58% G/E) translate to harvested yield.
Tuesday October 6: ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET · Q4’s first services read · 50 is the contraction boundary · mortgage-rate and equity catalystISM Services is the week’s highest-stakes data point. Above 52: services hiring and activity are intact, September’s payrolls miss is goods-and-manufacturing-only, the household income floor holds, and the equity rally’s fundamentals are confirmed. At 50-52: services are decelerating but not contracting. Below 50 with 29,000 payrolls is the broadest simultaneous labor softening of the cycle - that combination would likely push the 10-year below 5.20% and potentially break the mortgage-rate streak on Thursday’s PMMS. All three major markets (equities, bonds, real estate rates) have a stake in Tuesday’s number.
Thursday October 8: Freddie Mac PMMS weekly release · third consecutive above-7% probable · estimate ~7.18-7.28% at current 10-year of 5.281%The October 8 PMMS survey covers Friday through Wednesday (October 2-7). At Friday’s 10-year close of 5.281%, the survey opened above last week’s average - pointing toward a third consecutive above-7% week, the first such streak since 2007, rather than breaking it. ISM Services PMI on Tuesday is the swing variable: a below-50 reading would drive the 10-year lower through Wednesday, potentially pulling the survey average below 5.20% and the October 8 PMMS estimate toward 7.00-7.10%. An above-52 reading keeps the 10-year near 5.28% and virtually guarantees a third above-7% week. For borrowers deciding this week, Tuesday 10:00 AM ET is the last meaningful rate signal before Thursday’s PMMS lock.
Bottom line

Monday: USDA harvest acceleration read at 3:00 PM ET. Tuesday: ISM Services PMI at 10:00 AM ET - the week’s most consequential number, with stakes for equities, bonds, and mortgage rates. Thursday: Freddie Mac PMMS - third consecutive above-7% week likely unless ISM Services comes in below 50. October 28 remains the FOMC date; next week’s data refines December’s majority standing.
