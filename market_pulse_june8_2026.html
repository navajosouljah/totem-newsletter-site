Weekly Briefing • June 8, 2026

172,000 Jobs Shatter Forecasts. Markets Break a 10-Week Streak.

A blowout May jobs report triggers a $1T+ tech selloff, the VIX spikes 34%, and crypto drops 15-17%. The savings rate hits 2.6% - the lowest since 2008. Foreclosure completions surge 45% YoY. Wheat production forecast down 25%.

This Week in the News

The headlines shaping markets right now.

  • Jobs report crushes estimates, kills rate-cut hopes: The economy added 172,000 jobs in May - more than double the 85,000 consensus - while unemployment held at 4.3%. Treasury yields surged, and the Nasdaq dropped 4.2% on Friday alone as markets priced out any near-term Fed cuts.
  • Tech selloff wipes $1T+ in a single session: Friday's blowout jobs number triggered the worst Nasdaq session since April 2025, ending a 10-week winning streak for the S&P 500. The semiconductor index fell over 10%, exposing just how narrow the rally had been - 8 of 11 S&P sectors declined in May even as the index rose 5.3%.
  • US-China trade framework taking shape: Ahead of a June 11 announcement, both sides are finalizing terms that would retain 30% baseline tariffs while pausing higher levies for 60 days. Separately, the USTR proposed an additional 12.5% tariff tied to a forced-labor investigation covering 45 countries.
  • Oil stays elevated on Hormuz disruption: Brent crude spiked above $97 mid-week on Iran-Israel tensions before settling near $93. The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed since April, shutting in an estimated 10.5 million barrels per day and keeping energy inflation entrenched.

The Quick Read

The week's story is simple: a hot jobs report exposed how fragile this rally really is. Stocks snapped a 10-week winning streak on Friday's selloff, the VIX jumped 34% in a single session, crypto lost 15-17%, and the personal savings rate hit 2.6% - the lowest since 2008. Meanwhile, foreclosure completions are up 45% year-over-year, nearly half of buy-now-pay-later users are paying late, and subprime auto delinquencies are at a 32-year high. The consumer is stretched thin, and markets just got a reminder that the Fed isn't coming to help anytime soon.

The jobs beat was real, but the market's reaction tells you where sentiment actually sits. A strong number should be good news - instead it triggered over $1 trillion in tech losses because it means the Fed stays on hold at 3.50-3.75%. The 10-year Treasury pushed to 4.55%, and mortgages are stuck at 6.48%. With 8 of 11 S&P sectors declining even during May's 5.3% rally, this market is being carried almost entirely by a handful of tech names. That kind of concentration is a setup for exactly the kind of unwind we saw Friday.

Abroad, the Strait of Hormuz closure continues to distort energy markets, with WTI hovering near $94 and gas nationally at $4.19. Gold pulled back sharply to $4,337 as the dollar strengthened, while cattle hit record highs on the smallest US herd since 1951. The USDA's wheat crop is in deep trouble - only 26% rated good or excellent, with production forecast down 25% and nearly half the country in drought. These are the structural pressures that don't disappear with one good jobs print.

Stock Market

The 10-week winning streak ended with a thud on Friday's jobs-driven selloff.

S&P 500: 7,383.74 −2.64% Fri (first losing week in 10)
Dow Jones: 50,866.78 −0.32% wk
Nasdaq: 25,709.43 −4.68% wk
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,833.50 −3.5% wk
Fear gauge (VIX): 21.51 +34% Fri
Bottom Line
  • Friday was a reality check. The VIX jumping from 15.40 to 21.51 in one session tells you how complacent this market had become. The 10-week streak was built on narrow tech leadership, and the unwinding was swift once rate-cut hopes evaporated.
  • Eight of eleven S&P sectors declined in May even as the index rose 5.3%. When the headline number goes up but the average stock goes down, that's not a healthy market - it's a handful of names masking broad weakness.
  • Small caps got hit hard too. The Russell 2000 dropping 3.5% signals that the risk-off move wasn't just about overpriced tech - it was a broad repricing of the "rates stay higher" reality hitting the most rate-sensitive companies.

Interest Rates

The jobs blowout pushed yields higher and cemented expectations that the Fed holds steady at June's meeting.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (effective 3.62%)
2-year Treasury: 4.17%
10-year Treasury: 4.55% +11 bps wk
30-year Treasury: 5.01%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.48% −5 bps wk
Bottom Line
  • The 30-year Treasury crossed 5% - a psychological barrier that historically signals tighter financial conditions ahead. The 2s/10s spread remains inverted at -38 bps, a recession signal that has been flashing for months.
  • Markets are pricing over 95% probability of no cut at the June 16-17 FOMC meeting. This will be Jerome Powell's final press conference before Kevin Warsh takes over - the transition itself adds uncertainty to the rate outlook.
  • Mortgages ticked down 5 bps to 6.48%, but that's cold comfort when the median home costs $417,800. Monthly payments remain near record highs, keeping first-time buyers locked out.

Savings & CD Rates

The gap between the national average and the best available rates remains enormous.

National avg 1-yr CD: 1.55% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.10-4.11% APY (Popular Direct, E*TRADE)
Best credit union CD: 5.00% APY (A+ Federal Credit Union)
Best high-yield savings: 4.03-5.00% APY (Vio Bank, Varo Bank)
Bottom Line
  • If your savings are sitting in a big-bank account earning 0.3%, you're losing over 3% per year to inflation in real terms. The best CDs at 4-5% at least keep pace, and they're FDIC insured.
  • Six major high-yield savings accounts cut rates in May. With the Fed holding steady, expect top rates to drift lower through the summer - locking in a 12-month CD now may be the better move.

Energy & Gas Prices

Crude remains elevated on Middle East supply disruption, but pump prices are finally cooling.

WTI crude: $94.43/bbl elevated
Brent crude: $93.00/bbl spiked above $97 mid-week
National gas avg: $4.19/gal −$0.05 wk
West Coast: $4.76/gal
Cheapest region: Gulf South (Indiana $3.48/gal)
Bottom Line
  • The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since April, shutting in an estimated 10.5 million barrels per day. That's not a headline risk - it's an ongoing supply shock propping up prices well above where fundamentals suggest they should be.
  • Pump prices dipped 5 cents this week, which is welcome, but $4.19 national and $4.76 on the West Coast are still painful for working families. The $1.28 gap between Indiana and California highlights how much state policy and refinery geography drive what you actually pay.
  • Longer term, US actions to increase domestic supply and settle oil in USD point toward lower prices. But the Hormuz situation has to resolve first, and that's a geopolitical question, not a market one.

Commodities

Gold and silver pulled back sharply as the dollar strengthened on the hot jobs print.

Gold / oz: $4,337 −3.7% wk
Silver / oz: $68 −8% wk
Copper / lb: $6.32 +0.96%
Corn / bushel: $4.20 4-month low
Soybeans / bushel: $11.15 −0.6%
Live cattle / cwt: $258 record high
Bottom Line
  • Gold dropping $166 from its June 4 high of $4,503 shows how quickly the "safe haven" trade reverses when the dollar strengthens. At $4,337 it's still up 30% year-over-year, but the pullback reminds you that metals don't just go up in a straight line.
  • Cattle at $258/cwt is the highest on record, driven by the smallest US herd since 1951. This translates directly to higher beef prices at the grocery store - another squeeze on household budgets that CPI data captures with a lag.
  • Corn at four-month lows on favorable planting weather is one bright spot for food prices. But the wheat situation more than offsets it - production forecast down 25% year-over-year.

Crop Weather

Wheat is in deep trouble. Corn is off to a decent start. Drought covers nearly half the country.

Corn: 66% good/excellent (first rating of season, 1 pt below year-ago). Planting 93% complete.
Wheat: Only 26% good/excellent; 44% poor/very poor. Production forecast down 25% YoY to 1.048B bushels. Farm price projected at $6.50/bu, up $1.50.
Drought: 48.8% of contiguous US in drought (improving slightly from 50.8%). Extreme-to-exceptional drought entrenched in the West and Plains; expanding rapidly in the Upper Midwest.
Global: World wheat production forecast at 819M MT, down 24.7M MT from last year's record. El Nino probability 61-87% by mid-2026.
Bottom Line
  • The wheat crop is a genuine concern. Only 26% rated good or excellent, with 44% in poor or very poor condition. USDA projects Hard Red Winter production down 25% and the season-average farm price jumping $1.50 to $6.50/bu. This flows directly into bread, pasta, and cereal prices by fall.
  • A 61-87% probability of El Nino emerging by mid-2026 threatens production across South Asia, Southern Africa, and East Asia. Global grain stocks are tightening on multiple fronts simultaneously.
  • Corn at 66% good/excellent is a reasonable start, but drought stress in the Upper Midwest and Great Lakes is expanding rapidly. The next few weeks of weather will be decisive for the corn crop.

Real Estate

Foreclosure completions surged 45% year-over-year. The distress pipeline is accelerating.

Foreclosure starts: 82,631 (Q1 2026) +20% YoY
Completed foreclosures/REO: 14,020 +45% YoY
Total foreclosure filings: 118,727 +26% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: ~11.52% +126 bps YoY
Existing-home sales (annual pace): 4.02M +0.2% MoM
% listings cutting price: 35.4%
30-year mortgage: 6.48%
Bottom Line
  • REO completions up 45% year-over-year is the number that matters most. These are homes that went all the way through the foreclosure process and are now bank-owned. The pipeline from starts (+20%) to completions (+45%) shows acceleration, not stabilization.
  • FHA delinquency at 11.52% - roughly 6.5 times the conventional rate of 1.78% - reflects how stressed lower-income and first-time buyers are. MBA attributes part of the spike to a reporting change after pandemic-era FHA relief expired in September 2025, but the trend is clearly worsening.
  • Over a third of all listings are cutting prices, and sales volume at 4.02M annualized remains near multi-decade lows. At 6.48% rates and a $417,800 median price, the math simply doesn't work for most buyers. This market is frozen by affordability, and the distress underneath is growing.

Household Finance

The savings rate hit 2.6% in April - the lowest since 2008. The consumer is running on fumes.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion +$18B QoQ
Credit card debt: $1.252 trillion; avg APR ~21.0%
Avg. new-car payment: $772/mo record
Subprime auto 60+ days late: 6.90% (32-year high)
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 2.6% (lowest since 2008)
Bottom Line
  • A 2.6% savings rate means Americans are spending almost everything they earn. The long-run average is 8.4%. Every prior reading this low preceded significant economic stress. This is not a statistic you can spin positively.
  • Subprime auto delinquency at 6.90% is the highest since 1994. Nearly half of buy-now-pay-later users are paying late. These are the cracks that appear before a broader consumer pullback - people who can't keep up with their existing obligations aren't going to drive spending growth.
  • Credit card debt dipped $25B from Q4's record - a seasonal pattern as tax refunds arrived - but at $1.252 trillion and a 21% APR, the interest burden alone is staggering. The card debt dip is a brief pause, not a turnaround.

Crypto

A brutal week across the board as longs lost $1.28 billion on June 6 alone.

Bitcoin (BTC): ~$62,000 −15 to −17% wk
Ethereum (ETH): ~$1,611 −11% wk
Solana (SOL): ~$64 multi-week downtrend
XRP: ~$1.16 −15% wk
Bottom Line
  • Crypto sold off in lockstep with tech - a reminder that in a risk-off environment, digital assets behave like leveraged Nasdaq, not like the inflation hedge their proponents claim. Bitcoin dropping 15-17% in a week erases weeks of gains in days.
  • The $1.28 billion in liquidated longs on June 6 tells you how over-leveraged the market was. Some stabilization appeared over the weekend, but the total market cap sitting at $2.19 trillion is well off recent highs.
  • Institutional interest remains a structural tailwind, but weeks like this expose the gap between institutional positioning (patient, hedged) and retail behavior (leveraged, momentum-chasing). The divergence continues to widen.

Around the World

The US jobs surprise rippled through global markets, hitting tech-heavy indices hardest.

Japan: Nikkei 225 +0.39% wk. Hit all-time highs early in the week but gave back gains sharply Thursday and Friday, dropping 2.7% in two sessions as the global tech selloff hit semiconductor stocks.
Europe: Stoxx 600 −0.53% wk. Hit a two-week low on new US tariff proposals (10-12.5% on additional countries) and Iran-driven energy inflation hitting the continent harder than the US.
China: CSI 300 −1.54% wk. Uneven recovery continues, though the index remains up 27.5% over the trailing year. The US-China trade framework with 30% baseline tariffs is a structural headwind.
Emerging markets: Mixed. Strong YTD run (+14.7% in April alone) but late-week risk-off pressure from the US jobs surprise weighed broadly.
Bottom Line
  • Japan's Nikkei hitting all-time highs then reversing sharply exposes how correlated the Japanese rally has been to the global AI trade. The yen weakness that powered the rally is a double-edged sword when risk sentiment turns.
  • Europe faces a unique squeeze: new US tariff proposals, the ECB potentially hiking while the Fed holds, and Iran-driven energy inflation. The Stoxx 600 at a two-week low reflects that pressure from multiple directions.
  • China's CSI 300 dipping 1.54% is noise in the context of a 27.5% trailing-year gain. But the US-China trade framework taking shape with 30% baseline tariffs caps how far the recovery rally can extend from here.
