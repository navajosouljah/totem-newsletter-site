Supertanker Mine Claim Disputed at Hormuz - Brent Opens September at $91.28. S&P Closed August at 7,686 as Nasdaq Futures Lead Tuesday's Gap Lower at -0.8%.

September opens with Iran claiming mines struck a supertanker and the US calling it disinformation. The rate market's first real test arrives at 10am ET with the ISM Manufacturing PMI.

The Quick Read

The IRGC claimed Monday night that a supertanker struck two naval mines south of the Strait of Hormuz while using what Iran called an unauthorized route; US Central Command denied it flatly, calling the claim disinformation targeting commercial shipping. Both accounts cannot be true, but Brent at $91.28 is pricing both simultaneously: the Larak Island strike and corridor suspension are real regardless of the mine claim's accuracy, and the dispute itself adds a "could be true" insurance premium that shipping underwriters price even without confirmed hull damage. Brent's $91 level enters September CPI's inflation calculation on September 11 no matter which account proves accurate. That is the whole mechanism - a disputed narrative at a closed chokepoint adds risk premium that neither side can dissolve in one trading session.

ISM Manufacturing PMI at 10am ET is September's first data test and the first rate-sensitive domestic input since Warsh's August 28 Jackson Hole speech. S&P 500 futures are down approximately 0.5%, Nasdaq futures -0.8%, Dow futures -0.5% as September opens. The S&P closed August at 7,686.14 - down 0.33% on the final day of a month that produced 2.5%+ gains overall. At 57% September hike odds, the ISM reading does more rate-path work than the mine dispute: a reading above 55 (expansion) pushes September probability toward 60-62%; a contraction below 50 moderates it toward 50-53%.

Monday's USDA Crop Progress report stabilized corn nationally at 57% good-to-excellent - the four-week declining trend did not extend. Gas remains at $4.14-4.15/gallon as Brent's $91+ level won't reach pumps until September 14-21 given the standard 2-4 week lag. Credit card delinquency at 13.1% (15-year high). The CLARITY Act rose to 30% on Polymarket with five Senate working days remaining before September 15's cloture vote. September 4 jobs data remains the terminal binary before the FOMC votes September 15-16.

How Tuesday Opens

  • Why a disputed mine narrative at Hormuz still costs Brent $1-2 per barrel even if Iran is lying. A false Iranian claim does not net to zero in shipping markets. If Iran fabricated the mine report to deter commercial traffic from the corridor route - the US interpretation - the deterrence still functions: shipping companies add insurance riders, reroute tankers, or delay Hormuz transits under uncertainty, even without confirmed mines. Lloyd's and war-risk underwriters price the possibility of mines present, not the certainty. That behavioral response translates directly to spot premium within 24-48 hours independent of physical mine presence. The only evidence that would collapse this premium is a US Navy hull inspection showing the tanker undamaged - not a CENTCOM press release denying the claim. Until that visual proof reaches marine insurers, the "could be true" component of $91.28 holds. The second-order question: if Iran is conducting information warfare about mines to slow commercial shipping without the operational risk of actually laying mines, that is a new escalation tool with a lower cost-benefit threshold than physical mining - suggesting the Hormuz risk premium is not a one-session story regardless of today's resolution.
  • ISM Manufacturing PMI at 10am ET - what each scenario means for the September 15-16 FOMC vote. The July ISM manufacturing PMI read 55.6%, showing expansion in the manufacturing sector. The August reading arriving at 10am ET will be the first post-Warsh data point Warsh's "underlying trends" framework can absorb. Three scenarios matter for September hike odds: (1) A reading above 55 (continued expansion) pushes September from 57% toward 60-62% and confirms that manufacturing demand is not contracting under the existing rate structure - validating a September hike; (2) A reading between 50-55 (slowing expansion) leaves September odds near 56-59%, essentially confirming the wait-for-jobs-data posture; (3) A reading below 50 (contraction) pulls September from 57% toward 50-53% and introduces the first data argument against a September hike - the "underlying trends" framework Warsh described accommodates slowing growth as a reason to pause. The 2-year Treasury will move 5-10 basis points within 30 minutes of the 10am print. No Fed communication is available to interpret or correct the market's initial reaction.
  • CLARITY Act at 30% Polymarket - what changed since August 28 and what the five working days actually mean for the legislative outcome. The Polymarket probability rose from roughly 20-24% at Friday's August 28 baseline to 30% by Tuesday, driven by Senate Majority Leader Thune's cloture filing on the motion to proceed - a procedural step that confirms the bill's path to the floor remains open and the September 15 vote date has at minimum a scheduling commitment. The 30% level reflects "the path exists and is confirmed" without the votes being secured. The unresolved blocking issues are unchanged: the ethics divestiture provision (requiring officials holding more than $1 million in digital assets to divest) has no compromise language agreed, and nine Democratic crossover votes remain unsecured. The five working days are September 1, 3, 8, 9, and 10 - the Senate returns September 14, one day before the vote. No material legislative movement visible through Monday. For XRP specifically: if the cloture vote fails September 15, the 2026 legislative window closes entirely as both chambers are nearly fully out for October ahead of the November 3 midterms. A 30% probability is not a recovery toward passage; it is a floor under "zero chance" with a path that still requires nine Democratic votes in five working days.
  • Corn's second consecutive 57% G/E reading narrows the September 11 WASDE revision range without resolving the Pro Farmer gap. Monday's USDA Crop Progress stabilized national corn at 57% good-to-excellent for the week ending August 30 - unchanged from August 23. The four-week trend (65% Aug 9, 61% Aug 16, 57% Aug 23, 57% Aug 31) has now plateaued at the 57% level rather than continuing to fall. Iowa's state report showed 77% good-to-excellent - the regional divergence between Iowa (77%) and the national average (57%) reflects adequate subsoil moisture in the primary Iowa belt versus drought-stressed conditions in the central and southern Plains. The plateau at 57% for a second consecutive week reduces the probability of a severe September WASDE revision from USDA's 180.7-bpa August forecast: the "could fall to 174 bpa" scenario from a fifth consecutive G/E decline is now less likely, while the 175-177 bpa revision range appears more probable. Pro Farmer's 173.2-bpa crop tour estimate - a 7.5-bpa gap with USDA - remains the structural constraint. Corn futures above $5.10 are pricing approximately the Pro Farmer estimate, not USDA's August baseline. The September 11 WASDE is still the first formal revision event.
  • September's historical seasonality compounds what Hormuz and rate odds created - not the other way around. September is historically the weakest month for US equities, with the S&P 500 averaging approximately -0.7% returns since 1950 and the Nasdaq typically underperforming its August by an additional 40-50 basis points. This year's September setup layers that seasonal headwind onto a specific configuration: 57% September hike odds, an active Hormuz military exchange, corn near the Pro Farmer estimate, and a jobs report arriving September 4 with no Fed communication available to interpret either upside or downside. The S&P at 7,686 (August 31 close) sits 113 points below the 7,798.99 record set August 14 and 26 points below the pre-Warsh August 28 close of 7,711.76. Reclaiming the record in September requires jobs data and ISM PMI running against both the seasonal pattern and the hike-odds baseline. A September that ends below 7,686 on the monthly close would be the first monthly decline since April. The Nasdaq at approximately 26,298 (August 31 estimated close) faces the combined weight of rate-path repricing and seasonal tech weakness entering a historically difficult month.
