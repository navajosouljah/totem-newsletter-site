Weekly Briefing • Tuesday, July 28, 2026

Kospi Circuit Breaker Triggered at -11%. Nvidia's $250B OpenAI Guarantee Cracks the AI Thesis. Oil at $85 Brent. Fed Begins Today.

South Korea's Kospi plunged more than 11% Tuesday and triggered its eighth circuit breaker of 2026 as SK Hynix fell 13.5% and Samsung fell 13.1%. Japan's Nikkei declined 4.34% to a two-month low. The trigger was a weekend report that Nvidia is in talks to backstop up to $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's Ohio data center - a deal the market is reading as circular AI spending at a scale not previously seen. US premarket at 6 AM ET: Nasdaq-100 futures down 0.9%, S&P futures down 0.2%, Dow futures flat. Oil extended Monday's decline - Brent fell to $85.08 and WTI to $80.11 as the Iran pause held a third day. The FOMC begins its two-day meeting today; rate decision and Chair Warsh's press conference arrive tomorrow at 2:00 PM and 2:30 PM ET alongside Microsoft and Meta earnings after the close.

Today in the News

  • South Korea's Kospi plunged more than 11% on Tuesday and triggered its eighth circuit breaker of 2026, halting trading for 20 minutes after the index fell more than 8% below Monday's close. SK Hynix shed 13.5% and Samsung Electronics fell 13.1% - the two companies together account for nearly half of the KOSPI's weighting. The Korea Exchange circuit-breaker activation was its 14th since the mechanism was introduced. The selloff deepened Tuesday on the overnight spread of the weekend report that Nvidia is in talks to guarantee up to $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's Ohio data center project, which markets read as circular AI spending: Nvidia underwriting the ability of its largest customer to buy more Nvidia chips.
  • Nvidia is reportedly in talks to provide a $250 billion financing backstop for OpenAI's 10-gigawatt data center campus in Pike County, Ohio - guaranteeing lease and construction debt on the facility itself (not the chips inside), while also discussing financing for OpenAI's chip purchases worth up to $350 billion. The total project value including hardware exceeds $500 billion. A first phase of 800 megawatts of power capacity is expected to complete in 2028. Industry-wide AI data center and chip spending is now projected to exceed $700 billion this year. The deal's size alone - representing roughly 35% of total industry AI spending - is the structural concern the market is repricing, alongside questions about circular financial dependency between Nvidia and its largest growth customer.
  • Oil extended Monday's decline as the Iran pause held for a third consecutive day. Brent crude fell to approximately $85.08 per barrel (-3.7% from Monday's $87.75 settlement), and WTI fell to approximately $80.11 (-3%). Goldman Sachs issued a note Tuesday projecting Brent could moderate to $80 per barrel by year-end if the Strait of Hormuz fully reopens by Q4. Iran has not officially accepted a ceasefire and rejects reports of a 10-day truce; President Trump confirmed the US and Iran are "in diplomatic talks," a step up from the informal pause language of the past two days. The pause is military-logistics driven, not treaty-based.
  • The Federal Reserve begins its two-day FOMC meeting today (Tuesday, July 28). The rate decision arrives tomorrow (Wednesday, July 29) at 2:00 PM ET, with Chair Warsh's press conference at 2:30 PM ET. A hold at 3.50-3.75% is essentially certain. The statement language - particularly any mention of oil's 10%+ decline since July 21 and August CPI implications - will move the 2-year Treasury more than the decision itself. No Summary of Economic Projections (no dot plot) at this meeting. Microsoft and Meta both report after the close Wednesday, making July 29 one of the most data-dense evenings of the year.
  • The University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index rose to 54.4 in July, beating the 51.0 consensus estimate and marking the highest reading since February. The improvement was attributed to easing gasoline prices and reduced near-term inflation fear. The Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index is scheduled for release today; June's reading was 91.2, below the 94.4 forecast. If today's reading comes in above June's level, it would validate a two-month sentiment recovery trend.

The Quick Read

The AI-spending thesis is under its most direct structural stress test since January. The Kospi circuit breaker was the tell: when a major exchange halts trading because the benchmark is down 11% in a single session, the selling has moved beyond institutional repositioning. South Korea is not a peripheral player in the AI ecosystem - SK Hynix and Samsung manufacture the high-bandwidth memory that Nvidia's most advanced GPUs require. A 13% decline in both companies in a single session is the market asking a question it was not asking six months ago: if the Nvidia-OpenAI financing arrangement is real, who is actually bearing the risk, and what does a $500 billion construction project mean for the economics of the AI buildout?

The deal reads differently depending on what you emphasize. Nvidia's bulls say it secures demand - locking in the largest AI customer with a financing structure that guarantees revenue and creates durable infrastructure for the AI economy. The bears say it creates circular dependency: Nvidia is effectively underwriting the ability of its primary growth customer to continue buying Nvidia products. A financing backstop in which the supplier guarantees the debt of its buyer has historically signaled credit concern rather than growth momentum. The total project value exceeding $500 billion, with a first phase completing in 2028, is extraordinary even in the context of an AI investment cycle that has already produced some of the largest capital deployments in technology history. Industry-wide AI infrastructure spending exceeding $700 billion this year means the Nvidia-OpenAI arrangement alone represents roughly 35% of total sector investment. That is concentration risk at a scale no financial model had previously contemplated.

The oil story is running in parallel and pointing in a different direction. Brent at $85.08 and WTI at $80.11 means the full conflict premium built between July 8 and July 21 has now been erased and then some. The FOMC begins deliberating today with oil more than $10 lower than what the Fed saw at the June meeting. Goldman Sachs's Tuesday note projecting Brent at $80 by year-end - contingent on Hormuz fully reopening by Q4 - describes a scenario in which the summer's energy inflation chapter closes as quickly as it opened. That deflationary trajectory, arriving while household debt remains at records and the savings rate hovers at 3.0%, has real implications for the September and October CPI readings. The FOMC statement tomorrow will be read for any acknowledgment that oil's decline has changed the near-term inflation calculus - and the 2-year Treasury will move on that language before markets close Wednesday afternoon.

Stock Market

Asian markets bore the full force of the chip rout overnight. US premarket at 6 AM ET reflects a second session of pressure concentrated in technology - Nasdaq-100 futures down 0.9%, S&P futures down 0.2%, Dow futures essentially flat - the same split that characterized Monday's close.

Kospi (Seoul - Tuesday close): −11%+ (approx. −732 pts); circuit breaker activated; worst session since March; Samsung −13.1%, SK Hynix −13.5%
Nikkei 225 (Tokyo - Tuesday close): 62,112.91 −4.34%; 2-month low; chip and semiconductor names led decline; Topix −2.76%
S&P 500 futures (US premarket ~6 AM ET): −0.2%
Nasdaq-100 futures (US premarket ~6 AM ET): −0.9%; semiconductor and mega-cap tech names leading losses
Dow futures (US premarket ~6 AM ET): +0.1%; essentially flat; same rotation pattern as Monday
Nvidia (NVDA Monday close reference): Below $200 fell approximately 5% Monday on initial $250B OpenAI deal report; additional pressure in Asian hours Tuesday
VIX (Monday close): 18.86 elevated heading into Tuesday; fear gauge ticked higher despite oil decline Monday - now faces additional chip-rout catalyst
Bottom Line
  • The circuit breaker activation in Seoul - the eighth of 2026 and the 14th overall - is a market-structure event as much as a financial one. Circuit breakers are designed to interrupt panic selling and give investors time to recalibrate. The fact that Korea has triggered its mechanism 14 times reflects how concentrated the KOSPI is in semiconductor names, and how binary the market's view of the AI buildout has become. This is not a one-day event; it reflects a repricing of what AI infrastructure returns look like when the supplier of the chips is also guaranteeing the debt of the buyer of those chips.
  • The Dow-flat, Nasdaq-down-0.9% divergence in US futures is now the second consecutive session with this pattern. Rotation that repeats across consecutive sessions is no longer positioning noise - it is a developing trend. Energy-cost relief from lower oil continues to support transports, industrials, and financials. The AI-infrastructure names face a different ceiling: not cycle risk, but structural-dependency risk. Those are different problems. If this rotation holds through Wednesday's earnings and FOMC, the relative performance gap between tech-heavy and value-heavy indices will be the theme of August.
  • Every US megacap reporting this week - Microsoft and Meta tomorrow, Apple and Amazon Thursday - will be read through the AI-spending lens that Monday's chip selloff and Tuesday's Korean circuit-breaker have established. Revenue beats will not be enough if CapEx guidance disappoints. Meta's full-year AI CapEx guidance of $125-145 billion and Microsoft's Azure growth rate will be the decisive numbers. The market has already answered the question of whether AI spending is real; what it does not yet know is whether the financing structure of that spending is sound.

Interest Rates

Bond markets are waiting for Wednesday's FOMC statement rather than moving dramatically on Tuesday's chip rout alone. The 10-year dipped modestly to 4.62% overnight as oil continued to fall. The 2-year held near 4.33%, fully pricing a hold tomorrow.

2-year Treasury: ~4.33% held steady overnight; FOMC hold fully priced; rate-hike probability compressed by continued oil decline
10-year Treasury: ~4.62% down from 4.65% Monday close as oil extended decline; modest move; bond market waiting for FOMC
30-year Treasury: ~5.11% eased modestly from ~5.13% Monday
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.58% Freddie Mac July 23; next weekly reading Thursday July 31
Dollar index (DXY): ~101.5 firming ahead of FOMC; EUR/USD near monthly low of 1.1362; dollar strength pulling gold lower
Bottom Line
  • The bond market's restraint on Tuesday - a 3-basis-point dip in the 10-year while Asian equities are down 4-11% - reflects disciplined positioning ahead of Wednesday's FOMC statement. Bond investors have learned not to over-interpret single sessions ahead of Fed decisions. The most important rate market signal will come from the FOMC statement language: any phrase acknowledging that oil's 10%+ decline since July 21 represents a meaningful disinflation signal would push the 10-year toward 4.55-4.58%. If the statement maintains the June tone of "inflation remains elevated" without reflecting the oil move, the 10-year holds in the 4.60-4.65% range and cut expectations remain deferred to November at the earliest.
  • For mortgage borrowers, the 10-year at 4.62% is a small but real improvement from the recent range. The critical threshold is 4.60% - a decisive break below it is what mortgage rates need to begin moving in the right direction. A dovish-leaning FOMC statement Wednesday could provide that break. If Thursday's Freddie Mac reading comes in at 6.58% again or lower, it would represent the end of a four-week streak of mortgage rate increases and would be the first real affordability signal in two months. The gap between 6.58% and the roughly 5.50% needed to meaningfully expand buyer purchasing power remains wide - but direction matters for psychology as well as math.

The lock-in argument is unchanged today, but the window may be measured in weeks from tomorrow rather than months. A hawkish statement extending the rate-hold outlook would remove near-term compression risk and give savers more runway.

Energy & Gas Prices

Oil extended Monday's historic decline on Tuesday as the Iran pause held a third day and President Trump confirmed active diplomatic talks. Brent is now $13 below its recent peak above $98.

WTI crude (Tuesday morning): ~$80.11/bbl −3% from Monday's $81.92 close; conflict premium from July 8-21 entirely reversed
Brent crude (Tuesday morning): ~$85.08/bbl −3.7% from Monday's $87.75; Goldman Sachs projects Brent to $80 by year-end if Hormuz reopens fully by Q4
Iran diplomatic update: US and Iran halted active fighting for a third consecutive day. President Trump confirmed "diplomatic talks" underway - a step beyond the informal pause language of prior days. Iran rejects reports of a formal 10-day ceasefire. Status remains informal and military-logistics driven, not treaty-based.
National gas avg: $4.11/gal unchanged; pump prices trail crude by 2-3 weeks; first consumer relief expected mid-August if oil holds
West Coast (California): $5.57/gal still highest in the nation
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.65/gal (Mississippi)
Bottom Line
  • Brent at $85.08 means the oil market has erased the entire conflict premium built between July 8 and July 21 - plus additional ground. If Goldman Sachs's $80 year-end projection proves correct and Hormuz reopens by Q4, the summer's energy chapter will have been a temporary, fully-reversed disruption. That outcome has meaningful implications for Q3 and Q4 inflation data: the August CPI, which reflects July energy costs, may show a sequential decline in energy components for the first time since June. That would shift the Fed's optionality for a September or November cut from theoretical to real.
  • President Trump's confirmation of "diplomatic talks" rather than just a "pause" elevates the status of the de-escalation. A formal ceasefire - which neither side has officially confirmed - would trigger a Strait of Hormuz reopening scenario and anchor the Goldman $80 target. The asymmetry remains: diplomatic progress is gradual and measurable; a single overnight military reversal sends Brent back above $90 within hours. The market is pricing the center case, not the tail risks. The fact that gold has pulled back modestly to $4,045 but has not collapsed suggests the market still holds a meaningful tail-risk premium on re-escalation.
  • Gas at $4.11 nationally means consumers are still absorbing elevated fuel costs regardless of what crude does in the futures market. The 2-3 week refinery lag means pump relief arrives in mid-August at the earliest. The geographic split - California at $5.57 versus Mississippi at $3.65 - reflects refinery capacity constraints and state tax structures that the Iran situation doesn't resolve. Californians are paying $1.92 more per gallon for the same crude-input story.

Bottom Line
  • Gold's move to $4,045 from $4,087 on a day when oil is also falling is a conventional reaction: dollar-strength ahead of a Fed decision pulls gold lower regardless of geopolitical conditions. This is a different signal than Monday, when gold rose even as oil fell - that divergence told you the market did not believe the Iran pause was permanent. Today's gold decline driven by dollar strength tells you FX traders are positioning for a potentially hawkish-leaning FOMC statement Wednesday. If Warsh's statement acknowledges the oil decline as disinflationary, the dollar retreats and gold recovers toward $4,070-$4,080. If the statement is hawkish, the dollar strengthens further and gold tests $4,020-$4,030.
  • Corn touching $4.92 intraday before settling near $4.83 signals real weather concern rather than speculative positioning. Grain-fill stage for corn is the highest-stakes window of the growing season, and the western Corn Belt drought persisting through it is building a food-cost risk that has received less attention than the energy story. If corn breaks and holds above $5.00 - and the current weather pattern does not break before grain fill completes in roughly three weeks - the food component of the August CPI will begin to work against the oil component's relief. Those are two separate inflationary forces potentially landing in the same September CPI print.

Crop Weather

The USDA weekly crop progress report released Monday afternoon (week ending July 27) showed ratings holding at the prior week's levels. The grain-fill window is the highest-stakes period of the season; the next three weeks are decisive for yield outcomes.

Corn (week ending July 27): 67% good-to-excellent (steady from prior week). Grain fill advancing; yield outcome increasingly locked in over the next three weeks. Drought concentrated in western Corn Belt during the critical window.
Soybeans (week ending July 27): 64-66% good-to-excellent range; blooming progress above five-year average pace; condition holding despite heat.
Spring wheat: ~53% good-to-excellent; down 5 points over the prior two weeks; Northern Plains heat maintained pressure. Ratings are at the low end of the five-year range for this date.
Drought: ~19-20% of US corn acres in some degree of drought, concentrated in the western Corn Belt. Late July heat remains a factor before final grain fill.
Global: Europe's heatwave maintained pressure on French maize conditions, at a 13-year low rating. EU wheat estimate 140.8 Mt; Black Sea harvest partially offsets European losses.
Bottom Line
  • The next ratings release (week ending August 3, due Monday August 4) will be the most important crop data point of the summer. If corn ratings hold at or above 67%, the yield loss from this drought is manageable and grain prices moderate. If ratings slip below 65% - crossing into territory that signals a potentially significant yield reduction - corn and food prices enter a new trend higher that begins to show in August retail data and September CPI. The window to avert that outcome is roughly two weeks of weather. Forecast precipitation for the western Corn Belt in the August 1-10 window will be watched as closely as any economic data release this week.

Bottom Line
  • The 10-year Treasury at 4.62% Tuesday morning is 7 basis points below Monday's intraday range and directionally moving in the right way for mortgage borrowers - but 4.62% is still above the 4.60% threshold where rate relief begins to translate to meaningful mortgage movement. Wednesday's FOMC statement is the key variable. A dovish statement that acknowledges oil's disinflation signal could push the 10-year to 4.55-4.58%, setting up a potential pause or decline in Thursday's Freddie Mac survey for the first time since late June. Idaho (+59%), Colorado (+57%), and Georgia (+52%) lead, but no region is immune. Lower oil prices help this group indirectly, through lower transportation costs and modest future mortgage rate relief. They do not resolve the equity gap or the payment-to-income stress these borrowers face.

Bottom Line
  • Consumer Sentiment rising to 54.4 on lower gasoline prices is a real improvement, not a noise event. The University of Michigan survey is conducted over multiple weeks and a 3.4-point beat above consensus reflects a genuine shift in how households feel about their near-term financial conditions. What it doesn't yet reflect is the June PCE picture - that data arrives Thursday morning. May's 3.0% savings rate was captured before $4 gas and the Section 301 tariff transition hit household cash flows. Thursday's June reading will be the first post-conflict, post-tariff household data point. A savings rate at or above 3.0% in June despite those pressures would be a genuinely bullish consumer signal. A reading below 2.5% would confirm the savings buffer is being consumed by the higher-cost environment.
  • The structural K-shape this briefing returns to most often became a little narrower on Tuesday - falling oil prices are a broad consumer benefit, not concentrated in one income group. But a 23.8% APR credit card is still a 23.8% APR credit card whether gas costs $4.11 or $3.65. The household with a 90-day delinquency and a $777 monthly car payment is not made whole by $0.50 off per gallon. Oil relief is real for everyone; it helps the stressed household most at the margin but doesn't resolve the structural leverage problem that took years to accumulate.

Monday's chip news sent Korea down 11% while the Dow was essentially flat in the US. That divergence is not just a one-day phenomenon; it is a structural feature of how the AI-spending cycle distributes its volatility globally.

Japan's benchmark includes auto, industrial, financial, and consumer names that partially offset the chip-sector hit. The yen also benefited modestly from improved trade balance expectations as oil fell - lower crude reduces Japan's import bill directly, creating a tailwind for the currency that partially offsets the equity decline. The Bank of Japan's rate trajectory remains independent of Wednesday's FOMC decision, but is watching the yen path and the August CPI trajectory for its own September deliberations.
