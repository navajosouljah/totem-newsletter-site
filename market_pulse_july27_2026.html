Weekly Briefing • Sunday, July 27, 2026

US and Iran Pause Strikes. Oil Heads Below $90 as Markets Open Monday. FOMC and Four Megacaps This Week.

A weekend ceasefire attempt changed the energy picture just as the most data-dense week of the summer begins. Brent crude fell nearly 4% Friday on early diplomacy signals and is set to open sharply lower Monday. The Fed meets tomorrow with a hold now favored by a wide margin. Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon all report. Q2 GDP and June PCE land Thursday.

This Week in the News

  • The US and Iran halted strikes over the weekend after 13 consecutive nights of exchanges, pausing to "make space for diplomacy." Brent crude fell nearly 4% Friday to $96.80 as early signals emerged and is expected to open significantly lower Monday - a reversal that eases pressure on the Fed going into its July 28-29 meeting.
  • Section 301 forced-labor tariffs on 60 trading partners took effect at 12:01 AM on July 24, replacing the expiring Section 122 framework. Additional duties of 10% or 12.5% now apply to all products from the 60 affected economies; the net import cost impact varies by country, product classification, and available exclusions.
  • Mortgage rates rose to 6.58% (Freddie Mac, July 23), the fourth consecutive weekly increase and a full 15 basis points above four weeks ago. Rates have climbed in tandem with oil-driven inflation risk; the Fed's Wednesday decision will be the first test of whether the ceiling is in.
  • Four megacap earnings reports arrive this week - Microsoft and Meta on Wednesday, Apple and Amazon on Thursday - alongside Q2 GDP and June PCE. The spending-vs-returns question Alphabet raised four days ago follows every one of them.

The Quick Read

The biggest market story this weekend arrived without a press release. The US and Iran paused their escalating strikes after 13 consecutive nights of exchanges, halting fire over the weekend to create "space for diplomacy." Brent crude, which had climbed to $94 on Thursday and then slipped 4% to $96.80 Friday as early signals leaked out, is expected to open near or below $90 when markets resume Monday. That oil move does real work on the Fed meeting that begins tomorrow: the rate-hike odds that tripled from 10% to 34% last week have been retreating, and a further drop in crude between now and Wednesday's 2:00 PM decision narrows the case for hiking considerably.

The structural event already happened on Thursday. At 12:01 AM on July 24, the Section 122 temporary tariff framework expired and Section 301 forced-labor duties took its place - adding 10-12.5% additional duties on imports from 60 trading partners, including several major US trading partners. Unlike Section 122, which was always framed as emergency and temporary, Section 301 is a permanent-authority framework. The net impact on any given importer depends on the country of origin, product, and exclusion eligibility. The first full month of Section 301 flows through the August CPI, due September 11 - so the price impact will not show in the June PCE data landing Thursday.

What does not change is the earnings calendar. Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday after the close. Apple and Amazon arrive Thursday morning alongside the Q2 GDP advance estimate and June PCE. The questions Alphabet raised last Thursday - how much capital spending is too much, and when does the AI buildout show on the margin line - follow every one of these four companies. The tape closed Friday with the S&P 500 essentially flat for the day and the VIX at 18.6, elevated for a market that spent most of the year below 15. The week ahead will answer what that surface calm is hiding.

Stock Market

A mostly flat Friday closed a week of churn. The S&P 500 ended little changed while the Nasdaq lagged on chip weakness and the Dow held modest gains on energy and financials.

S&P 500: 7,412 +0.05% Friday; little changed for the week
Dow Jones: 51,947 +0.46% Friday
Nasdaq: 24,976 −0.64% Friday; chip names continued to weigh
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,930 −0.35%; down from 2,960 the prior week
Fear gauge (VIX): 18.58 elevated; up from 16.64 a week ago
Bottom Line
  • A VIX at 18.6 heading into the most consequential week of the summer - live Fed meeting, four megacap earnings, Q2 GDP, and a new tariff regime already in effect - is not calm. It is a market holding its breath. The surface reading of "flat" conceals the breadth divergence: the Dow held up on energy and defense; the Nasdaq and Russell both gave ground.
  • If the Iran pause holds and oil drops meaningfully Monday morning, the market will need to re-price the hike odds quickly. That could be a broad catalyst - particularly for rate-sensitive names that have been punished by the oil-driven yield move. The direction of oil by Wednesday afternoon may matter more than any single earnings report this week.

Interest Rates

Yields edged higher through the week as oil repriced inflation risk, then paused Friday as crude retreated. The FOMC meeting that starts tomorrow is the next forcing function.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (fourth straight hold; meeting July 28-29; hold now ~80% in futures, down from 66% hike-or-hold split last week)
2-year Treasury: 4.34% up from 4.26% last week
10-year Treasury: 4.69% continuing to press higher
30-year Treasury: 5.18% fourth week above 5%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.58% Freddie Mac July 23; fourth consecutive weekly increase
Bottom Line
  • The rate story this week is less about the Fed's decision and more about what the statement says. Markets are pricing an 80% hold, but the language around inflation, the tariff framework, and any hint about September will move the 2-year more than the decision itself. Chair Warsh's 2:30 PM press conference on Wednesday is the event to watch.
  • Mortgage rates at 6.58% on a four-week uptrend reflect both the oil-driven inflation risk and the 30-year Treasury above 5%. For borrowers, this is the tightest affordability squeeze of 2026. If oil falls below $90 and holds there through July, the August Freddie Mac readings could finally break the uptrend. If the peace holds.

Savings & CD Rates

Top CDs now reach 4.50% APY at select institutions, and the rate-hike scare - even if it fades at this meeting - has kept banks from cutting offers.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.70% APY (FDIC est.) edging up as competitive rates pull averages
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.50% APY (Southland Credit Union); 4.35% (Bankrate top pick); 4.20% (CNBC Select top pick)
E*TRADE 1-yr CD: 4.15% APY (no minimum deposit)
Best high-yield savings: ~4.15% APY (leading online banks)
Bottom Line
  • A 4.50% top CD against a trailing CPI of 3.5% still clears inflation by a full percentage point. The Iran pause does not immediately change this math - banks will not drop CD offers while the Fed's next move is unclear. The lock-in argument is intact for now; if the cease fire holds and oil settles into the $80s, the calculus could shift by fall as hike odds fade permanently and cut odds reenter the picture.

Energy & Gas Prices

Brent fell nearly 4% Friday as diplomacy signals emerged; the weekend pause announcement points to a significant further drop Monday. Gas prices are still rising, trailing crude by two to three weeks.

WTI crude (Friday close): $89.30/bbl −3.0% Friday
Brent crude (Friday close): $96.80/bbl −3.9% Friday; up ~15% since the conflict began
Weekend development: US-Iran pause announced Saturday; crude futures point sharply lower for Monday open
National gas avg: $4.11/gal still climbing; pump prices trail crude by 2-3 weeks
West Coast (California): $5.57/gal highest in the nation
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.65/gal (Mississippi)
Bottom Line
  • Oil falling below $90 on a genuine Iran pause would be the most meaningful deflationary signal of the summer. It would remove the primary argument for a July rate hike, ease the pressure on the August CPI, and eventually pull gas prices down from $4.11. The word "genuine" carries the weight here: the previous ceasefire attempt ended when Iran struck commercial vessels bypassing its preapproved route. The durability of this pause is the variable that matters most to every other number in this briefing.
  • Gas at $4.11 nationally is still a pump-price reality even if crude softens Monday. The relief takes weeks to reach the station. California at $5.57 versus Mississippi at $3.65 remains the sharpest geographic split in this cycle - a near-$2 spread on an identical gallon, driven by refinery capacity, state taxes, and blend requirements that crude diplomacy does not fix.

Commodities

Gold pulled back from last week's $4,135 as the safe-haven bid eased on Iran signals. Cattle rebounded slightly; copper and grains were mixed.

Gold / oz: $4,053 −$82 from last week's $4,135; safe-haven bid retreating on diplomacy
Silver / oz: $58.28 steady
Copper / lb: $6.30 −2.2% from $6.44 last week
Corn / bushel: $4.64 +0.4%; western drought conditions supporting prices
Soybeans / bushel: $12.48 +1.2%; bloom progress and European heat keeping bids firm
Live cattle / cwt: $227.07 +$3.87 from last week's $223.20; partial rebound after two-week slide
Bottom Line
  • Gold shedding $82 in a week is the market pricing a less frightening outcome on Iran. It held $4,053 because the uncertainty remains - a pause is not a deal - and because the tariff regime change and FOMC meeting still leave the macro picture unsettled. If the pause breaks down, gold returns to $4,100 and above quickly.
  • Cattle bouncing from $223 to $227 partially reverses the grocery relief from last week. The structural ceiling is unchanged: the US herd remains the smallest since 1951, and any sustained demand recovery hits that supply floor fast. Meanwhile grains are firming on Europe's heat - French maize at a 13-year low rating is now the food-cost risk to watch alongside domestic energy prices.

Crop Weather

The latest USDA crop progress data (week ending July 20) showed corn holding, soybeans recovering, and spring wheat under heat pressure. The next report is due Monday morning.

Corn: 67% good-to-excellent (as of July 20; Monday's report covers week ending July 27). Dough stage 20%+ with pollination largely complete.
Soybeans: 66% good-to-excellent (as of July 20; second straight weekly improvement). Blooming 66%, ahead of the 5-year average.
Spring wheat: 53% good-to-excellent as of July 20 −5 pts over two weeks; North Dakota dropped hardest.
Drought: Roughly 19% of US corn acres in some degree of drought, concentrated in the western Corn Belt and Plains. Last week's heat pulled soil moisture ahead of critical grain fill.
Global: Europe's record heatwave maintained pressure on French maize (13-year low rating). EU wheat estimate revised to 140.8 Mt. Black Sea harvest remains strong and partially offsets European losses.
Bottom Line
  • Monday's crop progress report (week ending July 27) is the next read on whether the western drought's expansion slowed or accelerated over the past week. The corn crop is in the grain-fill stage now - this is the period where weekly ratings matter most for final yield estimates. A further slip in corn ratings on top of deteriorating spring wheat would put grain prices on notice.

No new housing data this week; the June picture remains the baseline. A record median price, rising foreclosure starts, and now a mortgage rate at 6.58% heading into the Fed meeting.

Foreclosure starts (June): 26,217 +20% YoY
H1 2026 foreclosure filings: 227,548 +21% YoY
Completed foreclosures (June): 4,773 +23% YoY
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency: 11.88% highest since 2021
Existing-home sales (June): 4.09M ann. −2.4% MoM, below the 4.20M expected
Median existing-home price: $440,600 record; 36th straight month of YoY gains
Sellers cutting asking price: 35.4% near last August's record (Redfin)
Months of supply: 4.6
30-year mortgage: 6.58% fourth consecutive weekly increase
Bottom Line
  • The housing picture has not changed this week because the data has not updated. What has changed is the mortgage rate: 6.58% is 15 basis points higher than it was a month ago, and it landed on a market already running a record median price with frozen volume and accelerating foreclosure activity underneath. Every additional basis point on the mortgage rate is another buyer priced out of a market where sellers are already cutting prices to close.
  • The one thing that could change the trajectory: a Fed hold accompanied by dovish language Wednesday, and an oil price that sustains below $90 through August, could pull mortgage rates back toward 6.40% by fall. That is the path to real affordability relief. It requires both. Right now neither is guaranteed.

Household Finance

The metrics haven't moved this week - but the tariff transition and gas at $4.11 are new costs landing on a household sector already near its limits.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion +5.9% YoY; avg new-offer APR 23.8%
Card balances 90+ days late: 13.1% highest in roughly 15 years
Avg. new-car payment: $777/mo third consecutive record; avg $44,156 financed at 7.0% APR
84+ month auto loans: 23.9% of new financings record
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY; 29% now use BNPL for groceries
Personal savings rate: 3.0% (May 2026; June reading due Thursday with PCE)
Bottom Line
  • Thursday's June PCE and savings rate will be the first read on how household finances absorbed the oil price surge that began after the Iran conflict escalated. The May savings rate of 3.0% was already thin; $4.09-$4.11 gas and the tariff-driven import price increases that began with Section 301 on July 24 are the new headwinds not yet in any dataset.
  • The structural divide here is not between rich and poor - it is between balance-sheet-positive and balance-sheet-negative. A saver clearing 4.50% on a CD is net positive on inflation; a cardholder at 23.8% APR with a 90-day delinquency is in a different economy entirely. Section 301 tariffs falling on imported consumer goods will land hardest on the second group, not the first.

Crypto

Bitcoin held the $63,000-$66,000 range for a third consecutive week. The macro calendar will move these prices more than anything on-chain.

Bitcoin (BTC): $65,359 +$901 from Saturday; range-bound for three weeks
Ethereum (ETH): ~$1,862 retreated on Friday as Treasury yields rose
Stuck range: BTC has failed to hold above $66,000 or drop below $63,000 for nearly three weeks; $63,000-$66,000 acts as the current vice.
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin sitting at $65,359 reflects a market waiting on the same things equities are waiting on: the FOMC decision, the earnings reports, and the Iran situation. Crypto has been trading as a pure risk asset this cycle - it moves with Treasury yields and risk appetite more than with on-chain fundamentals. A Fed hold paired with a clear Iran pause could free up the range to the upside; a hawkish surprise or a geopolitical re-escalation does the opposite.
  • The overhead supply problem remains unchanged. Anyone who bought BTC between $75,000 and the $126,000 all-time high is sitting on a loss. That supply cap has capped every rally attempt at approximately $67,500, and nothing in this week's calendar resolves it structurally.

Around the World

Japan and China sold off Friday. European markets closed near flat. The Iran pause landed after markets closed and will reshape Monday's global open.

Japan: Nikkei at 64,611 −2.3% from last week's 66,116; energy and chip names led declines.
Europe: STOXX 600 near 647 held largely flat Friday as oil retreat offset tariff uncertainty.
China: Shanghai Composite at 3,814 −1.6% Friday; Hang Seng under continued pressure.
Emerging markets: MSCI EM still down sharply over the past month as oil-importing economies absorb dollar-denominated energy costs; dollar index near 101.
Weekend catalyst: US-Iran pause announced after Friday close; Asian and European markets open Monday without having priced it in yet.
Bottom Line
  • Monday's global open is unusually consequential. Japan, Europe, and Asia will all be pricing the Iran pause for the first time when they open. The net effect should be positive - lower oil, easing inflation risk, and less Fed pressure - but the tariff framework change and the US earnings calendar introduce offsets. Europe's energy and defense names that rallied on the conflict may give back ground; oil-importing emerging markets should see relief.
  • The Nikkei losing 2.3% in a week while the S&P barely moved shows how differently oil and currency exposure translate across markets. Japan imports virtually all of its crude; a sustained move below $90 Brent is unambiguously positive for Japanese corporate margins and the yen, which has been punished all year by the energy-driven trade deficit.
