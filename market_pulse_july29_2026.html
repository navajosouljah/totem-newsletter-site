The Quick Read

Two sessions of severe Asian chip losses in a row is now a trend, not a reaction. When a market event repeats on consecutive days without a new escalating trigger, it tells you the repricing is structural rather than reflexive. Tuesday's Kospi circuit breaker was triggered by a specific catalyst - the Nvidia-OpenAI financing report. Wednesday's Kospi decline of 6.8% came without a new development of comparable magnitude. The selling on the second day is the market deciding, with more time to deliberate, that Tuesday's repricing was correct and perhaps incomplete. That is the more unsettling signal: the first-day move is emotional, but the second-day continuation is considered.

The chip sector's two-day behavior creates an interpretive pressure on tonight's earnings calls that would not have existed a week ago. Microsoft and Meta both need to answer a question the market is now openly asking: at what point does AI capital expenditure at this scale become a risk rather than a moat? Meta's $125-145 billion full-year CapEx guidance is not new information - it was disclosed in April. But the framing around it has changed entirely since Monday. A week ago, that guidance was read as confidence. Tonight, depending on how management discusses return on that investment, it may be read as exposure. Azure's growth rate at Microsoft faces the same lens: strong cloud numbers will be welcomed, but the market's follow-on question will be whether cloud infrastructure margins are compressing as CapEx commitments scale. The AI-spending thesis is not in crisis - but it is under the first genuine structural scrutiny it has faced since January's DeepSeek correction.

The Fed's 2:30 PM press conference runs in parallel with all of this and adds a third variable to the session. Warsh holds a rate that is now below the current pace of oil-adjusted inflation but above the level that is actively restraining growth - the classic neutral-zone ambiguity. The signal the market most wants is whether he acknowledges that oil's roughly 10% decline since July 21 represents a genuine change in the near-term inflation trajectory. That acknowledgment would clear the path for September language. What the market does not want is a statement that treats the oil decline as temporary - the same framing that kept rates on hold through the spring inflation data. The 2-year Treasury's reaction in the 30 minutes after the 2:30 PM press conference begins will be the clearest real-time verdict on what Warsh delivered.

Markets can hold both in mind simultaneously, and on days like this week, they appear to be doing so.
