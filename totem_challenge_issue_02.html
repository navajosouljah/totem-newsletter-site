This Week's Voice: Jamie Dimon

You do not get a better read on the global economy than the man who runs the largest bank in the United States. So when Jamie Dimon talks, you stop scrolling and you listen.

7 min read Share

Last week he talked. Twice.

Who he is

Jamie Dimon is chairman and CEO of JPMorgan Chase, nearly $5 trillion in assets, the biggest bank in the country. He has steered that bank through 2008 and through the pandemic. When he reads the room on Wall Street, he is reading it from the best seat in the house.

What he said

On May 27 at the Bernstein conference, Dimon described a market running hot. Deals flowing. Bankers busy. Everyone leaning in. His words:

"It's gung-ho, folks."

Then the part that should make you sit up.

"There's a lot of exuberance out there. But it was in 1972, 1986, 2000, 2007. That doesn't give me comfort."

Four years. Four peaks. Four crashes that followed.

He did not stop at a feeling. He pointed at the plumbing. Weakening loan covenants. Stretched earnings. Refinancing risk piling up. And private credit values that have not yet caught up to the volatility of early 2026. On the credit cycle turning, he said:

"When it happens, it will be worse than people expect."

He is not the only one. Warren Buffett's own gauge, total stock market value against the size of the economy, just printed around 230%, near an all-time high. Buffett once said that crossing 200% means you are playing with fire.

Where we agree with him

Most of it. That is the honest answer.

Dimon is not calling a crash next week. He is saying the conditions that come before trouble are all present at once, and the people inside the party rarely see the door. We think he is right. Valuations are stretched. Credit is loosening at exactly the wrong moment. And the part nobody wants to hear, that private credit marks have not been tested by real stress yet, is the sharpest thing he said. Paper gains are not gains until someone pays you.

He is reading the same chart we read every morning. The difference is what each of us does about it.

What he is warning you about

Read his list again and notice what it is made of. Default risk. Covenant erosion. Refinancing that may not be there when the loan comes due. Asset values on a screen that have not met a buyer. Every item on Dimon's worry list is a risk that lives between the moment you put money in and the moment you get it back.

That gap is the whole problem. The longer your capital sits exposed, the more of his list you are carrying.
