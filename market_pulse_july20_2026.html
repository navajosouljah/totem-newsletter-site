Weekly Briefing • Sunday, July 20, 2026

CPI Drops to 3.5%. Oil Surges 13% on Iran. Nasdaq Falls 3%.

June CPI posted the largest monthly drop since April 2020 - down 0.4% to 3.5% year-over-year. But the Iran conflict sent oil up 13% in a week to $82, threatening to reverse the disinflation. The Nasdaq fell 2.9% as chip stocks sold off. Housing starts jumped 19% on a multifamily rebound. Goldman posted record trading revenue. The FOMC meets July 28-29, and the Section 122 tariff expires July 24.

This Week in the News

  • June CPI dropped 0.4% month-over-month - the largest decline since April 2020. Year-over-year inflation fell to 3.5% from 4.2%. Energy prices drove the move, with gasoline down 12% in the PPI report.
  • Oil surged 13% on Iran escalation. WTI hit $82.49 after Iran struck Kuwait infrastructure and the U.S. blockaded Iranian ports. The Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea shipping lanes are both disrupted.
  • Housing starts jumped 19% to 1.427 million. The headline number beat estimates by a wide margin, but single-family was flat - the entire surge was multifamily. Permits fell 3%.
  • Goldman Sachs posted record stock-trading revenue of $7.42 billion. Netflix hit a 52-week low despite 325 million subscribers after guiding Q3 revenue below consensus.

The Quick Read

The June CPI report delivered the best inflation news in two years - headline down 0.4% in a single month to 3.5% year-over-year, core at 2.6%. But before the market could celebrate, the Iran conflict sent oil from $69 to $82 in two weeks, gas is climbing toward $4.00 nationally, and the Nasdaq fell 2.9% as chip stocks sold off on TSMC spending concerns and Chinese AI competition. The S&P 500 closed at 7,458. Mortgage rates ticked up to 6.55%. Gold fell below $4,000 for the first time since November.

The CPI-versus-oil tension is the story of the summer. June's inflation print captured the oil crash from $89 to $69 - the energy disinflation that everyone was waiting for. But with crude now at $82 and the Iran conflict intensifying, the July CPI (reported in August) will likely show energy inflation returning. The question is whether the Fed looks through the oil spike as temporary or treats it as a reason to hold longer. The FOMC meets July 28-29.

Housing starts jumping 19% sounds like a recovery until you see that single-family was flat and permits fell 3%. The surge was entirely multifamily - large apartment complexes, not the starter homes that first-time buyers need. Meanwhile, ATTOM's mid-year report showed foreclosure filings up 21% year-over-year in H1 2026 with the fastest time-to-foreclosure since 2013. The Section 122 tariff expires July 24 - one of the most consequential trade policy dates of the year.

Stock Market

Iran headlines and chip selloffs dominated the week. The Nasdaq fell nearly 3% while small caps held up better.

S&P 500: 7,458 −1.6% wk
Dow Jones: 52,146 −0.9% wk
Nasdaq: 25,520 −2.9% wk
Russell 2000 (small caps): 2,962 −0.5% wk
Fear gauge (VIX): 18.77 +12.2% wk
Bottom Line
  • The Nasdaq falling 2.9% while the Russell lost only 0.5% is the rotation continuing. Chip stocks bore the brunt after TSMC raised spending and Chinese AI firm Moonshot introduced competitive pressure. Apple was the exception, gaining 5.8% on the week - the only Mag 7 name in the green.
  • The VIX jumping 12% to 18.77 signals the market is pricing in real uncertainty heading into the FOMC meeting and tariff expiration. Two binary events in the same week creates the conditions for outsized moves in either direction.

Interest Rates

The CPI miss pulled yields down initially, but oil and Iran reversed the move by Friday.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (held since December 2025)
2-year Treasury: 4.19%
10-year Treasury: 4.58%
30-year Treasury: 5.10% still above 5%
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.55% +12 bps from 6.43% two weeks ago
Bottom Line
  • The 30-year Treasury holding above 5% despite the best CPI print in two years tells you the bond market is watching oil, not the backward-looking inflation report. If crude stays above $80, the July CPI will show energy inflation returning and the rate-cut timeline pushes further out.
  • Mortgage rates at 6.55% have now reversed the entire drop from the June jobs miss. The brief window of 6.43% rates is closed. For buyers watching rates, the oil price is now the more important variable than the Fed.

Savings & CD Rates

Top CD rates holding near 4.30% APY. The CPI miss makes locking in rates more appealing if cuts eventually arrive.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.65% APY
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.30% APY (Newtek Bank, Popular Direct 4.17%, E*TRADE 4.10%)
Best credit union CD: 6.00% APY (Financial Partners CU, 8-month)
Best high-yield savings: 5.00% APY (Varo, first $5K)
Bottom Line
  • The CPI falling to 3.5% means a 4.30% CD now earns a real positive return above inflation for the first time in months. If the Fed eventually cuts on the back of disinflation, these rates fall with them. The argument for locking in is stronger this week than last.

Energy & Gas Prices

The Iran conflict reversed three months of energy disinflation in a single week. Oil is the most important price in the economy right now.

WTI crude: $82.49/bbl +13% wk - biggest weekly surge in years
Brent crude: ~$87/bbl +14% wk
National gas avg: $3.95/gal +$0.15 from last week
West Coast (California): $5.37/gal approaching May peak
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): $3.34/gal
Bottom Line
  • Oil surging from $69 to $82 in two weeks is the single biggest threat to the disinflation story. The June CPI captured the oil crash - the July CPI will capture the oil spike. If WTI stays above $80, the sub-3.5% CPI path the market was pricing in after Monday's report is off the table.
  • Gas at $3.95 nationally is heading toward $4.00. California at $5.37 is approaching the May peak. The Iran conflict involves both the Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea shipping lanes - the two most important oil transit chokepoints in the world. This is not a one-day headline risk.

Commodities

Gold fell below $4,000 despite the Iran escalation. Silver dropped 7%. Soybeans and corn firmed on crop weather.

Gold / oz: ~$3,990 −3%+ wk, below $4,000 first time since November
Silver / oz: $55.20 −7% wk
Copper / lb: $6.27 flat
Corn / bushel: $4.45 +0.7%
Soybeans / bushel: $12.05 +1.7%
Live cattle / cwt: $224.42 −$13 from two weeks ago
Bottom Line
  • Gold falling 3% while oil surges 13% on the same geopolitical catalyst is a significant divergence. It suggests the gold rally was driven by rate-cut expectations, not safe-haven demand. With the CPI miss making cuts more plausible long-term but oil making them less likely near-term, gold is caught between two narratives.
  • Cattle pulling back to $224 from the $255 record is welcome relief for beef prices, but the US herd at 86.2 million head - the smallest since 1951 - means every pullback is temporary. Grocery inflation runs on its own clock.

Crop Weather

Winter wheat conditions deteriorating. Corn tracking normal seasonal development. Drought pressure continues on the Plains.

Corn: Tracking normal seasonal development per USDA. Conditions stable through early July.
Winter wheat: 35% good-to-excellent - down 13 percentage points from year-ago levels. Ongoing Plains drought.
Drought: Significant pressure continues across the Plains wheat belt. Nebraska and western Kansas remain in extreme drought.
Bottom Line
  • Winter wheat at 35% good-to-excellent - down 13 points from a year ago - confirms the worst harvest since 1965 is locked in. Higher flour, bread, and cereal prices in Q3 and Q4 remain a near-certainty regardless of what oil does.

Real Estate

Foreclosures up 21% in H1. Housing starts surged on multifamily but single-family was flat. Median price hit an all-time record.

Foreclosure filings H1 2026: 227,548 +21% YoY, +28% from two years ago
Foreclosure starts: +18% YoY
REO completions: +33% YoY
Avg time in foreclosure: 563 days (shortest since 2013)
Mortgage delinquency rate: 4.44% +40 bps YoY
FHA delinquency rate: 11.88% highest since Q2 2021
Existing-home sales: 4.09M ann. −2.4% MoM
Median existing-home price: $440,600 all-time record
Pending home sales: −5.4%
Housing starts: 1.427M +19% MoM - but multifamily only
30-year mortgage: 6.55% still elevated
Bottom Line
  • ATTOM's mid-year report is the most important housing data of the week. Foreclosure filings up 21% year-over-year with the fastest time-to-foreclosure since 2013 means the distress pipeline is accelerating, not stabilizing. Florida leads at 1 in 373 homes with a filing.
  • Housing starts jumping 19% is a good headline hiding a mixed story. Single-family was flat. Permits fell 3%. The surge was large multifamily complexes - not the starter homes first-time buyers need. Meanwhile, pending sales fell 5.4%, which signals closings will weaken in the months ahead.
  • The median existing-home price hitting $440,600 - an all-time record - while foreclosures rise 21% and sales fall is the K-shaped housing market in its clearest form. Prices hold because inventory is tight, but the stress underneath is building.

Household Finance

Savings rate fell back to 2.6%. Retail sales rose 0.2% but gas station spending dropped 5.3%. The consumer is still spending - just barely.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.33 trillion record; avg APR 20.94%
Avg. new-car payment: $770/mo record, 20.3% of buyers pay $1,000+/mo
Subprime auto 60+ days delinquent: 6.90% 32-year high
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY
Personal savings rate: 2.6% April reading, down from 3.0%
Retail sales (June): +0.2% MoM; ex-gas +0.7%
Bottom Line
  • Retail sales at +0.2% with gas station spending down 5.3% means the consumer is redirecting savings from cheaper gas into other categories - online retail +1.9%, car dealerships +1.9%. The headline looks tepid but ex-gas spending was solid at +0.7%. The cheaper gas in June was doing real work.
  • The savings rate falling back to 2.6% from 3.0% is the wrong direction. If gas prices are now climbing back toward $4.00, the brief cash cushion from falling energy costs evaporates. The consumer below the median is running on margins thin enough that a $15-per-week gas increase changes the math.

Crypto

Bitcoin held above $64,000 despite equity weakness. T. Rowe Price launched a multi-crypto ETF. Range-bound week.

Bitcoin (BTC): $64,900 +1.5% wk
Ethereum (ETH): $1,855 flat wk
Solana (SOL): $75.86 −0.6% wk
XRP: $1.08 flat wk
Bottom Line
  • Bitcoin holding above $64,000 while the Nasdaq fell 2.9% is a notable decoupling. Last month crypto moved in lockstep with equities on every macro data point. This week it ignored the chip selloff entirely. The T. Rowe Price multi-crypto ETF launch on July 17 - covering BTC, ETH, and XRP - is another institutional on-ramp that adds structural demand regardless of sentiment.
  • The range-bound trading ($64K-$65K for BTC) suggests crypto is waiting for the same catalysts as everything else: the FOMC meeting and the tariff expiration. A dovish surprise on July 29 could push BTC toward $70K. A hawkish hold keeps it pinned.

Around the World

Japan's Nikkei fell 6.4% - the sharpest weekly drop of the year. Europe held up. China under pressure.

Japan: Nikkei at 64,140 −6.4% wk. Yen strengthening and AI spending concerns hit hard. Sharpest weekly loss of 2026.
Europe: STOXX 600 at 641 −0.4%. European banks fell on gas price inflation risk. BBVA and Deutsche Bank each down ~2.5%.
China: Shanghai Composite at ~3,796 −8.8% past month. Domestic stimulus not offsetting global risk-off.
Emerging: Under pressure from oil surge and strong dollar. Tariff expiration July 24 remains the key binary event.
Bottom Line
  • Japan's 6.4% weekly loss is the global tell. The Nikkei has been the best-performing major index of 2026, so when it sells off this hard - on yen strength and AI infrastructure doubts - it signals a broader reassessment of the AI trade that powered markets all year.
  • The July 24 tariff expiration is now four days away. If Section 122 expires without a replacement, the effective U.S. tariff rate drops from ~13% to ~7%. If USTR finalizes the Section 301 replacement (10% on 14 economies, 12.5% on 46 others), the tariff regime restructures entirely. Either outcome moves emerging markets and global supply chains.
