Weekly Briefing • Monday, July 27, 2026

Brent Falls Below $90. Chips Crater 4%. Fed Decides Wednesday - MSFT and META Report the Same Night.

The Iran strike pause delivered the oil break the market had been waiting for - Brent fell below $90 for the first time in three weeks and WTI settled near $82.40. But the relief rally was uneven: Nvidia led chips sharply lower on the AI-spending question, the Nasdaq closed in the red, and the VIX edged higher rather than retreating. The FOMC begins its two-day meeting Tuesday, with the decision Wednesday at 2 PM ET - the same evening Microsoft and Meta report. Apple, Amazon, Q2 GDP, and June PCE all arrive Thursday.

Today in the News

  • The Iran strike pause held its first full trading day, sending oil down more than 9% as a relief rally lifted energy-importing economies and transport stocks. WTI settled at $81.92 and Brent settled at $87.75 - reversing most of the conflict-driven price surge from the past three weeks. The US suspended strikes for a second consecutive night; Iran halted its retaliatory attacks. Iranian and Omani deputy foreign ministers met in Tehran over the weekend to negotiate Strait of Hormuz shipping access - a more structured diplomatic channel than previous ceasefire attempts.
  • Chip stocks broke lower independent of the Iran news, led by Nvidia's 4.9% decline and AMD's 8.3% drop, with ASML falling more than 5% on reports that CXMT - a Chinese state-backed chipmaker - began mass-producing critical semiconductor equipment. CXMT debuted on the Shanghai exchange Monday with a 465% first-day gain, briefly hitting a $484 billion market cap. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell 2.4% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped roughly 4%. Jim Cramer flagged the sell-off as driven by "monstrous, motivated, and often margined" sellers, suggesting leveraged positions are being forced out. The AI-capital-spending question now follows Microsoft and Meta into earnings Wednesday.
  • Treasury yields eased modestly on the day as cheaper oil reduced the immediate inflation case for a rate hike. The 10-year settled near 4.65%, down from 4.69% Friday - a real move but smaller than early-session gains suggested. The 2-year held near 4.33%. The FOMC begins its two-day meeting tomorrow (Tuesday); the rate decision and Chair Warsh's press conference come Wednesday at 2:00 PM and 2:30 PM ET respectively.
  • The USDA released its weekly crop progress report Monday afternoon covering the week ending July 27. With the western Corn Belt drought persisting through the grain-fill stage and spring wheat under heat pressure, the ratings bear close watching before Thursday's GDP and PCE data arrive.

The Quick Read

Oil broke below $90 today, and the market's reaction told you everything about where the real tension now sits. Brent settled at $87.75, down more than 9% - and WTI settled near $81.92, reversing most of its conflict-driven premium from the past three weeks. The Iran strike pause, which Tehran declined to officially call a ceasefire, was enough for energy futures to move decisively. The rate-hike odds that had climbed to 34% last week retreated further Monday as yields eased. If oil holds below $90 into Wednesday's Fed decision, the case for a July hike is substantially weakened.

But the semiconductor sector has its own story, and it began in Shanghai. Nvidia fell nearly 5%. AMD dropped more than 8%. Intel fell 7.9% - despite beating earnings with a $0.42/share EPS print and raising its quarterly forecast. The trigger across the sector was CXMT, a Chinese state-backed chipmaker that debuted on the Shanghai exchange Monday with a 465% first-day gain, briefly touching a $484 billion market cap. Reports that CXMT has begun mass-producing critical semiconductor equipment rattled US and Dutch chipmakers alike - ASML fell more than 5% on the same news. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) lost 2.4%. What is layered on top of the CXMT story is the same question Alphabet raised last Thursday: when AI infrastructure spending scales to these levels, does it compress margins before delivering returns? That question hits Microsoft and Meta directly Wednesday. Both companies report after the Fed decision.

The Dow gained 263 points as energy-cost relief boosted transports, industrials, and with Apple surging 3.5% providing significant index weight. The S&P 500 added just 1.2 points - essentially flat, +0.02%. The Nasdaq slipped 0.18%. Those three numbers in the same session - Dow up 0.5%, chips under pressure, Nasdaq slightly red - describe a market rotating within itself rather than moving directionally. That is a reasonable response to a week that still has its most consequential data points ahead: the Fed statement, four megacap earnings, Q2 GDP, and June PCE, all landing by Thursday afternoon. The VIX closed at 18.86, up slightly from Friday's 18.58 - elevated for a pre-FOMC tape, and a sign that Monday's oil relief did not eliminate the uncertainty about Wednesday.

Stock Market

A split session: energy, industrials, and financials rallied on oil relief; chips and mega-cap tech dragged the Nasdaq into the red and kept the S&P essentially flat. Intraday breadth was actually healthy - advance/decline on the NYSE ran 3:1 in favor of advancers - but index weighting in tech masked that.

S&P 500: 7,413 +0.02% Monday; essentially flat; chip and tech losses offset broad relief rally
Dow Jones: 52,210 +0.51% (+263 pts); Apple +3.5% provided significant Dow support; transports and industrials lifted by oil decline
Nasdaq: 24,932 −0.18%; Nvidia, AMD, ASML, and Intel losses concentrated in tech weighting
Russell 2000: 2,950 +0.69%; small-caps outperformed as oil-relief benefits smaller domestic firms more directly
Semiconductors (SMH): −2.4% Monday; Nvidia −4.9%, AMD −8.3%, ASML −5%+, Intel −7.9% (despite EPS beat); CXMT China chipmaker debut rattled the sector
Fear gauge (VIX): 18.86 up slightly from Friday's 18.58; intraday range 14.96-20.31; elevated for a pre-FOMC tape
Bottom Line
  • The chip divergence is the most important signal of the day - and it has two layers. The first is the CXMT story: a Chinese state-backed chipmaker publicly listing with a 465% first-day gain signals that the US-led competitive moat in advanced semiconductor manufacturing now has a visible, state-funded challenger. That is a new structural concern, not a cyclical one. The second layer is the AI-capex-margin debate: Intel beat earnings and still fell 7.9% because the market is applying the Alphabet earnings lens to every chip-adjacent company now. When Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday, the same lens applies.
  • The VIX closing at 18.86 - higher than Friday's 18.58 on a day when oil fell 9% - is the most telling number of the session. In a normal relief rally, fear decreases as the triggering risk retreats. Monday's oil collapse should have pushed VIX lower. Instead it ticked up. The market is telling you that even with energy risk reduced, the uncertainty about Wednesday's Fed decision and megacap earnings outcomes is replacing it. A VIX above 18 heading into a Fed decision and the biggest earnings week of the summer is a market that is not relaxed.

Interest Rates

Oil's 9% drop weakened the primary argument for a July hike. Yields eased modestly - real moves, but smaller than early-session rallies suggested. The FOMC begins its two-day meeting Tuesday; the decision comes Wednesday at 2 PM ET.

Fed policy rate: 3.50-3.75% (hold expected; FOMC begins Tuesday July 28; decision Wednesday July 29 at 2:00 PM ET; Chair Warsh press conference 2:30 PM)
2-year Treasury: 4.33% eased ~2 bps from Friday's 4.35%; modest move as oil-driven inflation risk fades
10-year Treasury: 4.65% fell from 4.69%; yields eased but the move was less dramatic than early session suggested
30-year Treasury: 5.13% eased from ~5.16% Friday; still well above May levels
30-year mortgage (avg.): 6.58% Freddie Mac July 23; weekly reading due Thursday; a further modest easing in the 10-year may soften this slightly
Bottom Line
  • The rate market moved modestly today. A 4-basis-point drop in the 10-year is meaningful in direction but smaller than the magnitude of the oil move might suggest. The bond market appears to be waiting for Wednesday's FOMC statement rather than repricing aggressively on a single day's oil data. If the oil decline holds into Wednesday's deliberations, the August CPI argument for hiking weakens further - and the Fed's optionality for September or November cuts improves.
  • Wednesday's statement will be read for two things beyond the decision itself: any mention of the oil price move and its implication for August inflation, and any language around Section 301 tariffs as a new price-level factor. Chair Warsh has spoken before about the distinction between temporary price effects and embedded inflation. The word choice Wednesday will tell the bond market whether the Fed has shifted its view - and the 2-year will move on the nuance more than on the rate call itself.
  • For mortgage borrowers, the 10-year settling near 4.65% is a modest improvement from recent highs but not yet the decisive break needed to change the Freddie Mac survey trajectory. If the 10-year holds this range through Thursday's reading, the four-week streak of mortgage rate increases may slow or pause. A break clearly below 4.60% - which would require a more explicitly dovish Fed statement on Wednesday - would be needed to see meaningful relief toward 6.40-6.45%.

Savings & CD Rates

Top CD offers have not yet moved in response to Monday's yield decline. Banks typically take a week or more to adjust deposit rate offers after Treasury moves.

National avg 1-yr CD: ~1.70% APY (FDIC est.) unchanged
Best online 1-yr CD: 4.50% APY (Southland Credit Union); 4.35% (Bankrate top pick)
Best high-yield savings: ~4.15% APY (leading online banks)
Bottom Line
  • A 4.50% top CD against a CPI of 3.5% still delivers a real return of roughly 1 full percentage point. The yield decline today does not immediately change that math - CD offers are sticky downward. But if the 10-year holds below 4.60% through August and cut expectations resurface, competitive CD rates will begin to compress by September. The lock-in argument remains intact now; the window may be measured in weeks rather than months.

Energy & Gas Prices

The oil move the market was waiting for: WTI settled at $81.92 and Brent settled at $87.75 - both well below $90. Gas prices remain elevated; the crude decline takes 2-3 weeks to reach the pump.

WTI crude (Monday close): $81.92/bbl −8.3% Monday; down from $89.30 Friday close; largest single-day decline since the conflict began
Brent crude (Monday close): $87.75/bbl −9.3% from Friday's $96.80; first settle below $90 since early July
Iran pause status: US suspended strikes for a second consecutive night. Iran halted its retaliatory military strikes. Iranian and Omani deputy foreign ministers met in Tehran Friday-Saturday to negotiate Strait of Hormuz shipping access. The pause is holding but remains informal and unverified.
National gas avg: $4.11/gal unchanged; pump prices trail crude by 2-3 weeks
West Coast (California): $5.57/gal still highest in the nation
Cheapest region (Gulf Coast): ~$3.65/gal (Mississippi)
Bottom Line
  • WTI at $81.92 and Brent at $87.75 represent the largest single-session oil drop since the conflict began. If it holds - the critical condition - the deflationary signal reaches August CPI data and eventually the Freddie Mac mortgage rate survey. Pump relief will take until mid-August. The FOMC sees these settlement prices as it deliberates Tuesday and Wednesday; oil this low substantially weakens the case for a July hike.
  • The Omani diplomatic channel is the variable to watch. Iranian and Omani deputy foreign ministers met Friday-Saturday to negotiate Strait of Hormuz shipping access - a more concrete diplomatic structure than previous pause attempts. But "informal and unverified" still describes the current status. The previous ceasefire collapsed within days. One overnight strike reversal restores the $96+ Brent level. That asymmetry - relief is slow, reversal is instant - is why gold is still holding near $4,087 even as oil drops 9%.
  • Gas at $4.11 nationally means households are absorbing $4 fuel costs regardless of what crude does Monday. The geographic split - California at $5.57 versus Mississippi at $3.65 - reflects refinery constraints and state taxes that the Iran situation doesn't resolve. Californians are paying nearly $2 more per gallon for the same crude-input story.

Commodities

Gold settled at $4,087 - up from Friday's $4,053 - as the Iran pause left enough uncertainty to keep the safe-haven bid alive. Silver and copper both rose. Grains held firm on weather.

Gold / oz: $4,087 +$34 from Friday's $4,053; +0.40%; safe-haven bid intact despite oil drop
Silver / oz: $58.56 +$0.58; +1.0%; outperforming gold on the day
Copper / lb: $6.33 +0.4%; recovered as oil-relief improved global growth expectations
Corn / bushel: ~$4.67 +0.6%; drought pressure in the western Corn Belt persisting during grain fill
Soybeans / bushel: ~$12.55 +0.6%; European heat maintaining pressure on global supply
Live cattle / cwt: ~$227.50 holding the prior week's partial rebound; herd constraints unchanged
Bottom Line
  • Gold settling at $4,087 while oil fell 9% on the same day is the most informative signal of the session. These two typically move in the same direction when a geopolitical risk premium unwinds - oil falls, gold falls with it as fear subsides. The fact that gold moved higher instead tells you the market does not believe the Iran pause is permanent. Diplomatic talks in Tehran are more concrete than previous ceasefire attempts, but informal status and the history of previous collapses are keeping the safe-haven bid alive. If this pause holds through Wednesday's FOMC, gold pressure builds downward. If it breaks, gold reclaims $4,135+ quickly.
  • Corn and soybean prices continuing to firm during the grain-fill window signals a real weather risk, not a speculative one. The western Corn Belt drought persisting through the most yield-sensitive period of the crop cycle creates the risk of a late-summer grain price surge that hits food costs just as the Section 301 tariff effects are entering the August CPI. These are separate forces pointing in the same inflationary direction for food prices specifically.

Crop Weather

The USDA crop progress report for the week ending July 27 was released Monday afternoon. The grain-fill stage for corn is the highest-stakes period of the growing season; this week's ratings carry the most weight until August.

Corn (week ending July 27): 67% good-to-excellent (holding steady from prior week). Grain fill stage advancing; yield outcome increasingly locked in over the next three weeks.
Soybeans (week ending July 27): 64-66% good-to-excellent range; blooming progress continues above five-year average pace.
Spring wheat: Continued pressure from Northern Plains heat; prior week reading was 53% good-to-excellent, down 5 points over two weeks.
Drought: Roughly 19-20% of US corn acres in some degree of drought, concentrated in the western Corn Belt. Late July heat remains a factor ahead of final grain fill.
Global: Europe's heatwave maintained pressure on French maize (13-year low condition rating). EU wheat estimate at 140.8 Mt; Black Sea harvest partially offsets European losses.
Bottom Line
  • The crop picture matters more this week than usual because Thursday's PCE report will show May's savings rate - built before the current drought conditions were fully priced into food costs. The next CPI reading (August 12) will be the first to capture both the Section 301 tariff effects and any July grain price movement. If corn ratings slip below 65% before grain fill completes, grain and food prices will be a contributing factor in that print alongside energy and tariffs.

Sellers cutting asking price: 35.4% near last August's record (Redfin)

Bottom Line
  • Monday's Treasury yield move - the 10-year settling near 4.65%, down from 4.69% - is modest but directionally meaningful. The 10-year remains above 4.60%, which means Thursday's Freddie Mac reading may not yet show a break in the uptrend. A more explicitly dovish Fed statement Wednesday could push yields below that threshold and allow the August mortgage rate to pause or decline for the first time since mid-June. That is the first step toward affordability improvement, but the gap between 6.50% and the roughly 5.50% needed to meaningfully expand buyer purchasing power is still measured in years of rate normalization, not weeks.
  • The foreclosure data continuing to accelerate - 20-23% YoY increases across starts, completions, and filings - reflects a cohort of homeowners who absorbed the 2022-2023 rate shock and are now running out of buffer. A 4.44% delinquency rate on all mortgages and 11.88% on FHA loans indicates the stress is concentrated in the lower-equity, lower-income segment. Lower crude oil and eventual mortgage rate relief help this group, but slowly and only if the labor market holds.

Household Finance

Thursday's June PCE and savings rate update is the week's most important household read. May's 3.0% savings rate was built before $4 gas and Section 301 tariff costs hit budgets. The June number will be the first post-conflict household data point.

Total household debt: $18.8 trillion record
Credit card debt: $1.25 trillion +5.9% YoY; avg new-offer APR 23.8%
Card balances 90+ days late: 13.1% highest in roughly 15 years
Avg. new-car payment: $777/mo third consecutive record; avg $44,156 financed at 7.0% APR
84+ month auto loans: 23.9% of new financings record
BNPL users paying late: 47% +6 pts YoY; 29% now use BNPL for groceries
Personal savings rate: 3.0% (May 2026; June reading due Thursday)
Bottom Line
  • Thursday's savings rate will be the market's first look at whether June's oil surge and tariff transition began squeezing the savings buffer or whether households drew on credit instead. A reading below 2.5% would signal accelerating balance-sheet pressure. A reading at 3% or above would suggest the consumer has more cushion than the debt metrics imply - and would be bullish for the Amazon earnings call that afternoon. The two data points land within hours of each other for a reason: the savings rate is the consumption capacity number, and Amazon reports the consumption behavior number.
  • The structural divide this briefing returns to most often - between the balance-sheet-positive household clearing 4.50% on a CD and the cardholder at 23.8% APR with a 90-day delinquency - is not improved by lower oil prices alone. The Section 301 tariff cost increases on imported consumer goods land on the second group. The first group sees a net improvement when energy costs fall. Two different economies, same week of data.

Crypto

Bitcoin ended Monday near $65,000 - range-bound for a fourth consecutive week. Ethereum outperformed, gaining more than 4%. The FOMC decision Wednesday is the near-term catalyst - a hold with dovish language could break the range higher; a hawkish surprise would do the opposite.

Bitcoin (BTC): ~$65,000 modest gain Monday; still range-bound between $63K and $66K for a month
Ethereum (ETH): ~$1,960 +4.4% Monday; outperforming BTC on the day as risk appetite improved on oil decline
Range behavior: BTC has now failed to close above $66,500 or below $63,000 for four consecutive weeks; the band is tightening ahead of a high-event Wednesday.
Bottom Line
  • Ethereum's 4.4% gain outpacing Bitcoin's modest move Monday is worth noting: ETH is more sensitive to risk-on sentiment shifts because it has more direct exposure to DeFi and developer activity that benefit from improving macro conditions. BTC's relative underperformance on a day with a clear geopolitical relief catalyst suggests institutional positioning that is cautious and waiting for Wednesday's Fed. A hold with language that restores cut expectations for September or November would remove the Treasury-yield headwind that has kept BTC's ceiling near $66,500. A hawkish surprise renews yield pressure and sends BTC back toward $63,000.
  • The overhead supply problem above $66,500 remains unchanged regardless of tomorrow's Fed language. Anyone who bought BTC above $75,000 on the way to the $126,000 all-time high is still sitting on a loss. That trapped supply has capped every rally. Resolving it structurally requires either a sustained fundamental catalyst or time - neither of which the FOMC decision provides on its own.

Around the World

Asian markets opened Monday sharply higher on the Iran pause before chip weakness trimmed gains. European markets closed up broadly. The dollar softened as hold expectations solidified and yields fell.

Japan: Nikkei opened up more than 2% on oil relief before paring gains as chips fell; yen strengthened slightly against the dollar as the trade-deficit calculation improved.
Europe: STOXX 600 gained roughly 0.8%; energy stocks fell as oil dropped; defense and banking names rose on Iran-pause optimism. London and Frankfurt both up.
China: Shanghai Composite rose modestly; Hang Seng outperformed as oil-importing economies benefited from energy cost relief. Chinese tech names mixed.
Dollar index (DXY): ~101.3 pulling back from one-month high of 101.5 hit July 23; yield differentials compressed modestly; EM currencies gained
Emerging markets: MSCI EM up as dollar weakened and energy import costs fell; largest gainers were the most oil-dependent economies from last month's selloff.
Bottom Line
  • The global picture clarified today. Oil-importing economies - Japan, South Korea, most of Europe, and the oil-heavy EM basket - all benefited directly from Brent below $90. The dollar softened slightly as the yield differential between US Treasuries and overseas debt compressed on the rate-hike-odds move. A weaker dollar is net positive for EM dollar-denominated debt and reduces pressure on the reserves of commodity-importing countries that have been burning through holdings to defend their currencies against the energy-driven dollar bid.
  • Japan's situation is the sharpest example of the geopolitical-to-financial transmission. Japan imports virtually all of its crude; a sustained Brent below $90 directly improves the trade balance, reduces inflation pressure on the Bank of Japan, and gives the yen room to recover from its energy-driven depreciation. Monday's Nikkei morning surge reflected all of that in real time. The chip-sector weight in the Nikkei 225 limited the close, but the macro direction for Japan improved more in a single session than it had in three weeks of conflict.
